# Chapter 22 – Waking

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 10215-10301 (expanded)*

---

A thin gray light filtered through the blinds when Jasper felt her fingers twitch.

He'd been drifting in that strange space between waking and sleeping, his body refusing to fully surrender to exhaustion even as the hours wore on. The hospital room had taken on the particular quality of early morning—the shift change rustling in the hallway, the coffee cart beginning its rounds, the world slowly remembering that time still passed.

Her hand moved again in his, and this time her fingers curled, weak but deliberate, around his palm.

"Elena?"

Her eyelids fluttered—a slow, uneven motion, like someone surfacing from deep water. Her gaze wandered the ceiling for a moment before finding his face, and when it did, something shifted in her expression. Recognition. Relief. And beneath both, something harder to name.

Her voice was a faint rasp, barely more than a whisper. "You're here..."

"I never left," he said quickly, leaning forward until his face was close to hers. "You scared the hell out of me."

She blinked slowly, processing. Her eyes tracked the room—the monitors, the IV stand, the institutional curtains that couldn't quite hide the view of the parking garage. When her gaze returned to him, it carried the weight of a woman who had been carrying too much for too long.

"How long?" she asked.

"Since they brought you in. I've been right here."

A small sound escaped her—not quite a laugh, not quite a sigh. "That's new."

---

The words should have stung more than they did. Maybe because he'd spent the whole night saying worse things to himself. Maybe because hearing them from her felt less like an accusation and more like a fact they both had to acknowledge before they could move past it.

"You weren't here before they took me in," she said, her voice still rough but gaining strength. Not a question. A statement of what she remembered.

Jasper's jaw tightened. "I know."

"Where were you?"

He could have lied. Could have minimized, deflected, offered the kind of smooth explanation he'd perfected over years of managing other people's crises. But something had shifted in him during the long hours of the night watch. The old reflexes felt hollow now.

"With Alexandra," he said. "At her office. There was a meeting—the launch provider was threatening to walk, and she said it would only take thirty minutes." He heard how inadequate the words sounded even as he spoke them. "It took longer. And when I got back, you were already..."

He couldn't finish the sentence.

Elena's eyes held his, searching. "Harper's still in Asia with the Wildcard dilemma, and Sara was out with Grace. We had the politicians in Singapore breathing down our necks about the security breach—if it went public, it could've taken down two client portfolios overnight." She wasn't asking; she was reciting. She'd heard enough of these explanations over the years to know the script by heart. "You thought you could get in and out before they wheeled me in."

"Yes."

"You thought wrong."

He exhaled slowly, feeling the weight of it settle into his bones. "I did. I know I did. That's on me."

---

The nurse came in to check Elena's vitals, offering a warm smile and a quiet greeting that gave them both a moment to breathe. Jasper stood back while she worked, watching Elena answer questions about her pain level and her memory of the night before. Her voice was steadier now, her color slowly returning, but the exhaustion was still visible in the shadows under her eyes.

When the nurse left, promising the doctor would be in within the hour, Elena turned back to him.

"You've missed birthdays," she said. Her voice wasn't angry—that was the worst part. It was tired. Resigned. The voice of someone who had stopped expecting things to be different. "Recitals. Even Grace's first swim meet. But this—" she gestured weakly to the hospital room, the wires, the monitor still beeping its steady rhythm "—this was different."

"I know."

"Do you?"

The question hung in the air between them, and Jasper felt the full weight of what she was really asking. Not just *do you understand what happened*, but *do you understand what it means that it happened*. The difference between recognizing a mistake and recognizing a pattern.

"It won't happen again," he said, his voice low and certain.

"Jasper—"

"I mean it. Asia's getting wrapped. Harper can handle the minister and the embassy team. The whole thing was a cluster—political finger-pointing, backchannel deals, and three different agencies claiming jurisdiction. But that's not your problem. Not anymore." He leaned forward, holding her gaze. "I'll take the hit. Whatever it costs me professionally, I'll take it."

---

She studied him for a long moment, her expression unreadable. He'd seen that look before—the careful assessment of someone who wanted to believe but had been disappointed too many times.

"You love your job," she said finally.

"I love you more," he said without hesitation.

The words came out clean and simple, without the polish he usually applied to important statements. No carefully chosen phrasing, no strategic framing. Just the truth, raw and undefended.

Something flickered in her eyes—surprise, maybe, or the ghost of hope. But the line of her jaw stayed tight, the wariness still present beneath the surface.

"Then you need to start proving it," she said. "Not just telling me."

---

*Flashback — The Missed Recital*

The memory hit him with the force of a wave he should have seen coming.

Grace on stage, seven years old, wearing the navy blue dress Elena had bought for the occasion. She'd practiced her song for weeks—every night at the piano, the same melody drifting through the house until it became the soundtrack of their evenings.

Jasper was supposed to be there. He'd promised. He'd put it in his calendar, blocked the time, told his team he was unavailable.

Then the call came. A client in Chicago, a contamination crisis that was about to break wide open. Someone said the word "media" and someone else said "federal investigation" and before he knew what was happening, he was on a plane, watching the clouds roll by beneath him while his daughter took her bow to an audience that didn't include him.

Elena had told him later, her voice carefully neutral. Grace had searched the crowd when she finished, her eyes scanning the rows of parents with their phones out and their proud smiles. She didn't find him. But she'd still smiled through the applause—because that's who Grace was. She smiled even when it hurt.

In the car afterward, her first words were: *Did Dad come?*

Elena had said something gentle, something about work and emergencies and how much he'd wanted to be there. Grace had nodded, accepting it the way children accept things they can't change.

But something had shifted in her that night. Jasper could see it now, looking back. A small adjustment in her expectations, a tiny wall being built brick by brick. She'd learned, at seven years old, that promises weren't always kept. That sometimes the people who loved you chose other things instead.

---

Jasper's voice was quiet when he spoke again. "I'm here now. And I'm not leaving until you tell me to."

Elena's hand moved on the blanket, finding his. Her grip was weak, but it was there—a connection neither of them was ready to break.

"I want to believe you," she said.

"I know."

"I've wanted to believe you before."

"I know that too."

For a long beat, neither of them spoke. The monitor beeped its steady rhythm, marking time that suddenly felt precious in a way it hadn't before.

Then Elena closed her eyes—not in retreat this time, but as if she was allowing herself to rest knowing he was there. Trusting, even if only for this moment, that he would still be sitting in that chair when she opened them again.

---

The morning light grew stronger, painting the hospital room in shades of pale gold. Outside the window, the city was waking up—traffic building on the highways, people heading to work, the machinery of ordinary life grinding forward.

Jasper sat beside his wife's bed and watched her sleep. Her breathing had steadied, the lines of tension in her face softening as rest finally claimed her. He thought about all the mornings he'd left before she woke, all the evenings he'd come home after she was already asleep. The parallel lives they'd been living under the same roof.

His phone sat on the table, still silent. He didn't reach for it.

Instead, he thought about what it would mean to really change. Not the cosmetic adjustments he'd made before—the promises to be home by seven that turned into eight, then nine, then "something came up." Real change. The kind that meant saying no to clients who expected him to drop everything. The kind that meant letting Harper and Addison handle things he used to insist on doing himself. The kind that meant accepting he couldn't be everywhere for everyone.

The kind that meant being here—fully here—for the two people who had been waiting for him all along.

Elena's fingers twitched in her sleep, and he covered her hand with both of his.

"I'll prove it," he said quietly. "Every day. For as long as it takes."

The monitor beeped. The light shifted. And somewhere in the distance, his phone began to vibrate with messages he would answer later—or not at all.
