# Chapter 6 – Crosswind

*Source: Five Feet From Home/Chapter 6.docx*

---

The next morning began with the sound of rain against the hospital window. Not the light, easy kind, but the thick drops that hit glass with intent.

Jasper had woken before dawn, the stiff ache of the recliner in his shoulders. Elena was still asleep, the heart monitor a soft, reassuring pulse in the room.

He stepped into the hallway to stretch his legs. The hospital smelled faintly of coffee and antiseptic. For a moment, he allowed himself the quiet. Then his phone lit up with the kind of push notification that snapped his pulse into a sprint.

> **BREAKING:** CEO of Argent Dynamics Under Fire as Bribery Allegations Surface in Asia.

The photo below the headline was grainy, cropped from some industry gala. The CEO, half-smile frozen mid-speech. The kind of image that could be repurposed into a dozen narratives — none of them good.

---

He ducked into the waiting lounge and opened his laptop. The comms channel was already boiling:

> **Addison:** Leak's gone wide. Three outlets running with it.
> **Legal:** Denial statement or holding pattern?
> **Addison:** Jasper?

He typed: *Holding pattern. Map chain-of-custody on the story. Identify earliest timestamp. We find the leak first.*

> **Addison:** Street's reacting. Stock down 7% pre-market.
> **Jasper:** No public until we control the vector. Keep CEO off mic.

A bubble from Addison appeared, then stopped. A phone call came in from the CEO before he could guess what she'd been about to type.

---

"Barrett, you seeing this?" the CEO's voice was sharp, cutting through the static of an unstable connection.

"I'm seeing it."

"I want a denial, full-throated, by market open."

"You want control, not noise," Jasper said evenly. "If we rush and miss one factual tripwire, we hand them ammunition."

"This is a reputational bleed-out. You're not here, and I need you here."

Jasper stared at the rain crawling down the glass. "I can't leave D.C. today."

"You won't leave," the CEO snapped.

"Call it what you want. My position doesn't change."

Static filled the pause. Then, colder: "If you're not on the ground by tomorrow, we'll find someone who is."

The line went dead.

---

He closed the laptop and stayed there in the quiet for a long moment, watching the reflection of the hospital's fluorescent lights in the wet window.

When he finally went back to the room, Elena was awake, eyes tracking him as soon as he stepped inside. "Work?"

"Work," he admitted.

"Bad?"

"Bad enough."

She studied him, and he could see the assessment in her gaze — not just the problem, but what it would cost him to fix it. "You should go if—"

"No," he said before she could finish.

---

### Flashback – Five Years Ago

They were in Italy for a client summit, the first time they'd extended a work trip into something resembling a vacation. They'd been eating breakfast on a terrace in Florence when his phone rang with a different "bad enough" call.

He'd taken it. She'd watched the city wake without him for three hours. When he returned, she'd asked if he even remembered what the coffee had tasted like. He hadn't.

---

Now, in the hospital, Jasper crossed to the chair beside her bed. "I'm staying until you're discharged. After that… we'll see."

Her expression softened in a way that hurt more than anger. "Then you'll have to decide who you are, Jasper. Not just who they pay you to be."

---

By noon, the board was in full panic mode. Addison called from a secure line. "We traced the leak to a mid-tier analyst in Singapore — got him on record admitting it's speculation. If we move now, we can flip this before close."

"Draft the statement," Jasper said. "But I'll deliver it from here."

"You sure?"

"Yes." He glanced at Elena, who was pretending to scroll her phone but listening. "I can run the storm without being in its eye."

---

The press call went live at 1:15 p.m. Jasper's voice was calm, the phrasing deliberate. "We have confirmed the recent bribery allegations are without basis in fact. Our internal review identified the original source as a speculative comment made without evidence or insider information…"

Reporters pressed. He held the line, deflecting what needed deflection, answering what could be answered, refusing to let the rhythm break.

By the time the call ended, the stock had clawed back two points. Not victory — but the bleeding had slowed.

---

Elena looked at him after he closed the laptop. "So? Did you save the day?"

He shook his head. "Days don't get saved. They get bought. I bought one."

"And tomorrow?"

"That depends who I decide to be."
