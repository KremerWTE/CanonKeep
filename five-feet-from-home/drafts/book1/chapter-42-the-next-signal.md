# Chapter 42 – The Next Signal

---

**Epilogue**

Two days later, the world had returned to something resembling normal.

Jasper stood in his downtown office, the Charlotte skyline hazy under a humid Carolina afternoon. The air conditioning hummed its steady rhythm, and the usual sounds of the city filtered up from the streets below—car horns, the distant rumble of construction, the ambient pulse of a metropolis that never quite stopped moving.

His desk was clean for the first time in months—files organized, papers sorted, the accumulated detritus of crisis after crisis finally cleared away. Harper had remarked on it when she'd stopped by earlier, her eyebrow raised in that way that said she'd noticed but wasn't sure what to make of it.

"You feeling okay, boss?" she'd asked.

"Better than I have in a while," he'd told her.

It was true. The weekend had done something—reset something, maybe, or reminded him of things he'd almost forgotten. The weight that had lived in his chest for so long had eased by a fraction. Not gone, but lighter.

---

But his phone wasn't quiet.

It sat on the desk beside his laptop, screen lighting up with notifications that arrived in steady intervals. The world hadn't stopped demanding his attention just because he'd decided to pay attention to something else.

Three messages waited, each one carrying its own weight of implication.

**From Harper:**
> You're going to want to see this in person. Something's happening in Singapore that doesn't match any of our intel. I'm booking a flight tonight unless you tell me otherwise.

**From Caroline:**
> Ownership group meeting moved up. Certain people want you in the room. This isn't a request, Jasper. They need you.

**From Rafe:**
> Africa's not cooling down. Call me before someone else does. This one has teeth.

---

Jasper leaned against the desk, scanning the three messages like a man weighing which fuse might burn fastest.

Harper's tone was controlled, but he could read the urgency beneath it. Something in Singapore had surprised her—and Harper didn't get surprised easily. For her to be booking a flight tonight meant whatever she'd found couldn't wait for video calls and encrypted messages.

Caroline's message carried a different kind of pressure. The NFL ownership battle had been simmering for months, but "this isn't a request" suggested the temperature had just risen significantly. Chris Cole had been navigating the politics carefully, but there were limits to what even a star quarterback could do without the right people in the room.

And Rafe. *Africa's not cooling down.* The crisis they'd thought was resolved was apparently spawning sequels. *This one has teeth* was Rafe-speak for "people might get hurt if we don't move fast."

---

Three messages. Three brewing storms. Three demands on his time, his expertise, his particular ability to walk into chaos and create order.

Six months ago, he would have been on the phone within seconds. Would have felt the familiar surge of adrenaline, the buzz that came from being needed, being essential, being the man who ran toward fires while everyone else ran away.

Six months ago, these messages would have meant canceled dinners, broken promises, another chapter in the long story of absence that had nearly cost him his marriage.

---

But that was before the hospital. Before the night watch by Elena's bedside. Before he'd learned what it felt like to be five feet from home and realize that was exactly where he needed to be.

He thought about Elena, probably at the house right now, maybe reading on the porch or watching Grace practice her piano. He thought about the weekend they'd just shared—the party, the laughter, the quiet moments that had meant more than any deal he'd ever closed.

He thought about Grace's voice: *Dad, you're here a lot now. I like it.*

And he thought about the promise he'd made—not just to them, but to himself. To be different. To choose differently.

---

But.

The messages were still there. The storms were still building. And the truth that Jasper Barrett had spent his entire career learning remained stubbornly true: some fires needed to be put out by someone who knew how to handle them.

He couldn't save the world and save his family if he let the world burn while he sat on the sidelines.

The trick was finding the balance. Learning to run toward the fires that actually needed him while letting the smaller ones burn themselves out. Learning to be present at home without abandoning the work that made him who he was.

It was a balance he'd failed at for years. But maybe—maybe—he was finally starting to learn.

---

In the reflection of the window, he saw his own expression—that quiet, crooked smile that meant he'd already made his decision.

Not to ignore the calls. Not to pretend the world didn't need him.

But to handle them differently. To set boundaries he'd never set before. To say "I'll be there in the morning" instead of "I'll be there in an hour." To protect the time he'd carved out for his family the same way he protected the clients who paid for his attention.

It wasn't a perfect solution. It wasn't the dramatic transformation of a man who suddenly stopped working entirely. That wasn't who he was, and pretending otherwise would only lead to a different kind of failure.

But it was a start. And starts, he was learning, were sometimes enough.

---

He picked up the phone and started typing.

**To Harper:** *Book the flight. Brief me on the way to the airport. But I'm staying through dinner tonight.*

**To Caroline:** *I'll be there. But we're setting limits on this one. Non-negotiable.*

**To Rafe:** *Call me in two hours. I need to have a conversation with my family first.*

---

Down below, the city moved at its usual pace—cars and pedestrians and the endless churning of commerce and ambition. But somewhere, three storms were building.

And Jasper Barrett was already lacing up his boots.

Not rushing toward the fire without a backward glance. Not abandoning his family for the adrenaline of another dilemma. But doing what he did best—managing chaos, solving problems, being the man who walked into impossible situations and found a way through.

He just had to remember to come home when he was done.

---

He looked at the photo on his desk—Elena and Grace at the beach last summer, both of them laughing at something outside the frame. Elena's hair was windblown, Grace's face was smeared with sunscreen, and neither of them was looking at the camera.

It was his favorite photo. Not because it was perfect, but because it was real.

He touched the frame briefly, a small gesture that no one would ever see.

Then he picked up his jacket, slid his phone into his pocket, and headed for the elevator.

There was work to do. There always would be.

But first, he had a dinner to attend. A daughter to tuck in. A wife to kiss goodnight.

The storms could wait until morning.

---

*The End of Book One*
