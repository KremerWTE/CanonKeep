# Chapter 1 – The Call

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 1215-1347 (expanded version)*

---

The phone rang at 2:17 a.m., and by the third vibration Jasper Barrett was already halfway out of bed.

Years of muscle memory had taught him that hesitation was the enemy — in his line of work, seconds weren't just time, they were opportunity, leverage, and sometimes survival.

He reached for the leather go-bag leaning against the dresser. Always packed, always ready. The zipper pull was smooth under his fingers, worn from countless dawn departures.

Beside him, Elena stirred under the duvet. "Work?"

He didn't answer right away. The screen's blue light lit the room, throwing angles across the walls. The subject line was short and ugly: URGENT: GLOBAL CLIENT DATA BREACH – LONDON.

A second message pinged in from his deputy: Critical. Need you on the ground.

"Yeah," he said finally. "London."

Her sigh was slow and tired, a sound like air leaving a balloon you'd been holding too long. "Of course it is."

Jasper's life didn't leave much room for *of course*. He had built his career on the unpredictable, the disasters no one saw coming: billion-dollar product recalls, cyberattacks that could ruin reputations overnight, executive scandals with bodies buried in footnotes.

He'd started in corporate PR in his twenties, managing launch events and brand partnerships. A decade later, he was the man companies called when the thing they feared most actually happened.

Crisis was his currency, and he was rich in it.

It wasn't adrenaline that kept him in it — not exactly. It was control. In chaos, he could see the threads no one else noticed and pull them in the right order until the whole thing tightened into something manageable. That skill had made him valuable. It had also made him absent.

Down the hall, Grace's bedroom door was cracked just enough for a thin blade of hallway light to spill across the carpet. Seven years old, light sleeper like her mother. He thought about opening the door, about brushing a hand over her hair, whispering he'd be back soon. But he'd learned that goodbyes before dawn always left her anxious all day. Better to slip out unseen. Better to make the reunion, not the departure, the thing she remembered.

In the shower, hot water beat against the back of his neck while he cycled through the facts he didn't have yet. Breach scope unknown. Media status unknown. Stakeholder alignment unknown.

Five feet at a time — that was the only way to move. Don't think about the week ahead. Don't think about what's outside the circle you can control. Just towel off, suit up, tie straight.

At the kitchen counter, he set the laptop next to the coffeemaker. While the machine hissed and dripped, he skimmed the initial breach report forwarded from London:

> Intrusion suspected through third-party vendor access.
>
> Possible personal data exposure.
>
> Internal detection delayed — risk of external leak imminent.

The sort of worst-case scenario that made boards sweat and shareholders start calling their lawyers.

His phone rang again, the team patched in from three time zones.

"Scope?" Jasper asked, voice level.

"Preliminary—" the security lead began.

"No preliminary," Jasper cut in. "Facts only."

Silence, then: "Admin account compromised. Vendor login. We're still confirming what was accessed."

"Still confirming" meant no one could yet tell if the house was burning or just smelled like smoke.

"Prioritize client PII," Jasper ordered. "First list of affected accounts before I land."

Elena appeared in the doorway, robe tied tight, hair tucked behind her ears. She leaned against the frame, watching him the way someone watches a clock they know will go off again soon.

"I guess breakfast is out," she said.

"I'll make it up to you."

Her mouth tilted, but it wasn't a smile. "You keep saying that."

He closed the laptop and walked over, kissing her temple. She didn't lean away, but she didn't lean in either. "Tell Grace I love her," he said.

"You tell her," Elena replied, eyes holding his longer than he wanted to admit he noticed.

Outside, the car service idled at the curb, exhaust curling into the cool air. The driver nodded as Jasper slid into the back seat, already dialing his London client. The voice on the other end was sharp with panic; Jasper's tone was the opposite — steady, measured, each word chosen to put the room on rails.

By the time they reached the airport, he had his first twelve-hour plan mapped out in his head: initial containment, internal comms, external posture, legal prep. The rest could be adapted in flight.

Somewhere between the mental bullet points, he realized he hadn't thought about Elena's reminder last week of the beach trip they'd promised Grace. Or the spelling bee she'd been practicing for every night. Those things were outside the five feet, and the five feet was how he won.

For now, there was only the job.
