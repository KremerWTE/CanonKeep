# Chapter 7 — The Offer

*Source: Five Feet From Home/Chapter 7.docx*

---

By the third day, the hospital's rhythms had imprinted themselves on Jasper like muscle memory.

Morning vitals. A nurse's quiet check of IV lines. Grace's arrival after school with a backpack full of homework she barely touched.

The crisis in Hong Kong had cooled enough to drift into the "monitor" category, which in Jasper's world meant someone else could watch it while he pretended to rest.

He'd expected the reprieve to feel like air. Instead, it felt like silence before an aftershock.

---

The call came just after lunch.

Unknown number. D.C. area code.

He almost let it go to voicemail. Almost.

"Barrett," he said, leaning against the hallway wall outside Elena's room.

"Mr. Barrett, this is Senator Kirkland's office. The Senator asked me to reach out directly."

Jasper's posture straightened without permission. "Go ahead."

"She's heading up a new bipartisan task force — critical incident management. High-stakes corporate, infrastructure, and public safety. They need someone who can operate at the intersection of crisis response and private-sector pressure. Your name came up. A lot."

Jasper let the words settle. "You're talking about a government role."

"A senior one. Stateside. Strategic, but with enough operational control to make a difference." The aide's tone shifted — from pitch to warning. "But it means stepping back from day-to-day crisis contracts. You'd have a team, but not your current seat at the table."

---

He looked down the hallway. Elena was sitting up, talking softly to Grace, their heads bent close together. The sight hit him harder than the offer.

"Why now?" Jasper asked.

"Because you've proven you can walk into a fire without catching it. And because there are too many fires coming."

He said nothing. The aide filled the space: "We need an answer by Friday. We'll send the briefing packet."

---

Back in the room, Grace was telling Elena about a school art project. Jasper sat in the visitor's chair, letting their voices run over him like warm water.

He should have felt flattered. Challenged. Tempted. Instead, the offer felt like a mirror — one that showed him how far he'd drifted from knowing what he actually wanted.

---

### Flashback — Two Years Ago

It was a hotel room in Singapore, 3 a.m. Jasper on a video call with a Fortune 500 board while Elena texted him a picture of Grace's science fair ribbon.

He'd written back, *Proud of her.* She'd replied, *Wish you'd been there to say it out loud.*

He'd told himself it was temporary. That every sacrifice stacked toward a payoff worth the absence. He'd never defined what that payoff was.

---

That night, after Grace was asleep in the fold-out chair, Elena looked at him. "You've been somewhere else in your head all day."

He hesitated, then told her about the call.

Her eyebrows lifted. "Government work? You hate bureaucracy."

"I hate inefficiency. This would be… different."

She studied him. "Stateside?"

"Stateside."

Her gaze softened, but her voice stayed careful. "Would it pay what you make now?"

"No."

"Would it mean you're home more?"

"Yes."

She nodded, just once. "Then the question isn't whether you can do it, Jasper. It's whether you're willing to be someone who does."

---

The next morning, the briefing packet arrived in his inbox. He didn't open it right away. He sat by the hospital window, rain starting up again, and watched the drops race each other down the glass.

He could feel both worlds tugging — the one that had made him, and the one that might save him.

And for the first time, he wasn't sure which one was the bigger risk.
