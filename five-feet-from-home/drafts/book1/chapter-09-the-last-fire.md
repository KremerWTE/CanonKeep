# Chapter 9 — The Last Fire

*Source: Five Feet From Home/Chapter 9.docx*

---

It was a Tuesday evening, one week after Elena came home.

Grace was in the living room doing homework, sprawled on her stomach, pencil tapping in uneven bursts.

Elena was upstairs, napping after her follow-up appointment.

The Senator's offer was still sitting unanswered in Jasper's inbox.

He'd read the briefing packet twice, each time putting it back like it was a letter from a life he wasn't sure he wanted.

---

At 6:42 p.m., his phone lit up with Addison's name.

He almost didn't answer — they'd agreed she'd only call after hours if something was burning.

Which meant this was burning.

"Go ahead," he said.

"Pipeline breach," she said. "Oil. South Texas. Environmental nightmare. And—" She hesitated. "Media's already spinning sabotage. The board's panicking. They want you on the 8:15 to Houston."

"How bad?"

"Bad enough the Governor's office just called us. Bad enough if the sabotage story sticks, we're looking at federal investigations."

---

He stood at the kitchen window, looking out at the darkening street.

He could be packed and in an Uber in fifteen minutes.

That was the reflex — the muscle memory of a man who'd built his life on being first into the smoke.

But upstairs, Elena was asleep in a house that had only just started feeling steady again.

And Grace was humming to herself as she erased something, her pencil scratching like it was the most important thing in the world.

---

"Put Riley in charge," Jasper said finally.

"She's good," Addison said, "but she's not you."

"She's exactly who you trained her to be. This is her op now. I'll advise from here."

There was a long pause on the line. "They're going to hate that answer."

"They can hate it." He glanced toward the stairs. "Family first. Non-negotiable."

---

### Flashback — Three Years Ago

The call had come during Grace's seventh birthday party.

He'd stepped away to take it, promising "five minutes."

He'd missed the cake entirely.

When he came back, Grace had frosting on her cheeks and no interest in showing him how she'd blown out the candles.

Elena had looked at him across the room like she'd just stopped believing in something.

---

Back in the present, Jasper ended the call with Addison and walked into the living room.

Grace looked up. "You're not going out?"

"Nope. Thought I'd help with math."

"You're bad at math," she said, grinning.

"True," he said, sitting down beside her. "But I'm good at showing up."

---

At 8:15 p.m., somewhere in Houston, a room full of executives would be scanning the door, waiting for him.

They'd see Riley instead.

He pictured her — steady, capable — and felt something unexpected: pride.

He didn't know if it would cost him contracts, clients, maybe even his position.

But he knew, for the first time, the five feet around him didn't feel like a cage.

It felt like a choice.
