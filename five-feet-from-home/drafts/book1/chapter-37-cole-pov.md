# Chapter 37 – Back in the Fold

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 26049-26170 (expanded)*

---

**Cole's POV**

Cole watched Harper sleep across the aisle, her head tilted against the leather seat, that emerald scarf still knotted at her neck like a badge of office she couldn't quite remove. Even in sleep, her phone was clutched in her hand, the screen dark but ready to light up at the first buzz.

The woman didn't know how to turn off. Didn't know how to separate herself from the work in a way that would let her actually rest. He'd seen soldiers like that—the ones who came back from deployment and couldn't stop checking their six, couldn't stop scanning for threats, couldn't stop being ready for a crisis that might never come.

Harper wasn't military, but she had the same wiring. The same inability to believe that the world wouldn't fall apart if she stopped watching for five minutes.

He respected it. He also worried about it.

---

The jet hummed around them, the quiet rhythm of pressurized air and distant engines. Dean was still snoring in the back, his mouth open, his posture sprawled in a way that suggested he'd finally given in to the exhaustion they'd all been carrying.

Cole pulled out his phone and typed:

> *Africa done. Whitaker done. The gang's all yours this weekend.*

Jasper's reply came thirty seconds later:

> *Copy. See you at the house.*

Brief. Professional. Classic Barrett. But Cole caught something in those four words that he might have missed six months ago—a sense of anticipation that had nothing to do with work. Barrett was actually looking forward to this party. Actually wanted to be there.

That was new.

---

Dean stirred in the back, rubbing his eyes with the particular disorientation of someone who'd slept deeper than intended. "Tell me we're not landing into another crisis."

"No crisis," Cole said. "Barrett's throwing a party."

"A party?" Dean sat up straighter, blinking. "Like, with actual food and drinks and people who aren't trying to kill us?"

"That's the rumor."

Dean grinned—the first real smile Cole had seen from him in weeks. They'd been running on fumes for too long, operating in that heightened state that felt normal until you stepped out of it and realized how abnormal it actually was.

Encrypted tunnels at 2 AM. Back-channel meetings with people whose real names they'd never know. The occasional extraction that involved more firepower than anyone would admit on paper. It was the kind of work that left marks you couldn't see—the mental calluses that built up when you spent too much time in places where trust was a liability.

A party sounded almost impossibly civilized.

---

Cole opened the courier app on his phone and scheduled a delivery to the Barrett house:

> *Single malt. Top shelf. Note: "For after the last guest leaves."*

It was a Barrett tradition—the private drink after the public performance. Cole had shared that drink with Jasper a handful of times over the years, during the quiet hours when the work was done and the masks could come off. Those were the moments when he saw the real Jasper Barrett, not the crisis manager or the master negotiator, but the man underneath all the armor.

He hoped Elena got to see that man more often now.

---

He thought about Elena. He'd only met her a handful of times—once at a work dinner where she'd charmed the entire table without seeming to try, once at a charity event where she'd spent more time talking to the catering staff than the donors.

And once at the hospital, when he'd dropped off intel for Jasper during the worst of the surgery wait.

She'd been pale then, IVs in her arm, monitors beeping their steady rhythm. But her eyes had been sharp, focused, present in a way that some people couldn't manage even at full health.

She'd looked at him, this stranger in tactical casual, and asked: "Is he okay?"

Not "Am I okay?" Not "What's happening?" Not any of the hundred questions a person in a hospital bed might reasonably ask.

"Is he okay?"

That had stayed with Cole. The way she'd been thinking about Jasper even while she was the one in crisis. The way her love showed up as concern for him when she had every right to be focused on herself.

He'd told her what he could—that Jasper was on his way, that the team was handling things, that she should focus on recovery. She'd nodded, satisfied, and closed her eyes.

He'd left the room thinking that Jasper Barrett was luckier than he probably realized.

---

Before they landed, Cole made a quick call. Elena answered on the third ring.

"Cole?"

"Hey. Just wanted to say—this party's important. Not just for you."

There was a pause, the sound of someone processing something unexpected. "What do you mean?"

"It's the first time in months we'll all be in one place without a crisis. No encrypted comms, no exit strategies, no contingency plans. Just people who've been through hell together, actually getting to celebrate something."

The line was quiet for a moment.

"That matters," Cole continued. "More than you probably know. The work we do—it can eat you alive if you're not careful. Having something to come back to, something that reminds you why you do any of it... that's not optional. That's essential."

---

When Elena spoke again, her voice was warmer, softer.

"Thank you. For everything you've done. For keeping him grounded out there."

Cole almost laughed. "Ma'am, keeping Barrett grounded is above my pay grade. But I try."

"I think you do more than try."

He didn't know how to respond to that, so he just said: "See you this weekend."

He hung up as the plane began its descent, the Charlotte skyline emerging through the clouds. The Bank of America tower. The sprawl of suburbs giving way to the blue ridge of mountains on the horizon. The particular quality of Carolina light that felt like home even when you'd been gone too long.

---

Dean leaned over from his seat, his grin still in place. "You going soft on me?"

"Shut up and put your seatbelt on."

"That's not a denial."

Cole ignored him, watching the ground rush up to meet them. The landing was smooth, the kind of touchdown that came from pilots who'd done military runs in conditions that made commercial aviation look like a joke.

At the airfield, they collected their bags and piled into the waiting SUV. Harper, now awake and already on her phone, rattled off logistics—hotel reservations, the party timeline, the dozen small details that seemed to organize themselves around her like iron filings around a magnet.

But Cole was thinking about the weekend ahead. No encrypted comms. No extraction plans. No senators or warlords or situations that could go sideways in a heartbeat.

Just people he trusted, in a house that felt like home, celebrating something that didn't involve crisis or catastrophe.

It shouldn't have felt as revolutionary as it did.

But it did.
