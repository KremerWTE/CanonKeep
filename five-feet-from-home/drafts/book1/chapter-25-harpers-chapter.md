# Chapter 25 – Harper's Chapter

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 10516-10661 (expanded)*

---

Harper Chen had known Jasper Barrett for almost a decade—long enough to know that when he was locked onto a target, the rest of the world blurred into background noise.

That focus had saved more than one client from ruin. It had also nearly cost him Elena.

She sat cross-legged on the cream sofa of her Singapore hotel room, still in her work clothes from a day that had started eighteen hours ago and showed no signs of ending. The charcoal pencil skirt was wrinkled at the knees from too many hours of sitting. The crisp white blouse—rolled to the elbows now, the silk scarf she'd knotted at her neck discarded somewhere around hour twelve—still carried the faint scent of cigarette smoke from a ministerial meeting that had dragged on far too long.

Her blazer hung over the back of the chair, and her heels sat abandoned by the door. Outside the window, Singapore spread out in a grid of lights—the Marina Bay skyline glittering like something out of a travel magazine, utterly indifferent to the chaos of the day she'd navigated.

The Wildcard dilemma was officially contained. Barely.

Harper picked up her phone and saw Sara's text: *Elena's home. Jasper's staying close.*

She smiled despite the exhaustion pulling at her bones. That was something. More than something—that was everything.

---

Harper had always liked Elena. From the very first time they'd met, there was something grounding about her—a steadiness that counterbalanced Jasper's intensity in ways Harper had never quite been able to articulate.

She remembered that first meeting with the clarity of a photograph.

It had been in D.C., right after a brutal fourteen-day crisis cycle. The kind of stretch that left you hollow and wired at the same time, where sleep felt like a foreign concept and the boundary between work and everything else dissolved completely. Harper had finally dragged Jasper out of the office, insisting that they celebrate closing the deal even if neither of them could remember what day it was.

The bar had been one of those upscale places where the lighting was designed to make everyone look better than they felt. Jasper had looked like hell—rumpled navy suit that had clearly been slept in, white shirt unbuttoned at the collar, tie knotted in his pocket because he'd given up on formality somewhere around day ten. Harper had been in her own armor: black leather jacket over a silk cami, dark jeans, boots that meant business.

And then Elena walked in.

She wasn't dressed to impress—not in the way Harper understood the term. Dark jeans, ankle boots, a soft gray cashmere sweater that probably cost a fortune but didn't scream about it. Her hair was pulled into a loose braid over one shoulder, and she wore minimal makeup, if any. No high heels, no overdone smile. Just eyes that landed on Jasper like she'd already decided he was the only thing in the room worth looking at.

The moment Elena sat down, the whole temperature around Jasper changed.

He leaned in more, laughed easier. The tension in his shoulders—the permanent hunch of a man who carried the weight of other people's catastrophes—softened into something almost relaxed. Harper watched from across the table, nursing her drink, and thought: *Well, damn. He's in trouble.*

Good trouble, she'd decided. The kind that might actually save him.

---

Over the years, Harper had watched them weather the little storms that came with Jasper's work. The missed dinners that turned into missed weekends. The late flights that became early departures. The "just thirty more minutes" calls that stretched into hours, then days, then entire trips that expanded like gas to fill whatever container would hold them.

She'd also seen the good stuff—the moments that reminded her why they worked:

The way Jasper kept Elena's number pinned at the top of his phone. Not in favorites, not in recents—at the very top, where he'd see it first thing every time he unlocked the screen. A small thing, maybe, but Harper knew Jasper well enough to understand that nothing he did was accidental.

The way he'd step out to answer if Elena called twice in a row. Mid-meeting, mid-dilemma, mid-sentence—it didn't matter. Two calls from Elena meant something was wrong, and Jasper would excuse himself with a terseness that left no room for argument.

The way his whole demeanor shifted when Grace came up in conversation. The sharpness would fade from his eyes, replaced by something softer, almost vulnerable. He'd talk about her spelling tests and soccer games with a pride that had nothing to do with achievement and everything to do with love.

Harper had seen a lot of marriages fall apart in their line of work. The hours were brutal, the stress was constant, and the temptation to pour yourself into work instead of relationships was always there, whispering that the next deal was more important than the dinner you were missing.

Jasper and Elena had something different. Something worth protecting.

---

But this latest scare—Elena's collapse, the surgery, Jasper's scramble to get back—it hit Harper in a place she didn't want to admit existed.

She set her phone down and walked to the window, pressing her palm against the cool glass. The city hummed below, indifferent to her thoughts.

If Jasper broke, if his marriage broke, it wouldn't just change him at home. It would change him at work. And in their world—where split-second trust could be the difference between containing a crisis and watching it spiral into catastrophe—Harper's job depended on Jasper being at his sharpest.

She'd built her career alongside his. They'd been partners in every sense that mattered: anticipating each other's moves, covering each other's blind spots, trusting that when things went sideways, the other would be there. That kind of partnership wasn't something you could replicate or replace.

If he started second-guessing himself, or if the weight of a divorce sat on his shoulders during every high-stakes decision, the calculations would change. The risks would feel riskier. The wins would feel hollow.

And Harper—who had staked her own future on the assumption that Jasper Barrett would always be the best at what they did—would have to figure out what came next.

---

She turned away from the window and poured herself a drink from the minibar. The whiskey was expensive and smooth, the kind that came with the suite the client had insisted on. She didn't taste it.

If this scare didn't reset Jasper's priorities, she wasn't sure what would. She'd seen him push through impossible situations, navigate crises that would have destroyed lesser people, find solutions where everyone else saw only dead ends. But she'd also seen him miss the things that mattered—the recitals, the birthdays, the quiet moments that couldn't be rescheduled.

Elena wasn't the type to keep standing in place forever, waiting.

Harper had known women like her—women who loved deeply and waited patiently and one day simply... stopped. Not with drama or ultimatums, but with a quiet closing of a door that had been open too long without anyone walking through it.

If Jasper wanted to keep Elena, he'd have to start choosing her in the same way he chose the toughest crisis—full attention, no fallback plan. The way he walked into a war room and didn't leave until the job was done. That kind of commitment, directed at his marriage instead of his career.

And if he didn't?

Harper drained her glass and set it down harder than necessary.

If he didn't, she'd have to figure out how to keep building her career with a partner who was only half present. How to trust someone whose judgment might be clouded by grief and guilt. How to navigate a future that looked nothing like the one she'd planned.

She didn't want to think about what that meant.

But as she stood in her expensive hotel room, twelve thousand miles from home, Harper Chen allowed herself one moment of brutal honesty:

She was scared. Not just for Jasper and Elena. For herself.

Because the man she'd bet her career on was standing at a crossroads, and she had no idea which direction he'd choose.
