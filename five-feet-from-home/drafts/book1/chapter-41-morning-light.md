# Chapter 41 – Morning Light

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 27311-27350 (expanded)*

---

The smell of coffee drifted in from the kitchen, carrying with it the promise of a new day.

Elena, hair in a loose knot, was stacking plates from the counter with the careful efficiency of someone who'd been moving since dawn. She was wearing one of his old Duke t-shirts over yoga pants, the hem worn soft from years of washing—the kind of outfit that had nothing to do with appearances and everything to do with comfort.

Jasper stood barefoot at the sink, rinsing out the champagne flutes from the night before. The morning light streamed through the windows, catching the soap bubbles in small rainbows that lasted for a moment before popping.

It was the most domestic scene he'd participated in for months. Maybe years.

"You know," Elena said, loading the dishwasher with practiced movements, "we could've hired someone to do all this."

"And miss the fun of waking up next to you and cleaning up together?" he teased, not looking away from the sink.

---

She shot him a look over her shoulder, one eyebrow raised in that particular way that meant she was both amused and skeptical.

"You're lucky last night happened, or I'd make you scrub the tent too."

He laughed, drying his hands on a dish towel. "I'll take my chances."

The easy banter felt foreign and familiar all at once—like returning to a language he'd been fluent in once but hadn't spoken in too long. This was who they'd been before work had consumed everything. Before "just thirty more minutes" became the defining phrase of their marriage.

This was who they could be again, if he let them.

---

Grace padded in wearing her pajamas, rubbing sleep from her eyes with the particular unfocused look of a child who'd been up too late and wasn't quite awake yet.

"Is there breakfast?"

"Pancakes in twenty," Elena said, kissing the top of her head as she passed.

Grace climbed onto a stool at the island and watched her father move around the kitchen with quiet curiosity. He was pulling ingredients from the refrigerator—eggs, milk, the buttermilk pancake mix Elena kept stocked because it was Grace's favorite.

Something about the scene felt unfamiliar to her, he realized. Her father in the kitchen on a weekend morning, making breakfast instead of disappearing into his office or leaving for an "urgent meeting."

"Dad," she said finally, "you're here a lot now."

---

Jasper paused, a mixing bowl in his hands, and looked at his daughter.

She wasn't accusing. Wasn't asking anything complicated. It was a simple observation, delivered with the unfiltered honesty that only children could manage.

"Yeah, bug." He set the bowl down and moved to her side. "I'm trying to be."

Grace studied him for a moment, her expression serious in that way seven-year-olds sometimes got when they were processing something important.

"Good," she said simply. "I like it."

---

The words hit him harder than he expected. Such a small sentence. Such a massive weight.

*I like it.*

Three words that contained everything he'd been missing. All the recitals he'd watched on video calls. All the bedtime stories Elena had read alone. All the mornings Grace had woken up to find his side of the bed empty and his shoes gone from the mudroom.

She liked having him here. She'd noticed the difference.

And the fact that she had to notice—that his presence was remarkable enough to comment on—told him everything about what his absence had cost.

"I like it too," he said, his voice rougher than he intended.

Elena caught his eye across the kitchen, and the look she gave him was full of things neither of them said out loud.

---

Later, after breakfast—pancakes that turned out slightly lopsided but were declared "the best ever" by Grace—Jasper carried the folded tables back to the barn while Elena supervised the rental company's pickup of the chairs and linens.

The garden looked different in the daylight. The magic of string lights and candlelit conversations had given way to the reality of flattened grass and a few forgotten champagne corks. But there was something satisfying about the cleanup—the physical evidence that they'd hosted something real, something that mattered.

His phone buzzed—once, twice—messages from Harper, Caroline, and Rafe. New threads starting to spin.

**From Harper:** *Whitmore wants to move the ownership call to Monday. Thoughts?*

**From Caroline:** *The mentorship initiative is generating interest. Lunch this week?*

**From Rafe:** *Something brewing in Lagos. Not urgent yet, but want to flag it.*

---

Jasper stared at the screen for a long moment.

Three months ago, he would have responded immediately. Would have felt the familiar pull of urgency, the adrenaline spike that came with being needed, being essential, being the man who kept everything from falling apart.

Three months ago, he would have stepped away from his family and into his phone, never even questioning the choice.

He slipped the phone back into his pocket without replying.

---

Elena was on the porch, coffee in hand, watching the late-morning sun burn the dew off the grass. She'd changed into a simple sundress, her feet still bare, her hair loose around her shoulders.

She looked over at him as he approached, and something in her expression made him stop.

"Everything okay?" she asked.

"Yeah." He moved to stand beside her, looking out at the same view—the garden, the mountains in the distance, the particular quality of Carolina morning light that made everything look softer. "Just thinking."

"About?"

"About how I used to answer every message the second it came in. Like the world would end if I didn't respond immediately."

She was quiet for a moment. "And now?"

"Now I'm thinking the world will keep turning whether I reply right now or in an hour." He turned to look at her. "And I'd rather spend that hour here."

---

The smile she gave him was small but real—the kind that reached her eyes, the kind he'd been working to earn back for months.

"That's a good thought."

"I have them occasionally."

She laughed, the sound warm in the morning air, and he felt something shift in his chest. Not the sharp urgency of a crisis demanding his attention, but something quieter. Something that felt like belonging.

---

The mountains rose in the distance, blue and hazy under the Carolina sky. The Blue Ridge, they called them—ancient and patient, witnesses to everything that happened in their shadow.

For the first time in a long time, the horizon didn't feel like a deadline. It didn't represent the next deal, the next crisis, the next fire he needed to run toward.

It felt like home.

Elena leaned into him, and he wrapped his arm around her shoulders, pulling her close. They stood there on the porch, watching the world wake up around them, the coffee growing cold in her hands and neither of them caring.

Grace's voice drifted out from inside: "Mom! Can we go to the park later?"

"Ask your dad," Elena called back.

There was a pause, then Grace's voice again, closer now, appearing at the screen door: "Dad? Can we go to the park later?"

Jasper smiled. "Yeah, bug. We can go to the park later."

"Yes!" Grace disappeared back inside, her excitement trailing behind her like a comet.

---

He knew it wouldn't always be this simple. The phone would keep buzzing. The crises would keep coming. The part of him that craved the adrenaline and validation of being indispensable wouldn't disappear overnight.

But for now, in this moment, he was exactly where he needed to be.

And he was finally learning that being here—really here—was the most important work he could do.
