# Chapter 13 – The Pull

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 8636-8818 (expanded)*

---

The call came in just after dawn.

The hospital corridor was quiet at that hour, the fluorescent lights humming their constant refrain while the night shift nurses completed their final rounds. Outside the windows, the sky was beginning to lighten—a thin band of gray pressing up against the darkness, the city not yet awake but stirring in its sleep.

Jasper was still at Elena's bedside, half-asleep in the uncomfortable vinyl chair, when his phone started buzzing against the armrest. He silenced it twice, his fingers fumbling in the dim light, before Harper's text came through:

*Asia's unraveling. Rocket contract's in trouble here, too. Need you.*

He blinked at the screen, the words taking a moment to arrange themselves into meaning. Asia. Rocket contract. Need you. The familiar language of professional dilemma, reaching out to pull him back into its orbit.

He looked at Elena. She was sleeping, her face peaceful in a way it hadn't been for days—the monitors beeping their steady reassurance, the IV drip catching light from the hallway. The worst was behind them, the doctors had said. Now it was just waiting. Healing. Being present.

Grace was curled up in the other chair, her feet tucked beneath her, a hospital blanket pulled up to her chin. She'd insisted on staying the night, and neither Jasper nor Elena had the heart to argue. Her sketchbook had slipped from her fingers and lay open on the floor, the latest drawing visible: their house, rendered in careful pencil strokes, with smoke curling from the chimney and three figures standing in the front yard.

Jasper picked up the phone and stepped into the hallway.

---

Harper answered on the first ring.

"Talk to me," Jasper said, keeping his voice low.

Harper's voice was sharp and tired—the particular exhaustion of someone who'd been managing catastrophe for too many hours. "Bangkok deal's hanging by a thread. Regulatory approval got held up, and our partners are threatening to walk unless we can get someone to the ministry by end of week. In Singapore, the satellite integration is stalled—engineering says the specs don't match what we promised, and now there's finger-pointing between the contractors."

"And here?"

"And here." Harper exhaled heavily. "The launch provider for Alexandra's project just flagged a major safety concern—one that could tank her whole timeline. She's already calling the CEO, demanding a meeting. Says she wants you there personally."

Jasper leaned against the corridor wall, the cold cinder block pressing through his shirt. Through the narrow window in Elena's door, he could see Sara talking quietly to a nurse, her posture calm and professional even in the soft blue scrubs she'd borrowed from the staff. She'd barely left Elena's side since arriving, handling everything from insurance paperwork to hospital cafeteria runs with the quiet competence that made her indispensable.

"I'm not leaving the hospital," Jasper said automatically.

"You don't have to go far," Harper replied. "Alexandra's office is twenty minutes from there. One meeting, maybe an hour. The launch provider needs to see your face—someone with gravitas who can tell them we take their concerns seriously without rolling over on the timeline."

"Send someone else."

"There is no one else. Not for this. She asked for you specifically, and you know how she operates. If she doesn't get what she wants..."

The unfinished sentence hung in the air. Jasper knew exactly how Alexandra operated. She was brilliant and demanding, the kind of client who expected excellence and rewarded it generously—but who could turn cold in an instant if she felt undervalued.

"I'll think about it," he said, and ended the call.

---

Before he could pocket the phone, it lit up again. Alexandra's name on the screen.

He hesitated, then answered.

"Jasper." Her voice was silk over steel, warm on the surface with something harder beneath. "I'm sorry to bother you. I know Elena's still recovering. I heard about what happened, and I wanted to say—I hope she's doing better."

"She is. Thank you."

"Good. That's good." A pause, the sound of papers shuffling in the background. "But this is urgent. I wouldn't call otherwise, you know that. The launch provider is getting cold feet—some safety audit that turned up questions about our payload specifications. My investors are nervous. If we can't calm everyone down by noon, they're going to walk. And we both know they're skittish already."

"Alexandra, I appreciate the situation, but I can't leave right now."

"You could be here in twenty minutes," she countered, her voice softening. "It's just one meeting. You wouldn't even have to stay the whole day. Show your face, say the right words, and let me handle the rest. An hour, maybe less. Elena will still be sleeping when you get back."

Through the narrow window, he watched Sara adjust Elena's blanket, her movements gentle and precise. Grace stirred in her chair but didn't wake.

The temptation flashed through him—not just the work, but something else. The image of Alexandra standing close, her hand on his arm the way it had been at that charity gala last month, her perfume expensive and unmistakable. There had been a moment, standing in the garden with champagne glasses in hand, when she'd leaned in to say something and her lips had nearly brushed his ear.

He'd stepped back then. He'd always stepped back. But for an instant, standing in the hospital corridor with his wife sleeping behind a glass door and his career pulling at him from three different directions, he imagined leaning into it. Just to see where it might go.

The thought dissolved as quickly as it came, leaving something cold in its wake.

---

"No," he said, and the word felt solid in his mouth. Final.

"No?" Alexandra's voice cooled by several degrees.

"Tell the launch provider the timeline stands. If they have safety concerns, they can send them in writing. I'll review them tomorrow and we can schedule a call for Friday. But I'm not leaving the hospital today."

A pause. When she spoke again, her tone was professional and distant—the voice of someone recalibrating their expectations. "Understood. I'll handle it."

"I know you will. You're good at this."

"I learned from watching you."

She ended the call without saying goodbye.

---

Jasper stood in the corridor for a long moment, the phone heavy in his hand. He thought about the years he'd spent building his reputation—the midnight flights, the crisis rooms, the clients who believed he was the only one who could save them. He thought about all the times he'd said yes when he should have said no, all the moments he'd missed because somewhere, someone needed him more.

The hospital corridor stretched away in both directions—fluorescent lights, closed doors, the distant murmur of machines and voices. Just another building, really. Just another collection of rooms where people dealt with the things that mattered.

But through that narrow window, his world was waiting.

He pushed open the door and stepped back inside.

---

Grace looked up, her eyes still heavy with sleep. "Was that work?"

Jasper settled into the chair beside Elena's bed and reached for his wife's hand. Her fingers curled around his, warm and familiar, an anchor in the quiet room.

"Not anymore," he said. "I'm right where I need to be."

Elena stirred, her lips curving faintly. Her eyes opened halfway, found him, and softened with something that looked like relief.

"Good," she whispered.

Outside, the sun was rising, painting the hospital windows with pale gold light. Somewhere in the city, phones were ringing with urgent calls. Somewhere, deals were being made and crises were unfolding and people were scrambling to put out fires that seemed, in the moment, like the most important thing in the world.

Let them scramble.

Grace pulled her blanket higher and closed her eyes again, her breathing settling back into the rhythm of sleep. Sara glanced through the window and smiled, then continued her quiet conversation with the nurse.

Jasper held Elena's hand and watched the light change on the wall.

The five feet around him were enough.
