# Chapter 34 – Last Fires Out

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 26191-26320 (expanded)*

---

By the time Harper closed the conference room door behind her, the air in the office felt different.

Lighter, somehow. Like a weight that had been pressing on the building for months had finally lifted, leaving behind the particular silence that came after a storm.

Whitaker was done.

Rafe's final push on the investors had landed exactly the way Jasper had hoped—clean contracts, cash moving through the proper channels, no more late-night calls from panicked board members wondering if their portfolios were about to implode. The regulatory complications that had seemed insurmountable a week ago had been untangled with the kind of surgical precision that only came from years of experience navigating corporate minefields.

It was over. Really over, this time.

---

Jasper stood at the window of his office, watching the Charlotte skyline fade into the purple haze of early evening. The sun was setting behind the Bank of America tower, painting the glass facades orange and gold, the city settling into the particular rhythm of a weekday winding down.

On his desk, his phone buzzed with an encrypted message from Cole. He picked it up, scanning the words:

> Africa wrapped. Interim gov in place. Dean sends his love.
>
> P.S. Get a haircut before the party.

Jasper allowed himself something he hadn't in months—a slow exhale without thinking about what came next. The tension in his shoulders, so constant he'd almost forgotten it was there, eased by a fraction.

He typed back:

> Copy. Drinks when you're in town.

He set the phone down and looked around his office—the certificates on the wall, the photos on the credenza, the stacks of files that had seemed like monuments to indispensability just a few weeks ago.

It all looked different now. Smaller, somehow. Less urgent.

---

The rest of the afternoon passed on autopilot—a few small clean-up calls, forwarding a contract to legal for final signatures, telling Harper she could shut her laptop for the first time in two weeks.

She'd looked at him like he'd lost his mind.

"Seriously?" she asked, one eyebrow raised in that particular way that suggested she was waiting for the punchline.

"Seriously. Go home. Get some sleep. Whatever you do when you're not saving the world."

"I wouldn't know," she said dryly. "It's been a while."

But she'd smiled as she gathered her things—her emerald scarf, her burgundy tote, the laptop that had been her constant companion for longer than either of them wanted to admit. At the door, she'd paused and looked back.

"This is good, you know. The thing you're doing. Choosing home."

"I'm just going to a party."

"Sure." Her smile widened. "That's what you're telling yourself."

---

By five, Jasper was already walking toward the elevator, jacket over his shoulder, phone in his pocket but not in his hand. The cold Charlotte air hit his face as he stepped onto Tryon Street, sharp enough to clear his head but not unpleasant.

For a moment, he just stood there, letting the city move around him. People flowing past on their way to restaurants and bars and homes, all of them caught up in their own small urgencies, none of them aware of the dilemmas that had consumed his life for the past six months.

He thought about Elena, probably at the house right now, setting up for the party she'd insisted on throwing despite his protests that she should rest. Grace was probably helping, or more likely getting in the way in that cheerful manner she'd inherited from her mother.

For once, he wanted to be there. Not out of obligation, not because it was expected, but because he genuinely wanted to see them both. To be part of something that had nothing to do with work.

The realization was so foreign it almost made him laugh.

---

Mason picked the spot, like always—a half-lit whiskey bar on the edge of uptown, quiet enough for deals and confessions. The kind of place where the bartender knew when to refill your glass and when to disappear, where the leather booths offered privacy without pretense.

"Look at you," Mason said when Jasper slid into the booth across from him. "Man who's been living on caffeine and adrenaline for half a year finally looks human."

"Don't jinx it." Jasper waved at the bartender. "Two rye, neat."

The drinks arrived with the efficiency of a well-trained staff, and they clinked glasses without ceremony before taking their first sips.

---

They talked shop first—the comfortable territory of work that had always formed the foundation of their friendship. Mason had been untangling some mess for a boutique investment firm in D.C., the kind of work that lived in gray areas Jasper tried not to ask too much about. Lobbying, influence, the delicate art of making problems disappear before they became headlines.

"Senator Williams is sweating," Mason said, swirling his whiskey. "Some reporter's been asking questions about the offshore accounts."

"You handling it?"

"I'm managing expectations." Mason's smile didn't reach his eyes. "There's only so much you can do when someone's been stupid for too long."

They both understood what that meant. Some messes couldn't be cleaned up, only contained. The trick was knowing which was which before you got your hands dirty.

---

Then Mason leaned back, glass in hand, and the tone shifted.

"So. Party this weekend?"

"Yeah." Jasper took another sip, feeling the burn settle warm in his chest. "Elena's idea."

"She's feeling better?"

Jasper nodded, staring into the amber liquid. "Better enough to plan a guest list that could fund a minor political campaign."

Mason smirked. "Good. Maybe she's reminding you there's more to life than the next contract."

"Easy to say from someone who thrives on chaos," Jasper said, matching the smirk with one of his own.

---

Mason's grin faded just a fraction—not entirely, but enough that Jasper noticed.

"Difference is, I pick my chaos. You let yours pick you."

The words landed the way Mason intended—not sharp, not angry, but heavy with the particular weight of truth delivered by someone who knew you too well to lie.

Jasper rolled them around with his next sip, thinking about all the times he'd told himself the work was necessary, essential, unavoidable. All the times he'd convinced himself that the dilemmas chose him, that he had no agency in the matter.

It was a comfortable fiction. Easier than admitting that every dilemma was also a choice—to answer the call instead of ignoring it, to prioritize the urgent over the important, to run toward other people's fires while his own home smoldered behind him.

---

By the third round, they'd shifted to safer ground—old Duke stories, late nights fueled by cheap beer and cheaper pizza, the women they'd been stupid enough to chase. Mason reminded him of the time Jasper had skipped a midterm for a girl and almost failed economics.

"You'd think you'd have learned something about balance back then," Mason said, his grin genuine now.

"I did," Jasper replied, smirking despite himself. "Don't skip the midterm."

Mason laughed, the sound warm in the quiet bar, but his eyes stayed on Jasper a beat too long. The look of someone who was glad to see improvement but remained cautious about celebrating too soon.

---

They parted outside the bar, Mason heading toward his car while Jasper lingered on the sidewalk, breathing in the cold night air.

"Hey, JB," Mason called back. "Do me a favor this weekend?"

"What's that?"

"Actually enjoy it. Don't think about what's next. Just be there."

Jasper nodded, not trusting himself to respond without sounding defensive.

Mason got into his car and pulled away, the taillights disappearing into the flow of uptown traffic.

---

The drive home was different than usual.

The long road toward the mountains felt calmer somehow, the darkness pressing in from the trees on either side not oppressive but peaceful. No constant buzz of alerts on his phone. No Harper's voice in his ear with updates about some developing situation. No Rafe sending encrypted messages about things that couldn't wait until morning.

Just the music playing low on the stereo, his headlights cutting across the quiet road, the knowledge that he was driving toward something instead of away from something.

Inside his jacket pocket, his phone was silent. And for once, that silence felt like a gift instead of a threat.

---

Inside the house, the warmth hit him immediately—the smell of something cooking, the soft glow of lamps that Elena preferred to overhead lights, the general atmosphere of a home that had been lived in instead of just inhabited.

Elena was at the kitchen island, phone in one hand, glass of wine in the other. She was wearing one of his old sweatshirts over yoga pants, her hair in a loose ponytail, looking more relaxed than he'd seen her in months.

She smiled when she saw him—a real smile, unguarded, the kind he'd almost forgotten she was capable of giving him.

"You look… less tense," she said.

---

"Wrapped up Whitaker. Africa's handled. Harper and the boys are on their way back."

"Then you can enjoy the party," she said softly.

Jasper poured himself one last whiskey from the bottle on the counter, standing across from her. The kitchen was quiet except for the distant hum of the refrigerator and the soft tick of the clock on the wall.

He thought about Mason's words. About Jessica's words. About all the people who'd been telling him to wake up, to see what he was missing, to stop running toward other people's crises and start paying attention to his own life.

Maybe—just maybe—he was finally starting to listen.

"I plan to," he said, raising his glass toward her. "This weekend, I'm not thinking about work. Not once."

Elena's eyebrows rose, skepticism flickering in her expression. "That's a big promise."

"I know." He held her gaze, letting her see that he meant it. "But I'm making it anyway."

She studied him for a long moment, searching for the catch, the escape clause, the "unless something urgent comes up" that had invalidated so many of his promises before.

Whatever she found must have satisfied her, because her smile returned, warmer this time.

"Okay," she said. "I'll hold you to that."

"Please do."
