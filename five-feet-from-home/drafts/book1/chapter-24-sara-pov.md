# Chapter 24 – Sara's View

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 10443-10484 (expanded)*

---

Sara Whitaker had been through her share of late-night hospital corridors.

There was the time Emma had gotten pneumonia at two years old—the terrifying hours of watching her daughter's tiny chest heave with each labored breath. There was the night their father had his first heart attack, before the second one took him. There was the car accident that had put her mother in the ICU for three weeks, back when Sara was barely out of college and still believed that bad things happened to other families.

But walking down this particular corridor, with Grace's small hand in hers and Elena somewhere behind those double doors, felt heavier than all of them combined.

Maybe because this time, she couldn't fix it. She couldn't run the drills or call the shots or do the thing she was good at—taking charge, making plans, solving problems. All she could do was hold Grace's hand and wait, and Sara had never been good at waiting.

Grace had been quiet all morning, her usual chatter replaced with quick glances at every open doorway they passed. She didn't ask where her father was—didn't need to. Sara had seen the text from him earlier: *With Elena now.* That was something, at least. More than she'd expected, if she was being honest.

---

They turned into the waiting room, and Sara felt the familiar weight of institutional spaces settle over her. The TV was muted in the corner, playing some morning show no one was watching. The coffee machine hummed its tired song. Magazines from three months ago sat in neat stacks on tables no one touched.

The air smelled like burnt coffee and hand sanitizer—the universal scent of places where people waited for news they didn't want to hear.

Grace pulled a coloring book from her bag, settling into one of the vinyl chairs like she'd done this before. Like waiting was something she'd practiced.

*She has,* Sara thought, the realization landing with unexpected weight. *She's been waiting for her father to show up her whole life.*

Sara sat beside her, watching the girl's crayon trace neat, careful lines across the page. Grace was coloring a house—two stories, peaked roof, chimney with curly smoke rising toward a sun with a smiley face. Three stick figures stood in front of it, holding hands. Mom. Dad. Grace. The perfect family that existed in coloring books but rarely in real life.

*She's too used to making her own quiet,* Sara thought. *Too used to being the one who adapts while the adults figure their stuff out.*

---

Sara had known Jasper for almost ten years now—since Elena had first brought him home for Christmas, all sharp suits and confident handshakes and the kind of easy charm that made you want to trust him even when you knew better.

She'd liked him. She still liked him. Hell, she'd vouched for him more than once when Elena's patience ran thin and she needed someone to remind her why she'd married him in the first place. He was smart, successful, devoted in his own way. He loved Elena—Sara had never doubted that. And he loved Grace with the particular fierceness of a man who knew he wasn't around enough to earn it.

But the truth was, Sara had lost count of the times she'd been the one covering for him.

The school pickups when a call ran long. The birthday parties where she'd been the one helping blow out candles while he texted apologies from another time zone. The nights she'd stayed late at Elena's house, keeping her sister company because the silence of an empty bedroom was too loud to bear alone.

It wasn't that Jasper didn't care. Sara knew he cared. She'd seen the look on his face when he missed things—the flash of genuine regret before the professional mask slid back into place. He always had a reason, always had an explanation, always had a client or a crisis or a dilemma that simply couldn't wait.

But reasons didn't make up for absence. And explanations didn't fill the empty chair at the dinner table.

*Love has to look like showing up,* Sara thought, watching Grace add a rainbow to her drawing. *And Jasper too often lets that come second.*

---

Her phone buzzed in her pocket—a text notification that made her heart skip before she could stop it.

*Surgery went fine. Still unconscious. Staying here.*

Sara let out a slow breath, her shoulders dropping an inch. That was good. That was something. Elena was okay. Elena would be okay.

But even as the relief washed through her, she couldn't help picturing the look on Elena's face a few weeks ago, over coffee at that café downtown. They'd been talking about nothing important—school schedules, meal planning, the kind of domestic logistics that filled the spaces between bigger conversations—when Elena had gotten quiet.

"He's missing the little things, Sara," she'd said, her fingers wrapped around her mug like she needed something to hold onto. "Those are the ones you can't get back."

Sara had reached across the table and squeezed her sister's hand. "Have you told him that?"

"A hundred times. A thousand. I'm tired of saying the same words and watching them bounce off."

"Then maybe—"

"Don't." Elena had shaken her head, a sad smile tugging at her lips. "I know what you're going to say. And I've thought about it. But I love him, Sara. I just wish loving someone was enough to make them choose you."

---

Grace shifted beside her now, showing Sara the finished page. The rainbow arched over the house, every color perfectly placed within the lines. The three stick figures had gained details—hair, clothes, tiny smiles that matched the sun above them.

"Do you think Mom will like it?" Grace asked, her voice small but hopeful.

Sara smiled, tucking a loose strand of hair behind Grace's ear. "She'll love it, bug. It's beautiful."

"I'm going to put it on the fridge when she comes home. Right next to the one I made for her birthday." Grace studied the drawing for a moment, then added, almost to herself, "She likes it when I make her things. She always puts them up."

*Unlike your father,* Sara thought but didn't say. *Who has to be reminded your artwork exists.*

Instead, she pulled Grace into a side hug, feeling the girl's small body settle against her side with the easy trust of childhood. Grace was resilient—all kids were, in their way—but resilience had a cost. It meant learning to expect less. It meant building walls before you even knew what you were protecting yourself from.

Sara made a silent promise, pressing her lips briefly to the top of Grace's head: *If Jasper doesn't learn to keep his five-foot world where it belongs, I'll make damn sure you still grow up knowing what it feels like to come first.*

---

The morning stretched on in that elastic way hospital time had—sometimes racing, sometimes crawling, never quite matching the clock on the wall. Grace colored three more pages, ate the snacks Sara had packed, and eventually curled up in her chair with her head on Sara's lap, her breathing evening out into the rhythm of sleep.

Sara sat there, stroking her niece's hair, and let herself think about all the things she couldn't say out loud.

About how she'd watched her sister slowly disappear into the role of single parent while technically still being married. About the dark circles under Elena's eyes that had become permanent fixtures. About the way Elena's voice went flat and tired when she talked about Jasper's schedule, like she'd given up expecting it to change.

About the fear Sara carried—the one she never voiced—that one day Elena would wake up and realize she'd spent the best years of her life waiting for a man who was always on his way somewhere else.

*He loves her,* Sara reminded herself. *He does.*

But love without presence was just a pretty word. A promise unkept. A house with no one home.

---

The double doors swung open, and Jasper stepped into the waiting room.

He looked different than usual—rumpled in a way Sara had never seen him. His suit jacket was missing, his shirt wrinkled, his tie loosened like he'd forgotten it was there. There were shadows under his eyes that matched Elena's, and his jaw carried the rough stubble of a man who hadn't thought about shaving in days.

He looked, Sara realized with something like surprise, like a man who'd been keeping vigil.

"She's awake," he said, his voice rough. "She's asking for Grace."

Sara nodded, gently shaking Grace's shoulder. "Hey, bug. Time to wake up. Your mom wants to see you."

Grace's eyes fluttered open, unfocused for a moment before landing on Jasper. "Daddy?"

"Hey, sweetheart." He crouched down, putting himself at her level. "Mom's awake. Want to go say hi?"

Grace was off the chair and into his arms before Sara could blink, her small body crashing into his chest with the force of a week's worth of worry finally finding release. Jasper caught her, held her, pressed his face into her hair in a way that made Sara's throat tighten.

Maybe he was learning. Maybe this time would be different.

Or maybe it would be like all the other times—a burst of presence followed by a slow fade back into absence.

Sara gathered the coloring book and crayons, tucking them back into Grace's bag. She'd give him the benefit of the doubt, for now. For Elena's sake. For Grace's sake.

But she'd be watching.
