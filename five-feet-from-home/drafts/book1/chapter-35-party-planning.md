# Chapter 35 – Strings of Lights and Honest Words

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 26340-26400 (expanded)*

---

**Elena's POV**

The late afternoon sun spilled over the back lawn, turning the stretch of grass into a golden stage for the party-to-be. Tables were scattered under the sprawling oak trees, draped in crisp white linens that rippled in the gentle breeze. The air smelled like fresh-cut grass and the first hints of autumn—that particular Carolina scent of ending summers and beginning changes.

Sara was on a ladder stringing the last row of bistro lights between the oaks, her athletic frame making the climb look effortless. She wore jeans and an old NC State t-shirt, her hair pulled back in a practical ponytail, moving with the efficient energy of someone who'd spent years coaching others toward their goals.

Harper stood below, unpacking boxes of glassware with the kind of careful precision she brought to everything. The sleeves of her cream cashmere sweater were rolled to her elbows, and she'd traded her usual heels for sensible flats—a concession to the uneven lawn that Elena suspected had taken some internal negotiation.

---

Elena knelt by the garden edge, tucking fresh hydrangea stems into low vases that would line the main table. The blossoms were soft blue and lavender, colors that would catch the string lights once evening fell. She'd chosen them herself from the florist downtown, taking more time than was probably necessary to find exactly the right shades.

She was determined the party wouldn't feel like a corporate gala—one of those polished affairs where everyone smiled too hard and no one said anything real. This was about gratitude. Gratitude for surviving. Gratitude for the people who had kept her and Grace afloat during the worst weeks of her life.

Every detail mattered because every person who'd helped them mattered.

"Tell me you're at least delegating some of this," Harper said, setting champagne flutes onto the bar cart with practiced efficiency.

"I delegated you," Elena replied, looking up with a smirk.

---

Sara climbed down from the ladder, brushing her hands on her jeans. Her eyes swept over the progress—the tables, the lights, the flower arrangements coming together—and settled on Elena with that familiar mix of affection and exasperation.

"Don't let her fool you, Harper—she's been micromanaging like Jasper on a bad day."

"I learned from the best," Elena said dryly.

Sara snorted. "Yeah, and look how well that's working out for him."

---

Elena didn't argue. The truth was, she needed this to be perfect.

Not for appearances—she'd never cared much about what people thought, not in the way Jasper's professional circle seemed to require. But for herself. For the feeling of accomplishment that came with creating something beautiful, something that would bring people together for reasons that had nothing to do with crisis or obligation.

For months, she'd felt like a passenger in her own life—watching things happen to her instead of making them happen. The surgery, the recovery, the slow rebuilding of strength she'd taken for granted. This party was her way of taking back control. Of saying: I'm still here. I can still do this.

She was still here.

---

When Sara went inside to grab more extension cords, Harper joined Elena at the hydrangeas, lowering her voice.

"How are you really doing? Not the polite answer."

Elena's fingers stilled on the ribbon around a vase. She appreciated Harper's directness—it was one of the things she'd come to value about Jasper's right hand. In a world of careful diplomacy and calculated responses, Harper's refusal to accept surface-level answers was refreshing.

"Physically, better every week. PT says I'm cleared for… everything." She smiled faintly at the implications. "Emotionally? That's trickier."

Harper nodded, her expression softening without losing its focus. "I know it's been hard."

"Hard doesn't begin to cover it." Elena turned the vase in her hands, watching the light play across the petals. "But I'm working on it. Working on what comes next."

---

"I know Jasper," Harper said. "He throws himself into the fire because he thinks that's how he keeps everyone safe. If he's solving problems, he's protecting people. If he's working, he's providing. He doesn't know any other way."

"I know." Elena's voice was quiet. "That's what makes it so frustrating. I understand why he does it. I even respect it, in a way. But understanding doesn't make it hurt less when you're the one standing in an empty kitchen at midnight, wondering if he's ever coming home."

Harper's hand found her shoulder, warm and steady. "He's trying. I've seen it. These past few weeks, he's been different."

"Different," Elena repeated. "Yes. But different doesn't mean permanent. Different might just mean 'until the next crisis.'"

---

The afternoon sun was starting its descent, painting longer shadows across the lawn. Somewhere behind them, Sara was wrestling with an extension cord that had gotten tangled in the shed.

"Here's the truth," Harper said, her voice low enough that only Elena could hear. "If you wait for the work to slow down, you'll be waiting forever. There's always going to be another deal, another client, another fire that needs putting out. You have to decide the line before the line decides you."

Elena met her eyes, feeling the weight of the words. "And what if I draw that line and he's on the other side?"

Harper held her gaze, unflinching. "Then you invite him over. And if he doesn't come, at least you know you didn't just wait by the phone."

---

The silence stretched between them, filled with the soft rustle of leaves and the distant call of birds settling for the evening.

Elena thought about all the times she'd waited. All the nights she'd fallen asleep with her phone on the pillow beside her, hoping for the text that said he was on his way home. All the mornings she'd woken to find his side of the bed cold, the coffee maker programmed but no one there to share it with.

She'd been waiting for years. Waiting for him to notice what he was missing. Waiting for the wake-up call that would finally make him see.

The surgery had been that wake-up call. She just wasn't sure yet if it had worked.

---

Sara reappeared with the cords and a tray of iced tea, breaking the moment with her characteristic energy.

"I'm putting Harper in charge of the bar. She looks like she could charm a man into drinking kombucha."

Harper laughed, the sound warm in the afternoon air. "Sugar, I could charm a man into drinking anything."

"I believe you," Sara said. "I've seen you work a room."

Elena smiled, grateful for the shift in tone. This was what she'd needed—not just the party, but the presence of women who understood. Who'd been through their own versions of waiting and wondering and deciding what mattered.

---

Later, as they finished the setup, a delivery van pulled up the gravel drive. The driver carried out an elegant bouquet—white roses and pale green eucalyptus, arranged with understated sophistication—and handed it to Elena.

The card read:

> *For the host who survived more than one kind of battle. — Harper*

Elena looked up, finding Harper's eyes across the lawn.

"You already sent flowers?"

Harper shrugged, adjusting a tablecloth that didn't need adjusting. "I sent those before I left Lagos. Figured you could use the encouragement."

"You were in Lagos when you sent these?"

"I was in Lagos for a lot of things." Harper's smile was smaller now, more genuine. "Including making sure you knew someone was thinking about you from the other side of the world."

---

Elena held the bouquet carefully, feeling the cool stems against her fingers. She thought about everything Harper had said—about lines and choices and inviting people over instead of waiting for them to show up.

She thought about Jasper, probably on his way home right now, hopefully thinking about the party and not about the next crisis waiting in his inbox.

She thought about the woman she'd been before the surgery—the one who'd been running on fumes and hope, waiting for something to change.

And she thought about the woman she wanted to be now—the one who decided what she deserved and didn't apologize for expecting it.

"Thank you," she said to Harper. "For the flowers. And for everything else."

"Don't thank me yet," Harper replied. "The real test is whether that man of yours shows up tomorrow ready to be present. Because I've been telling him the same things I've been telling you, and I'm not entirely sure which one of you is actually listening."

---

Elena laughed—the first real laugh she'd felt in weeks. Not because it was funny, but because it was true. Because sometimes the truth was so perfectly absurd that laughter was the only appropriate response.

"Then I guess we'll find out tomorrow."

"I guess we will."

The sun continued its descent, painting the sky in shades of rose and gold. The bistro lights were all strung now, waiting for darkness to show their glow. The tables were set, the flowers arranged, everything in its place for a gathering that would happen in less than twenty-four hours.

Elena stood in the middle of her garden, surrounded by preparations for a party that was about gratitude and survival and new beginnings.

And for the first time in a long time, she felt ready for whatever came next.
