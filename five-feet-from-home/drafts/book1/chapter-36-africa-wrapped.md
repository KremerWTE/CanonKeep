# Chapter 36 – Closing Africa & Coming Home

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 26031-26148 (expanded)*

---

**Harper's POV**

The hotel room in Lagos smelled like recycled air and old coffee—that particular scent of too many hours in the same space, the kind that seeped into everything no matter how expensive the linens.

Harper sat at the desk, laptop open, the final signed agreement glowing on the screen. The cursor blinked at the bottom of the document, waiting for nothing. There was nothing left to add. The interim government was in place. The coup leaders were out. The American interests were secure.

Done.

For weeks, this moment had felt impossibly distant—a finish line that kept moving every time she got close. Now that it was here, she wasn't sure what to feel. Relief, certainly. Exhaustion, definitely. But also something that felt almost like emptiness, the particular hollow sensation that came when a crisis that had consumed your life suddenly stopped existing.

---

She leaned back in the chair, the charcoal blazer draped over the armrest, silk blouse rolled to her elbows in a way that would have scandalized her mother. Through the window, Lagos sprawled in humid chaos—a city of contrasts where gleaming high-rises jutted up from crowded streets, where the sound of car horns and call to prayer mixed into a symphony that never quite stopped.

It was a world away from Charlotte. From Elena's garden party. From anything resembling normal.

Her phone buzzed on the desk. Cole's face flashed on the screen—that particular image she'd taken of him during the Singapore trip, looking more exhausted than she'd ever seen him but still managing to smirk at the camera.

"Tell me you're on a plane," he said when she answered.

"Twelve hours. You and Dean on board?"

"Already at the airfield. We're just waiting on you and your designer luggage."

---

Harper smiled despite herself. The "designer luggage" was a single carry-on and the burgundy tote she'd been hauling across continents for six years now. But Cole had been teasing her about her style since the day they'd met, and at this point it was more tradition than insult.

"I'll be there in twenty," she said. "And Dean better not have eaten all the protein bars."

"Can't make any promises. Man's been stress-eating since Kinshasa."

She could hear Dean's indignant protest in the background, Cole's low laugh, the ambient noise of wind and engine that meant they were already at the airfield. The boys—that's what she'd started calling them, even though both were older than her and had seen combat she couldn't imagine—had been in the field for three weeks straight. Encrypted comms, back-channel negotiations, the kind of work that never made it into any report and never would.

They deserved a drink and a weekend without gunfire.

Hell, they all did.

---

Before she left, she made one more call.

Elena picked up on the second ring, her voice carrying that particular warmth that Harper had come to associate with the Barrett home—genuine, unguarded, the kind of welcome that didn't require anything in return.

"Harper?"

"Hey. Just confirming—I'm wheels up tonight. I'll be there for your party."

There was a pause, the kind that carried emotion without words. "You didn't have to fly across the world for this."

"Please. I've missed enough of your life for one year." Harper stood, walking to the window, watching the city lights begin to flicker on as dusk settled over Lagos. "Besides, I expect champagne."

Elena laughed—a real laugh, the sound of someone who was finally starting to feel like herself again. "I think we can manage that."

---

Harper hung up and grabbed her tote—burgundy leather, scuffed at the corners from too many terminals and too many security checkpoints. The emerald scarf was already knotted at her neck, a habit she'd developed years ago as a way of looking put-together even when she felt like falling apart.

In the elevator, she caught her reflection in the mirrored doors. Tired eyes with dark circles that concealer couldn't quite hide. Hair that needed attention—the humidity had done a number on her careful styling. Lines around her mouth that hadn't been there a year ago.

But there was something else, too. The quiet satisfaction of a job finished. The knowledge that the work she'd done here had mattered, even if no one outside a very small circle would ever know about it.

She'd helped prevent a civil war. She'd kept American interests protected without starting an international incident. She'd navigated politics so delicate that one wrong word could have destabilized an entire region.

Not bad for a girl from Savannah who'd started out organizing charity galas.

---

At the airfield, Cole and Dean were leaning against the charter jet, duffel bags at their feet. Cole was in tactical pants and a faded polo, the casual look of a man who'd traded his uniform for civilian clothes but never quite stopped being a soldier. Dean was scrolling through his phone, his posture relaxed but his eyes still scanning the perimeter—a habit, Harper suspected, that would never fully disappear.

"You look like you survived a war," Cole said as she approached.

"I look like I ended one," Harper replied, climbing the stairs to the jet without breaking stride.

Behind her, Dean chuckled. "Same old Harper."

"Damn right."

---

The flight was long enough to feel endless but short enough to remind her that real rest would have to wait until Charlotte. Harper settled into the leather seat across from Cole, watching the African continent fall away beneath the clouds.

Dean was already asleep in the back, his snoring a low rumble that had become oddly comforting over the past few weeks. Some people found it annoying; Harper found it reassuring. It meant Dean felt safe enough to actually sleep, which was rarer than it should be.

Cole was scrolling through encrypted updates, his expression neutral in that way that meant he was processing more than he was showing.

"Barrett's clear on Whitaker," he said without looking up. "Africa's done. First time in months we're all off the grid."

"Don't jinx it," Harper murmured, but she felt the truth of it settling into her bones.

Off the grid. No active crises. No fires that needed putting out. For the first time in longer than she could remember, the world wasn't demanding her immediate attention.

---

She closed her eyes, thinking about Elena in that garden, setting up tables under the oaks, trying to reclaim something that work had almost stolen from her.

Harper had watched a lot of marriages strain under the weight of careers like theirs. She'd seen couples who started out passionate end up strangers, too exhausted to fight and too proud to admit defeat. She'd seen children grow up with absent parents and spouses grow old waiting for someone who never quite made it home.

Elena and Jasper's marriage could have gone that way. Might still go that way, if Jasper couldn't sustain the changes he'd been making.

But Harper respected Elena more than she'd realized until recently. The woman had held her family together through years of being second priority, had raised a daughter who adored a father she barely saw, had maintained a home that was warm and welcoming even when it was usually empty.

That took strength. The quiet kind that didn't make headlines but kept worlds from falling apart.

---

When Harper woke, the pilot was announcing their descent into Charlotte. The sky outside the window was Carolina blue, that particular shade that felt like home even after all these years away.

Dean was stirring in the back, rubbing his eyes. Cole was shutting down his phone, finally allowing himself to disconnect.

"You know," Dean said, his voice still rough with sleep, "I could get used to parties instead of crises."

"Don't get too comfortable," Cole replied. "Something always comes up."

"Thanks for the optimism."

Harper smiled, watching the familiar skyline come into view. The Bank of America tower. The new developments along Trade Street. The green spaces that Charlotte had somehow preserved despite all the growth.

Home.

Not permanently—she wasn't sure she was capable of that anymore. But for a weekend, at least, it would be enough.
