# Chapter 38 – The Thank You Party

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 27200-27242 (expanded)*

---

The evening arrived like a gift—warm air carrying the last breath of summer, golden light slanting through the oaks, the hum of string music drifting across the lawn.

Elena stood at the edge of the garden tent, watching the guests filter in through the soft glow of bistro lights. She'd chosen a simple dress—navy silk that skimmed her frame, the kind of elegant that didn't try too hard—and her hair fell in loose waves around her shoulders. For the first time in months, she felt like herself. Not the woman who'd been in crisis, not the patient recovering from surgery, but Elena Barrett, hosting a party in her own garden.

Harper was already at the bar, emerald scarf catching the candlelight, her laughter rising as she charmed a guest into trying the bourbon selection. Sara circulated with a tray of appetizers, stealing bites when she thought no one was looking, her athletic grace making even server duty look effortless.

Cole and Dean stood near the bourbon station, their postures still military-straight even in civilian clothes—Cole in dark jeans and a fitted blazer, Dean in a button-down that looked like it had been purchased specifically for the occasion. They were scanning the perimeter out of habit, probably without realizing they were doing it.

The garden was transformed. The tables Elena had worried over were perfect now, white linens catching the last of the daylight, hydrangeas spilling from low vases in blues and lavenders. The string lights created a canopy of warmth above them, and the music—something jazz and unhurried—set a tone that felt both sophisticated and welcoming.

---

Jasper moved through the crowd like he always did—handshakes, quick words, the practiced charm of a man who'd spent years making people feel heard. He was in a navy blazer over a crisp white shirt, no tie, the casual elegance that suggested he was finally relaxed enough to dress down.

But tonight, something was different.

Every few minutes, his eyes would find hers across the tent. Not checking in, not monitoring—just connecting. A look that said: *I see you. I'm here.*

She felt it every time.

---

Mason arrived with a bottle of whiskey and a grin that hadn't changed since college. "Figured Barrett could use reinforcements."

"Always," Elena said, kissing his cheek. She'd known Mason almost as long as she'd known Jasper—had watched their friendship weather distance and divergent careers, the kind of bond that survived because it was built on something real.

Behind him came Jessica, elegant in tailored trousers and a slate-blue blouse, her oxblood tote slung over one shoulder. Her silver hair was swept back, and her eyes—those sharp, assessing eyes that missed nothing—softened when they landed on Elena.

She pulled Elena into a hug that lasted longer than usual, the kind that communicated more than words could manage.

"You look strong," Jessica said quietly, close enough that only Elena could hear. "I'm proud of you."

---

The wives' circle arrived together, a tableau of elegance and influence that commanded attention without demanding it.

Maddie Cole in a flowy cream dress that moved with her, pearls at her throat, her smile warm and genuine as she surveyed the garden. Vivienne Ross in vintage Hermès, the kind of subtle wealth that came from generations of knowing exactly what to buy and when. Natalia Cruz in a sharp blazer over silk, her legal precision evident even in her fashion choices. Camille Whitmore in Southern florals that somehow didn't read as costume on her. Isabella Santoro in dramatic red, because Isabella always chose dramatic red.

They descended on Elena like a protective flock, all warmth and wine and carefully curated affection. These were women who understood the weight of being married to powerful, complicated men. Women who'd built their own empires while supporting their partners' ambitions. Women who knew the particular loneliness of waiting for someone who was always saving the world.

Maddie squeezed her hand. "You did this. All of it."

"We did this," Elena corrected.

---

As the night deepened, the tent filled with laughter and low music. Grace ran between tables with Sara's daughter Emma, their giggles cutting through the adult conversations with the particular joy of children who'd been given too much sugar and not enough rules.

Elena watched her daughter—the bright smile, the easy way she moved through the crowd, the complete absence of worry that children were supposed to have. Grace had handled the past months with a resilience that alternately amazed and concerned Elena. She'd been too quiet sometimes, too quick to disappear into her room. But tonight, she was pure seven-year-old energy, and that felt like a gift.

Jasper appeared at Elena's side with two glasses of champagne, the bubbles rising in steady streams.

"You did good," he said.

"I had help."

He didn't argue, just handed her a glass and let his shoulder brush against hers. The touch was small, almost incidental, but it sent warmth spreading through her chest.

---

"Walk with me?" he asked.

They slipped out of the tent, leaving the noise behind. The garden was quiet here, lit only by the string lights and the distant glow of the house. The night air was cool against her bare arms, carrying the scent of grass and the lingering sweetness of hydrangeas.

Elena stopped under the old oak tree—the one that had been there when they'd bought the house, the one Grace had learned to climb, the one that had witnessed a decade of their life together. She turned to face him, and something in his expression made her breath catch.

"You've been holding court all night," she said, trying to keep her voice light.

"That's the job." But his tone softened, the professional mask slipping away. "And tonight's about you."

---

She studied him—this man she'd married, this man she'd loved and resented and fought for in equal measure. The moonlight caught the silver at his temples, the lines around his eyes that hadn't been there when they'd met. He looked older now. He also looked more present than he had in years.

"You know," she said carefully, "when I was in the hospital, I thought about this. About us… and about how you can't turn that part of yourself off. Fixing things, connecting people. It's who you are."

He was quiet for a moment, the music drifting faintly from the tent behind them.

"Doesn't mean I don't choose you," he said finally.

"I know." She stepped closer, close enough to feel his warmth. "And for the record… PT cleared me for *all* activities."

---

Jasper's brows rose, a slow grin spreading across his face. "All activities?"

She bit her lip, a playful glint in her eye that she hadn't allowed herself in months. "All of them."

His hand found her waist, pulling her gently toward him. "That's good to know."

"But—" She pressed a finger to his chest, holding him at arm's length. "Don't you dare leave early from our own party."

He laughed—a real laugh, warm and unhurried—and leaned down to press a kiss to her forehead.

"Wouldn't dream of it."

---

They stood under the oak tree for another moment, wrapped in the quiet of the garden while their guests celebrated inside. The string lights swayed gently in the breeze, creating patterns of light and shadow across the grass.

Elena thought about all the parties she'd attended over the years—corporate galas and charity events and political dinners where the smiles never reached the eyes. This wasn't like those. This was real. These were people who'd shown up when it mattered, who'd held her family together during the worst weeks of her life.

This was gratitude made tangible.

And standing here with Jasper, his hand still warm at her waist, she felt something she'd almost forgotten was possible.

Hope.

Not the cautious, conditional kind. Real hope. The kind that believed tomorrow might actually be different.
