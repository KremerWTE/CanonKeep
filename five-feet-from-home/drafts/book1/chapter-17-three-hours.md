# Chapter 17 – Three Hours

*Source: book1_all_sources.txt lines 9307-9381 (expanded)*

---

Jasper had told himself it would be quick.

Thirty minutes in Alexandra's boardroom, sign a few papers, smooth a few egos, and he'd be back before anyone knew he was gone. That was the calculation he'd made standing in the hospital elevator, watching the numbers climb. That was the lie he'd told himself to make the decision feel reasonable.

But the launch provider dragged out every concern like they were being paid by the hour. Engineers asked technical questions that required technical answers. Lawyers circled every clause like vultures, picking at the bones of language until nothing recognizable remained. And Alexandra—Alexandra played the room expertly. A smile here, a lean there. Her hand on his arm when the conversation threatened to stall. Her voice, low and confident, steering them all toward the outcome she wanted.

Before he knew it, his watch read two hours, forty-eight minutes.

He'd checked it compulsively throughout the meeting, the numbers growing worse with each glance. Ninety minutes. Two hours. Two and a half. Every time he reached for his phone to excuse himself, Alexandra would touch his sleeve, give him a look that said *we're so close*, and he'd settle back into his chair like a man who'd forgotten how to stand up.

When it was finally over—when the contracts were signed and the handshakes exchanged and the launch provider's executives had filed out looking satisfied—Alexandra turned to him with that polished smile.

"See? That wasn't so bad."

He didn't answer. He was already reaching for his coat, already calculating how fast he could get back, already constructing the excuses he'd give.

---

The hospital felt different when he returned.

The waiting room was colder somehow, the fluorescent lights harsher. The magazines on the coffee table looked like they hadn't been touched since morning. A television played the evening news—something about the weather, something about a political scandal—but no one was watching.

A surgical nurse passed him in the hall without meeting his eyes. The way she walked—shoulders slightly forward, pace slightly faster than normal—made something twist in his chest.

He caught the surgeon's assistant at the desk, a young woman with dark hair pulled back in a tight ponytail and the kind of careful expression that told him nothing good.

"They're still in there?"

"Yes, Mr. Barrett." Her voice was professionally neutral. "The procedure is… more involved than expected. There were some complications. The doctor will update you when they can."

The words landed like a weight on his chest. Complications. More involved than expected. The kind of phrases they taught medical staff to use when they didn't want to say what they really meant.

"How long?" he asked.

"I'm afraid I can't give you a timeline at this point. The surgeon will—"

"How long?"

She looked at him then—really looked—and something in her expression softened. "Could be another hour or two. Possibly more. I'm sorry, Mr. Barrett."

---

He barely had time to sit before Sara appeared in the doorway.

She looked tired—the kind of tired that came from managing someone else's crisis while your own life kept demanding attention. Grace trotted beside her, practice bag bumping against her small legs, her eyes wide and searching as she scanned the waiting room.

"Dad!" Grace ran to him, throwing her arms around his waist. "Is Mom okay? Sara picked me up from school and she said—"

"Hey, kiddo." He crouched down, forcing a calm smile. "Mom's still in there. The doctors are taking good care of her."

"When can I see her?"

"Soon. I promise."

Grace nodded, but her eyes were too bright, and he could see the fear she was trying to hide beneath the bravery. She was seven years old, and she was learning too early that promises weren't always kept.

Sara handed Grace a coloring book and some crayons, settling her into a chair by the window. Then she turned to Jasper, her expression unreadable.

"You weren't here when I got the text to pick her up. Where were you?"

He met her eyes, then looked away. "Handling something."

"Something more important than being here?"

He didn't answer, and that was answer enough.

Sara's jaw tightened. She didn't yell—that wasn't her way—but her silence was louder than any accusation. She crossed her arms and sat down across from him, her posture saying everything she wouldn't put into words.

---

The clock on the wall ticked. Grace colored. Sara watched the news without seeing it. And Jasper sat there, feeling the weight of every minute he'd spent in that conference room.

His phone buzzed—Harper's name flashing on the screen. He stepped into the hallway to answer, keeping his voice low.

"Talk to me."

Harper's voice was crackling, hollowed out by distance and the particular exhaustion of a 2 a.m. call from the other side of the world. "I can't get away. Asia's a disaster. The Bangkok deal's dead—our partners pulled out three hours ago, and the regulators are making noise about fraud investigations."

"What about Singapore?"

"Teetering. The satellite integration is still stalled, and now there's a union issue with the contractors. Half of them are threatening to walk unless we guarantee overtime rates through the end of the project."

Jasper rubbed his eyes. "And the rocket contract?"

"That's why I'm calling." Harper's voice dropped lower. "I just found out the investors are threatening to pull unless you guarantee personal oversight. They don't trust Alexandra to handle it alone. They want your name on the paperwork."

"I already—" he started, then stopped. Alexandra had spun the earlier meeting into a "step forward," but nothing was actually settled. The launch provider had agreed to continue, but the investors were a different matter. Different egos, different fears.

"I'm sorry," Harper said. "You're on your own there."

"I can't leave again. Elena's still in surgery. There were complications."

A pause. When Harper spoke again, her voice was softer. "I know. I'm sorry. Do what you have to do. I'll hold things together here as long as I can."

She hung up, and Jasper stood in the corridor for a long moment, the phone heavy in his hand.

---

When he walked back into the waiting room, Grace looked up with hopeful eyes.

"Is Mom awake yet?"

"Not yet, sweetheart. Soon."

Sara's expression hadn't changed. She was watching him with that careful, assessing gaze that made him feel like a contract with too many red flags.

The clock ticked. The news anchor's voice droned on about weather patterns and stock prices. Somewhere in the surgical wing, Elena was lying on a table, and strangers were trying to fix whatever had gone wrong.

And somewhere across town, Alexandra was probably smiling to herself. The deal was saved, at least for now. The launch provider was on board, at least for the moment. The fire had been fought, at least until the next one started.

He wasn't sure which update would come first—the surgeon's, or the fallout from what he'd done.

But sitting there in that cold waiting room, watching his daughter color pictures of their family while her mother fought through complications she didn't know about, Jasper felt the truth settle into his bones:

Thirty minutes had become three hours.

And some costs couldn't be measured in time.
