## Why London?

With transatlantic financial clients and Catholic/royalty networks, London became the natural first international hub.

Harper's European connections opened the first doors. Elena's society links opened the rest. Together, they created an entry point that felt organic rather than aggressive.

"Americans trying to break into London society usually fail spectacularly," Elena warned. "They're too loud, too obvious. You have to be invited, not announced."

"Then get us invited," Jasper said.

She did.

---

## The Major Case

A European bank with Vatican-adjacent connections was caught in a scandal that threatened to embarrass everyone involved. The numbers were wrong, the optics were worse, and the people caught in the middle included cardinals and aristocrats.

"This is delicate," Harper said, reviewing the brief. "One wrong move and we're burned in Europe forever."

"Then we don't make a wrong move."

Jasper flew over personally. He met with bank executives in private clubs, with Vatican representatives in quiet offices, with aristocrats in country estates. He listened more than he spoke. He made no promises he couldn't keep.

Within three months, the scandal had been contained. Not erased—that was impossible—but managed. Channeled. Transformed from a crisis into a story about institutional reform.

The grateful parties sent referrals. The referrals built momentum.

BSS had proven they could handle aristocracy, monarchy, and Vatican ties with discretion.

## The Wives Club Expands

New "Wives Club" members in Europe tied into fashion, media, and political consulting.

Elena curated them carefully, introducing them through galas and dinners that felt social but were secretly strategic.

"You're building a network," Jasper observed, watching her work a London reception.

"I'm building *our* network," she corrected. "These women have access to rooms we can't enter. Their husbands run ministries and companies and foundations. When they trust us, doors open."

[OUTFIT DETAILS NEEDED - Elena's London society attire, elegant and understated]

It was partnership at its most elegant—his operational muscle, her social architecture.

## The London Rhythm

London became the bridge between American efficiency and European tradition.

The office was staffed with locals who understood the culture, Americans who understood BSS methods, and analysts who could translate between both worlds.

Harper visited quarterly, establishing the financial infrastructure. Elena visited for major events, reinforcing the social connections. Jasper came when there was trouble—and there was always trouble eventually.

But the hub held. It grew. It became a genuine second center of power.

"We're not just in London," Harper reported during one of their strategy calls. "We *are* London. At least, for the clients who matter."

"Then let's keep it that way."
