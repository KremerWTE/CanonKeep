# Chapter 4: Candlelit Kitchen

## Jasper & Elena Series - Book 1: "Empire and Home"
### Part Two: Year 2, Early Fall

---

The compound was blue-quiet by nine—crickets at the windows, a distant hum from the server room, the soft snap of the porch flag in a patient wind.

Elena padded into the kitchen barefoot, silk blouse untucked over black cigarette pants, hair gathered in a loose knot that had stopped pretending to be neat hours ago.

On the marble: a candle Addie had snuck in earlier (*"No more conference-room lighting in your marriage, please."*), two plates, and a bowl of olives that Bella had labeled with a sticky note: *for romance, not spreadsheets*.

Jasper sat at the island, jacket abandoned on the chair, sleeves rolled, blue oxford soft with years and a faint halo of jet-lag around his eyes. His phone glowed. His thumb hovered.

"Put it down," Elena said, striking a match. The candle flared; citrus and cedar drifted into the room like a cue.

He smiled. "You know the client in—"

"No clients," she said, sliding onto the stool beside him. "Date night. Here. Now."

---

## The Truce Food

In the baby monitor, a sigh. Then quiet. Kendra's text had arrived an hour before: *All good. I'm staying until they're fully asleep. Go be married.*

Elena forked pasta from a cooling pan. It wasn't plated; it was truce food—garlic, basil, the olive oil Bella swore changed lives. They ate from the same bowl, shoulders touching, the candle reflecting in the window where their silhouettes haloed the night.

He exhaled like a man stepping off a ledge onto solid earth.

"I missed the museum thing with Grace's class," he said quietly.

"You sent a video," Elena replied. "She watched it twice and then said you sounded like a voicemail."

Jasper winced. "That bad?"

"She wants you at the field trip next Friday." Elena tapped the countertop with her spoon. "So do I."

He stared at the pasta, then at her. "We're short on the Abu Dhabi file. Addie can hold it, but—"

"Addie *will* hold it," Elena said, softer now. "She's the one who lit this candle."

---

## The Message

As if on cue, a new message pulsed across the screen facedown on the counter.

Addie: *Boss, if you touch that phone, I'm changing your lockscreen to a photo of your disappointed children.*

Elena snorted. Jasper turned the phone further away.

A breeze slipped through the cracked window; the rosemary in the planter brushed the sill. Somewhere beyond the kitchen, the compound's new office wing hummed with sleeping machines. The women had designed it white-bright and close, no excuse to disappear into town. Jasper had called it a trap. They called it home court advantage.

He nudged her knee with his. "What would you be doing if we were twenty-five?"

"Wearing all black and pretending to enjoy a band with three chords." She twirled pasta; the candle flame bent, then steadied. "And I'd still be trying to keep you from leaving early."

---

## The Promise

He looked past her to the window, the reflection of her cheekbone in gold light, the spectral overlay of their kitchen on the black lawn.

"I want them to remember me like this," he said, voice low. "Not in an airport. Not as a voicemail."

Elena set her fork down. "Then be rememberable."

He reached for her hand, palm warm, the familiar constellation of ink on his wrist catching the candle. He didn't say anything else. He didn't need to.

They sat like that until the candle tunneled down and the kitchen smelled like lemon wax and olive oil and the sweet ache of saying no to the world and yes to home.

---

## The Sisterhood

At ten, Sara's footsteps crossed the hall. She leaned in, leather jacket over a tee, hair damp from the mist.

"Kitchen date? Finally."

"Go away," Elena said, smiling.

Sara blew them a kiss. "Bella's on at seven a.m. for the school run. Kendra left muffins. Addie says if you answer email before sunrise, she's changing your Wi-Fi name to *Boundaries-Or-Bust*."

Jasper groaned. Elena laughed and pressed her forehead to his.

The phone stayed facedown.

And the marriage felt, for the first time in too long, upright.
