# Chapter 5: Vineyard Weekend

## Jasper & Elena Series - Book 1: "Empire and Home"
### Part Three: Year 3, Late Winter

---

They reached the vineyard at golden hour, the hills rolled under linen sky, vines combed in rows like careful handwriting.

Elena stepped out of the car in a camel wrap coat over a cream sweater dress, suede boots sinking into the soft earth. Jasper moved around to open her door even though she'd already swung it wide; chivalry catching up to reality one gesture at a time.

"Kidnapping me from my own girls' trip?" she said, sunglasses tilting down her nose.

"Bella dared me," he said. "Kendra bet against me. Addie provided logistics and a playlist called *She Deserves Better Music*."

In the far lot, a Jeep idled. Bella waved from the driver's seat, Grace in the back holding up a construction-paper sign that read *BYE MOM WE GOT THIS*. Sara leaned across to honk, twice, like punctuation.

---

## The Tasting Room

The tasting room smelled like pear and oak, light catching in hung glass like it had decided to live there. They took their flight to the side patio where an old stone wall held the day's last warmth.

Elena tucked her hair behind her ear and pulled out a notebook—old habit, edges worn.

"Here," Jasper said, sliding it back toward the bag, gentle. "Tell me with your hands. Not the bullet points."

She laughed, surprised at herself. Then she did.

She described Rome—how a room breathed when it believed in itself, the geometry of tables, the choreography of waiters, how power gathered not at microphones but in the five inches between two chairs angled just so.

She talked about a ceremony that almost collapsed until she whispered a line to an anxious donor about his father's cufflinks, and everything exhaled. She talked about beauty like it was infrastructure.

---

## The Walk

The winter sun fell. He watched her the way people watch a fire: for warmth, for answers.

"Sometimes," she finished, cheeks pink from the cold and the wine, "I think I'm building cathedrals that last one night."

"You are," he said. "And they echo."

They walked the rows. Mud clung to his boots, to the hem of her dress; she didn't care. When they reached the hilltop, the wind lifted, and with it a memory—Grace on a stage in a paper crown, a phone held at arm's length. He closed his eyes and opened them again to Elena. To now.

"You're listening," she said, almost teasing.

"I'm memorizing," he said.

Below them, the Jeep arced back down the drive. Sara texted a photo: Grace and Bella at a diner, pancakes stacked like a dare. *Eat slow,* the caption read. *We got sunrise.*

---

## Small Somewhere

Elena slid her gloved hand into Jasper's bare one. The wind bit. The moment didn't.

"Let's go be small somewhere," she said. "No one to manage but each other."

They found a two-table restaurant in town with a chalkboard menu and a waiter who called everyone "dear." Jasper's jacket on the back of her chair, Elena's boot brushing his shin under the table, a candle shivering in a jelly jar—cathedral enough for a night.

When they finally made it to the bed and breakfast at the edge of the vineyard property, the room was simple—exposed beams, a fireplace already lit, white linens that smelled of lavender.

"This is what I needed," Elena whispered, standing at the window, watching the last light fade over the vines.

"What?" He came up behind her, arms wrapping around her waist.

"This. You. Present. Not just here—actually *present*."

He pressed his lips to her hair. "I'm learning."

"We both are."
