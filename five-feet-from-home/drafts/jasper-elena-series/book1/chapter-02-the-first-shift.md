## The Delegation

He started small.

Lunch meetings instead of late-night dinners. Delegating the endless travel to Addie and Bella. His analysts handled client fires while he sat in the war room at CLT, headset on, coffee cooling beside him.

At first, he twitched like an addict, glancing at flight schedules and hotel bookings. His fingers itched toward the keyboard whenever he saw an urgent flag pop up on the monitors.

"You're still here," Addie said, appearing in the doorway of the war room. "That's progress."

"I'm here because Harper threatened to change my passwords if I booked another flight this week."

## The Dinner Table

But then he came home on a Tuesday evening and sat at the dinner table.

His son spilled juice on his shirt. His daughter demanded help with a school project. The baby needed feeding. The chaos was overwhelming, loud, messy—nothing like the controlled precision of his boardrooms.

And for the first time, Jasper thought: *This is harder than a negotiation in Dubai.*

But it was also better.

"You're actually here," his daughter said, staring at him like he might disappear.

"I'm here," he confirmed.

"For how long?"

The question hit him harder than she knew. "For as long as you need me."

## Addie Steps In

When a client meltdown threatened to pull Jasper overseas, Addie leaned in with steel in her eyes.

"You trained us to do this," she said. "Let us do it. Go to your kid's recital. I'll handle Dubai."

It was the first time Jasper trusted her to carry an entire crisis.

She delivered.

He sat in a plastic chair at his daughter's school, watching her murder a violin solo, and realized he wouldn't have traded this for any deal in the world.

## Sara the Sister

Elena's younger sister, Sara, started coming by more often. She swept in with her easy laugh, helping with bedtime, sneaking pastries into the kitchen, teasing Jasper for his missed curfews.

"You can run empires, Jasp, but can't run bath time?" she asked one evening, finding him covered in soap bubbles while the kids shrieked in the tub.

"This is harder than anything I've ever done," he admitted.

Sara grinned. "Welcome to the club."

She became a fixture in their lives—the grounding presence that helped when both Jasper and Elena were stretched thin. The kids adored her. And Jasper, slowly, began to see her as more than Elena's sister. She was part of the infrastructure of his family.

## The New Rhythm

Jasper built a rhythm: office hours structured, family dinners sacred.

He brought his kids into parts of his world—office visits, small trips. He let them see the "why" of his work, walking them through the war room like it was a museum.

"This is where we help people," he explained. "When things go wrong, when nobody else knows what to do, they call us."

His son's eyes went wide. "Like superheroes?"

"Something like that."

---

## The Turning Point

He turned down a lucrative but high-drama client, shocking the team but proving he was serious.

"That's a fifty-million-dollar account," Harper said carefully when he told her.

"And it would have eaten my weekends for the next two years." Jasper shook his head. "I've done the math. The cost is too high."

Harper studied him for a long moment. Then she smiled. "Elena got through to you."

"Elena. The kids. Hawk." He shrugged. "Sometimes it takes a village to knock sense into one stubborn man."

Weekend routines with the family became non-negotiable. He forced himself to be "offline" Saturdays unless it was a national security-level crisis.

The first few Saturdays felt like withdrawal. By the third month, they felt like healing.
