# Chapter 5d: The Fallout

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

After the formal, everything changed.

Sophie didn't disappear exactly—they still sat in the same chapter meetings, still passed each other in the library, still existed in overlapping circles. But the easy intimacy was gone. The texts stopped. The coffee dates evaporated. The silent understanding they'd built over months dissolved like salt in water.

Bella felt the loss like a phantom limb.

---

She tried, once, to reach out.

"Hey, want to grab coffee? I miss studying together."

The reply took three hours: "Busy this week. Maybe later."

Later never came.

---

Tessa noticed, of course. Tessa noticed everything.

"What happened with you and Sophie?" she asked, flopping onto Bella's bed with her characteristic disregard for personal space. "You two were like, inseparable. Now she looks at you like you're a stranger."

"Nothing happened."

"Bella."

"Nothing happened." Bella stared at her laptop screen, pretending to read an article she'd lost track of two paragraphs ago. "We just... drifted."

"People don't 'drift' that fast. One day you're sharing psychology notes and doing that creepy mind-reading thing you both do, and the next she's actively avoiding you in the dining hall."

Bella didn't answer.

Tessa sat up, unusually serious. "Did something happen at formal? I saw you two talking outside."

"It was nothing."

"You keep saying that, but your face says it was very much something."

---

The truth was too complicated to explain.

What do you tell your best friend when you think you might have almost kissed a girl and panicked and now everything is ruined? What do you say when you're not even sure what you wanted, just that you wanted something and wanting felt dangerous and now you've lost the one person who seemed to understand you?

Bella had no words for it. So she said nothing.

---

Three weeks later, she saw Sophie at a party with a guy from Sigma Chi.

They were standing close, his hand on her waist, her head tilted up toward him in that posture that meant something was happening or about to happen. Sophie laughed at something he said—a real laugh, or close enough—and didn't glance in Bella's direction once.

Bella went home early.

She lay in bed and stared at the ceiling and told herself that this was fine. That Sophie was allowed to move on. That whatever had almost been between them had been a fever dream anyway, a moment of confusion that meant nothing.

She almost believed it.

---

The thing about almost-moments is they haunt you.

Not because of what happened, but because of what didn't. The road not taken. The question left unanswered. The version of yourself you glimpsed briefly before retreating to the safety of the familiar.

Bella buried the memory deep. She told herself she'd imagined the chemistry, the tension, the moment when Sophie's question had felt like an invitation.

But late at night, in the space between waking and sleeping, she'd hear that voice again:

*What do you want?*

And she still didn't have an answer.
