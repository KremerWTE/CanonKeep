# Chapter 10: The Charity Gala

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Four: The Bridge to BSS

---

The charity gala was falling apart, and Bella was the only one who seemed to notice.

It was a client event for the finance firm—a black-tie affair at the Boston Harbor Hotel that was supposed to be seamless, elegant, and impressive. Instead, the caterer had double-booked and was threatening to walk. The silent auction software had crashed. And the keynote speaker's flight had been delayed, leaving a hole in the program that no one knew how to fill.

---

Bella had spent forty minutes that evening getting ready in her small Back Bay apartment, staring at herself in the full-length mirror she'd propped against the bedroom wall.

The dress was a floor-length navy blue gown—not the trendy black that every other analyst would wear, but a deep, rich navy that caught the light like midnight water. She'd found it at a consignment shop in Cambridge, marked down from a designer price tag she couldn't have afforded on three months' salary. The silk charmeuse draped elegantly, with a modest V-neckline that was sophisticated without being revealing, and a subtle A-line silhouette that moved beautifully when she walked.

Her jewelry was understated—small pearl stud earrings that had been her grandmother's, and a thin gold chain with a tiny pendant she rarely took off. Nothing that screamed for attention. Nothing that competed with her dress.

She'd pulled her hair up into a low chignon, the kind of style that looked effortless but had taken her three attempts to get right. A few soft tendrils framed her face. Her makeup was natural—foundation that evened her skin, a touch of mascara to open her eyes, a nude lip that wouldn't leave marks on champagne flutes.

Looking at herself, Bella felt the familiar contradiction: she was dressed to be seen, but her entire purpose tonight was to be invisible.

*Smile. Network. Represent the firm. Don't outshine the senior partners.*

She slipped on her heels—modest three-inch pumps in a nude that elongated her legs without making walking difficult—grabbed her small clutch, and headed out the door.

She had no idea that across the harbor, a woman with ash-blonde hair and knowing hazel eyes was preparing to attend the same gala. That this woman—Addie Barrett—would be wearing an emerald green Valentino cocktail dress that fell just below the knee, her posture perfect, her gaze sharp. That Addie had already seen Bella's name on the firm's analyst roster and come specifically to observe her.

The chess game had begun before Bella even walked through the door.

---

Bella wasn't on the planning committee. She was just another analyst, there to smile and network and represent the firm.

But she saw the cracks. The panicked looks between the event coordinator and her assistant. The whispered phone calls that grew more desperate by the minute. The slow-motion disaster unfolding behind the ballroom's polished facade.

She told herself it wasn't her problem.

Then she picked up her phone and started making calls.

The caterer was talked down from the ledge—Bella found a secondary vendor who could fill the gap and convinced the original to honor their contract at a discount. The silent auction software was bypassed through a paper backup system she improvised from memory of sorority events gone wrong. The keynote speaker's absence was covered by a local philanthropist Bella cold-called after remembering her name from a news article.

It took ninety minutes. Bella spent most of it in a back hallway, phone pressed to her ear, heels kicked off, voice steady while her heart raced.

When she finally returned to the ballroom, the event was running smoothly. No one knew how close it had come to disaster.

Except someone did.

Bella felt eyes on her as she slipped back to her table. A woman across the room—elegant, composed, with the kind of presence that made people notice without demanding attention.

Their gazes met for a moment. The woman smiled slightly, almost like recognition.

Then she looked away.

Bella shook it off. Probably nothing. She was used to being invisible.

She didn't know yet that the woman had been watching her for the past hour. Taking notes. Reading her movements, her phone calls, her crisis management instincts.

She didn't know that the woman was already calculating whether Bella could be trusted with something much bigger than a charity gala.

She didn't know that this was the night everything began to change.
