# Chapter 3e: Tessa's Arrival

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Tessa Montgomery arrived at Boston College like a hurricane with a smile.

Bella remembered the moment exactly: she was sitting on her bare mattress, surrounded by half-unpacked boxes, feeling the crushing weight of homesickness she refused to admit, when the door burst open and a whirlwind of blonde hair and designer luggage blew into the room.

"You must be Bella! I'm Tessa! Oh my God, this room is so small—I mean, it's fine, totally fine, we'll make it work—is that your bed? Good, because I brought, like, seventeen pillows and I need the one by the window for my skin routine. Do you have a skin routine? You should have a skin routine. Your pores are great but you're, what, eighteen? You need to protect that now."

Bella blinked. "Hi."

"Hi! Sorry, I talk a lot when I'm nervous. Are you nervous? I'm nervous. This is my first time living away from home—well, except for summer camp, but that doesn't count because there were counselors and scheduled activities and no one expected you to, like, adult. Do you know how to adult? Because I definitely don't. My mom still makes my dentist appointments."

It was the beginning of everything.

---

Tessa was from Greenwich, Connecticut—old money, old family, old expectations that sat on her shoulders like a designer yoke. Her parents were both lawyers. Her brother was at Yale Law. Her entire existence had been mapped out before she was born: Greenwich to prep school to Ivy League to acceptable career to acceptable husband to acceptable children who would repeat the cycle.

She'd chosen Boston College specifically because it wasn't Ivy League.

"It's my tiny rebellion," she confided to Bella one night, two weeks into their friendship. They were sitting on the floor of their dorm room, eating Thai takeout and avoiding studying. "My mom wanted Brown. My dad wanted Princeton. I wanted... I don't know. Something that was mine."

"So you picked BC?"

"I picked the opposite of what they expected." Tessa grinned. "Also, their business school is actually really good, and I want to do event planning, and they have this whole hospitality track, and—okay, fine, it wasn't totally a rebellion. It was a calculated risk dressed up as a rebellion. But still. It felt like a choice."

Bella understood that. The desire to choose, even when the choices felt limited. The need to claim something as your own, even if it was just a college campus four hours from home.

"I think that counts," she said.

"You do?"

"My whole life, I've done what was expected. Helped at the family business. Got good grades. Never caused problems." Bella shrugged. "Coming here—even if it was the safe choice—still felt like choosing something. You know?"

Tessa reached over and squeezed her hand. "I think we're going to be really good friends, Bella Rossi."

She was right.

---

They were a study in contrasts.

Tessa was loud where Bella was quiet, extroverted where Bella was reserved, impulsive where Bella was calculated. Tessa spent money like it would regenerate overnight; Bella counted every expense in a spreadsheet her father would have approved of. Tessa made friends by the dozen; Bella collected acquaintances she never quite trusted.

But somehow, it worked.

Tessa pushed Bella out of her comfort zone—to parties she wouldn't have attended, to conversations she would have avoided, to moments of spontaneity that felt terrifying and exhilarating in equal measure. And Bella grounded Tessa, talked her down from bad decisions, helped her study for exams she would have failed without intervention.

"You're my person," Tessa declared one night, drunk on cheap wine and finals week stress. "Like, my actual person. The Christina to my Meredith. Do you watch Grey's Anatomy? You should watch Grey's Anatomy. The point is, you're stuck with me now. Forever."

Bella rolled her eyes, but she was smiling. "Forever is a long time."

"Yeah, well. Get used to it."

---

The thing about Tessa was that she saw people.

Not the way Bella saw them—analytically, strategically, reading patterns and predicting behaviors. Tessa saw people the way artists saw color: intuitively, emotionally, with a kind of careless precision that cut straight to the truth.

"You're hiding," she told Bella once, apropos of nothing. They were walking across campus between classes, fall leaves crunching under their feet.

"Hiding from what?"

"Everything. Yourself. Other people. You do this thing where you make yourself useful so no one notices you're keeping them at arm's length. It's very clever. It's also really sad."

Bella stopped walking. "I'm not—"

"You are. And I get it. You grew up feeling invisible, so you learned to be helpful instead of present. Being helpful is safe. Being present is terrifying." Tessa looked at her steadily. "But you can't build real relationships on usefulness alone. Eventually, you have to let people see the parts of you that aren't useful. The messy parts. The scared parts."

"When did you become a psychology major?"

"I'm not. I'm just your friend." Tessa linked her arm through Bella's. "And friends tell each other uncomfortable truths. That's part of the deal."

Bella didn't respond. But she thought about those words for a long time afterward.

Tessa was the first person who'd ever seen past the helpful facade.

It was terrifying. It was also, Bella realized slowly, exactly what she needed.
