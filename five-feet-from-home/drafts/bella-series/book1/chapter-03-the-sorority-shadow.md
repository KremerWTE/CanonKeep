# Chapter 3: The Sorority Shadow

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Boston College wasn't the Ivy League, but it had its own gravity—old brick buildings, autumn leaves crunching underfoot, and a sense that anyone who walked these paths was meant for something.

Bella chose Business as her major. Not because she loved it, but because it was practical. Stable. The kind of degree that would let her help her parents when the café and bookstore inevitably needed more than good intentions to survive.

She joined a sorority during rush week. It felt like the right thing to do—networking, sisterhood, a place to belong. And she did belong, in her own way.

Just never at the center.

---

Rush week taught Bella everything she needed to know about sorority style.

The other girls wore carefully curated outfits—coordinated pastels on Day One, school colors on Day Two, cocktail dresses for the final events. They had matching jewelry, perfect manicures, hair styled in loose waves that took more time than anyone admitted.

Bella showed up in what she had: a white button-down tucked into high-waisted jeans for the casual rounds, a sundress in butter yellow that her mother had bought her for graduation (it was last season, but she hoped no one would notice), and a navy cocktail dress for the formal rounds that she'd worn to her cousin's wedding two years prior.

She watched the other girls assess each other's outfits with quick, evaluating glances. She saw how they categorized each other—who had money, who was trying too hard, who belonged effortlessly and who was scrambling to fit in.

Bella landed somewhere in the middle: well-dressed enough to be accepted, not polished enough to be envied.

After she was initiated, her wardrobe slowly evolved. She learned to mix her affordable pieces with secondhand designer finds—a silk scarf from a vintage shop, structured blazers from consignment stores, jewelry that looked expensive but came from discount racks at Marshall's.

At sorority events, she dressed like everyone else dressed: fitted jeans, cute tops, wedges in the spring, boots in the fall. She learned to French braid her own hair for casual meetups and to set her curls with hot rollers for formals.

But she never felt like she was wearing the clothes. She always felt like the clothes were wearing her—a costume she put on to blend in, not a reflection of who she actually was.

---

Within weeks, Bella found her role: the fixer. When the philanthropy chair overbooked two events on the same weekend, Bella quietly reorganized the calendar. When the budget for Spring Formal spiraled out of control, she found vendors willing to negotiate. When two sisters got into a screaming match at a chapter meeting, Bella pulled them aside and talked them down before anyone else noticed.

She was always the #2 or #3 in the room. Never president. Never VP. Never the one giving the speech or accepting the applause. But when the applause happened, it was usually because Bella had fixed something behind the scenes that no one else could.

"What would we do without you?" her sisters would say, squeezing her arm before rushing off to the next party.

The answer, Bella suspected, was that they'd figure it out. Someone else would step up. They always did.

She earned a reputation as dependable. Reliable. The one you called when everything was falling apart.

But she never saw it as leadership. Just as cleaning up messes. Holding things together until someone more impressive took over.

The praise was nice when it came. But it came rarely, and it never stayed.

By junior year, Bella had learned to stop expecting it.
