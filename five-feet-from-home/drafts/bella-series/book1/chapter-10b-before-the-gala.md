# Chapter 10b: Before the Gala

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Four: The Bridge to BSS

---

Three hours before the gala, Bella stood in front of her bathroom mirror and tried to convince herself she wasn't terrified.

This wasn't her first black-tie event. Not even her tenth. But something about tonight felt different—charged, somehow, like the air before a storm.

She didn't know why. It was just another firm event. Just another evening of smiling and networking and making the partners look good.

But her hands were shaking as she applied her mascara.

---

The dress was perfect.

She'd splurged on it—really splurged, more than she'd spent on any single piece of clothing in her life. The navy blue gown she'd found at the consignment shop, silk charmeuse that caught the light like water at midnight. She'd had it altered to fit precisely, a rare indulgence that felt necessary for reasons she couldn't explain.

She laid it on her bed and looked at it. Then at herself.

*Who am I trying to be tonight?*

---

The preparation was ritualistic.

First: shower with the good soap, the kind that smelled like vanilla and cost too much per ounce.

Second: hair. She blow-dried it smooth, then set it in hot rollers while she did her makeup. Forty-five minutes later, she wound it into a low chignon—elegant, professional, the kind of style that said "I didn't try too hard" despite the three failed attempts hidden in the wastebasket.

Third: makeup. Light foundation. Subtle contouring (Tessa had taught her, FaceTime tutorials during lockdown). Mascara that elongated her lashes without clumping. Nude lip that wouldn't transfer onto champagne flutes.

Fourth: jewelry. Her grandmother's pearl earrings, small and perfect. The thin gold chain she'd worn every day since her mother gave it to her at graduation. Nothing else.

Fifth: the dress. She slipped it over her head carefully, zipping it up her back with the hook she kept specifically for this purpose. The silk settled against her skin like cool water.

She looked in the mirror.

*Not bad,* she thought. *You almost look like you belong.*

---

The heels were nude, three inches—high enough to matter, low enough to walk in after three hours of standing.

The clutch was small, just big enough for her phone, lipstick, ID, and a credit card.

She did a final check: hair secure, makeup perfect, dress unwrinkled, earrings in place.

Then she grabbed her clutch, called an Uber, and headed into the night.

---

In the car, she rehearsed the evening in her head.

The event was for the Kennedy Foundation—a major client, the kind that could make or break careers at the firm. Bella's role was simple: attend, network, look good, make no mistakes.

But she also knew the event itself was a mess behind the scenes. She'd overheard the whispers in the office—the caterer changing at the last minute, the silent auction software still glitching, the keynote speaker's flight status uncertain.

She'd offered to help. Her supervisor had laughed.

"You're an analyst, Rossi. Not an event planner. Just show up, smile, and let the professionals handle it."

But Bella knew what "professionals handling it" looked like when the professionals were overwhelmed.

She checked her phone one more time. No updates. No SOS texts.

*Maybe it'll be fine,* she thought. *Maybe I'm worrying about nothing.*

The car pulled up to the Boston Harbor Hotel. Bella stepped out, smoothed her dress, and walked toward the entrance.

She had no idea that this was the night that would change everything.
