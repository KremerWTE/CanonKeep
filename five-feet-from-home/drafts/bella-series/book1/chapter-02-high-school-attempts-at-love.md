# Chapter 2: High School Attempts at Love

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part One: Foundations

---

His name was Tyler, and he was everything a high school girl was supposed to want.

Star quarterback. Broad shoulders. The kind of easy confidence that made him the center of every room he walked into. When he asked Bella to the homecoming dance, her friends had squealed loud enough to draw stares in the cafeteria.

She said yes, of course. What else would she say?

---

The homecoming dress was dusty rose—a soft blush pink that her mother said brought out the warmth in her complexion. They'd found it at the mall two towns over, after three hours of searching and two nearly-tears-in-the-dressing-room moments.

It was a tea-length dress with a fitted bodice and a skirt that flared just enough to move when she danced. The fabric was a matte satin that didn't photograph cheap, with delicate spaghetti straps and a sweetheart neckline that was age-appropriate but still made her feel grown up. Her mother had added a thin belt at the waist—rose gold, borrowed from her own jewelry box—that cinched the dress perfectly.

Bella wore low heels she could actually walk in (her mother's practical advice) and curled her hair into soft waves that cascaded over her shoulders. Her makeup was minimal—lip gloss, mascara, a little blush—because her mother said she didn't need much. "You're already beautiful. Don't hide it under layers."

When Tyler picked her up, he'd stood in the doorway in his rented tux and stared at her for a long moment. "Wow," he said. "You look... wow."

Her father, standing behind her with crossed arms, had grunted something about "having her home by midnight."

Bella had smiled, posed for the obligatory photos, and climbed into Tyler's truck feeling like she was playing a part in someone else's story.

The dress was beautiful. Tyler's admiration was real. But as they walked into the gymnasium decorated with streamers and balloons, Bella couldn't shake the feeling that she'd dressed up to become what someone else wanted to see.

---

For six months, she was the quarterback's girlfriend. She sat in the bleachers during Friday night games, wore his letterman jacket when the autumn air turned cold, and smiled when he introduced her to his friends. She did what was expected.

But late at night, lying in bed and staring at the ceiling, Bella felt the hollowness of it. Tyler was kind. Tyler was popular. Tyler wanted the version of her that looked good next to him—the pretty, dependable girlfriend who never caused problems.

He didn't ask about the books she read. He didn't notice when she was quiet. He didn't see that she wanted more than Friday night games and after-parties where she stood at the edge of conversations, smiling and nodding.

She wanted depth. She wanted someone who saw the analytical mind behind the approachable smile.

The breakup was gentle—Bella's idea, presented softly so as not to bruise his ego.

"I think we want different things," she said, and he nodded like he understood, even though he probably didn't.

Her friends were confused. Tyler was a catch. Why would she walk away from that?

Bella couldn't explain it. She just knew that being chosen for the wrong reasons felt worse than not being chosen at all.

That was the first pattern. Stability over sparks. Then walking away when the stability felt empty.

And underneath it all, the first whisper of a question that would haunt her for years: Was she even capable of being loved the way she needed to be loved?

Or was she simply not worth that kind of effort?
