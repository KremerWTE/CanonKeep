# Chapter 8b: First Day at the Firm

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Three: Boston Professional Years

---

Bella arrived forty-five minutes early.

The lobby of the glass tower was all marble and chrome—designed to impress, to intimidate, to remind everyone who walked through those doors that they were entering a world where money was the only language that mattered.

She'd chosen her outfit carefully: a new black blazer (her first real professional investment, bought on credit she couldn't quite afford), charcoal gray trousers that she'd had tailored to fit perfectly, a crisp white blouse, and the black heels that would become her daily uniform. Her hair was pulled back in a low ponytail—professional, unremarkable, exactly what she was aiming for.

She signed in at security, received her temporary badge, and rode the elevator to the thirty-seventh floor.

The doors opened to chaos.

---

Not obvious chaos—nothing was ever obvious in finance. But Bella had spent a lifetime reading rooms, and this room was stressed.

The open floor plan buzzed with tension. People hunched over dual monitors, speaking in clipped tones on headsets. A glass-walled conference room at the far end held what looked like a crisis meeting—suits gesturing, voices raised but muffled.

"You must be Isabella." A woman appeared at her elbow, clipboard in hand, smile that didn't reach her eyes. "I'm Diana. HR. Welcome to Harrington Capital."

"Thank you. It's Bella, actually."

Diana's smile flickered. "Your paperwork says Isabella."

"Yes, but I go by—"

"Let's get you set up at your desk. IT will bring your laptop by noon. Here's your security card. Restrooms are down the hall, kitchen's behind you, don't touch anything in the refrigerator that isn't yours. Questions?"

"I—"

"Perfect. Someone will come collect you for orientation at eleven."

Diana disappeared into the crowd. Bella stood alone next to a desk that was now, apparently, hers.

---

The desk was small. Wedged between a window with a limited view and a conference room that seemed perpetually booked. Two monitors, a phone with too many buttons, a chair that had seen better days.

Bella set down her bag and looked around.

Nobody acknowledged her. Nobody looked up. Everyone was too busy surviving their own mornings to notice the new analyst standing uncertainly in their midst.

*This is fine,* she told herself. *You've always been invisible. At least here, it's expected.*

She sat down. She waited. She watched.

---

By the time orientation ended at three, Bella had learned several important things:

1. Harrington Capital managed $14 billion in assets and was "actively expanding their client acquisition strategy."
2. The company valued "teamwork, innovation, and excellence."
3. The man who said that—a senior partner named Malcolm Ross—had looked through her like she was made of glass.
4. The kitchen coffee was terrible but free, and the good stuff was in a locked cabinet only partners could access.
5. She was one of eight new analysts, and at least three of them had family connections that had gotten them hired.

"Where'd you go to school?" one of the connected ones asked during the lunch break. His name was Bradley. Of course it was Bradley.

"Boston College."

"Oh." His tone said everything. "That's... solid."

"Thanks. Where did you go?"

"Wharton." He didn't wait for her reaction before wandering off to talk to someone more important.

Bella ate her sandwich alone and told herself it didn't matter.

---

At 5 PM, most of the analysts started packing up.

At 6 PM, the floor was half-empty.

At 7 PM, Bella was still at her desk, reading the training materials she'd been given, trying to learn everything she could about the systems and processes she'd be expected to navigate.

"You're still here."

She looked up. A woman stood at the edge of her desk—early forties, sharp suit, the kind of presence that commanded attention without demanding it.

"I wanted to get a head start," Bella said.

"That's either very smart or very naive. Possibly both." The woman's mouth curved slightly. "I'm Margaret Chen. Managing Director. Your boss's boss's boss."

Bella stood quickly. "Ms. Chen. It's nice to meet you."

"Margaret is fine. We're not that formal when the partners aren't watching." She studied Bella for a moment. "What's your name?"

"Bella Rossi."

"The one from Boston College. The one without connections."

Bella felt her cheeks warm. "That's... accurate."

"Good. The ones with connections rarely work this hard." Margaret nodded toward the training materials. "Finish chapter four before you go home. Skip five—it's outdated. If you have questions, my assistant can find me."

She walked away before Bella could respond.

It was the first time since she'd arrived that someone had actually seen her.
