# Chapter 6b: Vee's World

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

The invitation came by text: "Dinner at the Ritz. My parents are in town. Come be impressive."

Bella stared at the message for a full minute before responding: "I'm not impressive."

Vee replied immediately: "You're the most impressive person I know. 7pm. Wear something elegant. I'll send a car."

---

The dress was borrowed from Tessa's closet—a deep burgundy sheath that Bella would never have chosen for herself but that Tessa insisted "made her look like a woman with secrets."

"You're going to Vee's parents' dinner at the Ritz-Carlton," Tessa said, zipping up the back. "You can't wear black. Everyone wears black. You need to be memorable without being ostentatious. This is the sweet spot."

Bella looked at herself in the mirror. The dress fit better than it should have—Tessa was slightly taller but somehow the hemline landed at exactly the right spot. Paired with nude heels (also borrowed) and simple pearl studs (her grandmother's), she looked almost like she belonged.

Almost.

---

The Ritz dining room was everything Bella had expected and worse.

Crystal chandeliers. White tablecloths that probably cost more than her parents made in a week. Waiters who materialized silently to refill water glasses that were never more than half empty. The quiet hum of money talking to money.

Vee's parents were polished in the way only generations of wealth could produce. Her mother, Margaret, had silver-blonde hair and a smile that never quite reached her eyes. Her father, William, exuded the confident stillness of a man who'd never had to raise his voice to be heard.

"So you're the one Vivian talks about," Margaret said, offering a hand that felt like cool porcelain. "The girl from the cafe."

Bella felt her cheeks flush. "My parents own a cafe and bookstore. I helped out growing up."

"Charming." Margaret's tone was unreadable. "Vivian says you're quite clever. That you fix things."

"I just... pay attention to what needs doing."

"Hmm." Margaret turned to Vee with an expression Bella couldn't decode. "She's modest too. Rare these days."

Vee caught Bella's eye across the table and winked. Hang in there.

---

The dinner lasted three hours.

Three hours of navigating questions about her major, her family, her "aspirations." Three hours of watching Vee code-switch between the warm friend Bella knew and the polished daughter her parents expected. Three hours of feeling like she was taking a test she hadn't studied for.

"Your daughter is exceptional," Bella said during a lull, surprising herself. "She sees people in a way that most can't."

Margaret's eyebrows rose slightly. "Does she?"

"She noticed me when I was just another sorority girl running logistics. She saw something no one else bothered to look for." Bella met Margaret's gaze steadily. "That's not charm. That's intelligence."

The table went quiet.

William Sinclair set down his wine glass. "My daughter is many things," he said slowly. "But she's never been particularly good at identifying talent. Too idealistic."

"With respect, sir, I think she's exactly that good." Bella's heart was pounding, but she didn't look away. "She just doesn't always tell you what she sees."

Vee's face was unreadable. But under the table, Bella felt a hand squeeze her knee briefly. Gratitude. Recognition.

---

Later, in the car on the way home, Vee was uncharacteristically quiet.

"I'm sorry if I overstepped," Bella said finally.

"You didn't." Vee turned to look at her in the dim light. "My parents... they don't see me the way you described. To them, I'm a project. An investment. Something to be managed." She paused. "No one's ever defended me like that."

"I just told the truth."

"That's exactly why it mattered."

They sat in comfortable silence for the rest of the drive. But something had shifted between them—a new level of trust, earned through courage Bella hadn't known she had.
