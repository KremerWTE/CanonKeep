# Chapter 5: The Girl in the Circle

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Her name was Sophie, and she had the kind of laugh that made Bella forget to breathe.

They'd known each other since rush week—Sophie was in the same sorority, always gravitating toward Bella at mixers and chapter meetings. At first, it seemed like normal sisterhood. Shared study sessions. Late-night talks about nothing and everything. The kind of friendship that formed in the space between parties and finals.

But somewhere along the way, it became something else.

Bella couldn't pinpoint the moment. Maybe it was the night Sophie reached for her hand during a scary movie and didn't let go. Maybe it was the way Sophie looked at her across the room, a softness in her eyes that felt different from the way anyone else looked at her. Maybe it was the flutter in Bella's chest when their shoulders brushed, the way her thoughts circled back to Sophie at odd moments—during lectures, while folding laundry, right before sleep.

It was emotional. It was quietly romantic. It was terrifying.

They never named it. Never had a conversation about what it meant. But they both knew something was there.

And so did the circle.

The whispers started slowly. Side-eyed glances when they walked in together. Sudden silences when they entered a room. Sisters who had always been friendly growing distant, their smiles thin and performative.

"People are talking," Tessa warned her one night, worried.

"About what?"

Tessa hesitated. "About you and Sophie. About... you know."

Bella's stomach dropped.

The circle did not approve. Not explicitly—no one ever said it outright. But the disapproval was there in every shifted gaze, every dropped invitation, every conversation that stopped when she approached.

Bella had spent her whole life reading people. She knew when she wasn't wanted.

So she pulled back. Stopped reaching for Sophie's hand. Stopped lingering after meetings. Stopped letting herself imagine what might have been.

Sophie noticed. Of course she noticed.

"Did I do something wrong?" she asked, hurt bleeding through her voice.

"No," Bella said. "I just... I can't."

The relationship never went anywhere. Not because Bella didn't want it to, but because she bought into the circle's silent judgment. She convinced herself that the disapproval was right, that something must be wrong with her for wanting what she wanted.

Sophie transferred to another chapter the next semester.

And Bella learned to fold under pressure, to swallow her own desires, to believe that other people's judgments mattered more than her own heart.

It was a lesson that would cost her for years.
