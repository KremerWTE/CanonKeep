# Chapter 3c: First Semester

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Business 101 was held in a lecture hall that seated three hundred students.

Bella sat in the middle—not the front (too eager), not the back (too disengaged)—with her laptop open and her notebook beside it, old habits from her father's bookstore asserting themselves in this new environment. Take notes by hand. Transfer to digital later. Let the act of writing help you remember.

The professor was a former Goldman Sachs executive who taught with the intensity of someone who considered every lecture a performance. He paced the stage, gestured dramatically, dropped names like breadcrumbs leading to success.

"Half of you will fail this class," he announced on the first day. "Not because you're stupid—you got into Boston College, you're not stupid—but because you're not hungry enough. Business isn't about intelligence. It's about wanting it more than the person sitting next to you."

Bella looked at the person sitting next to her: a guy in a lacrosse hoodie who was already scrolling through his phone.

She would be fine.

---

The coursework was harder than she expected.

Not conceptually—the ideas made sense, the math was manageable—but in scope. Every class assumed you had hours to spare for reading, research, group projects. Every professor acted like their subject was the only one that mattered.

She developed a system. Mornings for classes and studying. Afternoons for sorority obligations. Evenings for the part-time job she'd picked up at a campus coffee shop to help with expenses. Nights for more studying, more prep, more endless pursuit of the grades that would justify her presence here.

Sleep became a luxury she couldn't afford.

"You look like death," Tessa told her one morning, watching Bella apply concealer to the dark circles under her eyes. "When's the last time you slept more than five hours?"

"I don't remember."

"That's not a brag. That's a cry for help."

Bella shrugged, added another layer of mascara, tried to make herself look alive. "I'm fine. I just need to get through midterms."

She got through midterms. Then finals. Then the next semester. Then the next.

Fine became her baseline. Exhausted became her normal.

And somewhere along the way, she forgot what rested felt like.

---

Her grades were excellent. A's across the board, with occasional A-minuses that stung more than they should have.

Her father would have been proud. Her father was never proud enough.

"An A-minus in accounting?" he said during their weekly phone call. "What happened?"

"It was a hard test. I got one of the highest scores in the class."

"But not the highest."

Silence stretched between them, three hundred miles of phone line carrying all the words he didn't say.

"Next time," he added finally. "You'll do better next time."

She hung up and stared at her laptop, at the grade that represented her best effort, and wondered why her best effort never felt like enough.

---

The coffee shop job was supposed to be a means to an end.

Four hours a day, minimum wage plus tips, enough to cover textbooks and the occasional dinner out. But it became more than that. It became a refuge.

Behind the counter, she wasn't Bella Rossi, struggling business major. She wasn't the quiet sorority pledge, the adequate daughter, the exhausted overachiever. She was just a barista, making drinks, reading customers, falling into the rhythm she'd learned at her mother's cafe.

"You're good at this," her manager said, watching her handle a rush with practiced efficiency. "Most college kids take months to get this fast. You walked in ready."

"Family business," Bella said. The same answer she always gave.

"Well, whatever it is, keep doing it. You've got regulars asking for you by name."

It shouldn't have mattered. Tips and compliments from coffee shop customers weren't achievements, weren't things to put on a resume or brag about to her father.

But it mattered anyway. Being seen. Being valued. Even if it was just for making someone's latte exactly right.

She'd take whatever validation she could get.

---

By the end of first semester, Bella had established her pattern.

Work. Study. Sorority. Repeat. Occasional phone calls home where she performed fine well enough to avoid questions. Occasional texts to Megan that got shorter as their lives diverged. Occasional dinners with Tessa that reminded her what friendship felt like.

She was surviving. That was enough.

Or it should have been enough. But late at night, when the dorm was quiet and her roommate was asleep, Bella would lie in bed and wonder if this was all there was. Classes and coffee and making herself useful to people who barely knew her name.

She wanted more. She just didn't know what more looked like yet.

So she kept her head down, did her work, and waited for something to change.
