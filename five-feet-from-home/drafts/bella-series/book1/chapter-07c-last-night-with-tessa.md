# Chapter 7c: Last Night with Tessa

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Their last night in the dorm was a controlled disaster.

Everything was packed—Tessa's seventeen pillows shoved into oversized bags, Bella's careful boxes labeled in neat handwriting, the accumulated debris of four years reduced to things that would fit in cars and cargo holds.

They sat on Bella's bare mattress, passing a bottle of cheap champagne back and forth, watching the empty room grow darker as the sun set.

"Remember when you thought I was going to be your annoying rich roommate?" Tessa said, taking a long swig.

"You were my annoying rich roommate."

"Fair. But I was also your best friend, so it evens out."

Bella smiled. "It evens out."

---

They talked about everything and nothing.

The freshman year panic when Tessa had accidentally set off the fire alarm making ramen at 2 AM. The sophomore year formal where Bella had been so sick she'd almost passed out and Tessa had held her hair in the bathroom for an hour. The junior year spring break they'd spent at Tessa's family house in the Hamptons, Bella completely out of her element and Tessa making her feel like she belonged anyway.

"You're going to do amazing things," Tessa said, suddenly serious. "You know that, right?"

"I'm going to work at a finance firm and try not to embarrass myself."

"That's a start. But it's not the end." Tessa turned to face her. "You're meant for something bigger than spreadsheets, Bella. I don't know what it is yet, but it's coming."

"You sound like a fortune cookie."

"I sound like someone who knows you better than anyone." Tessa reached over and squeezed her hand. "You're going to get there. I believe that completely."

Bella didn't know what to say. So she just held on.

---

Around midnight, they finally gave up on sleep.

They grabbed blankets, snuck onto the roof through a window they'd discovered junior year, and lay under the stars—what few stars you could see in the light pollution of suburban Boston.

"I'm scared," Bella admitted quietly. "Of what comes next."

"Me too."

"You don't seem scared. You seem... ready."

Tessa laughed softly. "I'm terrified. New York is enormous. My new job is insane. I have no idea what I'm doing." She paused. "But I learned something at BC. Something you taught me."

"What's that?"

"That it's okay to not have everything figured out. That showing up and trying counts for something. That being scared doesn't mean you're failing—it means you're growing."

Bella turned her head to look at Tessa's profile in the darkness. "When did you become wise?"

"I've always been wise. You were just too busy being the smart one to notice."

They both laughed—the kind of laughter that was also tears, that held four years of friendship and all the uncertainty of goodbye.

---

The next morning, they loaded their cars separately.

Tessa's parents had sent movers for the big stuff, but she insisted on carrying her own suitcases, one last act of independence before returning to the Greenwich orbit.

Bella's father had driven up alone—the cafe couldn't spare both parents, and her brothers were "too busy" to help. They worked in silence, fitting boxes into the trunk with the kind of efficiency that came from years of working side by side.

When everything was loaded, Tessa stood next to Bella's car.

"This isn't goodbye," Tessa said firmly.

"I know."

"I'm serious. I will text you every day. I will call you every week. I will show up at your Boston apartment with wine and complaints about my job whenever I can escape."

"I'm counting on it."

They hugged—long and fierce, the kind of hug that said everything words couldn't.

"Thank you," Bella whispered. "For seeing me when I wasn't sure I was worth seeing."

Tessa pulled back, eyes bright. "Always. And don't you dare forget it."

---

Bella got in the car. Her father started the engine. In the rearview mirror, she watched Tessa wave until the campus disappeared behind them.

Chapter closed. Something new beginning.

She wiped her eyes and faced forward.
