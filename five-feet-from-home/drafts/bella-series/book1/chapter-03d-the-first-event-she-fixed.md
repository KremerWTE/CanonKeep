# Chapter 3d: The First Event She Fixed

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

The Spring Gala was supposed to be perfect.

It was DPE's biggest event of the year—a black-tie charity auction benefiting the local children's hospital, with tickets at $150 a head and corporate sponsors expecting a flawless evening. The chapter had been planning for six months. Everything was in place.

And then, four hours before doors opened, everything fell apart.

---

Bella was in her dorm room, struggling with the zipper on the only formal dress she owned—a burgundy number she'd worn to her brother's law school graduation—when her phone exploded.

Tessa: *911 at the venue*

Tessa: *COME NOW*

She arrived at the Boston Harbor ballroom to find chaos. The event coordinator was in tears. The philanthropy chair was screaming into her phone. Half the executive board stood in a cluster, their carefully styled hair and designer dresses incongruous with the panic on their faces.

"What happened?" Bella asked, finding Tessa in the crowd.

"Catering cancelled. Two hours ago. Something about a double-booking and a 'prior commitment.' We have two hundred guests arriving in three hours and no food."

The words hung in the air. Two hundred guests. Three hours. No food.

"And it gets worse," Tessa continued. "The DJ's van broke down on 95. The florist delivered half the arrangements and then stopped answering calls. And someone—nobody knows who—forgot to confirm the AV setup, so we might not have microphones for the speeches."

Bella looked around the room. Forty DPE sisters, most of them frozen with indecision. A venue that was half-decorated. A timeline that was already impossible.

Her father's voice echoed in her head: *There's a logic to everything. You just have to find it.*

"Give me a minute," she said.

---

She started with the catering.

Not by calling caterers—every decent option in Boston would already be booked on a Saturday night in April—but by thinking differently. Who owed them favors? Who might want exposure at a high-profile charity event?

She pulled up her phone, scrolled through contacts, and landed on a name she barely remembered adding: Marco, the manager at the Italian place where she'd picked up extra shifts last semester.

"Marco, it's Bella from BC. I need a huge favor."

Twenty minutes later, she had a commitment for pasta, salad, and garlic bread for two hundred, delivered by 7 PM, at cost. Marco wanted advertising—a mention in the program, a banner by the buffet, photos for his social media. Done.

"Catering handled," she announced to the stunned executive board. "What's next?"

---

The DJ was a bust—his van was genuinely broken—but Bella remembered a guy from her economics lecture who DJed fraternity parties on weekends. He wasn't sophisticated, but he had equipment and availability. She offered him $300 and a promise that every sorority girl in attendance would post about him on Instagram. He said yes.

The florist answered on the seventh call. Bella didn't yell—her mother had taught her that yelling rarely worked—she just explained, calmly and clearly, that the chapter had documentation of the full order and would be pursuing legal action if the remaining arrangements didn't arrive within the hour. They arrived in forty-five minutes.

The AV issue was the hardest. The venue's equipment was locked in a closet, and the event coordinator claimed not to have the key. Bella found the building manager, explained the situation with manufactured tears and genuine desperation, and walked away with a key and a promise that someone from maintenance would help them set up.

By 6:30, the ballroom looked like it was supposed to. Tables set. Flowers arranged. Food warming in the back. Music queued and ready.

Bella stood in the corner, her burgundy dress wrinkled from running around, her carefully styled hair falling out of its pins, and watched the other sisters take credit for the save.

"We did it!" the philanthropy chair announced, hugging the event coordinator. "I knew we could pull this off!"

Tessa caught Bella's eye from across the room. Raised an eyebrow.

Bella shrugged. She didn't need the credit. She just needed the crisis to be over.

---

The gala was a success.

The guests didn't know about the four-hour scramble. The sponsors were impressed. The hospital received a check for $47,000, the largest in chapter history.

And Bella, who had orchestrated the entire rescue, was thanked exactly once—a passing comment from Alex during cleanup.

"You saved us tonight. You know that, right?"

"I just made some phone calls."

"You made the right phone calls. There's a difference." Alex looked at her thoughtfully. "Most people panic when things fall apart. You got quiet and started fixing. That's rare."

Bella didn't know what to say. Praise still felt like a trap, like something that would be taken away as soon as she started believing it.

"Thanks," she managed finally.

"You should consider exec board next year. Philanthropy chair, maybe. Or operations. You'd be good at it."

"I'm not really a leadership person."

Alex laughed. "Bella, you just led the most successful crisis response I've ever seen. Leadership isn't about titles. It's about what you do when everything's on fire."

The words stuck with her long after the gala ended.

She still didn't see herself as a leader. But for the first time, she wondered if maybe everyone else did.
