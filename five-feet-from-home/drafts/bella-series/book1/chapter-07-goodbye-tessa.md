# Chapter 7: Goodbye Tessa

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Graduation day was bright and crowded, the kind of May morning that photographers love—blue skies, blooming trees, families clustered on the quad with tears and cameras.

Bella stood in her cap and gown, degree in hand, feeling like an imposter among the celebrating crowds. She'd done what she was supposed to do. Business degree. Sorority connections. Resume polished and ready for the real world.

But the person who made it bearable was leaving.

Tessa had been her roommate since freshman year. The one who saw through Bella's competent exterior to the uncertainty underneath. The one who held her while she cried over David, who listened when she couldn't explain what had happened with Sophie, who knew exactly how Bella's coffee order and didn't need to be asked.

"Event planning," Tessa said, grinning as they walked across the grass together. "Can you believe it? All those sorority disasters we fixed—turns out that's an actual career."

"You'll be amazing at it," Bella said, and meant it.

Tessa was heading to New York. A boutique event firm had offered her a position that paid almost nothing but promised experience and connections. She'd be living in a shoebox apartment in Brooklyn with three other girls, chasing the dream.

Bella was staying in Boston. A mid-tier finance firm had hired her as an analyst. Safe. Stable. The kind of job her parents could brag about to the café regulars.

They promised to stay in touch.

At first, they did. Weekly calls that turned into monthly texts that turned into sporadic likes on social media. Tessa's life was a whirlwind of Manhattan events and new friends and late nights that Bella could only glimpse through carefully curated Instagram posts.

Bella's life was spreadsheets and conference calls and the quiet grind of proving herself in rooms full of men who looked past her.

Their lives drifted apart. Not dramatically—no fight, no falling out. Just the slow erosion of distance and time.

By the time Bella realized she hadn't talked to Tessa in three months, it felt too late to pick up the phone.

She missed her. Missed having someone who knew her depths without needing explanations.

But she didn't reach out.

It was easier to let the silence grow than to admit she needed someone. Easier to convince herself she was fine alone.

Bella wouldn't find that kind of friendship again until BSS.
