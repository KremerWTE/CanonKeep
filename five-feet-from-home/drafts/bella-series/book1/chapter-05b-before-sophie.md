# Chapter 5b: Before Sophie

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Before Sophie, there was a year of watching.

Bella noticed her during rush week—a quiet brunette with sharp eyes and a smile that seemed calculated rather than genuine. Sophie Hartley. Sophomore. Transfer from somewhere in the Midwest, though she never talked about it.

She was different from the other sorority girls. While they giggled over boys and obsessed over Instagram likes, Sophie observed. She sat at the edge of conversations, taking in information like a sponge, revealing nothing about herself in return.

Bella recognized the behavior. She'd perfected it herself.

---

Their paths crossed at a philanthropy committee meeting. Sophie had volunteered to help with the silent auction logistics—thankless work that nobody else wanted.

"You're the first person who's shown up on time," Bella said, checking items off her list.

"I'm always on time." Sophie's voice was low, measured. "Punctuality is the easiest way to seem reliable."

"Seem?"

Sophie smiled—a real one, Bella thought, or at least closer to real. "Isn't that what we're all doing here? Seeming like something?"

It was the beginning of recognition. Two people who wore masks, spotting each other's disguises.

---

They started studying together. Sophie was a Psychology major—not the trendy pop-psych that some girls dabbled in, but the hard science: cognition, behavioral analysis, the mechanics of how people thought and felt and made decisions.

"You'd be good at this," Sophie told her once, watching Bella navigate a conflict between two sisters over formal committee positions. "You read people naturally. You just don't know you're doing it."

"I'm just... paying attention."

"That's what reading people is. Most people don't pay attention at all. They're too busy performing to notice anyone else's performance."

Bella felt seen in a way that was both thrilling and terrifying.

---

They'd grab coffee after chapter meetings, sit in the corner of the library during study sessions, text each other observations about the people around them like anthropologists comparing field notes.

"The chapter president is sleeping with the treasurer," Sophie texted once during a boring meeting. "Watch his face when she talks."

Bella watched. She saw it immediately. The way his eyes softened. The way he leaned toward her unconsciously.

"How did you know?" she texted back.

"Same way you knew exactly which vendor to call when the florist fell through. Pattern recognition."

It was a strange friendship—intellectual rather than emotional, built on observation rather than vulnerability. But it was the closest Bella had felt to being understood since... ever, maybe.

Sophie saw her brain, not just her usefulness.

And Bella was hungry for that kind of recognition.
