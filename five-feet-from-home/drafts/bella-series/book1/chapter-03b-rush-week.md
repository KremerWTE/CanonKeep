# Chapter 3b: Rush Week

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Rush week was a fever dream of forced smiles and desperate belonging.

Bella hadn't planned to go Greek. The whole thing seemed antiquated—paying for friends, performing enthusiasm, pretending that matching letters on a sweatshirt meant something about who you were as a person. But Tessa had dragged her along, and Tessa was persuasive in the way only truly extroverted people could be.

"It's networking," Tessa insisted, applying mascara in their tiny dorm mirror. "Think of it like investing in your future."

"I'm a business major, not a sorority girl."

"You can be both. Come on, it'll be fun. And if you hate it, we'll leave."

They didn't leave.

---

The houses blurred together after the first day.

Each one had a different vibe—the preppy athletes, the old money legacies, the aspiring philanthropists—but the underlying energy was the same. Perform. Impress. Be chosen.

Bella wore her interview outfit on the first day: a navy sheath dress her mother had helped her pick out, sensible heels that hurt by hour three, her grandmother's pearl studs (the only nice jewelry she owned). She looked appropriate. She looked forgettable.

Which, as it turned out, was exactly the wrong strategy.

"You're too quiet," one of the recruiters told her during a Kappa house tour. "We're looking for girls who can... you know. Light up a room."

Bella nodded, smiled, made a mental note to never set foot in this house again.

The next house was worse. The one after that was actively hostile. By the third day, she was ready to quit.

And then she found Delta Phi Epsilon.

---

DPE was different.

Not better, necessarily—they still had the matching letters and the choreographed door songs and the weird obsession with ritual. But the women seemed... real. Competent. Like they'd chosen this house for reasons beyond clout.

"We're not the party house," the chapter president told her during Preference Night. Her name was Alexandra—Alex—and she was exactly the kind of woman Bella wanted to be: confident without being aggressive, warm without being saccharine, wearing a simple black dress that probably cost more than Bella's entire wardrobe but somehow didn't make her feel inferior.

"We're the house that gets things done. Philanthropy, academics, career networking. If you're looking for mixers and drama, there are better options. If you're looking for women who'll have your back when it matters, you're in the right place."

Bella signed her bid card that night.

---

The pledge process was exhausting in ways she hadn't anticipated.

Not hazing—DPE prided itself on being above that—but the sheer volume of expectations. Memorize the history. Learn the songs. Attend the events. Bond with your pledge class. Represent the chapter at all times.

She threw herself into it the way she threw herself into everything: with competent, quiet determination. She wasn't the loudest pledge, or the prettiest, or the most socially connected. But she was reliable. When things went wrong—and things always went wrong—Bella was the one who fixed them.

The philanthropy chair double-booked the venue for the fall charity auction? Bella found an alternative in three hours. The budget for Spring Formal spiraled out of control? Bella negotiated with vendors until the numbers worked. Two sisters got into a screaming match during chapter meeting? Bella pulled them aside, talked them down, smoothed things over before the advisors noticed.

"You're really good at this," Alex told her one night, after Bella had single-handedly saved a recruitment event from disaster. "The crisis management thing. Where did you learn that?"

"Family business," Bella said. "Cafe and bookstore. You learn to handle emergencies."

Alex studied her with an expression Bella couldn't quite read. "You should consider leadership track. Exec board. You've got the skills for it."

Bella shook her head automatically. "I'm not really a leader type."

"That's not true. You just lead differently. From behind, instead of in front." Alex smiled. "There's nothing wrong with that. Some of the best leaders I know are the ones nobody sees coming."

It was the first time anyone had framed her tendency to stay in the background as a strength rather than a limitation.

She didn't believe it. Not yet.

But she remembered.

---

Initiation was the first week of spring semester.

She wore white—they all wore white—and walked through the candlelit ritual feeling like she was becoming someone new. The secrets, the symbols, the sisterhood. It should have felt silly. Instead, it felt sacred.

When it was over, Alex hugged her.

"Welcome to the house, Bella. We're glad you're here."

And for the first time since arriving at Boston College, Bella felt like she might actually belong.
