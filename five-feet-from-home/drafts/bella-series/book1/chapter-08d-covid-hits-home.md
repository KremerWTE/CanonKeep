# Chapter 8d: COVID Hits Home

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Three: Boston Professional Years

---

March 2020.

The office emptied slowly, then all at once.

First it was just a few people working from home "out of an abundance of caution." Then it was half the floor. Then it was everyone, laptops hastily packed into bags, the steady hum of the trading floor going silent for the first time in years.

Bella set up her remote office in her small Back Bay apartment—a card table she'd bought at Target, a second monitor she'd had shipped overnight, a chair that would destroy her back over the coming months.

She worked. The work never stopped.

But the phone calls from home got worse.

---

"We had to close," her mother said, voice cracking over the connection. "The cafe, the bookstore... everything. We can do takeout orders, but nobody's ordering. Nobody's coming."

"How long can you hold on?"

A pause. The kind of pause that said everything.

"We'll figure it out, sweetheart. We always do."

But Bella had grown up in that business. She knew the margins. She knew how thin they'd always been. A few weeks without customers was uncomfortable. A few months was catastrophic.

"I can send money," Bella said. "I've been saving, and—"

"We can't take your money."

"It's not charity. It's helping. The way I've always helped."

Another pause. Longer this time.

"We'll talk about it," her mother said finally. Which meant yes. Which meant things were worse than they were admitting.

---

Bella started sending money weekly.

Not a lot—she wasn't making partner salary, and Boston rent ate most of her paycheck—but enough to help cover utilities, insurance, the small expenses that added up.

She cut her own budget ruthlessly. Gave up the gym membership she never used anyway. Stopped ordering delivery. Learned to meal prep for the week, same simple recipes her mother had taught her years ago.

The money disappeared into her parents' account and never got mentioned again.

---

Her brothers were useless, as usual.

Marcus was "dealing with his own situation"—which apparently meant his law firm had frozen salaries and Catherine was unhappy about their delayed renovation. Daniel was "between opportunities"—which meant he'd been laid off and was too proud to admit it.

Neither of them sent money home.

Neither of them even asked if their parents needed help.

Bella didn't expect them to. She'd stopped expecting anything from her brothers years ago. But it still stung, in the quiet moments, knowing that the weight of the family fell on her shoulders as always.

The middle child. The daughter. The one who was supposed to be grateful for whatever crumbs she received.

She didn't feel grateful. She felt exhausted.

---

The pandemic stretched on.

Weeks became months. Months became seasons. Bella worked from her card table, attended endless Zoom meetings, tried to maintain the relationships and reputation she'd built when in-person contact had been possible.

Some things thrived. Remote work meant she could work even longer hours without anyone noticing she'd gone home. She became more essential than ever, the person who could be reached at any time, who never missed a deadline, who picked up the slack when others struggled with the adjustment.

Other things withered. The casual connections. The overheard conversations. The organic networking that happened in break rooms and elevators and after-work drinks.

She got a performance review that was "satisfactory." Not excellent. Not outstanding. Just... satisfactory.

"You're doing great work," her supervisor said, voice tinny over the video call. "But visibility matters. And right now, nobody can see you."

The irony wasn't lost on her.

She'd spent her whole life being invisible. Now it was official policy.
