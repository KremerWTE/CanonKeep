# Chapter 2b: After Tyler

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part One: Foundations

---

The breakup was barely a ripple in the social pond of Jefferson High.

Tyler had moved on within two weeks—a cheerleader named Brittany who wore his letterman jacket like a trophy and kissed him in the hallways where everyone could see. Bella watched from the edges, feeling nothing. Not jealousy. Not regret. Just a vague relief that the performance was over.

"You're weird about this," her friend Megan said at lunch, stabbing at her salad with more force than necessary. "He was, like, the perfect boyfriend. And you just... let him go?"

"We wanted different things."

"What things? He wanted to be with you. What more is there?"

Bella didn't have an answer Megan would understand. How could she explain that wanting someone wasn't enough if they didn't actually see you? That Tyler had loved the idea of her—the supportive girlfriend, the quiet beauty—without ever being curious about who she actually was?

She couldn't. So she didn't try.

---

The months after Tyler were lonely in a way she hadn't anticipated.

Not because she missed him—she didn't, not really. But because his presence had given her a role to play, and without it, she didn't know who she was supposed to be. The quarterback's girlfriend had been a costume, but it was a costume that came with a social position, a lunch table, a place in the hierarchy.

Now she was just Bella Rossi. The helpful one. The smart one. The one who was always around but never quite essential.

She threw herself into work. The cafe, the bookstore, her classes. If she couldn't figure out who she was, at least she could be useful. At least that was something.

Her mother noticed.

"You're hiding," Maria said one night, cornering Bella in the cafe kitchen after closing. "Working yourself to the bone so you don't have to feel anything. I've seen it before. Hell, I've done it before."

"I'm fine."

"You're not fine. You're seventeen and you just had your heart broken—"

"Tyler didn't break my heart."

Her mother paused. Studied her. "No. I don't think he did. But something did. Something broke, somewhere, and you're trying to fix it by making yourself indispensable to everyone else."

Bella's throat tightened. "I don't know what you want me to say."

"I don't want you to say anything, mi amor. I want you to feel it. Whatever it is. Because if you bury it now, it'll just grow roots. And one day you'll be forty years old wondering why you never let yourself have anything good."

The words hit harder than they should have. Bella blinked back tears she didn't understand.

"How do I stop?" she whispered.

"You don't stop. You just... make room. For the sadness and the work. For the hurt and the healing. You hold both. That's what being human is."

---

Senior year approached like a deadline.

Applications to fill out. Essays to write. Decisions to make about futures she couldn't quite imagine. Her brothers had made it look easy—Marcus to Georgetown, Daniel to Penn State, both of them certain about their paths in ways Bella envied.

She wasn't certain about anything.

She applied to ten schools. Boston College was the compromise—not as prestigious as her brothers' choices, but solid enough to avoid disappointment. Far enough from home to feel like escape. Close enough to come back when her parents needed her.

And they would need her. The cafe was struggling. The bookstore was worse. She saw it in the pinched lines of her father's face, the late-night calculations her mother thought she didn't notice.

"You should go wherever is best for you," her mother insisted when the acceptance letter came.

"Boston is best for me."

It wasn't entirely true. But it was close enough to the truth that she could live with it.

---

The summer before college was a strange limbo.

She worked double shifts at the cafe. Helped her father with inventory. Watched her high school friends scatter to their own futures while she counted down the days until she could start hers.

Tyler got engaged to Brittany the week before graduation. Bella heard about it from Megan, who seemed scandalized on her behalf.

"Can you believe it? After six months? They're insane."

"Good for them," Bella said, and meant it.

She'd learned something from Tyler, even if it wasn't what he'd intended to teach her. She'd learned that she could want more. That settling for what looked right wasn't the same as finding what felt right. That she was capable of walking away from something safe because safe wasn't enough.

It wasn't much. But it was a start.

And somewhere between the cafe and the bookstore, between her mother's warmth and her father's silence, Bella started to wonder what else she might be capable of.

She wouldn't find out for years. But the question had been planted.

And questions, like seeds, have a way of growing.
