# Chapter 11b: The Days After

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Four: The Bridge to BSS

---

The email came on Tuesday.

Bella had been checking her inbox obsessively since the gala—not for work, not for her supervisor's usual demands, but for something she couldn't even name.

The subject line was simple: "Coffee?"

The sender was unknown. No corporate signature. No surname. Just a first name and a professional address she didn't recognize.

*Adelaide Barrett.*

The woman from the gala. The one who'd been watching.

---

Bella read the email three times.

*Bella—*

*I enjoyed observing you at the Kennedy Foundation event. Your crisis management skills are remarkable, particularly given that crisis management wasn't your job that evening.*

*I'd like to discuss a professional opportunity with you. No commitments, just conversation.*

*Coffee this week? I'll accommodate your schedule.*

*—Adelaide*

---

She didn't respond immediately.

Instead, she did what she always did: she researched.

Adelaide Barrett. Google returned frustratingly little—a LinkedIn profile with minimal information (CEO, Barrett Security Solutions, Charlotte, NC), a few mentions in business journals about crisis management consulting, a photograph that matched the woman from the gala.

No social media. No personal information. No trail to follow.

It was like the woman existed only in the professional sphere, with no life outside it.

---

Bella called Tessa that night.

"Someone approached me," she said, pacing her small apartment. "From the gala. A woman named Adelaide Barrett."

"What kind of approached? Like romantically? Because that would be interesting—"

"Professionally. She said she wants to discuss an opportunity."

"What kind of opportunity?"

"That's the thing. I can't find anything about her. She runs some kind of consulting firm in North Carolina. Crisis management. But there's no details. No case studies. No client list."

Tessa was quiet for a moment. "That's either very shady or very exclusive."

"Those aren't mutually exclusive."

"True. What are you going to do?"

Bella looked at the email, still open on her laptop. "I don't know. Part of me thinks I should ignore it. It feels too good to be true."

"And the other part?"

"The other part..." Bella sighed. "The other part remembers the way she looked at me. Like she saw something. Something nobody else sees."

"That's either very romantic or very creepy."

"You're not helping."

"I'm being honest. Which is what best friends are for." Tessa paused. "Look. You've been stuck in that finance job for years, sending money home, being useful to people who don't appreciate you. If there's even a chance this is something real... don't you want to find out?"

---

She responded on Thursday.

*Ms. Barrett—*

*I'd be happy to meet. Friday afternoon works for me. Please send details.*

*—Bella Rossi*

The reply came within minutes:

*Perfect. 2pm. Address attached. Looking forward to it.*

*—Adelaide (call me Addie)*

---

That night, Bella lay awake staring at the ceiling.

She'd spent years learning to read rooms, to anticipate outcomes, to see patterns before they became visible. Her instincts had been honed by a childhood of watching, a college career of fixing, a professional life of navigating.

And every instinct she had was screaming that this was important.

Not dangerous—she didn't sense danger. But important. A door opening. A path diverging.

She could ignore the email. Keep her head down. Keep being useful. Keep surviving.

Or she could walk through the door and see what was on the other side.

The choice was obvious.

The fear was real.

But Bella had never let fear stop her from doing what needed to be done.

She'd just never known, until now, that what needed to be done might be for herself.
