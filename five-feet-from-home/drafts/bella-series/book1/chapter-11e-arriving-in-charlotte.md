# Chapter 11e: Arriving in Charlotte

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Four: The Bridge to BSS

---

Charlotte wasn't what she expected.

Bella had imagined something smaller—a southern town, maybe, all charm and slow pace. Instead, the skyline bristled with glass towers, and traffic hummed on highways that looked identical to Boston's.

But there was something different in the air. The light, maybe. The way the sun hit the buildings. Or just the knowledge that she was somewhere new, somewhere nobody knew her yet, somewhere she could become.

---

Addie had arranged everything.

An apartment in a building she could afford, close to the BSS offices. A car service from the airport. Even groceries in the refrigerator—basics, but thoughtful ones.

"You'll need a few days to settle in," Addie had said. "Take them. Report Monday."

It was strange, being taken care of. Bella wasn't used to it.

---

She spent the first evening walking.

Charlotte's Uptown was all restaurants and boutiques and people who moved with the confident energy of a city on the rise. She found a coffee shop, ordered something too sweet, sat by the window and watched strangers pass.

Nobody looked at her twice. Nobody knew her name. Nobody expected anything.

It felt like freedom.

---

On the second day, she explored.

She found the library (massive, modern, full of light). The parks (green even in late autumn). The neighborhoods that surrounded Uptown—Victorian houses and tree-lined streets that felt almost New England-ish, until you noticed the different plants, the different light.

She bought a plant for her apartment. A small succulent that the shop owner promised was impossible to kill.

"First time in Charlotte?" the woman asked.

"First time living here."

"Welcome. It's a good city." The woman smiled. "Plenty of opportunities for people willing to work for them."

---

Sunday night, Bella laid out her clothes for the next day.

A navy blazer, new but similar to her old uniform. Black trousers, tailored. A white blouse, crisp. Low heels she could move in.

Professional. Forgettable. The costume she'd worn for years.

Then she stopped.

Addie hadn't hired her to be forgettable. Addie had hired her because she saw something.

Bella went back to the closet. She chose differently.

A fitted burgundy dress—the one she'd worn to meet Vee's parents. Heels that added confidence. Her grandmother's pearls. Her mother's chain.

She looked in the mirror and saw someone ready to be seen.

---

Monday morning, she took an Uber to the address Addie had given her.

It wasn't a glass tower. It wasn't a corporate park. It was a renovated building in an upscale neighborhood—understated, unremarkable from the outside, exactly what you'd expect for a company that didn't want to be noticed.

Bella took a breath. Then another.

*This is it. This is the beginning.*

She walked through the door.

---

The lobby was quiet. Professional. A woman behind a desk looked up with a smile that actually reached her eyes.

"You must be Bella Rossi. Ms. Barrett is expecting you." She handed over a visitor badge. "Third floor. She said to go right up."

Bella nodded. She walked to the elevator. She pressed the button.

The doors opened onto a floor that buzzed with controlled energy—people at desks, screens displaying information she couldn't read from this distance, the quiet intensity of work that mattered.

And there, waiting by the elevator, was Addie.

She looked exactly as she had at the coffee shop: composed, elegant, with that sharpness behind her eyes that made Bella feel like every thought was being cataloged.

"Welcome to BSS," Addie said. "Ready to begin?"

Bella smiled. "I've been ready my whole life."
