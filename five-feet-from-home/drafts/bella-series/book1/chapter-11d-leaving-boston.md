# Chapter 11d: Leaving Boston

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Four: The Bridge to BSS

---

The apartment took three hours to pack.

Everything she owned fit into boxes that could be counted on two hands. Clothes. Books. A few photographs. The ceramic mug her mother had given her when she moved in ("Something from home for your new home").

Bella stood in the center of the empty living room and felt... nothing. No nostalgia. No sadness. Just the hollow echo of a space that had never quite been hers.

---

Tessa came to say goodbye.

She'd taken the train from New York, dragging a small overnight bag and a bottle of champagne that probably cost more than Bella's security deposit.

"This is insane," Tessa said, flopping onto the bare floor. "A month ago you were complaining about your performance review. Now you're moving to North Carolina to join a mysterious organization run by a woman you met at a gala."

"When you say it like that, it sounds crazy."

"It is crazy. That's my point." Tessa grinned. "I love it."

"You do?"

"Of course I do. You've been sleepwalking through your life for years, Bells. Safe job, safe apartment, safe routine. This is the first reckless thing you've ever done."

"It doesn't feel reckless. It feels..." Bella searched for the word. "Inevitable."

Tessa studied her. "Tell me about her. This Addie person."

So Bella did. The meeting. The conversation. The way Addie had looked at her like she was already something, not just potential.

"She sees me," Bella said finally. "Not what I can do for her. Not what I can fix. Just... me."

"That's terrifying."

"I know."

"And also wonderful."

"I know that too."

They drank the champagne sitting on the floor, watching the sun set through empty windows.

---

Her parents drove up the night before her flight.

It was the first time they'd both left the cafe at the same time since COVID hit. Bella protested, but her mother was having none of it.

"We're not missing this," she said, wrapping Bella in a hug that smelled like home. "Our daughter is having an adventure."

Her father was quieter, as always. But when he hugged her, he held on longer than usual.

"Be careful," he said gruffly. "Don't let them change who you are."

"I won't."

"And call your mother. She worries."

"I know."

He stepped back, looking at her with an expression she couldn't quite read. Pride, maybe. Or surprise. Like he was seeing her for the first time.

"You know," he said, "you remind me of my mother. She left her family's farm to start something new. Everyone said she was foolish. She wasn't."

It was the most personal thing her father had ever said to her.

---

The next morning, Bella took one last look around the empty apartment.

The card table where she'd worked through a pandemic. The window where she'd watched snowstorms and spring rains and the endless rhythm of a city that had never quite become home.

She picked up her suitcase. She closed the door. She didn't look back.

At the airport, she texted Vee: "I'm doing it. I'm taking the leap."

The reply came immediately: "FINALLY. I knew you had it in you. Go be magnificent."

Then Tessa: "Landed. Gate B17. Don't forget to hydrate on the plane."

She smiled. Some things never changed.

---

The flight to Charlotte was two hours.

Bella spent most of it staring out the window, watching the Northeast give way to forests and hills and unfamiliar geography.

Somewhere down there was a compound she'd never seen. A network she didn't understand. A mentor who'd chosen her for reasons she was still trying to believe.

Somewhere down there was the rest of her life.

She was terrified.

She was ready.

She was finally, after twenty-five years of waiting, becoming.
