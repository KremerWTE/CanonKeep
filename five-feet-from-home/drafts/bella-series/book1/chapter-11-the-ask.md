# Chapter 11: The Ask

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Four: The Bridge to BSS

---

The email came three weeks after the gala.

It was short—professional but warm, the kind of message that didn't explain itself. An invitation to coffee. A name Bella didn't immediately recognize. A time and place that felt deliberate.

She almost deleted it.

But something about the phrasing made her pause. This wasn't a networking request from another sorority alumna. It wasn't a follow-up from the firm's client event. It was... specific. Intentional.

Curiosity won.

---

Bella spent twenty minutes deciding what to wear.

It was just coffee. It shouldn't matter. But she'd googled the coffee shop and seen photos of the clientele—polished professionals, startup founders, people who looked like they belonged in magazines about urban living.

She chose dark jeans (designer look-alikes, actually from Target), a cream-colored silk blouse she'd splurged on after a good month at the firm, and a structured camel blazer she'd found at a consignment shop. Brown leather ankle boots. Simple gold hoops in her ears. Hair down, blown out smooth.

She looked in the mirror and saw someone who was trying. Not trying too hard—she'd learned to avoid that trap—but definitely making an effort to seem like she belonged in spaces where she wasn't sure she did.

The coffee shop was upscale but understated—the kind of place that served expensive drinks in handmade ceramic cups and smelled like fresh-roasted beans and money. The woman waiting for her matched the aesthetic perfectly.

Addie Barrett wore a dove gray cashmere sweater, perfectly fitted, over tailored black trousers. Her shoes were simple leather flats that probably cost more than Bella's entire outfit. A delicate gold watch on her wrist. Ash-blonde hair falling in soft waves. Minimal makeup that somehow made her look more put-together, not less.

She looked expensive without looking ostentatious. She looked like someone who had stopped trying years ago because she no longer needed to.

Bella felt immediately underdressed—not in style, but in essence. Addie wore her clothes like armor. Bella was still wearing hers like a disguise.

Bella recognized her immediately. The one who'd been watching at the gala.

"You're hard to track down," the woman said, standing to shake Bella's hand. "But I suppose that's part of your appeal."

Bella sat, confused. "I'm sorry—do I know you?"

"Not yet." The woman smiled. "My name is Addie Barrett. I work with an organization you've probably heard whispers about but never been able to confirm exists. We noticed you at the Kennedy Foundation gala. The way you handled that crisis—most people would have panicked. You didn't. You saw the problem, calculated the solution, and executed. Quietly. Competently. Without needing credit."

Bella's mouth went dry. "I was just helping."

"No," Addie said. "You were leading. You just don't know it yet."

The conversation lasted two hours. Addie talked about BSS in vague terms at first—a security consulting firm, a network of operators and strategists, a circle of women who shaped outcomes behind the scenes. The more she spoke, the more Bella's heart raced.

This was it. The thing she'd dreamed about. The circle Vee had whispered about years ago.

"We're not looking for resumes," Addie said. "We're looking for intangibles. Trust. Emotional intelligence. Adaptability. The ability to hold things together when everything is falling apart." She leaned forward. "You have all of that, Bella. The question is whether you're willing to use it."

Bella hesitated. Every familiar voice in her head screamed that this was too good to be true, that she didn't belong in a world like this, that women like Addie didn't choose women like her.

But another voice—quieter, braver—asked: What if they do?

"I don't know if I'm ready," Bella admitted.

Addie smiled, soft but certain. "No one ever is. That's not the question. The question is whether you're willing to try."

Bella took a breath.

And she said yes.
