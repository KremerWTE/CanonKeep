# Chapter 9: Galas and Whispers

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Three: Boston Professional Years

---

Bella stayed connected to her sorority network after graduation. It was the smart thing to do—Boston was full of alumnae events, charity galas, and networking mixers where connections could be made and maintained.

And so she showed up. Not for herself, really, but because showing up was what you did.

She bought dresses she couldn't quite afford. Practiced small talk in front of her bathroom mirror. Learned which caterers served the best hors d'oeuvres and which charity boards mattered most for visibility.

At every event, she listened.

The whispers came in fragments. A name dropped over champagne. A knowing glance between two women who seemed to share a secret language. References to something called the "Wives Club" that never quite came into focus.

Vee had mentioned it years ago, and the dream had lodged itself in Bella's chest like a seed that wouldn't stop growing.

She watched the inner circle from the edges. Women who seemed to glide through these events with a certainty Bella couldn't imagine possessing. They weren't flashy—not the kind of wealth that announced itself. They were elegant. Capable. The kind of women who made things happen without needing to explain how.

She wanted to be one of them.

But wanting wasn't the same as believing.

Every time she caught herself dreaming about it, the familiar voice in her head reminded her of the truth: Women like that don't choose women like you. You're the background. The support. The one who makes things work, not the one who matters.

Still, she kept attending the galas. Kept listening to the whispers. Kept hoping that maybe, one day, if she proved herself enough, someone would notice.

She stayed involved with the sorority subsection that seemed to orbit closest to the inner circle. Alumni events. Philanthropy committees. Anything that might create an opportunity.

The Wives Club dream became her quiet north star—a destination she didn't believe she'd ever reach, but couldn't stop walking toward.

Maybe one day, she thought. Maybe someday.
