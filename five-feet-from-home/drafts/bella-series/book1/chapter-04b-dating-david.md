# Chapter 4b: Dating David

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

David Chen noticed her before she noticed him.

Later, he would tell her about it—how he'd spotted her at a coffee shop near the library, head bent over a textbook, highlighter moving across the page with focused precision. How he'd watched her for three weeks, trying to work up the courage to approach. How he'd practiced his opening line until it sounded natural.

"You're always here," he said finally, sliding into the chair across from her. "Same table, same drink, same color highlighter. I'm starting to think you might be a very dedicated ghost."

Bella looked up, startled. Dark eyes, kind face, a crooked smile that seemed slightly embarrassed by its own charm. He was wearing a Boston University Law sweatshirt and holding a coffee cup like a prop.

"I'm not a ghost," she said carefully.

"That's exactly what a ghost would say."

He was trying too hard. She could tell. But there was something genuine underneath the performance—a nervous energy that suggested he wasn't used to this, wasn't the kind of guy who approached random women in coffee shops.

"I'm David," he said. "I'm in my second year at BU Law. I've been coming here for two months trying to figure out how to talk to you without sounding like a creep, and I think I might be failing at that last part."

Despite herself, she laughed. "You're doing okay."

"Okay is good. Okay I can work with." He grinned, more confident now. "Can I buy you another coffee? As a thank-you for not calling campus security?"

She should have said no. She had a study schedule to keep, a sorority event to prepare for, a dozen obligations pulling at her from every direction.

"Sure," she said instead. "Why not."

---

The first date was dinner at a Thai place near campus.

David talked about law school with the kind of enthusiasm Bella associated with people who actually loved what they did. Constitutional law. Civil rights cases. The dream of working for a nonprofit, fighting for people who couldn't fight for themselves.

"What about you?" he asked, pushing pad thai around his plate. "What's your dream?"

The question caught her off guard. No one had asked her that in a long time.

"I don't know," she admitted. "I'm business major. It's practical."

"Practical isn't a dream. That's a strategy." He leaned forward, genuinely curious. "If you could do anything—money no object, expectations no object—what would you do?"

Bella thought about it. Really thought about it, for the first time in years.

"I'd help people," she said slowly. "Not like... charity work, necessarily. More like... fixing things. Being the person who steps in when everything's falling apart and makes it okay again. Does that make sense?"

"It makes perfect sense." David smiled. "You want to be a fixer."

"A fixer?"

"Like in the movies. The person who solves impossible problems. Cleans up messes. Makes things happen that shouldn't be possible." He nodded approvingly. "That's a great dream, Bella. And you're already doing it—I can tell. The way you talked about the sorority stuff earlier. You're the one who makes things work."

She hadn't thought about it that way before. The fixer. The problem solver. The one who stepped in when everything was falling apart.

It wasn't just a coping mechanism. It was a skill.

Maybe even a calling.

---

David was patient in ways she wasn't used to.

He didn't push for more than she was ready to give. He didn't demand explanations for her walls, her hesitations, her tendency to pull back just when things were getting good. He just... stayed. Present. Attentive. Like he had all the time in the world.

"You're different," she told him one night, three months into whatever this was. They were walking along the Charles River, the city lights reflecting on the water. "Most guys would have given up by now."

"Given up on what?"

"Me." She gestured vaguely at herself. "I'm not easy. I know that. I'm busy and distracted and I cancel plans and I forget to text back and I'm basically terrible at being someone's girlfriend."

David stopped walking. Turned to face her.

"Bella, do you actually believe that?"

"It's not about believing. It's just... true."

"It's not true. You're thoughtful and smart and funny when you let yourself be. You remember every detail of every story I've ever told you. You bring me coffee when I'm stressed about exams. You're not terrible at being a girlfriend—you're just convinced you're not worth the effort."

The words hit her like a wave. She didn't have a response.

"I'm not going anywhere," David continued. "I like you. The actual you, not some performance you think I want. So stop waiting for me to figure out you're not worth it. I've already figured out you are."

She should have believed him. She should have let those words sink in, let them rewire the broken circuitry that convinced her she didn't deserve to be chosen.

Instead, she kissed him—hard, desperate, like she could physically silence the doubts—and didn't say anything at all.

---

They dated for eight months.

Eight months of Thai food dinners and late-night study sessions. Eight months of borrowed sweatshirts and shared playlists. Eight months of David being patient and kind and everything she should have wanted.

And all eight months, the voice in Bella's head whispered: *This won't last. He'll figure out you're not worth it. Better to leave first.*

She didn't listen. Not yet.

But the voice got louder every day.
