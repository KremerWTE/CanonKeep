# Chapter 4: The Boy Who Saw Her

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

His name was David, and he was different.

A law student with serious eyes and a quiet intensity that made Bella nervous in ways Tyler never had. They met at a coffee shop near the library—she was cramming for a business statistics exam, he was highlighting case law with the kind of focus that suggested the world outside didn't exist.

He asked if the seat across from her was taken. She said no.

That was junior year.

David was attentive in ways that confused her. He remembered small things—her order at the campus coffee shop, the fact that she always picked the chair closest to the window, the name of the book she'd been reading when they first met. When she talked, he listened like her words actually mattered.

"You're interesting," he told her once, after she'd gone on a tangent about sorority event logistics that she was sure had bored him to tears.

"I'm really not," she said.

He looked at her for a long moment. "You don't see it, do you?"

She didn't.

For months, David was patient. He showed up when he said he would. He asked questions about her family, her dreams, her fears. He held her hand when they walked across campus and looked at her like she was worth looking at.

And the more he looked, the more Bella pulled away.

She buried herself in sorority work. In her business courses. In weekend shifts at the café when she went home to help her parents. There was always something more urgent than dinner with David, always a crisis that needed her attention more than he did.

"You're running," he said one night, frustrated. "From what?"

From the moment you realize I'm not worth this, she thought but didn't say.

She ended it a week later. Gentle, apologetic, with all the right words to make it seem like timing rather than terror.

"I think we want different things," she said—the same words she'd used with Tyler, now weaponized against something real.

David didn't fight it. He just looked at her with those serious eyes and nodded.

"I hope you find whatever you're looking for, Bella," he said. "I hope you let yourself have it when you do."

She cried that night in her dorm room, Tessa holding her while she sobbed.

But she never called him back.
