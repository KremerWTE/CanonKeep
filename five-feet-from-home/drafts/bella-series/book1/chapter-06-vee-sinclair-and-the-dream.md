# Chapter 6: Vee Sinclair and the Dream

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Two: Boston College Years

---

Vivian Sinclair was everything Bella thought she should be but wasn't.

They met through the intercollegiate sorority circuit—Vee was at Harvard, majoring in Political Science with a minor in Art History, gliding through Ivy League social circles like she'd been born into them. Which, in a way, she had.

Old New England family. Quiet wealth. Deep Ivy League ties. Parents in law and academia. The kind of polish that came from generations of expectation and privilege.

Everyone called her Vee. She carried it easily, like everything else—effortless, approachable, but still elevated.

---

Vee's style was what magazines called "quiet luxury"—though Bella didn't learn the term until years later.

At events, Vee wore things that didn't announce themselves: cashmere sweaters in muted tones, tailored wool trousers that fit like they'd been made for her (because they probably had), silk blouses in cream and soft gray that whispered money without screaming it. Her jewelry was always real gold—never yellow, always warm-toned—and simple. A thin chain. A family signet ring. Pearl studs that had belonged to her grandmother.

She owned coats that cost more than Bella's monthly rent. She carried bags with no visible logos because the people who mattered knew the leather on sight. She wore flats to charity galas—delicate suede loafers—because she was tall enough and confident enough not to need the extra inches.

At the philanthropy event where they first spoke, Vee had been wearing a burgundy silk midi dress with long sleeves and a modest neckline—the kind of dress that looked almost understated until you touched the fabric and realized it cost four figures. Her hair was down, brushed straight and shining, with a tortoiseshell clip holding one side back from her face. Simple makeup. No effort visible. All the effort hidden.

Bella, by contrast, was in a black cocktail dress from a department store sale rack—perfectly acceptable, well-fitted, but obviously aspirational. She'd borrowed her roommate's heels (half a size too small, pinching her toes) and worn costume jewelry that passed for real unless you looked too closely. Her hair was up in a twist because she'd been too rushed to style it properly after spending the afternoon running event logistics.

The contrast was sharp enough to cut.

Vee looked like she belonged in every room she entered. Bella looked like she was trying to earn her place in it.

---

The first time they talked—properly talked—Bella was running logistics (as always), and Vee was working the room (as always). Their paths crossed near the silent auction table.

"You're the one who fixed the catering crisis," Vee said, a spark of recognition in her eyes. "I saw you on the phone for an hour. Impressive."

Bella blinked. "You noticed that?"

"I notice everything." Vee smiled, sharp and warm at once. "You're good under pressure. Most people panic. You didn't."

It was the kind of compliment Bella didn't know how to receive. She mumbled something awkward in response.

But the connection was made.

They stayed in touch after that. Coffee meet-ups when Vee came to Boston. Text threads that spanned weeks. Vee was busy with her Harvard life, her prestigious internships, her galas and fundraisers and networking events that seemed to multiply by the semester.

Bella admired her. Envied her, if she was honest.

Vee had the life Bella dreamed of—the confidence, the connections, the easy path toward influence and belonging. Where Bella felt like she was constantly fighting to be seen, Vee simply was.

It was Vee who first told her about the Wives Club.

They were at a coffee shop in Cambridge, senior year. Vee had just returned from a gala that Bella couldn't even imagine being invited to. Her eyes glittered as she talked about the women she'd met there—powerful, elegant, connected in ways that went beyond wealth or status.

"There's a circle," Vee said, lowering her voice like she was sharing a secret. "Not official. Not public. But real. They call it the Wives Club—women who shape things behind the scenes. The ones who actually run things while the men take the credit."

Bella's heart skipped. "How do you get in?"

Vee shrugged elegantly. "You get chosen. That's the thing—you can't ask. You just have to be good enough for someone to notice."

Good enough.

The words lodged in Bella's chest like a splinter.

For days afterward, she couldn't stop thinking about it. A place where women like her—fixers, organizers, the ones who held things together—might actually be seen. Valued. Chosen.

It was a dream she didn't believe would happen. But she couldn't let it go.

Maybe one day, she thought. If I work hard enough. If I prove myself. If someone finally notices.

Maybe one day, I could belong to something like that.
