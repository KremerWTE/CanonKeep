# Chapter 9b: The Sorority Network Events

## Book 1: "Wouldn't It Be Loverly"
### Part Three: Boston Professional Years

---

The sorority alumnae network was Bella's lifeline back into society.

As the world slowly reopened—vaccines rolling out, restrictions easing, people desperate for connection after months of isolation—the first invitations that came were from her sisters. Charity galas. Networking mixers. Fundraising dinners. The machinery of philanthropy grinding back to life.

"You should come," a text from her old chapter president read. "It's the Kennedy Foundation spring event. Black tie. Great for networking."

Bella stared at the message, then at her closet of professional monotony.

She needed something new.

---

She found it at the same consignment shop where she'd once hunted for college formal dresses.

The dress was dark green—not emerald, but something deeper, closer to forest or moss. It had a modest V-neck that was elegant without being revealing, a fitted waist that made her look like she had curves she'd always hidden, and a floor-length skirt that moved like water when she walked.

The original price tag was still attached, discreetly tucked inside the zipper: $1,200 from a designer whose name she'd seen in magazines.

The consignment price was $180.

"It looks like it was made for you," the shopkeeper said, and for once, the flattery felt true.

---

The Kennedy Foundation gala was held at a downtown hotel—crystal chandeliers, string quartet, the smell of expensive perfume and older money.

Bella moved through the crowd the way she always did: observing, cataloging, making herself useful without demanding attention. She refilled champagne glasses when she passed a frazzled waiter. She redirected a confused elderly donor toward the coat check. She noticed when the silent auction display began to tip and steadied it before anyone saw.

But she also networked.

She talked to alumnae who'd risen in their fields—law partners, startup founders, non-profit directors. She collected business cards. She asked questions about career paths and industry trends and "how did you get where you are?"

The answers were remarkably similar: right place, right time, right connections. Luck dressed up as merit.

But there was something else in their stories. A thread she couldn't quite name. References to networks that seemed to exist behind the visible ones. Mentions of "circles" and "connections" that opened doors before you even knew you needed them opened.

She filed it away.

---

At one of these events, she saw a symbol she'd seen before.

It was on a bracelet—delicate gold chain, a small pendant that might have been a bird or might have been something else entirely. The woman wearing it was in her fifties, silver-haired, with the kind of presence that made people clear a path without being asked.

Bella had seen that symbol before. On Vee's grandmother, briefly glimpsed at that dinner at the Ritz. On a painting in a private collection she'd passed at a charity auction. On a letter, once, that had been left on a desk she wasn't supposed to see.

"Excuse me," she said, approaching the woman during a lull in conversation. "That's a beautiful bracelet. The design is... distinctive."

The woman looked at her—really looked, the way few people ever did. Her gaze was assessing, curious, sharp.

"Thank you," she said after a moment. "It was a gift. From a very old friend."

"May I ask what it symbolizes?"

Another long pause. "Perhaps. When the time is right."

She walked away before Bella could ask what that meant.

---

Later that night, scrolling through her phone in her tiny apartment, Bella googled every variation she could think of. Sorority symbols. Philanthropy emblems. Secret society imagery.

Nothing matched.

Whatever that symbol meant, it wasn't something the internet could tell her.
