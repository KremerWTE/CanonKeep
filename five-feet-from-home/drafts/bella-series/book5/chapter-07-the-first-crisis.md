# Chapter 7: The First Crisis

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Four: New Beginnings

---

It happened three weeks after the wedding.

Bella was in the middle of a routine call with Elena when her secure line beeped—the tone that meant urgent, drop everything.

"I have to go," she told Elena. "Priority alert."

"Go. I'll handle the budget review."

---

It was Hawk.

"We have a situation."

The words were calm, but Bella knew him well enough to hear the tension underneath. She switched to encrypted mode, closed her office door.

"Tell me."

"One of our clients—Damien Vance—his daughter was taken. Eight years old. Ransom demand came in an hour ago."

Bella's stomach dropped. Kidnapping cases were the worst—the highest stakes, the narrowest margins for error.

"What's the ask?"

"Fifteen million. Cryptocurrency. Forty-eight hour deadline."

"Do we know who?"

"Not yet. That's why I'm calling." Hawk paused. "I need you here, daughter. This one is delicate. Vance is on the verge of making dangerous decisions. He needs someone to talk him down, and you're the best we have."

Bella looked at the mountains through her window. At the ranch she'd just started calling home.

"I'll be there in six hours."

---

Matt didn't argue.

"This is the job," he said, helping her pack. "I knew that when I married you."

"You're okay?"

"I'm not okay—I don't want you anywhere near danger. But I understand." He pulled her close. "Just come back to me."

"Always."

---

The drive to the airport felt endless. The flight, longer. By the time she reached Charlotte, exhaustion was settling into her bones—but there was no time for rest.

Hawk met her at the compound.

"Vance is in the conference room. He's been there since we called."

"Any updates?"

"We traced the cryptocurrency wallet. It's a shell—layers on layers, routed through countries we don't have relationships with. Whoever did this knows what they're doing."

"Ransom is rarely the whole story."

"I know. That's why I need you to talk to him. Find out what he's not telling us."

---

Damien Vance was a man coming apart.

Bella had met him before—a tech executive, slick and confident, the type who thought money solved everything. Now he sat hunched in a chair, eyes red, hands trembling.

"Mr. Vance."

He looked up. "You. You're the one they said could help."

"I'm going to try." She sat across from him. "But I need you to tell me everything. And I mean everything—not just what you think is relevant."

"I told your people—"

"You told them the sanitized version. The one that makes you look innocent." Bella's voice was gentle but firm. "I'm not here to judge you. I'm here to find your daughter. But I can't do that if you're protecting secrets."

Silence.

Then, slowly, Damien Vance began to talk.

---

The truth was uglier than the ransom suggested.

Vance had been involved in something he shouldn't have—a cryptocurrency scheme that crossed legal lines. He'd made enemies. Powerful ones.

"This isn't about money," Bella said, after he finished. "This is about revenge. Whoever took Sophie—they're using her to punish you."

"Then what do I do?"

"You cooperate with us. Completely. And you let us handle the negotiation."

---

The next thirty-six hours were a blur.

Bella coordinated with law enforcement—carefully, maintaining deniability where needed. She worked with the technical team to trace communications. She talked Vance down from three separate attempts to go rogue.

And through it all, she thought of Matt. Of the mountains. Of the life waiting for her on the other side of this crisis.

---

They found Sophie.

Alive. Scared. But alive.

The takedown happened at 3 AM—a coordinated strike on a property in rural Georgia. Bella wasn't on site, but she listened to the communications, holding her breath until she heard the words:

"Package secured. Minor injuries. En route to medical."

She exhaled so hard she saw spots.

---

Vance wept when they brought Sophie to him.

Raw, ugly sobs—a man confronting how close he'd come to losing everything. Bella gave them space, then quietly excused herself.

In the hallway, Hawk was waiting.

"Good work, daughter."

"It was the team. I just talked."

"You did more than talk. You saw what he wasn't saying." Hawk studied her. "How are you feeling?"

"Tired. Relieved. Ready to go home."

"Then go." He squeezed her shoulder. "Matt's waiting. The ranch is waiting. This world will be here when you need to come back."

---

She drove straight from the airport to the ranch.

Matt was on the porch when she pulled up, like he'd been watching for hours. Probably had been.

Bella got out of the car, walked up the steps, and fell into his arms.

"I'm home," she said.

"You're home."

And that was enough.
