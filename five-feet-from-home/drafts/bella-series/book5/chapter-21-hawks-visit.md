# Chapter 21: Hawk's Visit

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Eight: The Endgame

---

Hawk came alone.

Unusual for him—he rarely traveled without Addie now, their partnership having grown into something that required proximity. But this trip, he said, was about specific things. Father-daughter things.

"Is everything okay?" Bella asked, when she picked him up from the airport.

"Everything is fine." His eyes were warm but tired. "I just needed to see you. And her."

---

Charlotte recognized her grandfather immediately.

"Dada!" she shouted—her word for all adult males, to Matt's ongoing amusement—and reached for him.

Hawk lifted her with an ease that belied his years, a softness in his face that Bella had rarely seen before Charlotte's birth.

"Hello, little one. You've grown."

Charlotte grabbed his nose. He let her.

---

**[FATHER-DAUGHTER MOMENT - FIRE PIT]**

That evening, after Charlotte was asleep, they sat at the fire.

Just the two of them—Matt had tactfully found tasks elsewhere. The ritual was unchanged: flames, stars, two questions.

"How are you doing, daughter?"

Bella considered.

"Good. Really good. Tired, sometimes. Overwhelmed, occasionally. But good in a way I couldn't have imagined before."

"And how can I support you?"

"You already do. Just by existing. By being someone I can call when things get hard." She paused. "By being her grandfather."

Hawk was quiet for a moment.

"I came because I wanted to tell you something. In person."

Bella's heart rate increased. "Something bad?"

"Something true." He looked at her. "I'm getting older, Bella. Not sick—not yet—but aware of time in a way I wasn't before. And I realized I've never said the things I should have said. The things I assumed you knew."

"Dad—"

"Let me finish." He took a breath. "You saved me, daughter. When I first brought you into the family, I told myself it was mentorship. Investment. But the truth is, you gave me something I didn't know I was missing. A child of my own choosing. Someone to love who wasn't connected to me by obligation or legacy."

His voice cracked, just slightly.

"You chose me back. Every day, you chose me. When you called me 'Dad' for the first time—I've never told you this—I went to my room afterward and cried. Actually cried. Because I'd never thought I'd have that. A daughter who wanted me, not what I could provide."

"I've always wanted you."

"I know. I know that now." He smiled, tears in his eyes. "I came to tell you that you're my greatest achievement. Not BSS. Not the empire. You. The person you've become. The life you've built. That's my legacy."

Bella was crying now too.

"You've given me everything. The tools, the confidence, the family I never knew I needed."

"You were already becoming. I just... held the flashlight."

She crossed to him, wrapped him in a hug that was fierce and long.

"I love you, Dad."

"I love you too, daughter. More than I've ever loved anything."

---

Hawk stayed for a week.

He helped with Charlotte—changed diapers, handled midnight wakings, all the things that seemed beneath a man of his stature but which he did without complaint.

He sat with Matt, learning about the ranch, the youth program, the life his daughter had built.

He held fire pit sessions every night—sometimes with Bella alone, sometimes with both of them, once with Charlotte awake and babbling through the whole thing.

"She's going to run that program someday," he said, watching Charlotte command a meeting between her stuffed animals. "Look at that leadership style."

"She's sixteen months old."

"Great leaders start early."

---

The goodbye was hard.

At the airport, Hawk held Bella for a long moment.

"Don't forget what I told you."

"I won't."

"And visit Charlotte. The compound Charlotte. She misses being full."

"I know. Soon."

"Promise?"

"Promise."

He kissed her forehead, the way he always had. Then he walked toward security, and Bella watched until he disappeared.
