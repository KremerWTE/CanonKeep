# Chapter 6: The Honeymoon

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Four: New Beginnings

---

They didn't go far.

The plan had been Italy—two weeks exploring Tuscany, eating their way through Rome, watching sunsets in Positano. But as the wedding approached, something shifted.

"What if we stayed closer to home?" Bella had suggested. "What if the honeymoon was about us, not about destinations?"

Matt had agreed immediately.

So instead of transatlantic flights and tourist crowds, they drove. Just the two of them, Matt's truck, and the open road.

---

The first stop was Asheville.

A bed and breakfast in the mountains, all local wine and farm-to-table dinners. They hiked during the days, explored local galleries, found a bookshop where they spent three hours browsing.

"We should do this more often," Bella said, tucked against him on the porch that evening. "Just... disappear."

"We can. Any time you want."

"BSS might have something to say about that."

"BSS can survive a week without you occasionally." He kissed her temple. "Hawk's been running that company for decades. He doesn't need you every moment."

"I know. It's just..."

"Hard to let go."

"Yeah."

---

The second stop was Charleston.

Cobblestone streets and historic houses, seafood so fresh it practically swam to the table. They took a sunset sail in the harbor, champagne and salt air, the city glowing pink behind them.

"I've been thinking," Matt said.

"About?"

"The ranch. Our ranch." He turned to her. "What if we expanded? Not just horses—though we'd keep those. But something else. Something meaningful."

"Like Mandy's veteran program?"

"Like that, but different." His eyes were bright with possibility. "What if we worked with kids? At-risk youth, troubled teens. Give them structure. Teach them to work with animals. Show them they're capable of more than they think."

Bella's heart swelled.

"That's beautiful, Matt."

"It's just an idea."

"It's more than an idea. It's a vision." She took his hand. "Let's do it. When we get home. Let's start planning."

---

The third stop was Savannah.

Spanish moss and secret gardens, ghost tours that made Bella laugh more than shiver. They ate pralines until they felt sick, then ate more anyway.

"I've never been this relaxed," Bella admitted. "I don't think I knew how."

"Your body's learning."

"What?"

"Relaxation. It's a skill." Matt smiled. "You spent so many years in crisis mode, you forgot there was another way to exist. But it's coming back. I can see it."

"See what?"

"The tension in your shoulders. It's gone. The way your jaw stays unclenched. The way you sleep through the night instead of startling awake." He traced her face with gentle fingers. "You're healing. Not from anything specific—just from years of carrying too much."

Tears pricked her eyes.

"How do you see me so clearly?"

"Because I'm paying attention. Because you're worth paying attention to."

---

The fourth stop was home.

Or rather, what would become home—Matt's ranch in Colorado. They arrived at sunset, the mountains ablaze with color, the horses running to greet them at the fence line.

"Welcome home, Mrs. Holloway," Matt said.

Bella looked at the house—their house now. At the barn, the pastures, the endless sky.

"I'm home," she said. And meant it completely.

---

She'd dressed for the drive—comfortable jeans, worn soft with use. A cream-colored cable-knit sweater. Brown boots, practical and beautiful.

But as she stepped out of the truck, she kicked off the boots and walked barefoot across the property. Feeling the grass between her toes. Grounding herself in this new reality.

The ring caught the sunset light. The pendant lay warm against her chest.

Bella Holloway, standing in her own front yard, married to the man she loved.

It was, without question, the happiest she had ever been.
