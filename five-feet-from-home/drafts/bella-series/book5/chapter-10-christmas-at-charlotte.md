# Chapter 10: Christmas at Charlotte

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Five: Building Something New

---

They drove in on Christmas Eve.

The compound was decorated within an inch of its life—lights on every surface, wreaths on every door, a tree so tall it nearly scraped the ceiling of the main hall.

"Grace's influence?" Bella asked, surveying the spectacle.

"Mostly." Addie hugged her tight. "Though I'll admit, having a child around has brought out everyone's festive side."

Grace appeared from nowhere, launching herself at Bella.

"You're HERE! Finally! Look, look at my dress—it's red like a candy cane—and I helped decorate the tree—the angel on top is MINE—"

"Grace." Addie's voice was gentle. "Breathe."

"But Bella's HERE!"

"I know, sweetheart. And she'll be here for days. You don't have to fit everything into the first five minutes."

---

The gathering was everyone.

Hawk and Addie. Jasper and Elena, newly engaged, matching rings catching the light. Kendra and Chris with their easy partnership. Harper, flown in from Miami. Even Bella's biological parents, who'd made the trip from their small town, looking slightly overwhelmed but determinedly present.

And Matt, standing at her side, fitting into this complicated family like he'd always been there.

"It's a lot," Bella admitted to him, during a quiet moment in the kitchen.

"It's love. Just... loud."

"Very loud."

"I can handle loud." He kissed her cheek. "I grew up with four sisters."

---

Bella had dressed for the occasion.

A deep green velvet dress that skimmed her curves—festive without being costume-y. Her pendant, always. Simple gold hoops in her ears. Her wedding ring catching light every time she moved her hands.

Matt had managed a sport coat over his usual button-down. He looked uncomfortable but handsome, the way men often did when forced into formality.

"You look beautiful," he'd said when she came downstairs.

"You look like you're plotting escape routes."

"Only three so far."

---

Christmas morning was organized chaos.

Grace was up at dawn, unable to contain herself. By the time Bella and Matt emerged, presents were being distributed with military precision—Addie's doing, clearly.

The gifts were thoughtful, personal.

From Hawk: a leather-bound journal, the cover embossed with the ranch's new logo. A matching one for Matt.

From Addie: a beautiful cashmere throw for their living room. "For cold nights watching the fire."

From Elena and Jasper: a set of crystal wine glasses. "For celebrating all your wins."

From Grace: a homemade card with glitter (everywhere) and a drawing of horses (vaguely recognizable).

---

Bella's gift to the family was different.

She'd had it framed—a photograph from the wedding. Not the formal shots, but a candid one. All of them together, laughing at something Grace had said, caught in a moment of pure joy.

"Oh, daughter," Addie said, voice thick.

"For the compound wall. So I'm here even when I'm not."

Hawk cleared his throat. Twice.

---

The fire pit was tradition.

After dinner, after the chaos settled, the adults gathered in the cold December night. Flames against darkness. Stars overhead.

"I don't want to be sentimental," Hawk began.

"Too late," Kendra muttered.

"But this year has been... exceptional. We've weathered challenges. Celebrated victories. Built something that matters." He raised his glass. "To family. The one we're born into and the one we choose."

"To family," they echoed.

Bella felt Matt's hand tighten on hers. Looked around at the faces illuminated by firelight. Her fathers—both of them. Her mothers, biological and chosen. Her siblings of circumstance. Her husband.

"Thank you," she said, quiet enough that only Matt heard.

"For what?"

"For fitting. For making this possible. For being mine."

"I'm always yours."

---

They left on December 27th.

Hugs that lasted too long. Promises to visit. Grace crying actual tears until Bella promised a summer trip to the ranch.

"Drive safe," Hawk said, shaking Matt's hand.

"I'll take care of her."

"I know you will." Hawk's eyes met Bella's. "Both of you—take care of each other."

"Always, Dad."

The word still felt like a gift. After all this time. Always.
