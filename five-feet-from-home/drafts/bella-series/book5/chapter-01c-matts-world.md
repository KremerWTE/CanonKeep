# Chapter 1c: Matt's World

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part One: Courtship

---

Matt's property was adjacent to Mandy's ranch—smaller, but beautiful in its own way.

He'd built the cabin himself over two years. Learned as he went, made mistakes, fixed them. The result was something imperfect and wonderful—a home that felt like it had grown from the land rather than being imposed upon it.

"It's not much," he said, showing her around.

"It's yours. That makes it everything."

---

The vacation home business was his latest venture.

After the Army, Matt had worked a series of jobs—construction, ranch hand, property management. Eventually, he'd realized he could combine all three: buying run-down cabins, renovating them, and renting them to tourists who wanted the mountain experience.

"I've got four properties now," he explained, driving her past them. "Each one different. Some people want rustic. Some want luxury. I try to offer both."

"That's smart."

"It's practical." He shrugged. "I needed income that let me stay here. This was the answer."

---

They stopped at his favorite property—a cabin perched on a ridge with views of three valleys.

"This one I might keep for myself someday," Matt admitted. "When I'm old. When I need a place to watch the world turn without being part of it."

"That's a long time from now."

"Maybe. Maybe not." He sat on the porch swing, patted the space beside him. "The Army taught me that tomorrow isn't guaranteed. Might as well enjoy today."

Bella sat beside him. "You talk about the Army differently than the veterans at Mandy's place."

"I came home whole. They didn't." His voice was quiet. "That's not about being tougher or stronger. It's just luck. I was in the right unit, at the right time, with the right missions. Could easily have gone the other way."

"Does it bother you? Being okay when others aren't?"

"Sometimes. That's why I help at the ranch." He gazed at the horizon. "I figure if I got lucky, the least I can do is support the ones who didn't."

---

Over the following weeks, Bella learned more about his world.

The neighbors who waved from their trucks. The coffee shop owner who knew his order by heart. The horses he'd raised from foals, trained himself, loved like family.

"You've built something here," she said one afternoon.

"Something small."

"Something real." She turned to face him. "My world is all about big gestures. Crisis management. High stakes. But this..." She gestured at the property around them. "This is different. Quieter. Maybe better."

"Different doesn't mean better."

"No. But it might mean balance." She smiled. "I've never had balance, Matt. I've had intensity, always. Maybe you're the counterweight."

"That's a lot of pressure for a simple rancher."

"You're not simple. You just know how to be still." She took his hand. "I'm learning that from you."

---

That night, she called Hawk.

"I'm falling for him."

"I know."

"How do you know? I just figured it out myself."

"Daughter." She could hear his smile through the phone. "Your voice has been different for weeks. Softer. Happier. I was wondering when you'd catch up."

"Is it too fast?"

"After everything you've been through? After Mandy, and the work you've done on yourself?" Hawk's voice was warm. "No. It's not too fast. It's exactly the right pace for you."

"What if I mess it up?"

"Then you'll learn something. But you won't mess it up by loving him." A pause. "You might mess it up by not trusting yourself. Don't do that, Bella. You've earned the right to be happy."
