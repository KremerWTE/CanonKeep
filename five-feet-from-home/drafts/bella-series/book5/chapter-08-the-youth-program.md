# Chapter 8: The Youth Program

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Five: Building Something New

---

The idea had been simmering since the honeymoon.

Now, two months into married life, Matt and Bella were ready to make it real.

"We need a plan," Bella said, spreading papers across the kitchen table. "Structure. Funding. Partnerships. This can't just be a nice idea—it has to be sustainable."

"Says the woman who runs an international security company."

"Habit."

Matt smiled and handed her coffee. "Walk me through it."

---

The concept was simple: bring at-risk youth to the ranch for structured programs.

Not a boot camp—nothing punitive or militaristic. Instead, a place where kids could learn to work with horses, develop life skills, and discover their own capabilities.

"Horses teach patience," Matt explained, as they refined the proposal. "They teach consequences. You can't force a horse to trust you—you have to earn it. Kids who've never had anyone earn their trust? They respond to that."

"What about staffing?"

"We start small. Me, you when you're here, the ranch hands for support. As we grow, we bring in professionals—counselors, educators, people with experience."

"Funding?"

"That's where your world comes in." Matt looked at her. "The Wives Club. BSS. The people you know—they have resources. Connections. If we can get them on board..."

"We'll have everything we need to launch."

---

The first call was to Elena.

"A youth program at the ranch?" Elena's voice was thoughtful. "That's ambitious."

"It's meaningful," Bella corrected. "Matt's good with kids. The ranch is the perfect setting. We just need help with the structure."

"And funding."

"And funding."

Silence on the line. Then: "Send me the proposal. I'll review it, connect you with people in the nonprofit space. This could work, Bella. It really could."

---

The second call was to Addie.

She listened to the entire pitch without interrupting—unusual for Addie, who typically had thoughts on everything.

"This is what you want?" she asked finally. "Not just Matt—you?"

"Yes. I want to build something good. Something that helps people directly, not just... managing crises for the wealthy."

"There's nothing wrong with what we do, daughter."

"I know. But this feels different." Bella searched for words. "Like a legacy. Something that will outlast me."

Addie was quiet for a long moment.

"I'll talk to Hawk. We'll see what BSS can contribute—consulting, security protocols, maybe some seed funding."

"Addie—"

"You're family, Bella. This is what family does."

---

By month three, they had a name: Second Chance Ranch.

Matt hated it at first—too on the nose, he said. But the more they worked, the more it fit. Second chances for kids who'd never gotten a first one. Second chances for veterans who helped staff the program. Second chances for everyone involved.

"It's growing," Bella said one evening, reviewing applications. "We haven't even launched and we already have waiting lists."

"Word spreads." Matt looked up from the corral, where he was working with a new horse. "Good things always spread."

---

The infrastructure took shape over the summer.

A bunkhouse for overnight programs—nothing fancy, but clean and safe. A dedicated classroom for educational sessions. An expanded stable area where kids could learn horsemanship basics.

Matt built most of it himself, with help from the ranch hands and occasional volunteers. Bella handled the paperwork—permits, liability waivers, partnerships with local schools and juvenile programs.

"We make a good team," she said, watching the bunkhouse roof go up.

"Did you doubt it?"

"Never. But it's nice to have proof."

---

The first cohort was scheduled for fall.

Ten kids, ages twelve to sixteen. All referred through official channels—courts, schools, social services. All carrying weight that no child should have to carry.

"Are we ready?" Matt asked, the night before they arrived.

Bella thought about everything that had led to this moment. Charlotte. The Wives Club. Mandy. The wedding. All of it building toward something she couldn't have predicted.

"We're ready," she said. "Or we'll figure it out as we go."

"That's the spirit."
