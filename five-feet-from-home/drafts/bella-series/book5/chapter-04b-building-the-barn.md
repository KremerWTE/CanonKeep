# Chapter 4b: Building the Barn

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Two: The Engagement

---

The idea came the week after Matt proposed.

They were sitting on Mandy's porch at the ranch—the one on the border of North Carolina, Virginia, and Tennessee—when Bella realized she didn't want a venue. She didn't want a hotel ballroom or a rented space that meant nothing.

She wanted to build something.

"A barn," she said, the idea forming as she spoke. "Our barn. Here, on this land."

Mandy raised an eyebrow but didn't object. Matt set down his coffee.

"We won't get married until it's done," he said, measuring the idea with the same patience he brought to everything.

Bella looked at Mandy, expecting her to object. Instead, Mandy was already pulling on work gloves.

"Then we better get started," Mandy said.

---

The barn rose slowly, post by post, beam by beam.

They worked weekends. Sometimes evenings when the light lasted long enough. Bella learned to swing a hammer, to hold boards steady, to measure twice and cut once. Her hands blistered and calloused. Her arms ached in ways the gym never prepared her for.

But there was something sacred about it.

The three of them, working together. Mandy, who had loved Bella first and now loved her enough to help build her future with someone else. Matt, who treated every nail like a promise. Bella, who finally understood that family wasn't about who came first—it was about who stayed.

"This is weird, right?" Bella asked one Saturday, sawdust in her hair, sweat dripping down her back. "Building a barn with my ex and my fiancé?"

Mandy laughed, the sound echoing off the half-finished walls. "Weird would be pretending we don't all care about each other. This? This is just honest."

Matt nodded from the ladder. "She's right. Nothing weird about love that grows instead of shrinks."

Bella looked at them both—the woman who had taught her she could be chosen, and the man who was choosing her now—and felt something crack open in her chest.

Not pain. Just space. Room for all of it to exist together.

---

The dance lessons started in month three.

The barn was framed but not finished. Sawdust covered the plywood floor. Half the walls were still open to the wind.

But Matt had brought a speaker, and Mandy had brought champagne, and suddenly they were standing in the middle of their half-built future, learning to waltz.

"I don't dance," Bella protested, already stumbling over Matt's feet.

"You're learning," Matt said, steady as ever. "Same way I'm learning. Together."

Mandy sat on a sawhorse, calling out instructions. "One-two-three, one-two-three. No, Bella, that's a four. There is no four in a waltz."

"I'm a numbers person! Four exists!"

"Not in dancing, it doesn't."

They practiced every weekend after that. Sometimes to old country songs. Sometimes to classics. Sometimes to music Mandy chose specifically to make them laugh.

By the time the barn was finished—walls up, roof on, fairy lights strung from the rafters—Bella could waltz without counting. Matt could dip her without dropping her. And the three of them had built something that would last longer than any of them could imagine.

The wedding happened three weeks later.

In the barn they built together.

On the dance floor they'd practiced on a hundred times.

With everyone they loved watching as Bella and Matt moved through their first dance like it was the most natural thing in the world.

Because by then, it was.
