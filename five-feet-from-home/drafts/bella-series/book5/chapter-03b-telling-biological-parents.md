# Chapter 3b: Telling Biological Parents

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part One: Courtship

---

The trip to her hometown felt different this time.

Matt was in the passenger seat, watching the familiar landscapes pass—the exits she recognized from childhood, the small towns with their Main Streets and diners, the way the air changed as they crossed into familiar territory.

"Nervous?" she asked.

"A little." He adjusted his hat. "Meeting your chosen family was one thing. Meeting the people who made you is something else."

"They're simpler than Charlotte. Less... complicated."

"Is that good or bad?"

"Neither. Just different." She smiled. "They'll like you. You're exactly the kind of person my father respects—someone who works with their hands, builds things, doesn't talk too much."

---

Her parents were waiting on the porch.

Her mother, round and warm, unable to stay still with excitement. Her father, quieter, studying Matt the way he studied rare books—looking for signs of quality, of worth.

"Welcome, welcome!" Her mother pulled Matt into a hug before he'd even fully exited the car. "Bella's told us so much about you."

"Hopefully good things, ma'am."

"She said you're patient and kind and you make her happy." Her mother beamed. "That's all we need to know."

---

They spent the afternoon at the cafe.

Matt helped behind the counter without being asked—a natural instinct, the same way he helped at the ranch. Bella watched him learn her mother's system, adapt to the rhythms of the small-town coffee shop.

"He fits," her father observed, during a quiet moment.

"You think so?"

"He doesn't try to impress. He just works." Her father nodded approvingly. "That's rare these days."

"He's a rancher. Working is all he knows."

"Then he knows more than most." Her father met her eyes. "You look different, Bella. With him. Happier."

"I am happier."

"Good. That's all a father wants."

---

Dinner was at the family home—her mother's cooking, the same dishes Bella had grown up eating. Matt complimented everything, asked questions about recipes, told stories about ranch life that made her mother laugh.

"He charmed her," Bella's father said quietly, as they cleared dishes together.

"Is that bad?"

"Not at all. Your mother deserves to be charmed." He handed her a stack of plates. "But I'm more interested in what he does when no one's watching."

"He's exactly the same. That's the thing about Matt—there's no performance. What you see is what you get."

Her father considered this. "Then I like him. Genuinely."

"Even though he's a rancher from Colorado?"

"Bella, I'm a bookseller from a small town. I'm not exactly high society." He smiled—rare, genuine. "What matters is that he loves you. And that you love him. Everything else is just... details."

---

That night, Matt and Bella walked through her hometown.

The streets she'd walked as a teenager. The bookstore where she'd hidden during difficult days. The spot behind the cafe where she'd had her first kiss.

"I understand you better now," Matt said.

"How so?"

"You grew up learning to serve. To help. To disappear into usefulness." He squeezed her hand. "That's where the doubt came from, isn't it? Being invisible."

"Maybe."

"But you're not invisible anymore." He stopped, turned to face her. "I see you, Bella. Every part of you. The capable part and the scared part. The confident part and the part that still wonders if she's enough."

"And?"

"And I love all of it." He kissed her softly. "I'm not going anywhere. You don't have to earn me. You just have to let me stay."
