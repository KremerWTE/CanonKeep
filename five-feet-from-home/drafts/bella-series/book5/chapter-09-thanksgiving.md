# Chapter 9: Thanksgiving

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Five: Building Something New

---

The question came up in October.

"Where are we spending Thanksgiving?"

Bella looked up from her laptop. Matt was leaning against the doorframe, coffee in hand, the easy question carrying complicated weight.

"I don't know. Charlotte?"

"That's a lot of people."

"The ranch?"

"Also a lot of people, if we invite both families."

Bella closed her laptop.

"What are you thinking?"

"I'm thinking..." Matt crossed to sit beside her. "This is our first Thanksgiving as a married couple. Our first holiday in our home. Maybe it should be ours. Small. Just us."

"That sounds perfect. But also..."

"Also?"

"Also, I miss them. Charlotte. The compound. Grace's chaos and Hawk's whiskey and Addie's cooking." Bella sighed. "I want both. Impossible as that is."

---

The solution was Matt's idea.

"Split it. Thanksgiving here, just us. Christmas at the compound. We get our private holiday, they get us for the big one."

"What about your family?"

"My family is small. Mom would understand. Or she could come here for Thanksgiving—she'd love the ranch."

---

Thanksgiving arrived cold and clear.

Matt's mother came—a small woman with silver hair and Matt's easy smile. She'd brought three pies and a lifetime of stories Bella hadn't heard yet.

"He was a terror as a child," she said, over turkey. "Into everything. Couldn't sit still for five minutes."

"Mom."

"What? She should know what she married." She winked at Bella. "He once convinced his entire kindergarten class that horses could fly. Took the teacher a week to undo that damage."

Bella laughed so hard she cried.

---

After dinner, they sat by the fire.

Matt's mother had dozed off in the armchair. Matt was stretched on the couch, Bella tucked against him, watching flames dance.

"I'm grateful," Bella said quietly.

"For?"

"Everything. This house. The program. You." She traced his hand with her fingers. "A year ago, I couldn't have imagined this. Any of this."

"A year ago, you were at Mandy's ranch, trying to figure out who you were without Hawk's shadow."

"I found out."

"Yeah?"

"I'm myself. Just... more. More capable, more confident, more..." She searched for the word.

"Complete?"

"That's pretentious."

"But accurate?"

Bella considered. "Maybe. Something like that."

---

They video-called Charlotte after dessert.

The compound was chaos—Grace running around in a turkey costume, Hawk looking exhausted but happy, Addie coordinating what appeared to be thirty people in the dining room.

"We miss you!" Grace shouted into the camera.

"We miss you too, sweetheart."

"Are you coming for Christmas? Please say yes. Mom said I could stay up until MIDNIGHT if you come."

Bella laughed. "We'll be there. I promise."

"YAY!" Grace vanished, probably to tell everyone the news.

Hawk appeared in frame.

"How's the program?"

"Growing. We have three cohorts scheduled for spring."

"Good." His eyes were warm. "I'm proud of you, daughter. Both of you."

"Thanks, Dad."

Matt, beside her, smiled at the word. Dad. So natural now, after years of practice.

---

That night, in bed, Matt traced patterns on her shoulder.

"I want this," he said. "Every year. Every holiday. Growing old together, building traditions, being grateful for what we have."

"Even when it's hard?"

"Especially when it's hard." He kissed her forehead. "The hard parts are what make the good parts mean something."

"That's very philosophical for a rancher."

"I have depths."

"I know you do." She curled closer. "I'm still discovering them."

"Good. I'd hate to be boring."
