# Chapter 1b: The First Date

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part One: Courtship

---

Matt kept his word.

The next time Bella came to Colorado, he picked her up at the airport himself—truck polished, hat in hand, that quiet smile that made her heart do things it wasn't used to.

"I was going to rent a car," she said.

"Didn't make sense. We're going to the same place." He took her bag. "Unless you wanted the independence."

"No." She climbed into the passenger seat. "This is nice."

---

The restaurant he'd chosen was in a small mountain town, about an hour from the ranch. Nothing fancy—wooden booths, local beer on tap, the kind of place where everyone knew the owner's name.

"I thought about somewhere nicer," Matt admitted, as they settled in. "But this felt more... us."

"Us?"

"Real. Simple." He shrugged. "I'm not good at fancy, Bella. Never learned how. If that's a problem—"

"It's not a problem." She reached across the table, touched his hand. "I've had enough fancy for a lifetime. Real sounds good."

---

They talked for hours.

Not the careful, strategic conversation Bella was used to from networking events. Not the loaded discussions of relationship potential. Just... talking.

Matt told her about Wyoming. The ranch where he'd grown up, the parents who'd taught him to ride before he could walk. The brother who'd died young—car accident, nothing dramatic, just the cruelty of randomness.

"That's when I learned patience," he said. "Jackson was always in a hurry. Always chasing the next thing. And then he was gone, and I realized—what's the point of rushing toward the end?"

"I'm sorry."

"It was a long time ago." His smile was sad but genuine. "He'd have liked you, though. He always had good taste in women."

---

Bella told him about her own journey.

Not everything—she couldn't explain BSS, not fully. But she told him about feeling invisible growing up. About the self-doubt that had followed her through college and into her career. About finding Hawk and Addie and learning that she was worth something after all.

"You talk about them like family," Matt observed.

"They are family. The chosen kind."

"That's a good kind to have." He nodded slowly. "I'd like to meet them someday."

"You would?"

"If this goes where I think it's going? Yeah." His eyes met hers across the table. "I want to know the people who matter to you. That's how you learn someone—through who they love."

---

After dinner, they walked through the small town.

The main street was quiet, shops closed for the night, stars emerging overhead. Matt took her hand—the first time he'd initiated physical contact beyond the brief kisses at the barn.

"Thank you," Bella said.

"For what?"

"For taking your time. For not rushing." She squeezed his hand. "I'm not used to that."

"I know." His voice was gentle. "You've spent your whole life moving fast, proving yourself, staying ahead of something you probably couldn't even name. That's exhausting."

"It is."

"So maybe now you can rest. Just a little." He stopped, turned to face her. "I'm not going anywhere, Bella. You don't have to earn me. You just have to let me be here."

She kissed him.

Not like the soft pecks at the barn. A real kiss, deliberate and sure.

When they parted, Matt was smiling.

"That was worth waiting for," he said.

"Yeah. It was."
