# Chapter 16: Coming Home

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Seven: A New Chapter

---

They left the hospital on day three.

Charlotte in a car seat, looking impossibly small. Matt driving even more carefully than he had on the way in. Bella in the back, unable to stop staring at her daughter.

"We're taking a baby home," she said. "They're letting us take a baby home."

"We're her parents. That's how it works."

"But what if we do it wrong?"

"Then we adjust. That's also how it works."

---

The ranch was different now.

Same mountains. Same house. Same horses waiting at the fence line. But everything had shifted—reoriented around the small being in Bella's arms.

"Welcome home, Charlotte," Matt said, as they crossed the threshold. "This is your house. Your ranch. Your life."

Charlotte slept through the announcement, as newborns do.

---

The first night was chaos.

Feeding every two hours. Diaper changes in the dark. Charlotte's cries cutting through sleep like a knife.

"This is impossible," Bella said, at 3 AM, swaying with Charlotte in the rocking chair.

"It's only impossible until it isn't." Matt appeared with water and crackers. "We'll survive. People have been doing this for millennia."

"Millennia of sleep deprivation."

"And yet, here we all are."

---

The days blurred together.

Visitors came and went: Matt's mother, staying for a week. Addie and Hawk, who'd flown in the day after the birth and were reluctant to leave. Bella's parents, hovering helpfully. Even Mandy made the trip, bringing supplies and practical advice.

"Sleep when she sleeps," Mandy said. "I know everyone says it. But actually do it."

"I can't turn my brain off."

"Learn." Mandy's voice was gentle but firm. "This is a marathon, not a sprint. You need rest to run it."

---

**[OUTFIT DETAILS - NEW MOTHER]**

Bella's wardrobe had simplified again.

Soft, stretchy clothing that accommodated nursing. Hair that stayed unwashed longer than she wanted to admit. The pendant, always, resting above a heart that had grown to contain more love than she'd known was possible.

She didn't look polished. She didn't feel polished.

But when she caught her reflection—exhausted, rumpled, holding her daughter—she saw something else.

Joy. Raw, unfiltered, undeniable joy.

---

The breakthrough came at week two.

Charlotte slept for four consecutive hours. Four HOURS. Bella woke in a panic, convinced something was wrong.

"She's fine," Matt said, having already checked. "She's just... sleeping."

"Babies do that?"

"Apparently."

They stood over the crib together, watching their daughter breathe, wondering when the other shoe would drop.

It didn't.

---

By month one, patterns emerged.

Charlotte ate at predictable intervals. Slept in something resembling a schedule. Smiled—gas, probably, but Bella chose to believe it was deliberate.

"We're doing this," Bella said, one morning, watching Matt feed Charlotte while she drank her first coffee in weeks. "We're actually doing this."

"Did you doubt it?"

"Constantly."

"Me too." He grinned. "We're getting better at hiding it."

---

Work resumed, slowly.

Virtual meetings with Charlotte in her lap. Strategy calls with a baby monitor on the desk. A new normal that wasn't perfect but functioned.

"You don't have to work yet," Hawk said, during one call.

"I want to. Part of me needs it." Bella shifted Charlotte to her other shoulder. "I'm still me. I'm just also someone's mother now."

"You're Bella Holloway. You're also one of the best crisis managers I've ever trained. And now you're a parent." Hawk's smile was warm. "You contain multitudes, daughter. Always have."
