# Chapter 14: The Final Months

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Six: Growth

---

The third trimester brought its own challenges.

Sleep was impossible. Any position was uncomfortable. The baby seemed to have a personal vendetta against her bladder.

"How do people do this?" Bella asked, at 3 AM, for the fourth time that night.

"They survive it. And then they forget how bad it was, so they do it again." Matt's voice was groggy but patient. "That's literally the only explanation for siblings."

"Remind me of this conversation when I suggest having another one."

"Noted."

---

Her body was unrecognizable.

Not in a bad way—just different. The belly that seemed to grow exponentially each week. The swelling in her ankles. The way her center of gravity had shifted, making her walk like a ship in rough waters.

"You're beautiful," Matt said, daily.

"I'm enormous."

"Beautifully enormous."

"That's not a thing."

"It is now."

---

Work continued, adapted.

Virtual meetings replaced travel. Her team had stepped up—just as Hawk predicted—finding their own methods, solving problems she'd have handled differently but no less effectively.

"You're learning to let go," Hawk observed, during one of their weekly calls.

"I don't have a choice."

"You always have a choice. You're choosing to trust. That's different."

---

The nesting instinct intensified.

Every room was organized, reorganized, organized again. Matt learned to stay out of her way during these fits of productivity.

"The crib is perfect where it is," he said, watching her measure angles for the third time.

"The light is better by the window."

"You said that yesterday about the other window."

"I was wrong yesterday."

He helped her move the crib. Again.

---

**[SETTING DETAILS - THE NURSERY]**

The nursery was complete.

Soft sage walls—gender neutral but warm. The crib, finally positioned in its optimal spot. A rocking chair that Matt's mother had shipped from storage—the one she'd used with him.

Bookshelves filled with children's books: classics, new favorites, titles in multiple languages (Addie's contribution). A mobile above the crib featuring horses, naturally.

And in the corner, a small bench where Bella would sit when she couldn't sleep, imagining the child who would soon inhabit this space.

---

The due date approached.

Bella's biological mother arrived, wanting to help but uncertain how to fit into this extended family structure.

"You could show me how to make that thing you made when I was sick as a kid," Bella suggested. "The soup."

"You remember that?"

"I remember everything." Bella took her mother's hand. "Teach our child what you taught me. The small things. The quiet things."

Her mother's eyes filled.

"I thought... I was afraid I wouldn't have a place here."

"You have every place you want." Bella squeezed her hand. "This family is big enough for everyone. That's the whole point."

---

The false alarms started at week thirty-eight.

Three trips to the hospital. Three times, sent home.

"The baby will come when the baby is ready," the doctor said, with the patience of someone who'd said these words thousands of times.

"I'm ready NOW."

"That's often the case."

Matt kept a bag packed by the door. Kept the car fueled. Kept his phone charged to one hundred percent at all times.

"You're a little intense," Bella observed.

"Says the woman who reorganized the nursery seven times."

"Point taken."

---

At thirty-nine weeks, things got quiet.

The frantic preparation faded. The anxiety settled. In its place, a strange calm.

"This is it," Bella said, one evening, hands on her belly. "The last days of it being just us."

"Scared?"

"Terrified. Ready. Both, always."

"That seems to be our pattern."

"It's a good pattern." She leaned against him. "I'm glad I get to do this with you."

"There's no one else I'd want to do it with."
