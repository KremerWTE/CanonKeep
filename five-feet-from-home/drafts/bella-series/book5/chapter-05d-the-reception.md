# Chapter 5d: The Reception

## Book 5: "Black Moves Second"
### Part Three: The Wedding

---

The barn transformed again.

While guests mingled during cocktail hour in Mandy's garden, the crew had converted the ceremony space into a reception venue. Tables appeared, draped in white linen. The wildflower arrangements were redistributed. A dance floor materialized where the aisle had been.

By the time Bella and Matt made their entrance as husband and wife, it was a different room entirely.

"Ladies and gentlemen," the DJ announced, "for the first time—Mr. and Mrs. Matthew and Bella Holloway!"

The applause was thunderous.

---

Bella had changed for the reception—not a complete transformation, but a practical one.

The ceremony train had been bustled, allowing her to move freely. She'd swapped the delicate heels for dancing flats—pearl-white with subtle crystal accents. Her hair was still pinned, but looser now, a few more tendrils escaping to frame her face.

The sapphire bracelet from Hawk's mother remained on her wrist. The pendant from the Wives Club at her throat. The diamond studs in her ears.

Matt had shed his jacket and loosened his tie. He looked relaxed. Happy. Like a man who'd just gotten everything he'd ever wanted.

---

The first dance was "The Way You Look Tonight."

Matt had chosen it.

"Because it's what I thought the first time I saw you," he'd explained during rehearsal. "Standing in Mandy's barn, looking lost and beautiful and completely out of place. And all I could think was—that's the most stunning woman I've ever seen."

Now they swayed together, his hand warm on her lower back, her head resting against his chest.

"We're married," she whispered.

"We are."

"That's insane."

"The best kind of insane." He pulled back to look at her. "Any regrets?"

"Not a single one."

---

Hawk's toast came next.

He stood at the head table, whiskey glass in hand, and the room went silent. This was a man people listened to.

"I'm not good at speeches," he began. "I prefer action. But some moments require words, and this is one of them."

He looked at Bella.

"When I met Bella, she was lost. Not in a bad way—in the way people are lost when they haven't found their path yet. She had potential she couldn't see. Strength she didn't believe in. A light she'd been taught to hide."

He paused.

"I didn't save her. Let's be clear about that. I gave her tools. I gave her opportunities. But Bella did the work. Every breakthrough, every transformation—that was her. I just... held the flashlight while she found her way."

Laughter, soft and affectionate.

"Matt." Hawk turned to the groom. "I entrust her to you. Not because she needs protection—she can handle herself. But because she deserves someone who sees her. Really sees her. Someone steady and patient and good."

He raised his glass.

"You're that man. Don't make me regret saying so."

More laughter. Matt nodded, serious.

"To Bella and Matt," Hawk concluded. "May your love grow deeper with every passing year. May your challenges make you stronger, not weaker. And may you always remember—you're not just building a marriage. You're building a life."

---

Addie's toast was warmer, more personal.

"I've had the privilege of watching Bella grow into herself," she said. "From the uncertain young woman who joined our family, to the confident, capable leader she is today. Every step of that journey has been a gift."

She smiled at Matt.

"And then you came along. This quiet, patient cowboy who somehow saw through all her walls. Who didn't push or demand—just waited. Showed up. Loved her exactly as she was."

Addie's voice caught.

"That's rare. That kind of love is rare. And Bella, my daughter in every way that matters—you deserve rare."

She raised her glass.

"To finding home in each other. To building something beautiful. To love that lasts."

---

The father-daughter dance was complicated.

Elena had solved it elegantly: half the song with Hawk, half with her biological father. The transition happened seamlessly, a passing of hands, a shared smile.

Her biological father wasn't a natural dancer, but he tried.

"I'm proud of you," he said, as they shuffled awkwardly. "I know I don't say it enough. I know I never quite understood your choices. But I'm proud of who you've become."

"Thank you, Dad."

"He's a good man. Matt."

"He is."

"Your mother thinks so too." He smiled, small and sincere. "She said he reminds her of me. Steady. Reliable. Not flashy, but solid."

Bella laughed. "That's a pretty good comparison."

---

The mother-son dance was simpler.

Matt's mother was a small woman, gray-haired and warm. She'd hugged Bella for a full minute when they'd first met, welcoming her to the family without reservation.

Now she danced with her son, and Bella could see them talking—low, intimate conversation. Matt nodded several times. His mother dabbed at her eyes.

When the song ended, she crossed to Bella.

"He told me to tell you something," she said. "That he's the luckiest man alive. And that he'll spend the rest of his life trying to deserve you."

"He already deserves me," Bella replied. "He deserves everything."

---

The bouquet toss was chaotic.

Harper caught it—possibly not by accident. The veterans whistled. Grace pouted because she'd been too short to compete.

The garter toss was worse.

Matt slid it off slowly, eyes locked on Bella's, while the crowd whooped. Then he turned, launched it backward—

Jasper caught it.

Elena's face was priceless. Grace started chanting "KISS, KISS, KISS" and the whole room joined in.

---

By midnight, the reception was winding down.

The older guests had departed. The dance floor was populated by the diehards—Tessa, still going strong; Harper and her catch, whatever his name was; a cluster of veterans who'd clearly been overserved.

Bella and Matt stood at the edge, watching.

"Ready to go?" he asked.

"Almost." She scanned the room, memorizing it. "I want to remember this. Every detail."

"You will."

"Promise?"

"Promise." He kissed her forehead. "We'll tell our grandkids about it someday. The barn wedding at Mandy's ranch. The two fathers. The veteran groomsmen. All of it."

"Grandkids," she repeated. "That's a long way away."

"All the more reason to start now."
