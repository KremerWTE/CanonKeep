# Chapter 3: After Hawk

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part One: The Full Circle

---

The world without Hawk was different.

Not empty—too full of legacy for that. But changed. The particular gravity he'd exerted on everything around him was gone.

"I keep reaching for the phone," Bella admitted to Matt, weeks after the funeral. "To call him. To tell him things."

"That's normal."

"I know. It just... hurts."

---

The adjustment took time.

BSS continued, Jasper at the helm now, the transition smooth because Hawk had planned it meticulously. The board—which included Bella—met quarterly to ensure the values remained intact.

The Wives Club absorbed the loss differently. Hawk had never been a member, obviously, but his influence had shaped so much of what they were. His absence left a shadow.

"We carry him forward," Addie said, at a gathering. "That's all we can do. That's all he would have wanted."

---

**[GRIEF PROCESS]**

Bella found herself at the fire pit more often.

Their ranch fire pit now, not the compound's. But the ritual remained: the two questions, the honest answers, the space to process.

"How are you doing?" Matt asked, taking Hawk's role.

"Better. Still sad. But better."

"And how can I support you?"

"Keep doing this. Keep asking. Keep being here."

"Always."

---

Rose understood death better now, at ten.

"Grandpa Hawk is gone," she said, with the matter-of-factness of children. "But he's still in us. You said so at the funeral."

"That's right."

"So when I do things he taught me—like being brave, or asking good questions—that's him still being alive?"

Bella pulled her close.

"Exactly. That's exactly right."

---

James processed differently.

Quieter. More internal. He drew pictures—horses, mountains, a figure with Hawk's distinctive silhouette. He placed them around the house without comment.

"He's grieving in his own way," Matt observed.

"He's so like you. Feeling deeply, showing quietly."

"Is that okay?"

"It's perfect. As long as we make sure he knows he can talk when he's ready."

---

The letters helped.

Bella had found them in Hawk's study, after. Letters he'd written over the years—to her, to Jasper, to each of the grandchildren. Letters meant to be read after he was gone.

Hers was twelve pages long.

*Daughter—*

*If you're reading this, I'm gone. Don't be sad too long. I had a good life. You made it better.*

*You were my first choice. The first time I looked at someone and thought "this one—this one is family." Everything after that was shaped by what you taught me about choosing love over legacy...*

The letter went on. Every important moment, reflected. Every hope for her future, articulated. Every expression of pride and love that he'd sometimes struggled to say aloud.

She read it once. Then again. Then kept it in her jewelry box, next to the pendant.

---

Six months after:

Bella stood at the compound fire pit. Alone this time—Addie had insisted she take the space.

"How are you doing, Dad?" she asked the empty air.

The fire crackled. No answer.

"I'm doing okay. The kids are growing. The business is stable. Matt is... Matt. Steady as always."

She paused.

"I miss you. Every day. But I'm carrying you forward. Just like you wanted."

The wind stirred the flames.

It wasn't an answer. But it felt like one.
