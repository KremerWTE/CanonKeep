# Chapter 19: Passing Rosewood

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Six: The Legacy

---

Rosewood had been hers for twenty-five years.

From the first broken-down barn to a network of six venues across the Southeast. An empire built on beautiful spaces and meaningful moments.

Now it was time to let go.

---

The conversation with Sara had been coming for years.

Sara, who'd been there since the second venue. Who'd grown from manager to CEO to the obvious successor.

"I want you to take over," Bella said. "Fully. Not as manager—as owner."

Sara's eyes widened. "Bella—"

"You've earned it. You've been running this for years. I've just been... hovering."

"You built this."

"And now you'll take it further. That's how legacy works."

---

**[THEME: LETTING GO OF THE EMPIRE]**

The transition took a year.

Paperwork. Legal structures. Training Sara's team on the few things only Bella had handled. Making sure every system was documented, every relationship transferred.

"This is harder than I expected," Bella admitted to Matt.

"Letting go always is."

"It's not just a business. It's... proof. That I could build something. That I mattered."

"You proved that decades ago. This is just a building."

"Buildings where people got married. Where they celebrated the biggest moments of their lives."

"And those moments happened because of what you created. They'll keep happening without you."

---

The final event was a private dinner.

Sara, the senior team, key vendors who'd been partners for decades. Bella gave a short speech—not about her accomplishments, but about gratitude.

"I didn't build this alone. Every one of you contributed. And now it belongs to all of you."

She handed Sara the symbolic key—the same key that had opened the first barn, all those years ago.

"Make it yours," she said. "Make it better."

"I'll try."

"You'll succeed."

---

Walking away was strange.

For the first week, Bella kept reaching for her phone, expecting Rosewood emails. Kept thinking about events she should be monitoring. Kept feeling like something was missing.

"This is withdrawal," she realized. "I'm addicted to the business."

"Then use this as recovery." Matt held her. "Learn to exist without being needed."

"Is that possible?"

"You've been learning it for years. This is just the final exam."

---

Month two was easier.

Month three, she barely thought about Rosewood at all. Sara sent occasional updates—revenues, expansions, the new property in South Carolina—but they were informational, not requests for guidance.

"It's surviving without me," Bella said.

"Thriving, actually," Matt corrected. "Sara's implementing changes you'd never have approved."

"Are they working?"

"Beautifully."

Bella laughed—surprised, delighted. "She's better at it than I was."

"Different. Not better."

"Maybe different is enough."
