# Chapter 17: Addie's Decline

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Five: The Fullness

---

Addie had always seemed immortal.

The woman who'd held everything together after Hawk's death. Who'd maintained the compound, the traditions, the sprawling family network. Who, at eighty, still seemed more present than people half her age.

But bodies have limits.

The diagnosis came on Bella's fifty-first birthday. Addie waited until after the celebration to share it.

"I didn't want to overshadow your day."

"Mom." The word came naturally now, after decades. "This is more important than cake."

---

The illness was slow but certain.

Months stretched ahead—quality time, the doctors said, but finite. Addie accepted it with the grace she'd brought to everything.

"I've had a full life," she told the gathered family. "I've loved deeply. I've been loved. I've watched all of you become extraordinary. What more could I ask?"

"More time," Bella whispered.

"Time isn't the point, daughter. Depth is."

---

**[THEME: WISDOM BEFORE PASSING]**

The weeks that followed were precious.

Addie, too weak for much activity but fully present for conversation. The family coming in waves to sit with her, hear her stories, receive whatever wisdom she wanted to impart.

With Bella, she talked about the early days.

"When Hawk first brought you home, I wasn't sure. You were so wounded. So convinced you were worthless." Addie smiled. "I recognized it because I'd felt it myself, once."

"You?"

"Everyone, at some point. The lucky ones find their way through."

"How did you?"

"Hawk. Faith. Time. The slow accumulation of evidence that I mattered." She took Bella's hand. "You've found your way too. Differently than me—we all find our own path. But you've arrived."

---

The fire pit ritual happened one last time.

Addie couldn't sit outside anymore, so they brought it to her—symbolically. A candle. The two questions. The family gathered around her bed.

"How are you doing, Mom?" Bella asked.

"Tired. Ready. At peace."

"And how can we support you?"

Addie looked around the room—at her children, biological and chosen. At the grandchildren and great-grandchildren. At the family she'd helped build over decades of intentional love.

"Keep the fire pit going. Keep asking the questions. Keep choosing each other, even when it's hard." She smiled. "That's all I've ever wanted. For all of you to belong to each other."

---

She died three days later.

Peacefully. Surrounded by family. Her last words were to Bella:

"You were always enough, daughter. I just helped you see it."

---

The funeral was enormous.

Larger than Hawk's, somehow. Addie had touched so many lives in so many ways—mentoring, supporting, quietly ensuring that everyone felt seen.

Bella gave a eulogy.

"She never needed to prove herself," she said. "That's the thing about Addie. She knew her worth from the inside out. And she spent her life helping the rest of us find ours."

She looked at the crowd—the family, the friends, the strangers whose lives had been touched.

"The best way to honor her is to do what she did. Love without conditions. Show up without expectations. Choose each other, again and again, even when it's hard."

Her voice broke.

"That's her legacy. Not what she built—what she gave away."
