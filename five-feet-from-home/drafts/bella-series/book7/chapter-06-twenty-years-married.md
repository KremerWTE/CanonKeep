# Chapter 6: Twenty Years Married

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Two: The Next Generation

---

Twenty years.

The number felt even more impossible than ten had. Twenty years of waking up next to the same person. Twenty years of building, fighting, growing, loving.

"How are we the same people who got married in that barn?" Bella asked, on the morning of their anniversary.

"We're not." Matt pulled her close. "We're better versions."

---

They celebrated quietly this time.

No Italy trip—the kids were old enough to be left but young enough to resent it. Instead, a weekend at the cabin by the lake where they'd spent so many important moments.

"This is where you asked me to move to Colorado," Bella remembered. "That first summer."

"This is where I told you I wanted forever." Matt's voice was soft. "Long before I had any right to say it."

"You always had the right."

---

**[TWENTY-YEAR REFLECTION]**

The conversations that weekend were gentle.

Not about plans or problems. About gratitude. About the quiet miracle of two people choosing each other, again and again, for two decades.

"What's your favorite memory?" Bella asked.

"All of it. I can't separate them."

"Try."

Matt considered.

"The night Rose was born. Watching you become a mother. Seeing this whole new part of you emerge." He paused. "Or maybe every morning since. Waking up and knowing you're there."

Bella's eyes filled.

"Mine is simpler. The first time I realized I didn't have to prove anything to you. That you saw me, really saw me, and that was enough."

"When was that?"

"Years ago. Our first year married, maybe." She smiled through tears. "I'm still surprised by it sometimes. Still grateful."

---

They talked about the future.

Rose would graduate high school next year, start college. James was close behind. The nest was emptying.

"What do we do when they're gone?" Bella asked.

"What we've always done. Build. Love. Be together."

"That sounds simple."

"The best things are."

---

Rose and James called that evening.

"Happy anniversary!" Rose shouted, Grace and James echoing behind her.

"We made you a video," James added. "It's... sentimental. Grace cried."

"I did NOT cry," Grace protested.

"She absolutely cried," Rose confirmed.

Bella and Matt watched the video—twenty years of photos set to music, narrated by their children. The wedding. The pregnancies. The birthdays and holidays and ordinary moments that had become extraordinary in aggregate.

"How did we get so lucky?" Bella whispered.

"We got intentional. That's different from luck."

"Maybe they're the same thing."

"Maybe."

---

The pendant caught the firelight as they sat outside later.

Twenty years and it still hung at her throat. Would hang there forever, probably. A reminder of beginning, worn through every chapter of becoming.

"I love you," Bella said.

"I love you more."

"That's not a competition."

"Everything with you is a competition." Matt grinned. "I've just learned to let you win."

"Smart man."

"Smart enough to marry you. That's all that matters."
