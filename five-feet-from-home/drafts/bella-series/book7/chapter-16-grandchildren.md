# Chapter 16: Grandchildren

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Five: The Fullness

---

Rose called with the news on a Sunday afternoon.

"Mom. I'm pregnant."

Bella dropped the phone. Picked it up. Dropped it again.

"Mom? Are you there?"

"I'm here. I'm just—" She was crying. Laughing. Both at once. "I'm going to be a grandmother."

---

The pregnancy was healthy, uncomplicated.

Rose, true to form, had done her research. Had her healthcare team assembled. Had a birth plan that left room for flexibility while maintaining clear preferences.

"She's more prepared than I was," Bella admitted to Matt.

"She learned from watching you prepare for everything."

"And from watching me learn that sometimes plans don't matter."

---

**[THEME: GENERATIONAL LOVE]**

The baby arrived in spring.

A girl. Eleanor Rose Holloway-Chen, named for Daniel's grandmother and Rose herself.

Bella held her granddaughter for the first time and felt the world shift again.

"Hello, Eleanor," she whispered. "I'm your grandmother. And I'm going to love you without conditions, without performance expectations, without any of the nonsense that made my life harder than it needed to be."

Eleanor made a small sound. Agreement, probably.

---

The first year was revelation.

Watching Rose mother—confident, present, willing to ask for help. Seeing the patterns not repeat. Witnessing a new generation grow up knowing, from the start, that they were enough.

"You're doing beautifully," Bella told Rose, during a visit.

"I have a good example."

"I was a mess."

"You were a work in progress. There's a difference." Rose smiled. "And you figured it out. That's the part I'm trying to copy."

---

James announced his own news at thirty-two.

Not children—not yet—but a partner. A man named Michael, a fellow therapist who specialized in couples work.

"We're getting married," James said. "And before you ask—yes, we've done the communication work. Yes, we know what we're getting into. Yes, we're sure."

Bella laughed through tears.

"I wasn't going to ask any of that."

"I know you weren't. But old anxious James needed to cover the bases."

She hugged him, this thoughtful man who'd once been her quiet little boy.

"I'm so happy for you."

"I'm happy for me too. That's a new thing—being happy without guilt."

"You learned that on your own."

"I learned it from watching you learn it. Same thing."

---

Three grandchildren by the time Bella turned fifty.

Eleanor, Rose's daughter. The twins—Marcus and Elena—born to James and Michael via surrogate, named for the youth program graduate who'd changed their lives and the woman who'd helped Bella become herself.

"Our family got complicated," Matt observed, watching all three grandchildren play together.

"Our family got full." Bella smiled. "Complicated is just another word for rich."

---

She was different with the grandchildren than she'd been with her own kids.

Less anxious. More present. Willing to play without productivity, to read stories without checking her phone, to simply be with them without measuring the time.

"Grandma's more fun than you described," Eleanor said to Rose once.

"Grandma had to learn to be fun. She wasn't always."

"That's sad."

"That's growth. It's not sad—it's hopeful."
