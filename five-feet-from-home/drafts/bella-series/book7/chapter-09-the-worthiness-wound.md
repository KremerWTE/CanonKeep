# Chapter 9: The Worthiness Wound

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Three: The Reckoning

---

The deepest work came in year two of therapy.

Not the surface stuff—the delegation, the boundaries, the learning to rest. The bedrock. The wound that had driven everything.

"Tell me about the first time you felt not enough," the therapist said.

Bella closed her eyes.

---

She was seven.

A school project. She'd worked so hard, stayed up late, poured everything into a diorama of the solar system. Painted every planet by hand. Research beyond what was required.

Her father had looked at it and said: "Saturn's rings are wrong."

Not "good job." Not "I'm proud of you." Just the flaw. Just what was missing.

"That's when it started?"

"That's when I remember it starting. But I think it was already there. Something in how I was wired, or how I was raised, or both."

---

The pattern repeated through her childhood.

Straight A's weren't celebrated—they were expected. Anything less was failure. Achievement was the baseline, not the goal.

"I learned that love was conditional," Bella said. "That I had to earn my place in the family. That being wasn't enough—I had to be producing."

"And you carried that into adulthood."

"Into everything. Work. Relationships. Motherhood." Her voice cracked. "I've been running from that seven-year-old my whole life."

---

The healing wasn't linear.

Some weeks she felt transformed. Some weeks she was right back in the old patterns, working until 2 AM, convinced that if she stopped, everything would crumble.

"Progress isn't a straight line," the therapist reminded her. "It's a spiral. You revisit the same issues, but from a higher vantage point each time."

"It feels like I'm going backwards."

"It feels that way. Look at the evidence. Are you actually going backwards?"

---

The evidence said no.

Three years ago, she would never have taken a day off. Now she took two per week.

Three years ago, she micromanaged everything. Now Sara ran Rosewood with minimal oversight.

Three years ago, the idea of not being on the BSS board call would have given her hives. Now she missed them sometimes, trusting Jasper and the team.

"I'm changing," she realized. "Slowly, but I'm changing."

"You're healing. There's a difference."

"What's the difference?"

"Changing is about behavior. Healing is about belief." The therapist leaned forward. "You're starting to believe you're worthy. Not because of what you do—because of who you are."

---

The conversation with her father came unexpectedly.

He was older now. Softer. The rigid man of her childhood had mellowed with age.

"I was too hard on you," he said, during a visit. "I see that now. I thought I was preparing you for the world. I was just... repeating what was done to me."

"It hurt, Dad."

"I know. I'm sorry."

The apology didn't heal everything. But it helped. It validated what she'd always known but never heard acknowledged.

"I forgive you," she said. And meant it.

---

That night, fire pit with Matt.

"How do you feel?"

"Free. A little." She watched the flames. "Like a door that was locked finally opened a crack."

"That's good."

"It's terrifying. I've organized my whole identity around being not enough. If I let go of that... who am I?"

"You're Bella. The real one. The one who doesn't have to prove anything." Matt squeezed her hand. "I've been waiting to meet her for twenty years."

"She's a work in progress."

"Aren't we all?"
