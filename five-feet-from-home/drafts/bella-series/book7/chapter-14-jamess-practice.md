# Chapter 14: James's Practice

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Four: The Harvest

---

James opened his therapy practice at twenty-six.

Not in a city, as Bella had expected. In a small town near the ranch, serving a rural community that desperately needed mental health support.

"These people can't afford to drive three hours to see someone," he explained. "So I'm bringing the help to them."

"That's beautiful, James."

"It's practical. Which is kind of the same thing."

---

His specialty was what he called "healing the achiever wound."

Working with high performers who'd burned out. Professionals who'd accomplished everything and still felt empty. People who, like his mother, had hustled themselves into exhaustion trying to prove they were enough.

"I'm basically treating younger versions of you," he told Bella, with a gentle smile.

"That's... weirdly flattering."

"It's tribute. You showed me what the problem looks like and what healing looks like. I'm trying to help others find the same path."

---

James's approach was different from traditional therapy.

Practical interventions. Mindfulness without mysticism. Concrete strategies for learning to rest, delegate, trust. All grounded in the research, but also in lived experience—his mother's, his own.

"Your son is making a real difference," Bella heard, at a Charlotte gathering. A woman she barely knew. "He helped my daughter. She was working herself to death, and James showed her there was another way."

"I'll tell him."

"Tell him he saved her life. I'm not exaggerating."

---

Bella visited the practice on a quiet afternoon.

The office was simple but warm. Books on the shelves. Plants in the corners. A feeling of safety she recognized from her own therapy room.

"How does it feel?" she asked. "Helping people every day?"

"Exhausting. Rewarding. Meaningful in a way I can't fully explain." James looked at her. "You know how achievement never filled the hole? This is different. This actually fills it. Not because I'm accomplishing something—because I'm connecting."

"Connection over achievement."

"That's the whole thing, Mom. That's the secret you figured out. That's what I'm trying to teach."

---

His first book came out at thirty.

*The Hustle Wound: Breaking Free from Achievement Addiction.*

Bella read it in one sitting, tears streaming. Her story was in there—anonymized but recognizable. The seven-year-old with the wrong Saturn rings. The decades of proving. The slow, painful path to feeling enough.

"You told my story," she said.

"I told our story. With your permission, remember?"

"I remember. I just didn't expect it to feel so..."

"Seen?"

"Healing. Having it witnessed. Named. Turned into something that might help others." She hugged him. "I'm so proud of you, James."

"I learned it from watching you."

"You learned it from your own work. I just showed you the problem. You found solutions I never could."

---

The book became a bestseller.

James went on podcasts, gave talks, expanded his practice to include virtual sessions for people who couldn't access local care.

And everywhere he went, he told people: "You don't have to earn your worth. You already have it."

The message Bella had taken decades to learn, spreading to millions.
