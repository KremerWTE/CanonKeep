# Chapter 13: Rose's Wedding

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Four: The Harvest

---

Rose announced her engagement at twenty-four.

His name was Daniel—a doctoral candidate in environmental science she'd met at a conference. Quiet. Thoughtful. The opposite of Rose's intensity in all the best ways.

"He balances me," Rose explained. "Like Dad balances you."

"That's a good sign."

"I learned from watching."

---

The wedding planning was different than Bella's had been.

Rose delegated naturally. Hired help without guilt. Set boundaries around her time and energy.

"I'm not doing the martyr thing," she told Bella. "One beautiful day isn't worth months of misery."

"Where did you learn that?"

"From watching you almost kill yourself during your wedding planning. And then hearing you say you wished you'd done it differently."

---

**[OUTFIT DETAILS - ROSE'S WEDDING]**

The wedding was held at Rosewood Farm.

Full circle. The venue Bella had built, hosting her daughter's marriage.

Rose wore her grandmother's dress—Bella's biological mother's wedding gown, altered to fit. Something old, something borrowed, something connected across generations.

Bella wore sage green, elegant but understated. She wasn't the focus today. She was just a mother, watching her daughter begin a new chapter.

"You look beautiful," she told Rose, before the ceremony.

"I feel beautiful." Rose grinned. "Not because of the dress. Because I'm marrying someone who sees me clearly and loves me anyway."

"That's the best kind of love."

"You taught me that too."

---

The ceremony included both fathers.

Daniel's biological father and his stepfather, who'd raised him since age seven. Both walked him down the aisle.

"Sound familiar?" Matt whispered to Bella.

"Some patterns are worth repeating."

---

Bella didn't give a speech.

Rose had asked her not to—not as rejection, but as a gift. "You don't need to perform today, Mom. Just be present."

Being present. The hardest task Bella had ever undertaken.

But she did it. Watched her daughter marry without mentally composing emails. Celebrated without checking her phone. Danced without thinking about what came next.

"You're actually here," Matt observed, during a slow dance.

"I'm learning."

"Only took twenty-five years."

"I'm a slow learner on the important things."

---

That night, after the guests left:

Bella walked the property alone. The venue she'd built from a broken-down barn. The empire she'd created. The life she'd fought for.

And now her daughter was married. Starting her own story. Carrying forward the lessons—and hopefully not the wounds.

"We did okay," she said to the empty barn. To Hawk's memory. To Mandy's. To all the ghosts who'd helped her become this.

A breeze stirred the decorations. Not an answer. But an acknowledgment.

She'd done okay.

Actually, she'd done beautifully.
