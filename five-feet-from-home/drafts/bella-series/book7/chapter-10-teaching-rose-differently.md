# Chapter 10: Teaching Rose Differently

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Three: The Reckoning

---

Rose was in her junior year of college when she called, panicking.

"I got a B on my economics exam. A B, Mom. What if I lose my scholarship? What if I'm not good enough for grad school? What if—"

"Rose. Stop."

Silence on the line. Rose wasn't used to being interrupted.

"One B is not going to destroy your future."

"But—"

"No. Listen to me." Bella took a breath. "I spent decades believing every imperfection was a catastrophe. That I had to be perfect to deserve good things. And it nearly broke me."

"That's... you're different now."

"I'm learning to be different. And I don't want you to need twenty-five years of therapy to get there."

---

The conversation lasted an hour.

For the first time, Bella told Rose about the hustle. The real depth of it. The 4:30 AM mornings and 1:30 AM nights. The constant proving. The way achievement had been a hamster wheel, never actually delivering the peace it promised.

"I thought if I just did enough, I'd finally feel worthy," Bella admitted. "I never did. The goalpost always moved."

"Then how did you start feeling worthy?"

"Therapy. Time. Matt. Watching you and James grow up and realizing that I loved you unconditionally—and if I could love you that way, maybe I could love myself that way too."

---

Rose was quiet for a long moment.

"I do that," she said finally. "The hamster wheel thing. I always think the next achievement will make me feel okay."

"And does it?"

"For like a day. Then I need the next one."

"That's the trap." Bella's voice was gentle. "And you don't have to stay in it."

---

Rose started therapy that semester.

Her own journey, her own pace. But she called Bella weekly now, sharing what she was learning.

"The therapist says I have 'achievement addiction,'" Rose reported. "That I use accomplishment to regulate my emotions instead of actually processing them."

"That sounds familiar."

"Wonder where I got it from."

"Guilty as charged." Bella laughed, but it wasn't funny. "I'm sorry, Rose. I didn't mean to pass that on."

"You also passed on determination. Drive. The ability to build things from nothing." Rose's voice softened. "The good parts are real too, Mom."

---

James had a different perspective.

"I saw you both running on the wheel," he said, when Bella visited him at graduate school. "That's part of why I chose this path. I wanted to understand why people do that. Why they can't stop."

"Have you figured it out?"

"Partly. It's usually trauma. Fear. The belief that rest equals death." He looked at her. "You've changed, Mom. The last few years. You seem... calmer."

"I'm learning that I don't have to earn being alive."

"That's a big one."

"The biggest."

---

The pattern-breaking extended to mentoring.

Young women in the Wives Club. Junior team members at Rosewood. Anyone Bella saw hustling the way she used to hustle.

"You don't have to prove yourself to me," she'd tell them. "You're already valuable. The work proves your skills, not your worth."

Some heard it. Some didn't.

But she kept saying it anyway. Planting seeds she wished someone had planted in her.

---

At forty-five, Bella looked in the mirror and saw something new.

Not the driven achiever who never rested. Not the insecure overachiever still running from childhood wounds.

Just a woman. Worthy. Enough. Allowed to exist without justifying her existence.

"Who is she?" Matt asked, coming up behind her.

"Someone I'm still getting to know." Bella smiled. "But I think I like her."

"I've always liked her."

"I know. Thank you for waiting while I caught up."
