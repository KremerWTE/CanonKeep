# Chapter 27: Thirty Years Married

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Eight: The Hymn

---

Thirty years.

Three decades of choosing the same person, every day, through every season.

"Do you ever wonder what life would have been like if we'd never met?" Bella asked.

"No." Matt's answer was immediate. "Because this life is the only one I want."

---

They celebrated at the cabin.

The same place they'd gone for twenty years, for ten. A tradition now—returning to the beginning whenever they wanted to mark the journey.

"The lake looks the same," Bella observed.

"We don't."

"Is that good or bad?"

"Both. Neither." Matt smiled. "We're just more ourselves than we were."

---

Bella had dressed simply.

A flowing dress in deep blue—her favorite color now, though it hadn't been when she was young. Comfortable shoes because heels didn't matter anymore. Her grandmother's earrings, passed down through generations.

No pendant. That was Rose's now.

Matt had made an effort too—button-down shirt, actual slacks. The dressed-up version of the ranch man she'd married.

"You clean up well," she told him.

"Only for special occasions."

"Thirty years counts."

"I should hope so."

---

They talked about everything and nothing.

The children, grown now, building their own legacies. The grandchildren, each one becoming distinct individuals. The businesses they'd passed on, thriving without them.

"We did good," Matt said.

"We did. Somehow."

"Not somehow. Intentionally. With effort and patience and a lot of learning."

---

The reflection was deeper than usual.

Thirty years had seen transformation on every level. Personal, professional, spiritual. They'd built businesses and raised children and weathered crises and emerged on the other side.

"What do you want for the next thirty?" Bella asked.

"Less drama. More presence." Matt took her hand. "And you. Always you."

"Same."

"That's enough?"

"That's everything."

---

They walked along the lake at sunset.

Holding hands like teenagers. Moving slower than they once did. Feeling the weight of years in their bodies, the lightness of peace in their hearts.

"I love you," Bella said.

"I love you more."

"Still not a competition."

"Still going to say it anyway."

---

That night, fire pit by the lake:

"How are you doing?"

"Grateful. Content. Deeply, profoundly at peace."

"How can I support you?"

"Keep being exactly who you are. Keep showing up. Keep reminding me that this is real, even when it feels too good to be true."

"I can do that."

"I know you can. That's why I married you."

Thirty years later, and the answer was still the same.
