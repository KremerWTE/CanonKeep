# Chapter 1: The Legacy Continues

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part One: The Full Circle

---

Ten years of motherhood had taught Bella one thing above all else:

You never stop becoming.

Rose was ten now—fierce, brilliant, already asking questions about the world that Bella struggled to answer. James was eight—thoughtful, steady, more like Matt every year.

"Mom," Rose said one evening, doing homework at the kitchen table. "What's the Wives Club?"

Bella set down her tea.

"Where did you hear about that?"

"Grace mentioned it. She said you're part of something called the Wives Club, and that's how you know so many people."

---

The explanation took time.

Not the full history—Rose wasn't ready for all of it. But the essence: a network of women who supported each other. Who opened doors, shared resources, helped each other succeed.

"So it's like a club for moms?" Rose asked.

"It's like a club for women who want to lift each other up. Moms, businesswomen, artists, leaders. Anyone who believes we're stronger together than apart."

Rose considered this.

"Can I join?"

"When you're older. Much older."

"How much older?"

"We'll see."

The pendant still hung at Bella's throat.

Rose had asked about it too—the curious child, always asking, always wanting to understand. Bella had told her a simplified version: a gift, a symbol, a reminder of people who believed in her.

"Will I get one someday?"

"That depends on the path you choose."

"What path did you choose?"

Bella smiled.

"The one that led to you."

---

The kids were old enough now to understand, in broad strokes, what their parents did.

Rose knew Mommy ran beautiful places where people got married. She knew Daddy had horses and helped kids who needed extra support. She knew Grandpa Hawk did "security stuff" and Grandma Addie made things happen.

James knew the same, but processed it differently. He asked fewer questions, observed more carefully. Filed everything away for later consideration.

"They're going to be remarkable," Matt said, watching them.

"They already are."

"Where do you think they got that?"

"From us. From the whole village." Bella smiled. "From everyone who invested in us, now investing in them."

---

The legacy conversation happened that summer.

Hawk initiated it—he was seventy-five now, slowing down, thinking more about what came after.

"I want to talk about inheritance," he said, during a Charlotte visit. "Not money—though that's part of it. I want to talk about values. What we pass down that matters."

"I'm listening."

"I've watched you become everything I hoped you would. But more than that—I've watched you raise Rose and James in a way that gives them what you didn't have."

"Security. Belonging."

"Yes." His eyes were warm. "And now I want to make sure that continues. Generationally. In structures, not just intentions."

---

The structures took shape over months.

Family trusts. Clear succession plans. Values statements that would guide decisions after Hawk was gone.

"You're building this for them," Bella realized. "For Rose and James. For all the grandchildren."

"I'm building it for the future. The one I won't see but you will." He took her hand. "Promise me something, daughter."

"Anything."

"Promise me you'll keep the fire pit tradition. The two questions. The space for honest conversation."

"I promise, Dad."

"That's enough." He smiled. "That's everything."
