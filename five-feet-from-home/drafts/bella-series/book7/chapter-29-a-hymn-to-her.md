# Chapter 29: A Hymn to Her

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Conclusion

---

The song came from Rose.

Something she'd written for Bella's sixty-fifth birthday. A hymn, she called it—not religious, but reverential. A tribute to the woman who'd fought so hard to become herself.

*She came to us broken, convinced she was less*
*She worked and she struggled, she gave all her best*
*But best wasn't measured in achievement or gain*
*It was learning to stay when she wanted to run from the pain*

*She is enough, she has always been*
*The worthiness inside was never thin*
*She just couldn't see what was always there*
*Until she stopped running, learned to stay, learned to care*

---

The performance was at fire pit.

The whole family gathered. Rose with her guitar, singing words she'd crafted over months. Every verse a chapter of Bella's life. Every chorus a reminder of the journey.

Bella cried through the whole thing.

Not sad tears—full tears. The kind that come when you've been truly seen. When someone has taken your story and turned it into art.

"That was beautiful," she said, when Rose finished.

"It's true. Every word."

"I know. That's what makes it beautiful."

---

**[THEME: BEING SEEN AND CELEBRATED]**

The song became part of family lore.

Sung at gatherings. Referenced in conversations. A shorthand for the worthiness message that had become the family's core value.

*She is enough, she has always been.*

Eleanor learned to play it on piano. Marcus memorized the words. Even the smallest grandchildren could hum the melody.

"You've become a song," Matt teased.

"A hymn, technically."

"Same thing."

---

But the hymn wasn't really about Bella.

It was about all of them. Every person who'd ever felt not enough. Every achiever running from worthlessness. Every hustler trying to prove they deserved to exist.

"The song could be about anyone," Rose explained. "Mom just happened to live it first."

"I didn't live it first. I just lived it publicly. Hawk went through it. Addie went through it. Everyone does, in their own way."

"Then why does it feel so specific to you?"

"Because I'm your mother. And because I was willing to tell the truth about it."

---

The truth was the gift.

Not the achievement. Not the businesses or the board seats or the family she'd helped build. The gift was the vulnerability. The willingness to say: I was broken, and I healed.

That gave others permission to heal too.

---

At sixty-five, Bella understood her purpose.

Not building. Not achieving. Not proving.

Witnessing. Holding space. Reminding everyone she encountered that they, too, were enough.

"That's the real legacy," she told James.

"What is?"

"The permission. The modeling. Showing people what's possible when you stop running from yourself."

"That's what I try to do in my work."

"Then we're in the same business."

---
