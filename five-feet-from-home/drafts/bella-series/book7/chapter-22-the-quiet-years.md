# Chapter 22: The Quiet Years

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Seven: The Settling

---

For the first time in her life, Bella had nothing she had to do.

No businesses demanding attention. No board meetings requiring preparation. No crises waiting for her expertise.

Just... time.

"This is terrifying," she told Matt.

"This is retirement."

"I'm fifty-five. That's not retirement age."

"It's when you choose to stop. Same thing."

---

The adjustment took months.

Old habits died hard. She kept waking at 5 AM, mind spinning with tasks that no longer existed. Kept reaching for her phone, expecting urgent messages that never came.

"Your nervous system is rewiring," her therapist explained. "It was programmed for high alert. Now it has to learn rest."

"How long does that take?"

"Depends on the person. Years, sometimes."

"Great."

---

**[THEME: LEARNING TO JUST BE]**

The first breakthrough came in the garden.

Bella was planting—something she'd started when she first felt the worthiness shift, now a daily practice. Hands in soil. No agenda. No productivity goals.

And she realized she was... enjoying it. Not as a break from work. Not as self-care to fuel more hustle. Just... for itself.

"This is what rest feels like," she told Matt that evening.

"You're just figuring that out?"

"I'm slow on the important things."

---

The quiet years had their own rhythm.

Mornings: coffee on the porch, watching the sunrise without needing to do anything about it.

Middays: gardening, reading, occasionally meeting with the people who'd taken over her former roles.

Evenings: dinners with Matt, sometimes with grandchildren, sometimes just the two of them.

Nights: actual sleep. Eight hours. Uninterrupted.

"I feel like a different person," Bella said.

"You're the same person. Just healthier."

---

The grandchildren became the organizing principle.

Not in a frantic way—in a present way. Bella actually showed up for school plays, soccer games, birthday parties. Not checking her phone. Not thinking about work. Actually there.

"You're so different with them than you were with us," Rose observed.

"I'm sorry for that."

"Don't be. You did your best. You were fighting battles I'll never fully understand." Rose smiled. "And you won. That's the part that matters."

---

The fire pit became a weekly gathering.

Every Sunday, whoever was available came to the ranch. The ritual continued: the two questions, the honest answers, the space for connection.

"How are you doing?" Bella asked the circle.

Answers came from children, grandchildren, extended family, friends who'd become family. All of them sharing their lives, their struggles, their growth.

"And how can we support each other?"

That question had evolved. It wasn't just about Bella anymore. It was collective. The whole family, holding each other.

---

At fifty-seven, Bella wrote in her journal:

*I spent forty years chasing peace. Building things that would make me feel worthy. Proving myself to people who mostly never doubted me.*

*Now I have peace. Not because I achieved it—because I stopped chasing.*

*The hustle was never the path. It was the obstacle. The path was always here, waiting for me to slow down enough to see it.*

*I am enough. I have always been enough. And now I finally, fully believe it.*
