# Chapter 7: The Hustle She Inherited

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Three: The Reckoning

---

Rose was eighteen when she said the words that broke Bella open.

"Mom, you're never actually present. You're always working, even when you're here."

They were in the kitchen. Bella had been answering emails during what was supposed to be a family dinner. The phone was still in her hand.

"That's not true."

"Your phone is literally in your hand right now."

Bella set it down. Slowly.

---

The conversation that followed was brutal.

Rose had inherited Bella's intensity—which meant she didn't soften blows.

"I grew up watching you prove yourself. Every day. Like you were scared someone would realize you didn't belong." Rose's voice was steady but her eyes were wet. "And now I do the same thing. I work until I'm exhausted. I never feel like I've done enough. I don't know how to rest."

"That's not—"

"It IS, Mom. I learned it from watching you." Rose took a breath. "And I love you. But I don't want to live like that. And I don't think you should either."

---

**[THEME: THE INHERITED HUSTLE]**

Bella lay awake that night, Matt's breathing steady beside her.

Rose was right. She knew Rose was right.

Twenty-five years since Hawk had first recruited her, and she was still proving. Still hustling. Still working 4:30 AM to midnight like she was a junior analyst terrified of being found out.

She had everything now. Multiple businesses. A family. Resources most people couldn't imagine. And she was still living like she had something to prove.

The old voice whispered: *Because you do. Because you're still not enough.*

But for the first time, another voice answered: *What if that's not true anymore? What if it was never true?*

---

The therapy started the following month.

Not crisis therapy—she'd done that years ago, after particularly hard stretches. This was different. Excavation. Understanding the roots of the hustle.

"You believe you have to earn your place," the therapist observed. "Every day. Like belonging is a job, not a state."

"Isn't it?"

"What would happen if you stopped working so hard?"

"I don't know."

"That's what we need to explore."

---

The exploration was painful.

Layers peeled back: the childhood where achievement was the only currency. The early career where she'd been told she wasn't enough. The years of building, always building, never stopping to enjoy what she'd built.

"You've achieved remarkable things," the therapist said. "But achievement won't fill the hole. The hole isn't about doing—it's about believing you're worthy of being."

"How do I start believing that?"

"By practicing. By letting things go and watching them still work. By trusting others and seeing that trust rewarded."

---

Matt found her crying in the barn one evening.

"What's wrong?"

"I don't know how to stop." Her voice was ragged. "I've been hustling my whole life. I don't know who I am without the work."

He held her while she sobbed.

"You're my wife. You're Rose and James's mother. You're a daughter, a sister, a friend." He stroked her hair. "None of those require you to earn them. You just... are."

"It doesn't feel that simple."

"The hardest things to learn are the simplest ones."
