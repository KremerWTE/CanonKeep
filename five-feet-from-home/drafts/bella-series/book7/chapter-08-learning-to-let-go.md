# Chapter 8: Learning to Let Go

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Three: The Reckoning

---

The experiment started small.

"Take three days," the therapist suggested. "No work. No email. No checking in. Let your teams handle everything."

"Three days?"

"Start there."

Three days felt like eternity.

---

Day one was agony.

Bella's hands kept reaching for her phone. Her mind kept cycling through tasks that needed doing. She was physically present at the ranch but mentally everywhere else.

"You're twitching," Matt observed.

"I'm fine."

"You're literally twitching."

She was. Her leg was bouncing. Her fingers were drumming. Her body didn't know how to be still.

"Go for a ride," he suggested. "Tire yourself out."

She rode for three hours. Came back exhausted. Slept twelve hours straight.

---

Day two was marginally better.

The panic had subsided to a dull anxiety. She kept waiting for the call that everything had fallen apart without her.

The call never came.

"How's Rosewood?" she asked Matt, who was checking in with the team on her behalf.

"Fine. Sara handled the Whitman wedding. Everything went smoothly."

"The BSS board meeting?"

"Jasper ran it. No issues."

"The POH—"

"Handled, Bella." Matt's voice was gentle. "Everything is handled."

---

**[THEME: DELEGATION AND TRUST]**

Day three: a revelation.

Bella sat on the porch, watching the sunset, and realized something was different.

The world hadn't ended. The businesses hadn't collapsed. Nobody had died because she'd taken three days off.

And more than that—she felt... lighter. Like she'd been carrying weight she didn't realize was there until she set it down.

"I think I've been sick," she told Matt. "For years. Decades maybe."

"Sick how?"

"Sick with the belief that I was the only one who could do things right. That trusting others was dangerous. That rest was laziness." She shook her head. "That's not drive. That's fear."

"What are you afraid of?"

"That if I stop proving myself, people will realize I don't deserve any of this."

The words hung in the air. Raw. Honest.

Matt took her hand.

"You know that's not true."

"My head knows. The rest of me is catching up."

---

The three-day experiment became a weekly practice.

One day per week—completely offline. Protected. Non-negotiable.

Then two days. Then entire weekends.

"The teams are stepping up," Sara reported, during a check-in. "Actually, they're thriving. Having more autonomy has been good for morale."

"I should have done this years ago."

"Better late than never."

---

The shift cascaded through everything.

Board meetings she used to dominate became collaborative. Decisions she used to make alone became team processes. The iron grip she'd held on every detail loosened, and instead of chaos, there was... growth.

"You're different," Elena observed. "Less... controlled."

"Less controlling, you mean."

"That too." Elena smiled. "It looks good on you."

---

The hardest part was the internal work.

The hustle had been a coping mechanism. A way to outrun the voice that said she wasn't enough. Without it, she had to face that voice directly.

*You're worthless. You're lucky anyone keeps you around. The moment you stop producing, they'll leave.*

"That's not my voice," she realized, one session. "That's... old. From before."

"Whose voice is it?"

"Everyone who ever made me feel small. Teachers. Bosses. My own fears, compounded." She paused. "But they're not here anymore. I am."

"Then whose voice do you want to listen to instead?"

---

She chose differently.

Slowly. Imperfectly. With setbacks and relapses into old patterns.

But she chose to believe she was worthy. Chose to trust her teams. Chose to rest without guilt.

And the world didn't end.

If anything, it got better.
