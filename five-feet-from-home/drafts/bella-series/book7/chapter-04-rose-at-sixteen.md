# Chapter 4: Rose at Sixteen

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Two: The Next Generation

---

Rose at sixteen was everything Bella had been, intensified.

The same fierce determination. The same restless energy. The same refusal to be smaller than she was.

But also: stability. Security. A foundation Bella had never had.

"I want to do an internship," Rose announced, at dinner. "This summer. At Rosewood."

"You're sixteen."

"And?" Rose's chin lifted—a gesture Bella recognized from her own mirror. "I've been going to weddings there since I was a baby. I know the business. I want to learn more."

---

The conversation continued after dinner.

Matt excused himself—this was mother-daughter territory. James had long since disappeared to his room, content to avoid drama.

"Why Rosewood?" Bella asked.

"Because it's yours. Because it matters." Rose was earnest now, the bravado softening. "I want to understand what you built. How you built it. What it means to create something from nothing."

"You don't have to follow my path."

"I'm not following. I'm exploring." Rose smiled. "Big difference."

---

**[ROSE'S DEVELOPMENT]**

The internship happened.

Rose spent the summer at Rosewood Farm, learning from Sara and the team. She was good—better than Bella had expected. Natural at the client interface. Creative with problem-solving. Already developing a vision of her own.

"She's got your instincts," Sara reported. "But her own style. More collaborative. Less controlling."

"That's probably healthier."

"Probably." Sara grinned. "But your way worked too."

---

The conversations that summer were precious.

Mother and daughter, driving between venues, discussing business and life and everything in between.

"Did you always know you wanted this?" Rose asked.

"No. I stumbled into it. Made it up as I went along."

"That's terrifying."

"That's life." Bella glanced at her. "The secret is, nobody knows what they're doing. Some people are just better at pretending."

"Even you?"

"Especially me. At your age, I was a complete mess."

Rose laughed. "I don't believe that."

"Believe it. Ask Grandma Addie. She has stories."

---

The summer ended with a proposal.

Not from Rose—from Bella.

"I want to start preparing you," she said. "Not to take over—you're too young for that, and the choice is always yours. But to understand. The businesses. The board seats. The family structures."

"Why now?"

"Because Grandpa Hawk taught me that preparation takes time. And because I've seen enough to know you might want this someday."

Rose was quiet for a long moment.

"What if I don't want it? What if I want to do something completely different?"

"Then you do something different. And James, or Grace, or someone else steps into these roles." Bella took her daughter's hand. "The structures exist to serve the family. Not the other way around."

"Okay." Rose nodded. "I'll learn. But I'm not promising anything."

"I wouldn't want you to."

---

That fall, Rose started attending board meetings as an observer.

BSS. Rosewood. The veteran program trust. Each one a window into the empire her family had built.

"This is a lot," she admitted, after a particularly complex session.

"It is. That's why you start learning now."

"Did you learn at sixteen?"

"No. I learned at twenty-two, in a crisis. This is better."

Rose considered.

"Yeah. This is probably better."
