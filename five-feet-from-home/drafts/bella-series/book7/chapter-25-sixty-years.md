# Chapter 25: Sixty Years

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Eight: The Hymn

---

Bella turned sixty on a spring morning.

The number felt impossible. Sixty years of life. Forty years since Hawk had first recruited her. Thirty years of marriage. Two children raised. Four grandchildren growing.

"How do you feel?" Matt asked, bringing her coffee in bed.

"Old. Young. Everything in between."

"That sounds about right."

---

The celebration was intimate.

Family only—which still meant dozens of people. The compound fire pit, expanded over the years to accommodate the growing clan. Stories shared. Tears shed. Laughter that echoed across the property.

"Tell us about the beginning," Eleanor asked. She was ten now—Rose's oldest, Bella's first grandchild. "When you first came to Charlotte."

Bella looked at the faces around the fire.

"I was lost. Completely lost. I didn't believe I was worthy of love, of belonging, of any of this." She gestured to the gathering. "And then your great-grandfather Hawk found me. Saw something in me I couldn't see in myself."

"Like in Daddy's book," Marcus said—James's son, eight years old.

"Exactly like Daddy's book."

---

**[THEME: THE FULL ARC]**

Rose gave the toast.

"My mother spent decades believing she wasn't enough. She hustled, achieved, built empires—all trying to prove something she shouldn't have had to prove."

She looked at Bella.

"And then she stopped. She learned. She became the woman sitting here tonight—peaceful, present, worthy. Not because of what she did, but because of who she is."

Rose raised her glass.

"To sixty years. To the journey. To the arrival."

---

James spoke next.

"I write about the hustle wound. I help people heal from it. And everything I know, I learned from watching my mother fight her way to wholeness."

His voice caught.

"She's the bravest person I know. Not because she never struggled—because she struggled every day and kept going anyway. Because she chose healing even when it was the hardest path."

---

The grandchildren performed something they'd prepared.

A song. A poem. A dance. It was chaotic and imperfect and absolutely beautiful.

"We love you, Grandma," they said in ragged unison.

Bella couldn't speak. Could only nod. Could only let the tears fall.

---

Later, when the crowd had thinned:

Bella sat alone at the fire pit. The same ritual she'd shared with Hawk decades ago. The same questions he'd asked her when she was young and broken.

"How are you doing?" she asked herself.

The answer came easily now: *Good. Really, genuinely good. At peace.*

"How can you support yourself?"

*Keep resting. Keep being present. Keep trusting that the work is done—you don't have to prove anything anymore.*

She smiled in the darkness.

Sixty years. A full arc. From worthless to worthy. From hustle to peace. From alone to surrounded.

It had taken so long. But she'd arrived.
