# Chapter 26: Writing Her Story

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Eight: The Hymn

---

The idea came from James.

"You should write it down, Mom. Your story. The journey from worthlessness to worth."

"That's essentially what you already wrote."

"I wrote about the concept. You could write about the experience."

---

Bella had never considered herself a writer.

She'd written reports, strategies, business plans. Never anything personal. Never anything that required excavating the depths.

"I don't know how," she admitted.

"Start with what you remember. Start with the moments that mattered."

So she started.

---

**[THEME: NARRATING THE JOURNEY]**

The writing was harder than any business she'd ever built.

Every sentence required honesty she'd spent years avoiding. Every paragraph forced her to face who she'd been—the lost young woman, the relentless achiever, the person so convinced of her worthlessness that she couldn't stop working.

"This is painful," she told Matt.

"Growth usually is."

"I thought I was done growing."

"You're never done. You just get better at it."

---

The story emerged over months.

Not a memoir exactly—more like a letter. To her children. To her grandchildren. To anyone who might recognize themselves in her struggle.

*I spent forty years believing I wasn't enough. I built empires trying to prove otherwise. And then I learned that the proving was the problem—not the solution.*

*The worthiness was always there. I just couldn't see it.*

*If you're reading this and you can't see yours either, please know: it's there. It's always been there. You don't have to earn it. You just have to believe it.*

---

Rose read the draft first.

She came out of the reading room with red eyes and a full heart.

"This is the most important thing you've ever written."

"I've never written anything."

"That's what makes it important." Rose held her. "You finally told your truth. The real one. Not the achievement highlight reel."

---

James read it next.

His feedback was professional and personal: "This is what I've been trying to help people understand. You said it better than any textbook."

"That's because I lived it."

"Exactly. That's the power."

---

The question of publication came up.

Not for fame—Bella had never wanted that. But for impact. To reach people who might need to hear what she'd learned.

"I don't know if I want my story that public," she said.

"It doesn't have to be public. It could be family only. A legacy document."

"Or it could help people."

"That too."

The decision took months.

In the end, she chose to share it—not widely, but with the Wives Club network. Women who might recognize themselves. Who might benefit from seeing that the hustle could be overcome.

"You're still paying it forward," Matt observed.

"I can't seem to stop."

"Maybe that's your version of rest. Contribution without depletion."

"I like that framing."
