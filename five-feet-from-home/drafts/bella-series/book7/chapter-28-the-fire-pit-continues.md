# Chapter 28: The Fire Pit Continues

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Eight: The Hymn

---

The tradition had survived three generations now.

Hawk had started it. Bella had continued it. And now Rose led the weekly gatherings, her own children participating in the ritual they'd inherited.

"How are you doing?" Rose asked Eleanor, who was twelve and going through her own season of doubt.

"I don't know. School is hard. Friends are confusing. I feel like I don't fit."

"Tell me more."

And the conversation unfolded, just as it had for decades.

---

Bella watched from the edge of the circle.

She wasn't leading anymore—that was Rose's role now. But she was present, holding space, offering wisdom when asked.

"Grandma, did you ever feel like you didn't fit?" Eleanor asked.

"Every day. For years and years."

"What changed?"

"I stopped trying to fit into spaces that weren't made for me. I started building spaces that fit who I was."

---

The questions had evolved slightly.

Rose had added a third: "What are you grateful for?" It shifted the tone—not just checking in on struggles, but also noticing blessings.

"I'm grateful for this," Eleanor said, looking around the circle. "Even when I don't feel like I fit anywhere else, I fit here."

"You always will," Rose told her. "This is your family. This fire pit is your birthright."

---

James brought the twins to fire pit whenever he visited.

Marcus and Elena, now six, were learning the ritual. Still too young to fully understand, but old enough to participate.

"How are you doing, Marcus?" James asked.

"I'm doing fine. I had ice cream today."

Everyone laughed. The profound and the mundane, mixed together. That was family.

---

The ritual had spread beyond the family.

Rose had introduced it to her friend groups. James used a version in his therapy practice. Even the youth program had adopted it—fire pits at every location, the two questions asked at the end of every cohort.

"Dad's legacy," Bella observed. "Hawk's legacy. Spreading everywhere."

"Your legacy too," Matt said. "You kept it going. You taught the next generation."

"I just showed up."

"That's what legacy is. Showing up, consistently, until the pattern becomes permanent."

---

At sixty-two, Bella attended fire pit more as observer than participant.

She'd said what she needed to say over the years. Now she mostly listened. Watched the younger generations work through their own versions of the struggles she'd faced.

"It's different for them," she told Matt.

"Better?"

"Different. They have language we didn't have. Resources we couldn't access. But they still struggle. Just in new ways."

"That's the human condition."

"Apparently."

---

One night, Eleanor asked Bella directly:

"Grandma, what's the most important thing you've learned in your whole life?"

Bella thought about it. Really thought.

"That you're already worthy. You don't have to earn love, or belonging, or a place in this family. It's already yours."

"But what if I mess up?"

"Then you mess up. And we love you anyway. That's how family works."

Eleanor considered this.

"That's different from what the world says."

"The world is often wrong. Trust the fire pit."
