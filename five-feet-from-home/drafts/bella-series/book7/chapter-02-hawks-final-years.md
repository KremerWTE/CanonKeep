# Chapter 2: Hawk's Final Years

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part One: The Full Circle

---

The decline was gradual, then sudden.

Hawk at seventy-six was still sharp, still present. By seventy-eight, the heart condition that had been managed for years began to overwhelm.

"We need to talk," he said, during a visit. His voice was different—thinner, more fragile.

Bella sat beside him, preparing for what she knew was coming.

"I'm not getting better this time."

---

The months that followed were precious.

Bella traveled to Charlotte more often, sometimes with the kids, sometimes alone. Each visit felt weighted with significance.

Hawk spent his lucid hours on the things that mattered. Conversations with Jasper about BSS's future. Time with Grace, now eighteen and preparing for college. Hours with Rose and James, telling stories, answering questions, being the grandfather he'd never thought he'd become.

"You're the best investment I ever made," he told Bella, during one quiet afternoon.

"You've said that before."

"Because it keeps being true."

---

**[FINAL CONVERSATIONS]**

The fire pit became sacred space.

Hawk couldn't sit outside for long anymore, but they adapted. A portable heater. Shorter sessions. The ritual preserved even as the circumstances changed.

"How are you doing, daughter?"

"Scared. Grateful. Both, as always."

"And how can I support you?"

"Let me support you, for once." Bella's voice cracked. "Let me be the one who gives back."

"You've been giving back for years. Every success. Every child you've raised. Every person you've helped." Hawk took her hand. "That's what support looks like, stretched across time."

---

Addie was managing better than expected.

The years had prepared her, in a way. She'd always known this would come. But preparation and reality were different things.

"I'm going to be fine," she said, when Bella worried. "I have decades of practice at being strong. And I have all of you."

"We're here. Whatever you need."

"I know." Addie smiled, tired but present. "That's what family means."

---

Hawk died on a Tuesday morning.

Peacefully, at home, Addie beside him. The way he would have wanted—controlled, dignified, surrounded by love.

Bella got the call at 7 AM. She was already crying by the time she answered.

"He's gone," Addie said. "He asked me to tell you—he said 'Tell her I'm proud. Always was. Always will be.'"

---

The funeral was enormous.

BSS personnel. Wives Club members. POH representatives. The countless people whose lives Hawk had touched over eight decades of relentless living.

Bella gave one of the eulogies.

"He called me daughter," she said, standing at the podium. "From the first day I met him, he saw something in me that I couldn't see in myself. He invested when there was no reason to. He believed when I couldn't believe."

She looked at the crowd—at Addie, at Jasper and Elena, at Grace holding Rose's hand.

"He taught me that family isn't blood. It's choice. It's showing up. It's the daily decision to love someone, even when they're difficult, even when it's hard."

Her voice broke, recovered.

"He showed up for me. Every day. For twenty years. And now I have to learn how to live without that. But I'm not alone—because he built this."

She gestured to the gathered family.

"We are his legacy. Every one of us. And as long as we carry his values forward, he's not really gone."
