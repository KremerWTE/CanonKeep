# Chapter 5: James Finds His Path

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Two: The Next Generation

---

James at fourteen was everything Rose wasn't.

Quiet where she was loud. Patient where she was urgent. Content to observe while she insisted on participating.

"I worry about him sometimes," Bella admitted to Matt.

"Why?"

"He's so internal. I never know what he's thinking."

"He's like me. You didn't know what I was thinking either, at first."

"And that worked out."

"Exactly." Matt smiled. "Give him time. He's finding his path."

---

The path, when it emerged, was unexpected.

Not business. Not crisis management. Not the strategic frameworks that Rose consumed like oxygen.

Therapy.

"I want to be a counselor," James announced, at fifteen. "Or a therapist. Someone who helps people talk through hard things."

Bella had not seen that coming.

"That's... wonderful, James."

"You don't think it's weird?"

"I think it's perfect for you." She studied her son—Matt's eyes, her intensity turned inward. "You've always understood people. You've always known how to listen."

---

**[JAMES'S DEVELOPMENT]**

James started volunteering at a local youth center.

Not the ranch program—something different. Urban kids, different challenges. He was good at it. The quiet presence that made people feel safe to share.

"He's got a gift," the center director reported. "The kids open up to him in ways they don't with our regular staff."

"He's been practicing his whole life," Bella said. "Listening in a family full of loud personalities."

---

The fire pit conversations with James were different.

Deeper. More philosophical. Questions about meaning and purpose that made Bella think harder than Rose's operational queries ever did.

"How do you help someone who doesn't want to be helped?" he asked, one evening.

"You can't. Not directly." Bella considered. "But you can be there. Consistently. So that when they're ready, they know where to turn."

"That's hard. Waiting."

"It's the hardest thing. But it's often the most important."

---

James shadowed Matt at the youth program.

Not for career exploration—he knew what he wanted. But for understanding. How to build structures that supported healing. How to create space for people to become themselves.

"Your dad's philosophy is basically therapy," James observed, after a week.

"His therapy just involves horses."

"Same principles, though. Safety. Patience. Letting people set the pace."

"You're going to be good at this, James." Bella felt pride swelling. "Really good."

"I hope so." His voice was quiet, sincere. "I want to help the way everyone helped you."

---

By sixteen, James had decided on his educational path.

Psychology undergraduate. Counseling graduate program. Maybe a specialty in trauma-informed care.

"That's a long road," Matt observed.

"The best things take time." James smiled—rare, but radiant when it happened. "Someone taught me that."
