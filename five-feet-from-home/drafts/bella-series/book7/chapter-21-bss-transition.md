# Chapter 21: BSS Transition

## Book 7: "A Hymn to Her"
### Part Six: The Legacy

---

The final board meeting was ceremonial.

Bella had been stepping back from BSS for years—the company Hawk had built, that she'd helped shape, that now operated in fifteen countries.

Today, she resigned from the board entirely.

"I've served for twenty-three years," she said. "It's time for new voices."

---

Jasper, now in his sixties, had been running the company for over a decade.

"You've been more than a board member," he said, after the meeting. "You've been a conscience. A connection to what Dad built."

"The company doesn't need me for that anymore. It's internalized the values."

"Has it?"

"Look at how decisions get made. Look at who gets protected and who doesn't. Look at the culture." Bella smiled. "It's Hawk through and through. Just expressed by new people."

---

**[THEME: LEAVING WHAT YOU HELPED BUILD]**

The transition felt different from Rosewood.

Rosewood had been hers—built from scratch, shaped entirely by her vision. BSS was something she'd joined, contributed to, but never owned.

Letting it go was easier in some ways. Harder in others.

"I feel like I'm losing Dad again," she admitted to Matt.

"He's in the company. He'll always be there."

"But I won't be. Not in the same way."

"You'll be in the stories. The culture. The way people treat each other." Matt held her. "That's legacy too. Less visible. More permanent."

---

Grace had joined the board the year before.

Elena's daughter. Hawk's granddaughter. The next generation, taking their place at the table.

"Any advice?" Grace asked, during the transition.

"Trust your instincts. Ask questions. Don't assume you know less than the experienced voices—your fresh perspective is the point."

"And for the hard days?"

"Remember why we do this. The people we protect. The crises we solve. The difference between having resources and not having them when you need them most."

Grace nodded. "Grandpa used to say something similar."

"I learned it from him."

---

The exit interview with the CEO was recorded.

A video for future leaders. Bella talking about what she'd learned in twenty-three years of board service.

"The company isn't the point," she said. "The company is a vehicle. For protection, for opportunity, for the kind of security that lets people live their lives without fear."

She paused.

"When the vehicle becomes more important than the destination, you've lost the thread. Keep the destination in sight. Always."

---

Walking out of the Charlotte headquarters for the last time as a board member:

Bella stood in the lobby, looking at the portrait of Hawk that hung near the entrance. Younger than she'd ever known him. Already carrying the intensity that had defined his life.

"I did my best, Dad," she whispered.

The portrait didn't answer. But she felt heard anyway.
