# Chapter 7c: Choosing to Stay

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Four: New Directions

---

Three months at the ranch.

Longer than she'd planned. Longer than anyone expected. But leaving felt wrong—like abandoning something unfinished.

"You can stay as long as you need," Mandy said.

"I don't know what I need."

"Then stay until you figure it out."

---

**[THEME: CHOOSING REST]**

The decision to extend came gradually.

Not dramatic—no crisis, no revelation. Just a growing sense that she wasn't done. That the healing happening here couldn't be rushed.

She called Addie to discuss it.

"Take the time you need. The Page role will be here when you're ready."

"What if people think I'm failing?"

"What people?"

"Everyone. The team. You."

Addie's laugh was gentle. "Bella. Taking time to heal isn't failure. It's wisdom. Very few people are brave enough to prioritize their wellbeing."

"It doesn't feel brave. It feels... indulgent."

"That's the wound talking. Recovery isn't indulgence. It's necessity."

---

Matt took the news quietly.

"You're staying longer."

"Is that okay?"

"More than okay." His smile was warm. "I wasn't ready for you to leave anyway."

"We haven't even... I mean, we're not..."

"I know what we are and aren't." He reached for her hand. "I told you—I'm patient. However long this takes."

---

The extended stay changed things.

No longer a visitor—more like a resident. She knew the rhythms of the ranch, the names of every horse, the quirks of every veteran in the Thursday group.

She had a purpose here. Not proving anything—just helping. Being useful in ways that didn't drain her.

"You're different," Mandy observed one evening.

"How?"

"Less frantic. More present. Like you finally believe you're allowed to be here."

"Am I allowed?"

"You always were. You just couldn't see it."

---

Journal entry:

*Three months. Staying longer. And the strange thing is—I don't feel guilty.*

*Part of me is screaming that I should go back. That I'm falling behind. That everyone's moving forward while I stand still.*

*But another part knows the truth: I'm not standing still. I'm healing. Which is its own kind of movement.*

*Choosing rest is harder than choosing work. Work has external validation. Rest requires believing I'm worth the pause.*

*I'm starting to believe it. Slowly. Day by day.*

*Maybe that's enough.*
