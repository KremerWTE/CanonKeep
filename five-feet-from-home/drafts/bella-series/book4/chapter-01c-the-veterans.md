# Chapter 1c: The Veterans

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part One: Denver

---

She met them one by one.

Earl, the Marine, showed her around the horse barn. "Therapy horses," he explained. "Different from regular horses. Trained to work with us, pick up on our moods. Some of these guys have helped me more than any shrink ever did."

"What do you do with them?"

"Everything. Grooming. Training. Trail rides. It's not about riding, really. It's about relationship." He stroked a bay mare's nose. "This one's called Hope. Cheesy name, but it fits. She found me on a bad day and just... stood with me. Didn't need anything. Didn't want anything. Just present."

Bella thought about the fire pit. About Hawk's way of just being present without demanding.

"I understand that."

---

There was James, a Navy SEAL who'd lost his left arm below the elbow.

He worked in the workshop, building furniture with adaptive tools. His pieces were beautiful—simple, functional, crafted with care.

"I thought I was done when I lost the arm," he told Bella, planing a piece of oak. "Everything I knew how to do was physical. Combat. Climbing. Swimming. Without the arm, I was useless."

"And now?"

"Now I build things." His smile was soft. "Different kind of useful. Harder to measure. But real."

---

There was Maria, an Army medic who'd come home with trauma she couldn't name.

She worked in the garden, her hands in the soil more often than not. "I spent years trying to save people," she said. "Watching them die anyway. Now I grow things instead. It's slower. But I can actually watch them live."

"Does it help?"

"Some days. Other days, I still see the faces." Maria wiped her forehead with a dirty hand. "But the dirt helps. The quiet helps. Being somewhere nobody expects me to fix anything—that helps most of all."

---

Each veteran had a story. Each story had weight.

And Bella found herself listening—really listening—in a way she hadn't expected.

"You're good at that," Mandy observed, after a particularly long conversation with a younger veteran named Tyler who hadn't spoken more than three words to anyone in weeks.

"Good at what?"

"Drawing people out. Making them feel safe enough to talk." Mandy's expression was assessing. "It's a skill. Most people can't do it."

"I just ask questions."

"No. You ask questions and then you actually care about the answers." Mandy smiled. "That's different. That's rare."

---

That night, around the fire pit (every ranch had one, apparently), Bella sat with a group of veterans.

The conversation was loose, drifting from topic to topic without pressure. Someone told a story about a bad date. Someone else shared a memory of their grandmother's cooking. Laughter mixed with comfortable silence.

It reminded her of the Barrett compound. Of the fire pit rituals that had shaped her over the past two years.

*This is what healing looks like,* she thought. *Not therapy in an office. Not medication alone. But community. Connection. Being seen without judgment.*

She understood now why Mandy had built this. Why it mattered.

And she wondered, not for the first time, what her own contribution could be.
