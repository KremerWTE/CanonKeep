# Chapter 5c: The Question

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Three: The Fork

---

The question came out before Bella could stop it.

They were on the porch, watching the sun set over the mountains. The usual quiet evening. The usual comfortable silence.

But Bella's mind was racing.

"Do you want kids?"

Mandy went very still.

"That's a big question."

"I know. I'm sorry. I shouldn't have—"

"No." Mandy's voice was careful. "You should have. If it's something you're thinking about, you should ask."

---

The silence stretched.

Bella watched Mandy's face—the slight tension around her eyes, the way her jaw tightened. She knew that look. She'd seen it in herself, in mirrors, when confronting truths she didn't want to face.

"I can't have children," Mandy said finally. "Biologically. There were... complications. After some of the things that happened in my past."

"I'm so sorry—"

"Let me finish." Mandy took a breath. "I can't have children biologically. But that's not why I never had them. I never had them because I don't want them."

The words landed like stones in water.

"You don't want children?"

"I've never wanted them. I knew that about myself before the Agency, before the damage, before any of it." Mandy turned to look at her. "I'm not built for motherhood, Bella. I don't have the instincts. I don't have the patience. I built this ranch to help other people's children—to give veterans a chance to heal so they can go home to their families. But I don't want that for myself."

---

"I didn't know."

"I know you didn't. We never talked about it." Mandy's voice was gentle. "Do you want children?"

Bella hadn't let herself answer that question. Not consciously. But now, confronted with it directly, the answer rose up from somewhere deep.

"I think I might. Someday."

The admission felt like a confession. Like something shameful, even though it wasn't.

"Then we have a problem," Mandy said quietly.

---

They didn't talk about it that night.

Or the next day. Or the day after that.

The question hung between them like a storm cloud—visible, ominous, impossible to ignore but also impossible to face directly.

Bella threw herself into work. Mandy threw herself into the ranch. They moved around each other with careful politeness, neither willing to break the fragile peace.

But Bella couldn't sleep.

She lay awake, staring at the ceiling, trying to imagine a future without children. Trying to convince herself it would be enough. Trying to believe that love—this love, this specific love—was worth any sacrifice.

But something inside her kept saying no.

---

"You need to talk to her," Hawk said during their weekly call.

"I know."

"Then why aren't you?"

"Because I'm afraid of what happens after." Bella's voice cracked. "I love her, Dad. I really love her. And I don't want to lose her."

"You might not have to lose her. But you will lose yourself if you pretend you want something different than you actually want." His voice was kind but firm. "That's not sustainable. Eventually, the truth comes out. And it's worse when it comes out in resentment rather than honesty."

"What if she can't give me what I need?"

"Then you grieve that. And you figure out what comes next." A pause. "But you can't know until you have the conversation. And avoiding the conversation just prolongs the pain."

---

That night, Bella found Mandy at the fire pit.

"We need to talk," she said.

Mandy nodded. "I know."

They sat. The fire crackled. The stars emerged.

And finally, finally, they faced the fork in the road.
