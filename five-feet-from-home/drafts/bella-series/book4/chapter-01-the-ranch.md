# Chapter 1: The Ranch

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part One: Denver

---

The ranch spread across the Colorado foothills like something out of a painting—rolling pastures, weathered fences, mountains rising in the distance like silent guardians.

Bella arrived on a Tuesday, her car crunching over gravel as she pulled up to the main house. The drive from the airport had taken two hours, winding through small towns and open country that felt a world away from Charlotte and the BSS compound.

She wasn't entirely sure why she was here.

Addie had arranged it—something about a veteran support program that needed assessment, a connection to BSS through channels Bella didn't fully understand. The ranch was run by a woman named Mandy, former Agency operative, now helping others heal from the kind of wounds that didn't show on the surface.

"You'll like her," Addie had said with a knowing smile. "She sees things the way you do."

Bella parked and stepped out, the mountain air sharp and clean in her lungs.

The main house was large but unpretentious—wood and stone, built for function rather than show. Around it, smaller cabins dotted the property, and in the distance, she could see horses grazing in a fenced pasture.

It was quiet. The kind of quiet that felt intentional.

A screen door banged open, and a woman stepped onto the porch.

Bella knew immediately—this was Mandy.

She was stunning in a way that didn't try too hard. Sun-weathered skin. Steel-green eyes that seemed to read everything at once. Hair pulled back in a practical ponytail. The kind of beauty that came from living fully rather than performing.

"You must be Bella." Mandy's voice was warm but measured, like she was already taking Bella's measure. "Addie said you were coming. Didn't say much else."

"That's Addie for you," Bella said, surprising herself with the ease of the response.

Mandy smiled—a real smile, not the polished kind Bella was used to from networking events. "Come on in. I'll show you around. Then we can talk about why you're really here."

Bella followed her inside, the screen door banging shut behind them.

Something about this place felt different. Safer. Like the walls weren't watching to see if she'd measure up.

Maybe that was the point.
