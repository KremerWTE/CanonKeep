# Chapter 5e: What Mandy Taught

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Three: Love and Learning

---

Mandy wasn't just a therapist or a ranch owner. She was a case study in survival.

The more time Bella spent with her, the more she understood: this woman had been broken completely—and had rebuilt herself into something stronger.

"How did you do it?" Bella asked one evening.

"Do what?"

"Survive. Heal. Become... this." She gestured at the ranch, the peace, the woman who seemed at home in her own skin.

"I didn't. Not alone." Mandy smiled. "I had help. Lots of it. And I made a choice, every single day, to keep going."

---

**[THEME: LEARNING FROM SURVIVAL]**

Mandy's story came out in pieces.

The childhood abuse she'd escaped at seventeen.
The addiction that nearly killed her at twenty-five.
The recovery that took a decade—not a moment, a decade—of daily choices.
The ranch, built from scratch at thirty-five, when she finally believed she deserved a life.

"I didn't think I was worth saving," Mandy said. "For years, I agreed with the people who hurt me. They said I was worthless. I believed them."

"What changed?"

"I got tired of agreeing. Realized I'd been letting abusers write my story." Her eyes were steady. "Decided to write my own instead."

---

The parallels were impossible to ignore.

Bella's worthiness wound wasn't born from abuse—but it was still a story written by others. By every voice that had told her she wasn't enough. By every system that required her to prove her value.

She'd been letting them write her story too.

*What if I wrote my own?*

---

"The hustle thing," Mandy said one afternoon. "It's not really about work."

"What is it about?"

"Control. You can't control whether people love you. Can't control whether you're enough. But you can control how hard you work. So you work yourself into the ground trying to earn what should be given freely."

The insight hit like cold water.

"That's... exactly right."

"I know. I did the same thing with different tools." Mandy shrugged. "Mine was substances. Yours is productivity. Same wound, different bandage."

---

"So how do I stop?"

"You don't stop working. You change why you work." Mandy leaned forward. "Right now you work to prove worth. What if you worked because you enjoyed it? Because it served others? Because it's what you're meant to do—not what you're required to do?"

"I don't know the difference."

"You will. Once you believe you're worthy without the work, the work changes. It becomes gift instead of tax."

---

Bella sat with that for days.

*Gift instead of tax.*

Her entire career, she'd been paying dues. Proving she deserved her place. Working like her life depended on it—because in her mind, it did.

What would it feel like to work because she wanted to? Because she was good at it? Because the work mattered, not because she had to earn love?

She didn't know. But she wanted to find out.
