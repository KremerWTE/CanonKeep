# Chapter 3: Building Trust

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Two: Slow and Safe

---

Mandy read her carefully.

That was the only way to describe it. From the first morning Bella woke up in the guest cabin, Mandy seemed to know exactly how much space to give her. When to push. When to pull back. When to be present without demanding anything.

"You're not used to this, are you?" Mandy asked one afternoon, as they walked the fence line checking for repairs.

"Used to what?"

"Someone paying attention without wanting something in return."

Bella stopped walking. The observation landed harder than it should have.

"I'm not trying to figure you out so I can use you," Mandy continued, her voice matter-of-fact. "I'm paying attention because I want to understand you. There's a difference."

The weeks that followed were unlike anything Bella had experienced.

Mandy took things slow. Not because she was tentative, but because she seemed to understand that Bella needed time to believe this was real.

They worked together during the days—feeding horses, meeting veterans, running the administrative side of the program that Bella naturally gravitated toward. In the evenings, they sat on the porch and watched the sun set behind the mountains, talking about everything and nothing.

Mandy asked questions nobody else had thought to ask.

"What did you want to be when you were little?"

"Before the café and the bookstore—what made you happy?"

"When's the last time you did something just because you wanted to, not because someone needed you to?"

Bella didn't have good answers at first. She'd spent so long defining herself by usefulness that she'd forgotten what she actually wanted.

But Mandy didn't push for answers. She just kept asking. And slowly, tentatively, Bella started to remember.

"I used to love reading," she admitted one night. "Not for school. Just for me. I'd curl up in the bookstore after closing and read until my eyes burned."

"When did you stop?"

"I don't know. Somewhere along the way, reading became something I did for information instead of joy."

Mandy reached over and squeezed her hand. "We're going to fix that."

The next morning, there was a novel on Bella's pillow. Something light. Something fun. Something that had no purpose except pleasure.

Bella read the whole thing in two days.

And something in her chest—something that had been locked for years—started to crack open.
