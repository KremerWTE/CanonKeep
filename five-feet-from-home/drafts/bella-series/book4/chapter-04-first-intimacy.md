It happened on a Thursday night.

Not dramatically—no sweeping declarations or lightning-bolt moments. Just the natural culmination of weeks of trust built one conversation at a time.

They were on the porch, the way they often were. The mountain air was cool, the stars impossibly bright. Bella had been talking about her childhood—the café, the bookstore, the brothers who took up all the space—when she realized Mandy was looking at her differently.

Not studying. Not assessing.

Just... seeing.

"What?" Bella asked, suddenly self-conscious.

"Nothing." Mandy's voice was soft. "I just like listening to you."

The silence that followed wasn't awkward. It was full.

Mandy reached out slowly—giving Bella time to pull away if she needed to—and brushed a strand of hair from her face.

"I'm going to kiss you now," Mandy said. "Unless you don't want me to."

But Mandy waited. Patient. Present.

"Okay," Bella whispered.

"Sorry," she said, embarrassed. "I don't know why I'm—"

"Because you've never let yourself have this before," Mandy finished for her. "It's okay. Take your time. We've got nothing but time."

That night was the first time Bella allowed herself to be truly vulnerable in love.

Mandy made it slow. Made it safe. Made it feel less like a performance and more like coming home.

In the morning, Bella woke up to sunlight streaming through the cabin window and Mandy already awake, watching her with those steel-green eyes.

"You okay?" Mandy asked.

Bella thought about it. Really thought about it.

"Yeah," she said, surprised by the truth of it. "I think I am."
