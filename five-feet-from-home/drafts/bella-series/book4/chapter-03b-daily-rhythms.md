# Chapter 3b: Daily Rhythms

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Two: Slow and Safe

---

Two weeks in, Bella had found her rhythm.

Mornings started early—5:30 AM, helping with the horses before breakfast. There was something meditative about the work: filling water troughs, distributing hay, mucking stalls. Simple tasks that didn't require thought, just presence.

"You're up before most of the veterans," Mandy observed one morning, finding Bella already in the barn.

"I like the quiet."

"So do I." Mandy picked up a pitchfork, joining her in the work. "That's why I chose this life. The quiet. The simplicity."

"Do you miss it? The old life?"

"Sometimes. The adrenaline. The feeling of purpose." Mandy tossed a forkful of hay into a stall. "But then I remember what it cost me, and the missing goes away."

---

Breakfast was communal, as always.

Bella had claimed a spot at the veteran table—Earl on one side, Maria on the other. They'd accepted her without fanfare, the way the ranch accepted everyone. Present. Contributing. That was all that mattered.

"You're staying longer than the consultation required," Maria noted.

"I am."

"Any particular reason?"

Bella considered the question. "I'm learning things here. About myself. About what I want."

Maria nodded wisely. "The ranch does that. Strips away the noise until you can hear your own voice."

"Is that what happened to you?"

"Eventually. It takes time." Maria smiled. "For me, it was the garden. For you, it might be something else."

Bella thought about Mandy. About the fire pit conversations that went on longer each night. About the way her heart lifted when she heard that particular voice.

"I think it might be."

---

Afternoons were for work.

Not ranch work—BSS work, conducted remotely from her cabin. Video calls with Kendra. Case files reviewed on her laptop. The machinery of crisis management continuing even from two thousand miles away.

"You're splitting your focus," Addie observed during one call.

"Is that a problem?"

"Not if it's intentional." Addie's eyes were knowing through the screen. "Sometimes we need space to process things. Sometimes the work becomes the excuse to avoid the processing."

"I'm not avoiding anything."

"Good. Then enjoy the space." Addie smiled. "And Bella? Whatever's happening there—whatever you're figuring out—I trust you. You'll know what to do when the time comes."

---

Evenings belonged to the fire pit.

Mandy was usually there, sometimes alone, sometimes with veterans or staff. But whoever else was present, Bella always found herself gravitating toward her.

Their conversations had deepened. Past the polite getting-to-know-you phase, into something more honest.

"What do you want?" Mandy asked one night. "Really want, if you could have anything?"

"I used to know the answer to that." Bella stared into the flames. "Success. Belonging. Being chosen by people who mattered."

"And now?"

"Now I have those things. And I'm realizing they're not enough."

"What's missing?"

"I don't know. Something personal. Something that's just mine, not connected to work or family or achievement." Bella turned to look at her. "What do you want?"

"Peace." Mandy's answer was immediate. "I want to go to sleep without nightmares. I want to wake up without dread. I want to feel like the past is actually past."

"Do you have that here?"

"Some days. Not all of them." Mandy smiled. "But more than I used to. That's progress."
