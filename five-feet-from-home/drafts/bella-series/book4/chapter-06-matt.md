# Chapter 6: Matt

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Three: The Fork

---

Matt had been there all along.

Bella just hadn't noticed him. Not the way she noticed him now.

He was one of the ranchers at Mandy's place—a quiet, steady presence who fixed fences and gentled horses and seemed to exist in the background of every scene without demanding attention. Weathered hands. Kind eyes. The kind of man who said more with silence than most people said with speeches.

Mandy introduced them properly on a Tuesday afternoon.

"Bella, this is Matt. He runs the vacation home business on the adjacent property. Helps us out with the horses when we need an extra hand."

Matt tipped his cap. "Ma'am."

Something about the gesture—old-fashioned, genuine—made Bella smile.

Over the following weeks, she noticed him more. The way he treated the horses: patient, respectful, the same way you'd gentle a horse—reading its moods, adjusting his approach, never forcing anything. The way he treated the veterans: present without hovering, helpful without being condescending.

One afternoon, he offered to take her on a trail ride.

Bella said yes without thinking about it. The ride was quiet, the mountains rolling out beneath them, the sky impossibly wide. By the time they returned to the barn, something had shifted.

Matt helped her dismount, his hands steady at her waist.

"I had a good time," Bella said, and then, before she could stop herself: "I don't have a lot of experience with this. Relationships. Being... chosen."

Matt looked at her for a long moment. Then he tipped his cap again and gave her a soft peck on the cheek.

"I had a good time too, Bella," he said. And he walked away.

Later, Mandy found her sitting on the porch, staring at nothing.

"You met Matt," Mandy said. It wasn't a question.

"You set that up."

Mandy smiled, steel-green eyes crinkling. "I positioned a piece on the board. That's different."

"Hawk would be proud," Bella muttered.

"He taught me a few things." Mandy sat down beside her. "Bella, I love you. That hasn't changed. But I can see the life you want, and I can see that Matt could be part of it. I'm not pushing you away. I'm clearing a path."

"The black king on the chessboard," Bella said quietly.

Mandy raised an eyebrow. "You've been talking to Hawk."

"He's my father. He talks about chess a lot."

Mandy laughed—the first real laugh Bella had heard from her in weeks.

"Then you already know," Mandy said. "Some pieces are meant to move together. And some are meant to help each other find the right square."
