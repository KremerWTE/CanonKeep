# Chapter 5b: Six Months

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Three: The Fork

---

Six months.

Six months of Colorado skies and mountain air. Six months of early mornings with the horses and late nights at the fire pit. Six months of Mandy's steel-green eyes and rough hands and the way she laughed when Bella said something that surprised her.

Six months of being happy.

Bella hadn't known it was possible to be this happy for this long.

---

"You're different," Hawk said during one of their calls.

"Good different?"

"Settled different." His voice was approving. "You were always a little scattered before. Like you were waiting for the other shoe to drop. Now you're just... present."

"I feel present."

"That's growth, daughter." A pause. "When are you coming home?"

"I don't know. Soon, maybe." Bella looked out her cabin window at the mountains. "I miss everyone. But I don't want to leave what I've found here."

"You don't have to choose. You can have both."

"Can I?"

"Family isn't geography. It's connection." His voice was warm. "Whatever you decide, we're still here. And if you need to stay in Colorado longer, that's okay."

---

Her work had evolved.

She wasn't working full-time for BSS anymore—she'd transitioned to consulting, taking cases that could be handled remotely, flying back to Charlotte for the ones that couldn't. It was a compromise that worked, mostly.

But Addie had started talking about the future.

"You can't consult forever," she said during a strategy call. "At some point, you need to decide what you're building."

"I thought I was building a life."

"You are. But your skills are still needed. The question is how to use them in a way that fits your new circumstances." Addie paused. "Have you thought about building something at the ranch? Some way to combine your fixer skills with Mandy's mission?"

The idea had crossed Bella's mind. More than once.

"I'm not sure I'm ready for that."

"You're more ready than you think. But there's no rush." Addie smiled. "Take your time. The vision will come when it's ready."

---

Life with Mandy was easy in ways Bella hadn't expected.

They'd fallen into routines: morning coffee together, work during the day (sometimes overlapping, usually not), evenings at the fire pit or in Mandy's house or out on the property somewhere.

They argued sometimes—about ranch decisions, about Bella's work travel, about the small frictions of two strong-willed people sharing space. But the arguments were productive, not destructive. They talked things through. They found compromises. They made up.

"This is what healthy looks like," Mandy said one night, after a disagreement about a veteran who needed more intensive care than the ranch could provide.

"What do you mean?"

"I mean we disagree without destroying each other. We fight without fighting dirty." Mandy pulled her close. "I've never had that before. I thought it was impossible."

"Me too."

"Then we're learning together." Mandy kissed her forehead. "That's probably how it's supposed to work."

---

But underneath the happiness, something was shifting.

Bella felt it in quiet moments—a longing she couldn't name, a question she wasn't ready to ask. She pushed it aside, focused on the present, told herself there was no reason to think about the future yet.

Until one night, Maria said something that changed everything.

"You're good with kids," Maria observed, watching Bella help a veteran's daughter with homework during a family visit. "Natural."

"I just remember what it was like to need help."

"It's more than that. You see children. Really see them." Maria smiled. "You'd make a good mother someday."

The words hit Bella like a physical blow.

*Someday.*

She'd never thought about someday. Never let herself think about it. But suddenly, standing in the dining hall with a child's spelling words spread across the table, she couldn't think about anything else.
