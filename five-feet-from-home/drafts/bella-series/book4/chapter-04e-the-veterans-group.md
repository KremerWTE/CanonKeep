# Chapter 4e: The Veterans' Group

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Two: Building Trust

---

Every Thursday, the veterans came.

Men and women with different stories but similar wounds. PTSD, traumatic brain injuries, moral injury, grief. They arrived at the ranch for equine therapy, and Bella watched from a distance, learning.

"You can join them," Mandy offered one week.

"I'm not a veteran."

"Trauma doesn't check credentials." Mandy paused. "The horses don't care what caused your pain. Neither do these people."

---

**[THEME: SHARED WOUNDS]**

The first session she attended, Bella mostly listened.

A Marine who couldn't sleep since Afghanistan.
A medic who still heard screaming in quiet moments.
A pilot who'd made a call that haunted her—right decision, wrong outcome, infinite guilt.

Different wounds. But the underlying rhythm was the same.

*I'm not enough. I should have done more. It was my fault.*

---

"You're quiet," one of them—Marcus, former Army—observed.

"I'm not sure I belong here."

"Why not?"

"My trauma isn't... big enough. Compared to what you've been through."

Marcus laughed. Not unkindly. "Trauma isn't a competition. Pain doesn't come in sizes. Either it hurts or it doesn't."

"Mine hurts."

"Then you belong here." He nodded toward the horses. "They don't judge. Neither do we."

---

The sessions followed a pattern.

Ground work with the horses—leading, grooming, sometimes just standing in their presence. The horses responded to emotional states, reflecting back what each person carried.

"Storm likes you," the pilot—Jamie—noted, watching Bella with the gray mare.

"We understand each other."

"The damaged ones usually do." Jamie smiled, no offense in her voice. "She picked me too, first time I came here. Saw something she recognized."

---

Over the weeks, Bella found herself talking.

Not about everything—not yet. But pieces.

The worthiness wound. The hustle as coping. The crash in Europe. The constant voice that said she wasn't enough.

"That voice is a liar," Marcus said flatly. "Sorry to be blunt, but it's true. We all have one. Says different things, but lies all the same."

"How do you make it stop?"

"You don't. You just stop letting it drive."

---

The metaphor landed.

*The voice is the passenger, not the driver. It can scream all it wants. I still choose where to go.*

New concept. Revolutionary, even. She'd spent her whole life obeying the voice, letting it dictate her choices.

What if she didn't have to?

---

Journal entry:

*Joined the veterans' group today. Didn't expect to fit—their trauma is so much bigger than mine.*

*But Marcus said something that stuck: "Pain doesn't come in sizes. Either it hurts or it doesn't."*

*My pain is real. Different from theirs, but real. The worthiness wound, the voice, the constant need to prove—it all hurts. Has hurt for years.*

*Maybe that's enough to claim space. To say: I belong here too. My wounds count.*

*Still terrifying to believe. But I'm practicing.*
