# Chapter 6b: After the Fork

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Four: New Directions

---

The weeks after were strange.

Bella and Mandy were no longer together—not in the way they'd been. But they weren't apart either. They still ate dinner together. Still talked at the fire pit. Still existed in each other's orbits.

"This is weird," Bella admitted to Earl one morning.

"What's weird?"

"We broke up. But we're still... here. Together but not together."

Earl considered this. "Some endings are actually transitions. Maybe that's what this is."

"What do you mean?"

"You're not ending the relationship. You're changing it. That's harder in some ways, but healthier." He patted her shoulder. "Give it time. The new shape will emerge."

---

Mandy was the one who brought up Matt.

They were walking the property, checking fences, the way they often did on weekend mornings. Bella had noticed Mandy glancing at her—assessing, calculating.

"You've noticed Matt," Mandy said finally.

"What?"

"The rancher. The one with the vacation home business next door." Mandy's eyes were knowing. "You've noticed him."

"I've noticed a lot of people."

"Not like that." Mandy smiled slightly. "He's been watching you too. For months. He's just too polite to say anything."

Bella felt heat rise in her cheeks. "You're setting me up."

"I'm observing. There's a difference."

---

"Why are you doing this?"

Mandy stopped walking. Turned to face her.

"Because you deserve to be happy. Because I can see the life you want, and I can't give it to you—but maybe he can." Her voice was gentle. "I'm not pushing you away. I'm clearing a path."

"Hawk would call that moving pieces on the board."

"He taught me well." Mandy's smile was sad but genuine. "Bella, I love you. That hasn't changed. But love isn't about possession. It's about wanting the best for someone, even when the best isn't you."

"What if I'm not ready?"

"Then you're not ready. Matt will wait—he's patient that way." Mandy shrugged. "But I see you looking at him. I see the way your eyes follow him across the barn. You're ready to explore that, even if you're afraid to admit it."

---

That night, Bella called Hawk.

"She's setting me up with someone else," she said, still processing.

"Is that bad?"

"I don't know. It feels... strange. Like she's giving me permission to move on."

"Maybe that's exactly what you need." Hawk's voice was thoughtful. "Some people need permission. To let go. To try again. To believe they deserve another chance at happiness."

"What if I'm not ready?"

"You've asked that question three times in two weeks." His tone was amused. "The fact that you keep asking suggests you're more ready than you think."

"Or that I'm terrified."

"Being terrified and being ready aren't mutually exclusive." A pause. "Tell me about this Matt person."

So Bella told him. The quiet presence. The gentle hands with the horses. The way he tipped his cap, old-fashioned and genuine. The soft peck on the cheek after their first trail ride.

"He sounds like a counterbalance," Hawk said, when she finished.

"To what?"

"To everything you're used to. Crisis management. High stakes. Intensity." His voice was knowing. "Sometimes we need someone who lives at a different tempo. Someone who reminds us that not everything is urgent."

"Is that what you found with Addie?"

"Addie taught me to breathe. To exist without constantly scanning for threats." He smiled—Bella could hear it in his voice. "I was a wound when I met her. She helped me heal. Maybe Matt could be that for you."
