# Chapter 1b: Arrival

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part One: Denver

---

The guest cabin was simple.

Wooden floors. A bed with a quilt that looked handmade. A window overlooking the pasture. A small bathroom with a claw-foot tub that seemed impossibly charming.

"We don't do luxury here," Mandy had said, showing her in. "We do real."

Bella set down her bag and looked around. The space felt comfortable in a way her apartment in Charlotte never had. Like it was built for living, not impressing.

"Dinner's at six," Mandy added from the doorway. "Communal. You eat with everyone or not at all."

"Everyone?"

"The veterans. The staff. The people who make this place work." Mandy's expression softened slightly. "It's how we build community. Shared meals. Shared space. Nobody hides here."

Bella nodded. "I'll be there."

---

She spent the afternoon exploring.

The property was larger than she'd realized—hundreds of acres, some in active use, some left wild. She found a barn with horses, a workshop where someone was building furniture, a garden that stretched in careful rows toward the mountains.

And everywhere, people.

Not many—maybe fifteen, twenty total. But present. Working. Talking. Existing alongside each other without the performance that defined most social spaces.

She recognized veterans by the way they carried themselves—a certain alertness, a particular set to the shoulders. She recognized staff by the way they moved with purpose, always heading somewhere specific.

And she recognized herself, standing at the edge, watching.

*Old habits,* she thought. *Time to break them.*

---

The dining hall was a converted barn.

Long tables. Benches instead of individual chairs. A buffet line where everyone served themselves from platters of roasted chicken, vegetables, fresh bread.

Bella loaded her plate and looked for a seat.

"Over here." A man at one of the tables waved her over. He was older—sixties, maybe—with a graying beard and eyes that had seen too much. "New arrival. We save a spot for new arrivals."

"Thanks." She sat down. "I'm Bella."

"Earl." He extended a hand. "Marine. Three tours. Been here six months."

"Is it helping?"

"Getting there." His smile was tired but genuine. "The horses don't care what you did. They just care how you treat them. That's... valuable."

---

Mandy sat at the head of the main table, flanked by what looked like senior staff.

Bella watched her through the meal—the way she engaged with everyone, the way she remembered names and details, the way she commanded respect without demanding it.

*She runs this like Addie runs BSS,* Bella realized. *With competence and care.*

After dinner, the crowd dispersed. Some went to the fire pit outside. Some retreated to cabins. Some stayed to help clean up.

Bella helped.

"You don't have to do that," Mandy said, appearing beside her at the dish station.

"I want to."

"Most guests don't."

"I'm not most guests." Bella handed her a dried plate. "I grew up in a cafe. This feels like home."

Mandy studied her for a moment. Then she smiled—a real smile, the kind that crinkled her steel-green eyes.

"Good. I think you'll fit here."

---

That night, in her cabin, Bella wrote in her journal:

*First day at the ranch. It's nothing like I expected.*

*I expected a retreat—quiet rooms, meditation sessions, whatever rich people do when they're trying to find themselves. Instead, I found a working operation. Real people doing real work.*

*Mandy is... something. I can't quite describe it yet. She sees things. Understands things without asking. There's pain behind her eyes, but also strength. She's built something here that matters.*

*I don't know why I came. But I'm glad I did.*
