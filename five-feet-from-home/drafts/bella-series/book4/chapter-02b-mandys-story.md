# Chapter 2b: Mandy's Story

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part One: Denver

---

It was her third night at the ranch when Mandy told her.

They'd finished dinner, helped with cleanup, and drifted to the porch while the veterans headed to their cabins or the fire pit. The sun was setting over the mountains, painting everything gold and crimson.

"You've been watching me," Mandy said. Not accusatory. Just observing.

"Is that obvious?"

"To me." Mandy settled into a rocking chair. "I'm trained to notice when I'm being assessed. Part of the job I used to have."

"The Agency."

"The Agency." Mandy's voice was flat. "You want to know about that?"

Bella considered lying. Politeness suggested changing the subject. But something about this place—this moment—invited honesty.

"Yes."

---

Mandy rocked for a while before speaking.

"I was recruited in college. Languages—I speak six fluently—and a talent for reading people. They saw something they could use. I didn't know enough to say no."

"What did they have you do?"

"Everything. Nothing. The work is... it's not like the movies. It's slow. Methodical. Years of building relationships that might never pay off." She paused. "And then, sometimes, it's fast. Brutal. The kind of things you can't un-know."

"What happened?"

"I broke." Mandy's voice was quiet now. "Not all at once. Slowly, piece by piece, until there was nothing left. I remember the exact moment I realized I couldn't do it anymore."

"What moment?"

"I was in a hotel room in Vienna. I'd just finished an operation—successful, by their standards. Nobody died who wasn't supposed to die. But I sat in the shower until the water went cold. Couldn't feel anything. Couldn't cry. Couldn't move." She turned to look at Bella. "That's when I knew. I was done. Whatever I'd had before—the fire, the purpose, the ability to compartmentalize—it was gone."

---

"How did you get from there to here?"

"I resigned. They let me go—I knew too much for them to push me out badly. I had money saved. I bought this land." Mandy gestured at the property. "And I started building something different."

"Why veterans?"

"Because they understood. What I'd been through—it's not the same as combat, but it rhymes. The trauma of doing things you believed were necessary. The guilt of surviving when others didn't. The way civilians look at you when they don't know what to say."

"So this place is as much for you as it is for them."

"Every day." Mandy's smile was thin. "I heal by helping them heal. It's not selfless. It's survival."

---

They sat in silence as the sun finished its descent.

"Why are you telling me this?" Bella asked finally.

"Because you'll understand." Mandy turned to face her directly. "Addie told me about you. Not details—Addie never gives details. But she said you were someone who knew what it meant to break and rebuild. I wanted to see for myself."

"And what do you see?"

"Someone still in the middle of rebuilding." Mandy's eyes were kind. "Someone who doesn't quite believe she's worth the effort yet. But working on it."

Bella's throat tightened. "That's... accurate."

"I know." Mandy reached over, touching her hand briefly. "That's why I wanted you to come. Because people like us—we recognize each other. And sometimes, we can help each other see what we can't see alone."
