# Chapter 4c: Learning to Receive

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Two: Slow and Safe

---

The hardest part wasn't the intimacy.

The hardest part was learning to receive.

"You're doing it again," Mandy said one evening, as Bella jumped up to help with dinner cleanup.

"Doing what?"

"Making yourself useful to avoid being vulnerable." Mandy gently pushed her back into her chair. "Sit. Let someone else work for once."

"But I can help—"

"You don't have to earn your place here." Mandy's eyes were kind. "You're not here because of what you do. You're here because of who you are."

---

It went against everything Bella knew.

Her whole life, she'd made herself indispensable. In her parents' cafe—always helping, always anticipating needs. In her sorority—always fixing crises, always being dependable. At BSS—always proving her worth through work.

Being valuable was safety. Being useful was armor.

But Mandy kept chipping away at it.

"I brought you coffee," Mandy said one morning, appearing in Bella's cabin with a steaming mug.

"You didn't have to—"

"I wanted to." Mandy sat on the edge of the bed. "That's different. I saw you working late last night. Figured you could use the caffeine."

"Thank you."

"You're welcome. And you don't have to repay me." Mandy smiled. "Just accept it. Let it be enough."

---

Bella's journal that week was scattered with revelations:

*I don't know how to let people take care of me.*

*Every kind gesture feels like a debt I need to repay. Every gift feels like an obligation. I keep waiting for the catch—for the moment when someone says "I gave you all this, now you owe me."*

*Mandy doesn't do that. She gives without keeping score. She helps without expecting return. It should feel natural, but it feels foreign. Like learning a language I never knew existed.*

*Maybe that's what love is supposed to be. Not transaction. Not exchange. Just... giving. Because you want to.*

*I'm not sure I know how to do that yet. But I want to learn.*

---

"Tell me about your self-doubt," Mandy said one night, at the fire pit.

"What do you mean?"

"You carry it like a second skin. This constant sense that you're not good enough, that you have to prove yourself, that love is conditional on performance." Mandy's voice was gentle. "Where did that come from?"

Bella stared into the flames.

"Everywhere. Nowhere specific. Just... my whole life, I was the one who helped. Never the one who was helped. I learned that being useful was the only way to matter."

"That's not true."

"I know that now. Sometimes." Bella smiled weakly. "Other times, I forget."

"That's okay. Unlearning takes time." Mandy reached for her hand. "You don't have to be useful right now. You just have to be here."

"That's harder than it sounds."

"I know." Mandy squeezed. "I know."

---

Slowly, Bella started to believe it.

Not all at once—belief didn't work that way. But in moments. In flashes.

When Mandy brought her coffee without being asked.
When Earl told a joke specifically to make her laugh.
When Maria shared vegetables from her garden, insisting they were "extra" and didn't need to be repaid.

Each small gift, accepted without deflection. Each kindness, received without the armor of reciprocity.

It felt like thawing. Like ice she hadn't known was there finally starting to melt.

*Maybe this is what Hawk meant,* she thought. *Finding someone who sees all of you and stays anyway.*

Maybe this was what she'd been looking for all along.
