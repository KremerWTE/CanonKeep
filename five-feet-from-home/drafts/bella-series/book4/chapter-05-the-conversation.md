# Chapter 5: The Conversation

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Three: The Fork

---

The conversation came on a Sunday morning.

They were sitting in the kitchen, coffee cooling between them, the ranch quiet outside the windows. Bella had been at the ranch for three months now, splitting her time between Denver and Charlotte, building something real with Mandy that she'd never believed was possible.

But something had been growing in her chest. A question she was afraid to ask.

"What do you see for us?" Bella asked. "Long-term, I mean."

Mandy set down her coffee. Her steel-green eyes were steady, unafraid.

"I see this," she said. "You. Me. The ranch. The work we're doing here. A life that means something."

"And kids?"

The word hung in the air.

Mandy was quiet for a long moment. When she spoke, her voice was gentle but certain.

"I don't want kids, Bella. I never have. The life I lived, the things I saw—I made peace a long time ago with not bringing children into that. Not because I think you'd be a bad mother. You'd be incredible. But it's not the future I want."

Bella's heart cracked open.

She'd known. Somewhere in the back of her mind, she'd sensed it. But hearing it said out loud made it real.

"I do," Bella whispered. "Want kids, I mean. I didn't always know it. But lately... I can see it. A family. My own family."

Mandy reached across the table, took Bella's hands in hers.

"Then you need to have that," she said. "And I'd rather watch you grow into everything you're meant to be than keep you small."

Bella started crying. "I don't want to lose you."

"You're not losing me." Mandy's voice was fierce and tender at once. "I love you, Bella. That doesn't change because our roads are forking. It just means we have to be honest about where they lead."

They sat together for a long time, hands intertwined, the coffee going cold.

The fork in the road had been visible for weeks. Now they were both looking at it clearly.

Love wasn't always about staying together. Sometimes it was about wanting what was best for the other person, even if it meant letting go.
