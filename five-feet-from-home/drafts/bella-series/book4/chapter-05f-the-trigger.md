# Chapter 5f: The Trigger

## Book 4: "Show Me"
### Part Three: Love and Learning

---

The email arrived on a Wednesday.

A routine update from Charlotte—project status, team changes, a passing mention of the work continuing without her.

*Continuing without you.*

The voice seized on those words.

---

**[THEME: SETBACK AND RECOVERY]**

Within minutes, Bella was spiraling.

*They don't need you. You're irrelevant. By the time you go back, they'll have forgotten you ever mattered.*

The old pattern, roaring back. The panic rising. The urge to open her laptop, send emails, prove she was still valuable.

She was halfway to the main house—where Wi-Fi was strongest—before she caught herself.

*What am I doing?*

---

She stopped in the middle of the path.

Breathed. Felt her feet on the ground. Noticed the mountains, the sky, the late afternoon light.

*This is a trigger. The wound is talking. I don't have to listen.*

The insight didn't make the feelings go away. But it created space. A pause between stimulus and response.

She sat down on a rock. Let the panic wash through without acting on it.

---

Mandy found her an hour later.

"You okay?"

"I got triggered. Email from work. Fell into the old spiral."

"And?"

"And I noticed. Stopped myself before I did something reactive."

Mandy sat beside her. "That's huge."

"It doesn't feel huge. It feels like barely surviving."

"Barely surviving is still surviving. And noticing is how you learn to do better."

---

That evening, she processed it at the fire pit.

Not Matt's fire pit—the one at the ranch, where the veterans sometimes gathered.

Marcus was there. The Army vet who'd welcomed her weeks ago.

"Bad day?"

"Trigger. Old wound stuff." She paused. "I almost let it drive. Almost went straight back into prove-myself mode."

"But you didn't."

"I didn't."

"Then it's a good day." His voice was matter-of-fact. "In recovery, not doing the thing is victory. Every time."

---
