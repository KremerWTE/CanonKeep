# Chapter 8: Elena's Mentorship

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Two: Integration

---

Addie taught her crisis management. Hawk taught her strategy. Kendra taught her operations.

Elena taught her elegance.

---

"You dress like someone trying not to be noticed," Elena said during their first real conversation about image. They were in Elena's office at Maison Aurelia—a space that looked like it belonged in a design magazine, all cream and gold and carefully curated sophistication.

"Isn't that the point? Fixers work behind the scenes."

"Fixers work wherever they need to work. Sometimes that's behind the scenes. Sometimes that's at the center of a ballroom, making sure the right people see you talking to the right people." Elena circled Bella like an artist appraising a canvas. "You have good bones. You just don't know how to use them."

"I don't know what that means."

"It means you could walk into any room in this city and command attention—if you believed you could." Elena stopped in front of her. "That's what I'm going to teach you. Not fashion. Presence."

---

The first lesson was wardrobe.

Elena took her shopping—not to the consignment shops Bella was used to, but to boutiques where attendants brought champagne and clothes appeared as if by magic.

"Try this," Elena said, handing her a dress in a color Bella would never have chosen. Deep teal, almost green, with a structure that suggested formality without rigidity.

"It's too much."

"It's exactly enough. Try it."

Bella tried it. The dress fit like it had been made for her—which, given the boutique's services, it probably could be. She stared at herself in the three-way mirror.

"You see it now," Elena said. "The version of yourself you've been avoiding."

"I'm not avoiding anything."

"You're avoiding visibility. You've built a career on being useful without being seen. That's a skill. But it's also a limitation." Elena adjusted the dress's collar. "Power requires presence. You can't influence a room you're invisible in."

---

The second lesson was movement.

"Walk across the room," Elena instructed. They were in the Maison Aurelia event space, practicing for a gala Bella would be attending in two weeks.

Bella walked.

"Again. This time, like you belong here."

Bella walked again.

"Again. Like every person in this room is watching you, and you're comfortable with that."

It took an hour before Elena was satisfied.

"Better. You're starting to inhabit the space instead of moving through it." Elena demonstrated—gliding across the floor with an ease that seemed effortless but was, Bella now understood, the product of decades of practice. "The secret is this: you're not performing for the room. You're inviting the room to notice what you already know about yourself."

"What if I don't know anything about myself?"

"Then fake it until you do. Eventually, the performance becomes reality."

---

The third lesson was conversation.

"You're good at listening," Elena observed during a practice session. "You ask questions, you remember details, you make people feel heard. That's valuable."

"Thank you."

"But you deflect when people try to learn about you. Watch." Elena took on the role of a gala guest. "So, Bella, tell me about your work. What do you do?"

"I work in consulting. Crisis management, mostly."

"See? You answered without answering. No specifics, no personality, nothing memorable." Elena leaned forward. "Try again. This time, give them something to hold onto."

Bella thought about it. "I solve problems other people don't want to deal with. The kind that make headlines if they're handled badly and disappear entirely if they're handled well."

"Better. Now add something human."

"I... grew up in a family business. Learned to read people over the cash register. Turns out that skill translates pretty well to preventing corporate disasters."

Elena smiled. "Now someone remembers you. Not as a consultant—as a person with a story. That's the difference between networking and connecting."

---

The fourth lesson was hardest.

"You don't believe you deserve attention," Elena said bluntly, during their final session before the gala.

Bella didn't argue.

"Where does that come from?"

"My family. My brothers were the stars. I was the helper." Bella shrugged. "It's hard to unlearn."

"It's not impossible. Just slow." Elena took her hand. "Listen to me. You have value that has nothing to do with being useful. You're intelligent, perceptive, resilient. You see things others miss. You care about outcomes without needing credit."

"That sounds like being useful."

"It sounds like being exceptional." Elena's grip tightened. "The women in the Club—we're not useful. We're powerful. There's a difference. Useful people serve others' agendas. Powerful people shape them."

"I don't feel powerful."

"You will. Give it time." Elena released her hand. "Now. Are you ready to walk into that gala like you own it?"

Bella took a breath. "I'm ready to try."

"That's all I'm asking."

---

The gala was a revelation.

Bella wore the teal dress. She walked with intention. She answered questions with personality. She stood at the center of conversations instead of the edges.

And people noticed.

Not because she'd become someone different—but because she'd finally allowed herself to be visible. To take up space. To believe that the room might be better for her presence in it.

"You're glowing," Addie said, finding her near the silent auction.

"Elena's coaching."

"Elena's coaching revealed what was already there." Addie's smile was proud. "You're not the shadow anymore, Bella. You're the light."
