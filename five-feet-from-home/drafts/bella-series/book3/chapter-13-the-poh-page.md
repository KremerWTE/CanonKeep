# Chapter 13: The POH Page

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Four: The New Normal

---

The Palace of Honor wasn't a building.

It was a network—ancient, invisible, woven through society like thread through fabric. And Bella had been given a needle.

"Page duties start tonight," Addie said, during their morning briefing. "I'll walk you through the first few weeks, but eventually you'll own it."

"What exactly does a Page do?"

"Everything the Knights and Dames don't have time for. Research. Coordination. Preparation." Addie smiled. "Think of it as being the stage manager for a play that never ends."

---

Her first assignment was donor research for an upcoming gala.

The Palace of Honor's charitable arm funded scholarships, medical research, disaster relief—quiet philanthropy that never made headlines but changed thousands of lives. The gala would bring together some of the wealthiest families in America, and Bella's job was to know everything about them.

She spent three evenings building profiles:

**The Harringtons:** Old steel money, third generation. The patriarch was dying and his sons were jockeying for control. Approach the wife—she made the actual decisions.

**The Mendozas:** Tech wealth, new to the circuit. He wanted to be taken seriously; she wanted to be seen as more than arm candy. Give them separate introductions.

**The Ashfords:** Banking dynasty, seven generations. The current heir had a gambling problem the family was hiding. Don't mention Monte Carlo.

"This is good work," Addie said, reviewing the files. "You see the patterns."

"I grew up in a cafe. You learn to read people or you don't get tips."

"That's exactly why you're here."

---

The gala itself was held at a private estate outside D.C.

Bella wasn't a guest—she was staff. She wore black (the Page uniform for events), carried a tablet with donor profiles, and positioned herself near the main entrance where she could whisper briefings to the hosts as VIPs arrived.

"Dr. Reynolds at three o'clock," she murmured to the Knight on duty. "His wife's charity just lost their director. Perfect time to suggest a POH partnership."

"How do you know that?"

"Press release this morning. And she posted on social media about needing help."

The Knight smiled. "Hawk said you were good."

---

The work was exhausting in a way BSS wasn't.

BSS was crisis management—high intensity, clear stakes, definable victories. POH work was cultivation—slow, patient, invisible. A conversation here. A connection there. Seeds planted that might not bloom for years.

But Bella found she was suited for it.

"You have the patience," Harper observed during one of her Charlotte visits. "Most young Pages burn out because they want immediate results. You understand delayed gratification."

"I spent years being invisible. Might as well make it useful."

"That's one way to look at it." Harper's gaze was knowing. "Another way is that you're finally learning to play the long game. White moves first—but the best moves set up positions that pay off twenty turns later."

---

Her Page duties included evening communications.

From 8 PM to midnight, she was the on-call contact for certain POH families. Not crises—BSS handled those. But questions, requests, coordination needs. A Knight's wife needed a recommendation for a surgeon. A Dame's son was applying to a program and needed a connection. A longtime donor wanted to discuss changing their bequest.

Bella became a switchboard. A bridge. A facilitator.

"You're not supposed to solve their problems," Addie explained. "You're supposed to connect them to the people who can. The value isn't in your answers—it's in your network."

"Then I need a bigger network."

Addie laughed. "Now you're thinking like a Page."

---

Three months into her Page duties, she was given her first ceremony role.

A new Knight was being invested—a tech entrepreneur who had donated significantly to veterans' causes. The ceremony was private, held at a historic estate, attended by perhaps thirty people.

Bella's role was procession coordinator: making sure every participant was in the right place at the right time, carrying the right items, saying the right words.

"No mistakes," Addie warned. "Ceremony is ritual, and ritual is sacred. Every detail matters."

Bella spent a week memorizing the protocol. The order of entrance. The placement of candles. The specific phrases used in the oath. Who stood where. Who spoke when.

On the night, she stood in the shadows, tablet in hand, feeding cues to participants through discrete earpieces.

"Dame Harrison, you're up in thirty seconds."
"Sir Mitchell, move to the east position."
"Knight-Elect, bow when the Patroness approaches."

The ceremony flowed perfectly. The new Knight was invested, tears in his eyes as he took the oath. The Patroness—a woman in her seventies with the bearing of a queen—nodded once in Bella's direction afterward.

That nod was worth more than any bonus check.

---

"You're not just a Page," Hawk said that night at the fire pit. "You're a keeper."

"A keeper of what?"

"Tradition. Secrets. The things that hold us together." He passed her the whiskey. "The modern world thinks everything should be transparent. Every process documented. Every decision explained. But some things are meant to be held quietly. Protected. Passed down person to person."

"Is that what POH is? An old institution hiding in plain sight?"

"All worthy institutions hide in plain sight. The ones that advertise themselves are usually selling something." He stared into the flames. "POH doesn't advertise. It doesn't recruit publicly. It selects. Cultivates. Invests in people who will carry the tradition forward."

"Like you invested in me."

"Like I'm still investing in you." His eyes met hers across the fire. "You have decades of growth ahead, daughter. The question isn't whether you'll become powerful. The question is what you'll do with that power."

Bella didn't have an answer yet.

But she was starting to see the question.
