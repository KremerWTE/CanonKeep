# Chapter 11: Kendra's Team

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Three: The Transformation

---

Addie had mentored her. Harper had polished her. Hawk had shaped her.

But Kendra made her operational.

---

The transition from Addie's direct oversight to Kendra's team happened gradually. One day, Bella was shadowing Addie on client calls. The next, she was reporting to Kendra on daily operations, running analysis for the Watch Desk, and coordinating with field teams on active cases.

"The work is the same," Kendra said during their first official team meeting. "The stakes are higher."

"How so?"

"When you're learning, mistakes are expected. Corrected. Absorbed as lessons." Kendra's gaze was level. "When you're operational, mistakes have consequences. For clients. For colleagues. For people who trusted us to get it right."

Bella nodded. She'd already learned this during Europe. She had no intention of making those mistakes again.

---

Kendra's team was small: four analysts, two field coordinators, and a rotating roster of specialists who came and went based on case needs.

The analysts were the core.

**Maya** was the one Bella had clashed with initially—the Harvard MBA who'd called her "Addie's latest project." Over time, Bella had come to respect her: sharp, competitive, brilliant at financial forensics.

**Jordan** was quiet, almost invisible, but produced the cleanest intelligence reports Bella had ever seen. She had a background in government work—something she never discussed directly—and a knack for finding patterns that others missed.

**Priya** was the youngest, recruited straight from MIT, a genius with data visualization who could turn spreadsheets into stories that clients actually understood.

**Sam** was the oldest, a former journalist who'd spent twenty years chasing stories before burning out on media. She brought a narrative sensibility that made even dry briefings compelling.

Together, they covered every angle. Bella had to figure out where she fit.

---

Her niche emerged during a crisis.

A client's daughter had been photographed at a party with the wrong people. The photos weren't incriminating—not yet—but they were circulating, and the client's political enemies were waiting to weaponize them.

"We need to get ahead of this," Kendra said during the briefing. "Before the photos become a story."

"What's the goal?" Bella asked. "Suppression or reframing?"

"We don't suppress. That creates more problems. We need to reframe—give the media something else to focus on."

The team brainstormed. Maya suggested financial misdirection—planting a bigger story about a competitor. Jordan offered to trace the photos' origin and expose whoever was leaking. Priya started mapping the social media trajectory, predicting when and where the photos would peak.

Bella thought about the daughter.

"What does she want?" she asked.

The room went quiet.

"The daughter," Bella clarified. "We're protecting her reputation. But does she know? Does she get a say in how we do it?"

Kendra's expression shifted. "I don't know. We usually work through the client."

"Maybe we should work through her. She's twenty-two, not twelve. If she's going to be the subject of a narrative, she should help shape it."

---

They brought the daughter into the strategy session via secure video call.

Her name was Alexis. She was smart, scared, and angry—at herself, at the situation, at the adults who'd been making decisions about her life without consulting her.

"I wasn't doing anything wrong," she said, voice tight. "I was at a party. I talked to some people. Now my dad's acting like I committed a crime."

"You didn't," Bella said. "But perception isn't about truth. It's about narrative. Right now, someone else is controlling your narrative. We want to give you back that control."

"How?"

"By deciding together what story you want to tell. Not the story your father wants. Not the story the media would invent. Your story."

Alexis was quiet for a moment. Then: "I want people to know I'm not ashamed. I didn't do anything wrong, and I'm not going to hide like I did."

"That's a good starting point. Let's build from there."

---

The resolution was elegant.

Instead of suppression or misdirection, they arranged for Alexis to give an interview to a friendly journalist—carefully coached, thoroughly prepared, but in her own words. She talked about the party, the photos, and the manufactured outrage around both.

"I'm not a scandal," she said in the interview. "I'm a person. And the people trying to turn normal life into ammunition? They're the ones who should be embarrassed."

The story flipped. Instead of "politician's daughter caught in compromising photos," it became "young woman pushes back against manufactured scandal." The photos lost their power. The political enemies scrambled.

---

"That was good work," Kendra said afterward. "Changing the approach mid-crisis. Bringing the client's daughter into the process."

"I just asked a question nobody else was asking."

"That's the job. Asking the questions nobody else asks." Kendra smiled. "You found your niche."

"What's that?"

"Human connection. Maya does forensics. Jordan does intelligence. Priya does data. Sam does narrative." Kendra paused. "You do people. You understand what they need, how they feel, what they want. That's not teachable. It's rare."

Bella thought about the cafe. The bookstore. The years of reading customers, reading family, reading rooms.

Maybe it had all been preparation.
