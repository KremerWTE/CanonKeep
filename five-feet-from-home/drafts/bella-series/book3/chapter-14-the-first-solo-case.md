# Chapter 14: The First Solo Case

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Four: The New Normal

---

"This one's yours."

Kendra dropped a file on Bella's desk. It was thin—maybe twenty pages. The client's name was redacted on the cover, replaced with a code: MAGNOLIA-7.

"Fully solo?"

"You'll have backup available if things escalate, but the strategy is yours. The execution is yours." Kendra's expression was neutral, but there was something like pride in her eyes. "You're ready."

Bella opened the file.

---

The client was a corporate executive—female, forty-two, married with two children. She'd discovered her husband was having an affair. Normally that would be a matter for divorce attorneys, not BSS.

But the husband was connected. His family owned the company she ran. And the affair was with his secretary—a woman who had already started making demands.

"If I divorce him, I lose my position," the client explained during their first call. "His family will push me out. Twenty years of building this company, gone."

"And if you stay?"

"Then I stay married to a man who cheats on me while his mistress threatens to go public." Her voice was bitter. "Either way, I lose."

"Not necessarily."

---

Bella spent a week mapping the situation.

The mistress: young, ambitious, not particularly bright. She wanted money and status, not true love. The affair was leverage, and she was testing how much it was worth.

The husband: weak, entitled, accustomed to getting what he wanted without consequences. He didn't love the mistress—he loved the excitement. The novelty.

The family: wealthy, image-conscious, terrified of scandal. They'd thrown money at problems before. They'd do it again.

The client: competent, respected, angry. She'd built something real, and she wasn't willing to lose it to her husband's weakness.

"What do you want?" Bella asked her. "Really want, if you could have anything?"

The client was quiet for a long moment. Then: "I want to keep my company. I want my children protected. And I want my husband to understand that his actions have consequences."

"Do you want to stay married?"

"I don't know yet. I want options."

"Then let's build you some."

---

The strategy had three phases.

Phase One: Documentation. Bella worked with Jordan to build a comprehensive evidence file—not just the affair, but the husband's other lapses. Expense account abuse. Questionable business relationships. A pattern of behavior that painted him as a liability, not just a cheater.

Phase Two: Positioning. Through carefully placed conversations—some through POH connections, some through the client's own network—Bella shifted the narrative. The husband wasn't a victim of his wife's ambition. He was a millstone around the company's neck.

Phase Three: The offer. Not a threat—an opportunity. The husband could accept a quiet separation, a generous settlement, and a carefully managed public story about "mutual priorities." Or the board could see the documentation, and the family could face the fallout.

"You're not blackmailing him," Kendra observed during a strategy review. "You're giving him an exit ramp."

"He's going to take the exit ramp because it's the only road that doesn't end in a cliff." Bella shrugged. "People do the right thing when it's also the easy thing."

---

The husband took the deal.

Within three months, the separation was announced—amicable, mutual, about "different life directions." The client retained her position. The children were protected with ironclad custody arrangements. The mistress received a settlement (smaller than she'd hoped) and a non-disclosure agreement.

"How did you know he'd fold?" the client asked, during their final call.

"He's not a fighter. He's a coward who pretends to be a prince." Bella paused. "Cowards don't pick battles. They pick exits."

The client laughed—the first genuine laugh Bella had heard from her. "I underestimated you."

"Most people do. I'm learning to use that."

---

"Solid work," Kendra said afterward. "Clean strategy. Good execution. No collateral damage."

"I learned from watching."

"You learned from doing. That's different." Kendra leaned back. "You've got good instincts, Bella. But instincts aren't enough. You need to develop judgment—knowing when to push, when to hold, when to walk away."

"How do I develop that?"

"More cases. More mistakes. More wins." Kendra smiled slightly. "You just finished your first solo. You'll have a hundred more before you're done. Each one teaches you something."

"What did this one teach me?"

"That you can do this." Kendra's voice was matter-of-fact. "That you're not just an analyst who got promoted. You're a fixer who was born for this work. The question now is whether you believe it."

Bella thought about the client's laugh. The relief in her voice. The future she'd been able to keep.

Maybe she was starting to.
