# Chapter 29: The Miami Weekend

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Six: The Test

---

Harper invited her to Miami.

"It's not work," Harper clarified on the call. "Or mostly not work. The family's having a weekend gathering. Daniel's parents are visiting, the kids will be there, it's chaotic and wonderful. I thought you might like to see how the other half lives."

"The other half?"

"The people who try to have normal lives while doing this job." Harper's voice was dry. "It's an ongoing experiment."

---

Bella flew down on a Friday afternoon.

Miami was different from Charlotte—brighter, louder, more alive. The air itself felt different, thick with salt and heat and something almost tropical.

Harper's home was in a gated community near the water—not ostentatious, but unmistakably wealthy. The kind of house that said "we have money" without screaming about it.

"Welcome to chaos," Harper said, greeting her at the door.

---

The chaos was immediate.

Izzy—Harper's oldest, fourteen and poised beyond her years—was coordinating a game of tag that had somehow expanded to include both her siblings and three neighborhood children. Nico, at eleven, was the loudest of the group. Sofia, at eight, was the most determined, chasing after kids twice her size with single-minded focus.

"They're always like this," Harper observed, leading Bella inside. "I've given up trying to impose order. I just aim for 'no one needs stitches by bedtime.'"

"That's a reasonable goal."

"It's a hard-won wisdom."

---

Daniel was in the kitchen when they entered—tall, tanned, the kind of handsome that suggested sailing and golf and a life of comfortable prosperity.

"Bella." He shook her hand warmly. "Harper's told me a lot about you."

"All good, I hope."

"Mostly terrifying." His grin was easy. "Anyone who can keep up with Harper is impressive. Anyone who impresses Hawk is scary."

"I'm working on the scary part."

"Give it time."

---

The weekend unfolded in layers.

Saturday was beach time—the family had access to a private stretch of sand, and the kids built elaborate sandcastles while the adults talked under an umbrella. Bella learned about Daniel's shipping business, about his family's expectations, about the careful balance he and Harper maintained.

"She travels constantly," Daniel said, watching Harper wade into the water with Sofia. "And when she's home, there's always a crisis on the horizon. But we make it work."

"How?"

"By being intentional. We schedule our time the way other people schedule meetings. Date nights are sacred. Family weekends are protected." He smiled. "It's not romantic, but it's functional."

"It seems like more than functional."

"It is, now. It wasn't always." He looked at her directly. "We almost divorced twice. The first decade was brutal. But we figured it out—mostly by learning to communicate what we actually needed instead of what we thought we were supposed to need."

---

That evening, after the kids were in bed, Harper and Bella sat on the back porch.

"You see why I do it this way," Harper said, gesturing at the house, the water, the life she'd built.

"Separate base. Protected family time."

"Exactly. Charlotte is the center, but it can't be my only center. I need this—" she gestured around them—"to remember why the work matters."

"Does it ever feel like too much?"

"Every day." Harper's voice was matter-of-fact. "There's always another crisis. Always another client who needs saving. But if I don't protect this space, I'll burn out. And then I'm no good to anyone."

"How do you know when to stop?"

"I don't always. Daniel helps with that. The kids too—they have no patience for my work persona. They want Mom, not the COO." She smiled. "It's humbling. And necessary."

---

Sunday, before Bella left, she played with the kids.

Sofia had attached herself to Bella specifically—something about her seemed to fascinate the eight-year-old.

"Mommy says you're really smart," Sofia announced, during a lull in their game.

"Your mommy's being generous."

"No, she said you're smart AND you work hard. She said that's rare." Sofia tilted her head. "Are you going to have kids?"

"I don't know. Maybe someday."

"You should. You're good at playing." Sofia paused, considering. "Also, you don't get mad when I win. Some grown-ups get mad when kids win."

"Winning is the best part of games. Why would I be mad?"

Sofia beamed. "See? You should definitely have kids."

---

On the flight back to Charlotte, Bella thought about what she'd seen.

Harper's life wasn't perfect. It was messy, complicated, a constant negotiation between competing demands. But it was full. It had purpose and love and something to come home to.

*That's what I want,* Bella realized. *Not just the work. Not just the purpose. But someone to share it with. Something to protect.*

Hawk's question echoed in her mind: *What's your romantic situation?*

Maybe it was time to change her answer.
