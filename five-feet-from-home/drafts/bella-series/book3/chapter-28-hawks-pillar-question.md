# Chapter 28: Hawk's Pillar Question

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Six: The Test

---

It was a quiet February evening when Hawk finally asked the question.

They'd been at the fire pit for an hour, discussing a case Bella had just closed. The conversation had wound through strategy and ethics, through the choices she'd made and the outcomes she'd achieved. Comfortable. Familiar.

Then Hawk set down his whiskey and looked at her directly.

"What's your romantic situation?"

Bella blinked. "What?"

"Your romantic situation. Dating. Relationships." His voice was matter-of-fact. "You've built a career, a family, a purpose. But you haven't talked about that part of your life. I'm curious why."

---

She hadn't expected this.

In all their fire pit conversations, Hawk had never pressed her on romance. He'd asked about her past—the boyfriends, the girl she'd almost loved—but always in the context of understanding who she'd been. Not who she might become.

"I don't have a romantic situation," she said finally. "I'm focused on work."

"That's an evasion."

"It's the truth."

"It's a truth. Not the whole truth." Hawk leaned back. "You're thirty years old, Bella. You've got a career most people would kill for. You've got family. You've got purpose. But you don't have a pillar."

"A pillar?"

"Someone who's yours. Someone you go home to. Someone who knows you completely and chooses you anyway." His eyes were steady. "Every great leader needs a pillar. Someone who grounds them when the work gets too heavy."

---

Bella stared into the flames.

"I've tried relationships," she said slowly. "They don't work out."

"Because you end them before they can fail."

It wasn't a question. It was an observation, and it landed with uncomfortable accuracy.

"I've been focused on building myself. On becoming someone worth choosing." She paused. "Maybe once I'm complete, I'll think about that part."

"You'll never be complete." Hawk's voice was gentle. "None of us are. That's not how it works. You build yourself alongside someone, not before them."

"What if I choose wrong?"

"Then you learn. You grow. You try again." He shrugged. "Love isn't about avoiding mistakes. It's about finding someone whose mistakes you can live with. Someone who can live with yours."

---

"I'm scared."

The admission came out before she could stop it.

"Of what?"

"Of being vulnerable. Of letting someone see all of me—the doubt, the fear, the parts I haven't fixed yet." She wrapped her arms around herself. "What if they realize I'm not worth the trouble?"

"Then they're not the right person." Hawk's voice was certain. "The right person doesn't see your struggles as trouble. They see them as part of who you are. They love the whole package, not the highlight reel."

"You're making it sound simple."

"It's not simple. It's hard as hell." He smiled slightly. "But it's worth it. I've been married to Addie for decades. She's seen me at my worst—things I don't talk about, things that would break most people. And she's still here. That's what a pillar gives you."

---

"I don't know how to start."

"You start by being open." Hawk passed her the whiskey. "Not actively looking—that rarely works. Just... open. To the possibility. To the people who might fit."

"What if they're not who I expect?"

"They probably won't be." His eyes were knowing. "The person you need isn't always the person you picture. Sometimes they come from places you didn't think to look."

Bella thought about her past—the boyfriend she'd left before he could leave her, the girl she'd never pursued, the patterns of preemptive rejection that had defined her romantic life.

Maybe it was time to try something different.

"I'll think about it," she said.

"That's all I'm asking." Hawk raised his glass. "To pillars. The ones we find, and the ones we become."

---

That night, in her journal, Bella wrote:

*Hawk asked about my romantic life. I didn't have an answer.*

*He says I need a pillar. Someone who grounds me. Someone who sees all of me and stays anyway.*

*I've spent so long building myself alone. Learning to believe I'm worth something. But maybe being alone isn't the finish line. Maybe it's just a step toward finding someone to share it with.*

*The idea terrifies me.*

*But maybe that's exactly why I should try.*
