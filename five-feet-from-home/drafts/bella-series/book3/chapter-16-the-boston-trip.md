# Chapter 16: The Boston Trip

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Four: The New Normal

---

She was in Boston for a client meeting when Vee's text came.

**Vee:** Heard you're in town. Dinner?

Bella stared at her phone. She hadn't seen Vee in person since before joining BSS—three years of sporadic texts and the occasional phone call, but no face-to-face contact.

**Bella:** I have time tonight. Where?

**Vee:** The usual place. 7?

---

The usual place was a restaurant near Beacon Hill that Vee's family had been going to for generations. White tablecloths. Quiet efficiency. The kind of establishment where the staff knew your preferences before you asked.

Vee was already there when Bella arrived.

She looked the same—tall, elegant, effortlessly polished in a camel coat and silk blouse. But something in her eyes was different. Tired, maybe. Or just older.

"Look at you." Vee rose to embrace her. "You look..."

"Different?"

"Confident." Vee stepped back, assessing. "You wear it well."

---

They ordered—Vee's usual, Bella's compromise between what she wanted and what was expected—and caught up on the surface details. Vee's work at her family's foundation. Bella's "consulting" (the sanitized version). The rhythms of their separate lives.

But Bella could tell Vee was circling something.

"The pendant," Vee said finally. "In the photos you sent. I recognized the symbol."

"I know."

"My aunt has one. My grandmother had one." Vee's voice was careful. "The Wives Club isn't something you stumble into."

"I didn't stumble. I was chosen."

"By whom?"

Bella considered how much to say. "By people who saw something in me. People who became family."

---

Vee was quiet for a long moment.

"I've known about that world my whole life," she said finally. "It was always there—hovering at the edge of family dinners, whispered about at holidays. My aunt's friends who seemed to control things without anyone noticing."

"And?"

"And I was never asked. Not formally." Vee's smile was brittle. "I assumed it would come eventually. The right time. The right introduction. But it never did."

Bella felt the weight of what Vee wasn't saying.

"Why do you think—"

"I don't know." Vee cut her off. "Maybe I wasn't the right fit. Maybe my family's connections weren't as strong as I thought. Maybe I did something wrong without knowing it." She met Bella's eyes. "Or maybe I just wasn't what they were looking for."

---

"Vee—"

"Don't." Vee held up a hand. "I'm not looking for pity. Or an explanation. I just..." She took a breath. "I spent years assuming I was the one who had it figured out. You were always trying so hard, always worried you didn't belong. And I was coasting on certainty."

"I still don't know if I belong."

"But you're there. You're in the room." Vee's voice was soft. "And I'm still outside, looking at the light through the windows."

Bella didn't know what to say. The reversal was dizzying—Vee, who had always seemed to have everything, envying Bella for something she'd accidentally achieved.

"You could still—"

"I don't think so." Vee shook her head. "These things happen or they don't. You can't campaign for them." She smiled, and it was almost genuine. "Besides, I have my own thing. The foundation work. My family's legacy. It's not the same, but it's mine."

"Is it enough?"

"Most days." Vee picked up her wine glass. "Let's talk about something else. Tell me about Charlotte. Tell me about your life. The version you can share."

---

They talked until the restaurant closed.

Bella told her about the compound, edited for secrecy. About finding family in unexpected places. About the fire pit lessons and the chess metaphors and the way Hawk called her "daughter."

"That's what I always wanted," Vee admitted, when Bella finished. "Not the secret society part. The belonging part. The being seen part."

"You have that. Your family—"

"My family sees what they expect to see. The daughter who fits the mold. The legacy carrier." Vee's eyes were distant. "Nobody asks me how I'm doing. They assume they know."

Bella thought about the fire pit rules. Two questions. Real answers.

"How are you doing, Vee?"

Vee looked startled. Then her eyes grew bright.

"I'm tired," she said quietly. "I'm lonely. I'm thirty years old and I feel like I've been performing my whole life without anyone noticing it was a performance."

"I see it."

"I know." Vee smiled, and this time it reached her eyes. "That's why I wanted to have dinner."

---

Walking back to her hotel that night, Bella thought about the girl she'd been at Boston College. Watching Vee. Wanting what Vee had. Never imagining she'd end up somewhere Vee couldn't follow.

The world was strange that way.

You spent years climbing toward a light you thought was unreachable, only to discover that the person you thought was already standing in it was actually watching you from below.
