# Chapter 1d: The Book

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part One: The Ceremony

---

The third gift came when everyone else had gone.

The party had wound down—champagne glasses collected, guests departing with hugs and promises to call. Bella was gathering her things, still wearing the pendant, still floating on the unreality of the evening.

That's when Hawk appeared at her elbow.

"One more thing," he said quietly. "Not for the crowd."

Addie was already waiting in a small sitting room off the main hall. Two armchairs by a fireplace. A bottle of something amber. Three glasses, already poured.

"Sit," Addie said. It wasn't a request.

Bella sat.

---

The book was leather-bound.

Rich brown leather, worn soft at the edges, with gilded lettering on the spine: *My Fair Lady*.

"Open it," Hawk said.

Bella turned to the first page. A title page, elegant and simple. The story she knew—of Eliza Doolittle, of transformation, of becoming.

But that wasn't what they wanted her to see.

"The back cover," Addie prompted. "Inside."

Bella flipped to the end. And stopped breathing.

---

The signatures were simple.

*"Mom" — Addie*

*"Dad" — Hawk*

Below them, in Hawk's sharp handwriting:

*"You've always been on the board. Now you know it."*

---

"I don't understand," Bella whispered.

But she did. She understood exactly.

"The fire pit ritual," Hawk said. "How are you doing. How can I support you. That wasn't just mentorship. That was audition. Every time we sat together, I was watching to see if you were who I thought you were."

"And?"

"You were. More." His voice was rougher than usual. "I told you once that in my tradition, family is chosen. That when it's chosen, it's more binding than blood."

Bella nodded slowly.

"This is the choosing," Hawk said. "Public, in the check and the pendant. Private, in this book. We're not just your mentors, Bella. We're your parents. If you'll have us."

---

Addie spoke then, softer than Bella had ever heard her.

"When I was your age, I was lost. Talented but directionless. Capable but unconvinced. Someone saw me—really saw me—and chose to invest in what I could become." She reached over and squeezed Bella's hand. "Now I see you the same way. And I'm not going to let you stay lost."

"But I'm not—I don't—" Bella struggled to form words. "I have parents. My actual parents."

"Of course you do. And they love you, and you love them, and nothing changes that." Addie's eyes were bright. "But love isn't finite. You can have more than one mother, more than one father. You can have people who chose you, specifically, because of who you are."

---

Hawk stood and moved to crouch in front of Bella's chair, putting himself at her eye level.

"I need you to hear something," he said. "You're not being made into someone new. The whole point of this story—" he tapped the book in her lap "—is that Eliza was always capable. She just needed someone to believe in her enough to show her."

"Hawk—"

"You've always been on the board," he repeated. "The pendant isn't making you part of the Club. The check isn't creating your value. This book isn't making us your family. All of it is just... making visible what was already true."

Bella's tears came then—silent, spilling over without her permission.

"I don't know how to be a daughter," she said. "Not like this."

"You'll learn. Same way you've learned everything else." Hawk smiled. "How are you doing, daughter?"

The question broke her open.

"Overwhelmed," she admitted. "Grateful. Terrified. Happier than I've ever been."

"And how can I support you?"

She clutched the book tighter. "Just... be here. Keep being here."

"That's the easiest request you've ever made." Hawk stood and pulled her into a hug—solid and certain, the embrace of a father who meant what he said. "I told you once: you're stuck with me until you die. I meant it."

---

Later, alone in her apartment, Bella sat on her bed with the book in her lap.

She traced the signatures with her fingertips. The leather was warm, soft, like something that had been waiting for her.

*Mom.*

*Dad.*

Words she'd used for other people her whole life. Words that now belonged to Addie and Hawk too. Not replacing anyone. Just... expanding.

She thought of her biological parents—the cafe, the bookstore, the practical love they'd given her in their practical ways. She loved them. That would never change.

But this was different.

This was being chosen. Deliberately, specifically, without any obligation of blood or history.

*You've always been on the board. Now you know it.*

Bella held the book close and let herself believe it.
