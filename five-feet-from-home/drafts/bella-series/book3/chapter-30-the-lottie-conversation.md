# Chapter 30: The Lottie Conversation

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Six: The Test

---

Lottie found her at a Club event.

Bella had been working the room, making connections, when a hand touched her elbow. She turned to find the woman she'd met at her first Wives Club gathering—round face, genuine smile, the one who'd called herself the "designated ugly fat friend."

"You looked like you needed a break," Lottie said. "I'm an expert at identifying escape opportunities."

---

They retreated to a quiet corner, away from the main crowd.

"How are you settling in?" Lottie asked. "For real, not the polite version."

"I'm... finding my footing." Bella surprised herself with honesty. "Some days I feel like I belong. Other days I still feel like an imposter."

"That never fully goes away." Lottie's voice was knowing. "I've been doing this for six years. I still have moments where I look around and think, 'How did I get here?'"

"Really?"

"Really. We all do." Lottie gestured at the room. "See Catherine over there? Old money, born into this world, and she still gets nervous before major events. Elena built a literal empire, and she told me last week she worries about being exposed as a fraud. It's universal."

---

"How do you handle it?"

Lottie considered the question. "I stopped trying to be what I thought they wanted. Started being what I actually am."

"Which is?"

"The logistics person. The one who makes seating charts sing and event timelines dance." Lottie grinned. "It's not glamorous. Nobody writes articles about perfect table assignments. But it's essential. And it's mine."

"You found your niche."

"I claimed my niche." Lottie's emphasis was deliberate. "There's a difference. Finding suggests it was waiting for you. Claiming means you decided what you're good at and refused to apologize for it."

---

Bella thought about her own trajectory.

The "people person" label Kendra had given her. The human-centered crisis management Harper had observed. The way she connected with clients not through strategy alone, but through understanding what they actually needed.

"I think I'm still figuring out what to claim."

"That's okay. It takes time." Lottie sipped her wine. "But here's what I've learned: the women who thrive here aren't the ones who try to be everything. They're the ones who become indispensable at one thing. The irreplaceable specialists."

"What about the generalists? The ones who do a little of everything?"

"They're valuable. But they're replaceable." Lottie's voice wasn't unkind. "You can always find someone who does a little of everything. What's hard to find is someone who does one thing better than anyone else."

---

"Can I ask you something personal?"

Lottie raised an eyebrow. "Sure."

"The way you introduced yourself—the DUFF thing. Does that... hurt?"

Lottie laughed—a genuine, warm sound. "It used to. God, it used to kill me. Being the 'ugly friend' in every social situation. Watching prettier women get attention I wanted."

"What changed?"

"I stopped competing in a game I couldn't win." Lottie's eyes were soft. "I'm never going to be the stunning one. The head-turner. That's not my superpower. But I can be the indispensable one. The essential one. The one people need when everything's falling apart."

"That sounds lonely."

"Sometimes. But it's also powerful." Lottie smiled. "When a gala is about to crash and burn, they don't call the pretty ones. They call me. And that's its own kind of beautiful."

---

They talked for another hour—about the Club, about the work, about the strange parallel lives they led.

Lottie, Bella discovered, was married to a quiet professor who had no idea about the true scope of her activities. "He thinks I do corporate event planning. Which is technically true. Just... incomplete."

"How do you manage the secrecy?"

"By remembering it protects him. If he knew everything, he'd worry constantly. This way, he gets to believe I have a normal job. He gets to have a normal life." Lottie shrugged. "It's a kind of gift. A weird one, but a gift."

---

As the evening wound down, Lottie pressed a card into Bella's hand.

"Call me if you need anything. A friend. A sanity check. Someone who understands what this world is really like."

"Thank you."

"We look out for each other here. That's how it works." Lottie's smile was warm. "You're one of us now, Bella. That means you're never as alone as you feel."

Walking away, Bella turned the card over in her fingers.

*Charlotte "Lottie" Hayes*
*Strategic Logistics*
*"Making the impossible merely improbable"*

She smiled. She'd found an ally. Maybe even a friend.

In a world of beautiful, dangerous, complicated women, that felt like a victory worth celebrating.
