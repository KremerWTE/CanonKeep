# Chapter 24: Selene at the Edge

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Five: Becoming

---

Bella had heard stories about Selene.

The dangerous beauty. The brilliant mind. The something broken that lurked behind her stunning eyes. Grace called her "scary." Harper called her "complicated." Addie just shook her head and changed the subject.

She'd seen Selene at Club events—always at the edge, always watching, always slightly removed from the group. Beautiful in a way that felt almost aggressive, like her face itself was a challenge.

She'd never spoken to her directly.

Until now.

---

It was a POH reception—smaller than the galas, more intimate. Bella was working the room, making connections, when she felt someone's gaze on her back.

Selene stood near the windows, champagne untouched in her hand.

Their eyes met.

"You're the new one," Selene said, not moving from her spot. It wasn't a question.

"I'm Bella."

"I know who you are. Addie's project. Hawk's daughter." Selene's voice was low, melodic. "The stray they brought in from Boston."

"That's one way to put it."

"It's accurate." Selene smiled, but it didn't reach her eyes. "Don't worry. I'm not being cruel. I was a stray once too."

---

Bella approached, despite every instinct telling her to walk away.

"What happened?"

"To me?" Selene laughed. "You don't want to know. It's not an inspirational story."

"Try me."

Selene studied her for a long moment. Then, as if making a decision, she turned to face the window.

"I was like you. Bright. Eager. Full of potential." Her voice was flat now, stripped of performance. "They found me. Mentored me. Made me feel like I belonged."

"And then?"

"And then I made a mistake." Selene's reflection was visible in the glass—the perfect profile, the dark hair, the shadows under her eyes. "A big one. The kind that costs you everything you built."

"What kind of mistake?"

"The kind I'm not going to tell you about." She turned back, meeting Bella's gaze. "But I'll tell you this: when you fail here, you don't get a second chance. You get tolerance. You get the edge of the room. You get to watch everyone else live the life you were supposed to have."

---

Bella felt the weight of the warning.

"Why are you telling me this?"

"Because no one told me." Selene's smile was bitter. "They let me believe I was invincible. That my potential would protect me from consequences. That being special was enough."

"And it wasn't?"

"Being special just means falling further." Selene finished her champagne in one long swallow. "You're good at this, Bella. I can see that. You have instincts, relationships, the trust of people who matter. But don't get complacent. Don't assume your position is secure."

"I don't assume anything."

"You will eventually. Success breeds complacency. That's when you become vulnerable." Selene set down her empty glass. "I'm not trying to scare you. I'm trying to prepare you. Because I was you, once. And I wish someone had given me this warning."

---

After the reception, Bella found Harper in the hallway.

"I talked to Selene."

Harper's expression flickered. "I noticed."

"What happened to her? Really?"

Harper was quiet for a moment, calculating what to share. "She was brilliant. One of the best fixers we had. But she got too close to a client. Emotionally involved. And when the situation went sideways, she made choices that put everyone at risk."

"People got hurt?"

"People could have died." Harper's voice was matter-of-fact. "They didn't, thanks to intervention from others. But the damage was done. Trust was broken. And in this world, trust is everything."

"So she's being punished."

"She's being... contained. She still has value. She still contributes. But she'll never be at the center again. She'll never have the responsibility she once had." Harper met Bella's eyes. "It's not cruel. It's just... what happens when you cross certain lines."

---

That night, Bella wrote in her journal.

*I met Selene today. She's everything they say—dangerous, beautiful, broken. But she's also a warning. A mirror of what I could become if I lose my way.*

*She wasn't always at the edge. She was at the center once. She was trusted, valued, important. And then she made one mistake—one choice that seemed right at the time—and everything fell apart.*

*I don't know what that mistake was. I'm not sure I want to know. But I understand the lesson: this life has costs. The higher you rise, the further you can fall. And the people here don't forget.*

*I need to remember that. Even when things are good. Especially when things are good.*

---

The next time she saw Selene, at a different event, they exchanged nods across the room.

Not friendship. Not even rapport. But something like recognition—an acknowledgment between two women who understood things others didn't.

Bella hoped she'd never become what Selene had become.

But she was grateful for the warning.
