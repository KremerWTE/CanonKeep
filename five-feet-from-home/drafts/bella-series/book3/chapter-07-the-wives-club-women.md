# Chapter 7: The Wives Club Women

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Two: Integration

---

Bella learned the hierarchy through observation.

The Wives Club wasn't formal—there were no officers, no meetings, no Robert's Rules of Order. But there was structure. Invisible, ancient, absolutely real.

---

**The Vestals** were the inner circle.

Addie. Elena. Harper. Women who'd been in the Club for decades, who'd shaped its direction, who were consulted on matters that affected the whole network.

"We don't vote," Addie explained once. "We discuss until consensus emerges. If consensus can't emerge, we table the issue."

"What if there's urgency?"

"Then whoever has the most relevant expertise takes point, and everyone else supports." Addie smiled. "It works because we trust each other. When trust breaks, everything breaks."

The Vestals dressed like women who didn't need to impress anyone. Classic pieces, expensive but understated. Their jewelry was real but minimal. Their makeup enhanced rather than transformed. They looked like exactly what they were: powerful women who had nothing left to prove.

---

**The Owls** were the high-society tier.

Women connected to old money, political families, cultural institutions. They provided social access—introductions, invitations, the soft power of knowing the right people.

Bella met several at a Maison Aurelia event Elena was hosting. They moved through the room like birds of paradise—colorful, ornamental, surprisingly sharp-eyed.

"You're Addie's protégée," one said—a silver-haired woman whose name Bella would later learn was Catherine Aldridge. Old Boston family, married to a shipping magnate. "She's spoken of you."

"Positively, I hope."

"She doesn't waste words on people who disappoint her." Catherine's smile was knowing. "Welcome to the circle, dear. It's smaller than it looks from the outside."

The Owls dressed for events like they were attending them, even when they were hosting. Gowns and jewels, hair professionally styled, the full production of wealth on display. But beneath the performance, Bella sensed the same sharpness she recognized in the Vestals.

These weren't ornaments. These were observers. Every smile catalogued information. Every conversation gathered intelligence.

---

**The Shieldmaidens** were the operators' wives.

Women married to men like Hawk—former military, private security, the human infrastructure of organizations like BSS. They understood crisis in their bones, had lived through deployments and dangerous assignments, had learned to keep families together while their husbands faced the unthinkable.

Kendra was technically a Shieldmaiden, though her role at BSS blurred the lines. She introduced Bella to others—quiet women with steel in their eyes and practical wardrobes that could transition from school pickup to crisis briefing without changing clothes.

"The Shieldmaidens are the backbone," Kendra said. "The Vestals plan. The Owls connect. We execute. When something needs to actually happen—logistics, coordination, the unglamorous work—that's us."

Bella felt at home with the Shieldmaidens in a way she didn't quite with the others. They reminded her of herself—capable women who got things done without needing recognition.

---

**The newer members** were harder to categorize.

Women like Bella herself, still finding their place, still proving themselves. Some came through sorority connections, others through professional networks, a few through marriages that brought them into the orbit of existing members.

One of them was a woman named Charlotte—"Lottie"—who'd been brought in through Elena's events company. She wasn't beautiful in the way the other women were beautiful. She was, as she'd cheerfully announced to Bella, "the DUFF of the group."

"The what?"

"Designated Ugly Fat Friend. It's a joke, but also not." Lottie had a round face and a genuine smile that made her instantly likable. "I'm not the one people notice at galas. I'm the one who makes sure the galas happen."

"What do you do?"

"Seating charts, mostly. Logistics. The invisible work." Lottie's eyes sparkled. "Elena calls me her secret weapon. I can tell you where every person in this room should sit to maximize productive conversation and minimize drama."

"That's... remarkable."

"It's a superpower. Useless in most contexts, invaluable here."

---

**Selene** was a special case.

She was technically a member—wore the symbol, attended events, had all the access. But she existed at the edges, a cautionary tale that no one talked about directly.

"She's brilliant," Harper said when Bella asked. "Also unstable. The kind of beautiful that makes people stupid, combined with the kind of chaos that makes everyone around her exhausted."

"Is she dangerous?"

"Only to herself, usually." Harper's expression was complicated. "She used to be at the center of things. Then there was an incident. Now she's... tolerated. Loved, in a complicated way. But not trusted with anything important."

Bella saw Selene occasionally—at events, at the compound, always on the periphery. She was stunning in a way that made it hard to look away: dark hair, dramatic features, the kind of beauty that launched ships and started wars.

But there was something broken in her eyes. Something that made Bella want to help and step back in equal measure.

Grace had said Selene was "scary." Bella was starting to understand why.

---

The hierarchy wasn't about power, Bella realized.

It was about trust.

The Vestals were trusted with decisions. The Owls were trusted with connections. The Shieldmaidens were trusted with execution. The newer members were trusted with potential.

And trust, once earned, had to be constantly maintained.

"The Club has survived for generations," Addie told her, "because we understand that we're only as strong as our weakest member. We don't abandon people. We don't exclude people without cause. But we also don't pretend that everyone is equal in every way."

"That sounds hard to navigate."

"It is. But it's also what makes us effective." Addie's smile was warm. "You'll find your place. You're already finding it."
