# Chapter 15: Grace and the Diorama

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Four: The New Normal

---

The text came at 7 PM on a Thursday.

**Grace:** Aunt Bella can you help me with something

Bella smiled at her phone. She'd given Grace her number months ago, told her she could text anytime. This was the first time Grace had taken her up on it.

**Bella:** Of course. What's up?

**Grace:** It's a school project and it's due Monday and I don't know what I'm doing

**Bella:** Tell me about it

---

The project was a diorama.

Grace had to build a 3D representation of a historical event for her social studies class. She'd chosen the Boston Tea Party—ambitious for a fifth-grader, but Grace had never been one for easy choices.

"Mom said she'd help but she's been really busy," Grace explained when Bella arrived at the compound's main house. "And Dad's traveling, and Aunt Addie had meetings all day, and—"

"Hey." Bella knelt to meet Grace's eyes. "I'm here now. Let's figure this out together."

Grace's shoulders relaxed slightly. "Really?"

"Really. Show me what you've got."

---

What Grace had was a cardboard box, some paint, and approximately zero plan.

"I know what happened," Grace said, spreading her research notes across the kitchen table. "The colonists dressed up as Mohawk Indians and threw tea into the harbor because of taxation without representation. But I don't know how to make it look like anything."

"Okay. Let's break it down." Bella pulled out a notepad. "What are the key elements? What does someone need to see to understand the story?"

Grace thought. "The ship. The harbor. The colonists. The tea chests."

"Good. What else?"

"The dock? The water?"

"Perfect. Now—what's the most important part? If someone only looked at your diorama for five seconds, what should they remember?"

Grace's eyes narrowed. "The tea being thrown in. That's the whole point."

"Exactly. So that's our focal point. Everything else supports that moment."

---

They worked for three hours.

Bella showed Grace how to measure and cut—careful, precise, thinking ahead. They built a ship from a smaller box, painted blue paper for the water, fashioned tiny tea chests from folded cardboard.

"The colonists are hard," Grace said, frustrated. "I can't make them look like people."

"Then don't. Use silhouettes instead." Bella demonstrated, cutting simple shapes from black paper. "Sometimes suggestion is more powerful than detail. Your viewer's brain fills in what's missing."

"Like how Aunt Addie always says 'less is more' about outfits?"

Bella laughed. "Exactly like that."

---

Around 9 PM, Addie appeared in the kitchen doorway.

"I was looking for—" She stopped, taking in the scene. The table covered in supplies. Grace's tongue poking out in concentration. Bella guiding her hands as she glued a silhouette in place.

"Aunt Bella's helping me with my project," Grace said without looking up. "She's really good at this."

Addie's expression softened. "I can see that."

"I texted her. She came right away." Now Grace did look up. "She always comes when I ask."

Something passed between Addie and Bella—a recognition, a confirmation. Bella felt the weight of it settle in her chest.

"I'll leave you to it," Addie said. "Grace, bedtime in thirty minutes."

"Okay, Mom."

---

With the diorama mostly complete, they cleaned up together.

"Aunt Bella?" Grace's voice was hesitant. "Can I ask you something?"

"Always."

"Why do you always come when I text?"

Bella stopped wiping down the table. She thought about the question—really thought about it.

"Because when I was your age, I wanted someone who would come when I called. Someone who made me feel like I mattered enough to show up for." She met Grace's eyes. "I want you to know that you matter. That you're worth showing up for."

Grace was quiet for a moment. Then, with the devastating honesty of a child: "Sometimes I feel invisible. Like people are too busy to see me."

"I know that feeling."

"You do?"

"I felt it for a long time. Sometimes I still do." Bella sat down, bringing herself to Grace's level. "But here's what I've learned: being invisible isn't the same as not mattering. Sometimes the most important people are the ones nobody notices—until everything falls apart without them."

Grace considered this. "Like how the diorama wouldn't work without the dock? It's not the exciting part, but everything stands on it?"

"Exactly like that." Bella smiled. "You're not invisible, Grace. You're foundational. The people who matter—they see you."

Grace nodded slowly. Then she hugged Bella—fierce and quick and genuine.

"Thanks for helping with my diorama."

"Thanks for trusting me to help."

---

Later, walking back to her room in the compound, Bella passed Addie in the hallway.

"She got an A," Addie said, without preamble.

"The diorama?"

"No. The more important test." Addie's eyes were warm. "She trusts you. That's not something she gives easily."

"Neither did I, once."

"I remember." Addie touched her arm briefly. "You're good for her, Bella. You understand something about her that most adults miss."

"What's that?"

"That being seen is more important than being helped. You don't just assist her—you witness her." Addie smiled. "That's a gift."
