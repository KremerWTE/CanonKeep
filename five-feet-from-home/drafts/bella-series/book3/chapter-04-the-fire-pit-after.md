# Chapter 4: The Fire Pit After

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Two: After

---

The next day, the compound hummed with its usual rhythm.

But Bella felt different. Raw. Like something had been scraped away and the new skin underneath hadn't hardened yet.

She'd tried to work—analyst reports, case files, the familiar rhythms of productivity. But her focus kept drifting to the book in her room, the pendant around her neck, the weight of everything that had shifted overnight.

Late afternoon, she found herself walking toward the fire pit without making a conscious decision to go there.

Hawk was already waiting. Whiskey poured. Cigar lit. The flames catching the first hints of evening light.

He didn't ask why she'd come. Just nodded toward the empty chair.

She sat.

"I know you were in the gym last night," Hawk said. Not accusatory. Just present.

Bella nodded, not trusting her voice.

"How are you doing?"

The question was the same as always. But tonight, for the first time, she gave an honest answer.

"Scared," she said. "Grateful. Confused. Like I don't know how to hold all of it at once."

Hawk didn't rush to fill the silence. He just watched the fire.

"Let me tell you something," he said finally. "Every person I've ever watched grow into who they're meant to be—every one of them hit this moment. The moment where the thing they wanted finally arrives, and instead of joy, they feel terror."

He looked at her. "You know why?"

Bella shook her head.

"Because getting what you want means you can lose it. And for people like you, Bella—people who've spent their whole lives convinced they don't deserve anything—losing it feels inevitable. So you armor up before it happens. You try to protect yourself from the grief you're sure is coming."

The words hit her like a wave. She'd never heard it articulated so clearly.

"But here's the thing," Hawk continued. "That armor? It doesn't protect you. It just keeps you from feeling the good parts too."

He leaned forward. "I'm not going anywhere. Addie's not going anywhere. The pendant, the book, the keys—those aren't temporary. They're not going to be taken back. You belong to this family now, and families don't quit on each other."

Bella's eyes stung. "How do I believe that?"

"Practice," Hawk said simply. "Every time the doubt comes, you tell it to shut up. Every time you feel like running, you stay. Eventually, the believing gets easier. But it takes time."

He raised his glass. "And how can I support you?"

Bella thought for a long moment. Then: "Help me clear a path to get home. To see my parents. And help me believe I'm not letting anyone down by needing that."

Hawk smiled—the quiet, proud smile she was learning to treasure.

"Already done," he said. "Next weekend. I'll make sure you've got clear days. And if you need tickets, I'll pay for them myself."

Bella blinked. "You don't have to—"

"I know I don't have to. But that's what fathers do. They clear paths. They cover costs. They make sure their daughters know they're supported."

He clinked his glass against hers. "Welcome to the family, Bella. Now drink your whiskey and watch Ranger TV."

She laughed—actually laughed—and for the first time, the joy didn't feel borrowed.
