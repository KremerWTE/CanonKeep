# Chapter 31: Spring at the Ranch

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Seven: Transition

---

The invitation came through BSS channels.

A veteran support organization was expanding their program—therapeutic ranch work for former service members dealing with PTSD and transition challenges. They wanted BSS's advice on security protocols, and they specifically requested someone with both operational experience and emotional intelligence.

"This is right up your alley," Kendra said, handing her the file. "The founder is former Agency. She's built something impressive. You'll like her."

Bella looked at the location. "This is near Denver."

"Is that a problem?"

"No. I just..." She paused. "I've never been to Colorado."

---

The flight was smooth.

Bella spent it reading about the organization—Second Chance Ranch, founded five years ago by a woman named Amanda "Mandy" Chen. Former intelligence operative. Left the Agency after a breakdown. Rebuilt her life through land and horses and purpose.

The file included a photograph. Steel-green eyes. Dark hair. A face that managed to be both hard and beautiful.

Bella felt a strange flutter in her chest.

*You're being ridiculous,* she told herself. *This is a work assignment.*

---

The ranch was stunning.

Rolling hills. Mountain backdrop. Horses grazing in paddocks that seemed to go on forever. The air was thin and clean, different from anything Bella had ever breathed.

Mandy met her at the main gate.

"Bella Rossi?" Her voice was low, confident. "I'm Mandy. Thanks for making the trip."

"Thanks for having me."

They shook hands. Mandy's grip was firm, her eyes assessing. Bella felt herself being evaluated and tried not to squirm under the scrutiny.

"BSS sent me your file," Mandy said. "Impressive trajectory."

"They sent me yours too. Impressive doesn't cover it."

Mandy's smile was brief but genuine. "Come on. I'll show you around."

---

The tour took most of the afternoon.

Mandy explained the program—how veterans came for weeks or months at a time, working with horses, learning to regulate their nervous systems through animal contact, building skills they could take back to civilian life.

"The work is simple," she said, as they walked past a training paddock. "But simple isn't easy. These men and women have been through hell. Some of them don't know how to exist without a mission. We give them one—care for the horses, maintain the property, help each other heal."

"It sounds powerful."

"It is." Mandy's eyes were distant for a moment. "I needed something like this when I left the Agency. It didn't exist, so I built it."

"Why did you leave?"

The question hung in the air. Bella immediately regretted asking—too personal, too soon.

But Mandy answered anyway.

"I broke. Completely. Spent months in my shower crying until the water went cold, unable to feel anything but numbness and pain." Her voice was matter-of-fact. "The work I did... it takes a toll. I ignored the toll until it collected all at once."

"I'm sorry."

"Don't be. It led me here." Mandy gestured at the ranch. "Sometimes breaking is the only way to rebuild into something better."

---

The security consultation took two days.

Bella reviewed their protocols, identified vulnerabilities, suggested improvements. Standard work, nothing complicated. But she found herself extending conversations, asking questions that weren't strictly necessary, looking for reasons to stay.

Mandy noticed.

"You're not in a hurry to get back to Charlotte," she observed, on the second evening.

"Is that obvious?"

"I'm trained to read people." Mandy's smile was knowing. "This place has that effect. People come for business and realize they need something else."

"What do they need?"

"Depends on the person." Mandy studied her. "What do you need, Bella Rossi?"

---

Bella didn't have an answer.

But she thought about the question on the flight home. And for days afterward. And in the quiet moments between crises and assignments.

What did she need?

Hawk's pillar question. Harper's work-life balance. The feeling at the ranch—like time moved differently there, like the noise in her head finally went quiet.

*Maybe that's what I need,* she thought. *Quiet. Space. Someone who understands what it's like to break and rebuild.*

The thought surprised her.

And then it scared her.

And then, slowly, it started to feel like the beginning of something important.
