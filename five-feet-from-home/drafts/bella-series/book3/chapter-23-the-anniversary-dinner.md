# Chapter 23: The Anniversary Dinner

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Five: Becoming

---

One year.

One year since the engagement dinner. One year since the three gifts. One year since Bella's life had changed in ways she was still processing.

Addie marked the anniversary quietly—a private dinner, just the four of them: Addie, Hawk, Bella, and Grace.

"We don't make a big deal of it," Addie explained. "But we wanted to acknowledge the milestone."

---

The dinner was at the main house, in the formal dining room that was usually reserved for significant occasions.

**[OUTFIT DETAILS - ANNIVERSARY DINNER]**

Bella wore a deep blue wrap dress—elegant but not ostentatious. The pendant at her throat, as always. Her hair was down, curled softly in a way she'd learned from watching Elena.

Addie was in emerald green—her signature color, a silk blouse and tailored slacks that managed to be both casual and impeccable.

Hawk wore civilian clothes instead of his usual tactical-adjacent attire—a button-down shirt, dark jeans, the kind of effort that signaled the occasion's importance.

Grace had chosen her outfit herself: a sundress with a cardigan, trying to look grown-up. Bella's heart swelled at the sight of her.

---

The meal was simple—Hawk's cooking, which meant excellent meat and straightforward sides.

"I remember the first time you sat at this table," Addie said, as they ate. "You looked terrified."

"I was terrified."

"And now?"

Bella considered the question. "I'm still scared sometimes. Of messing up. Of not being good enough. But I know this is where I belong. That's different from before."

"What changed?"

"You showed me who I could be. And you gave me the chance to become her."

---

After dinner, Grace demanded a toast.

"It's important," she insisted. "Aunt Bella's been here a whole year. That deserves a toast."

Hawk poured wine for the adults, sparkling cider for Grace. They raised their glasses.

"To family," Addie said. "Chosen and given. To the ones who find their way home."

"To family," they echoed.

Grace clinked her glass enthusiastically. "And to Aunt Bella for helping me with my diorama. Which got an A. Which I've mentioned before."

"Only about fifty times," Bella said.

"It's an important detail."

---

After Grace went to bed, the adults lingered in the living room.

The conversation drifted to lighter topics—travel plans, upcoming events, the ordinary business of life. But eventually Hawk steered it somewhere deeper.

"Where do you see yourself in five years?" he asked Bella.

"I don't know. That's honest." She curled into the corner of the couch. "A year ago, I couldn't see past the next day. Now I can imagine a future. But I don't have specifics."

"No goals? No vision?"

"I have questions more than goals." She thought about how to articulate it. "I want to know what kind of impact I can have. I want to understand what I'm building, and why. I want..." She trailed off.

"What?"

"I want to matter. In a way that lasts."

Hawk nodded slowly. "That's a good want. More specific than most people manage."

"But it's not a plan."

"Plans are overrated." Addie interjected, surprising them both. "I never planned to build what we've built. I followed instincts. I seized opportunities. The plan emerged from the doing, not the other way around."

"You're saying I should just... keep doing what I'm doing?"

"I'm saying you should trust yourself. You have good instincts, Bella. Better than you realize." Addie's smile was warm. "The specifics will come. Your job right now is to keep growing. Keep learning. Keep being open to what emerges."

---

Later, at the fire pit, Bella and Hawk sat alone.

"One year," he said. "Seems longer."

"It does."

"You've changed more than you realize." He passed her the whiskey. "The woman who walked into that engagement dinner—she would barely recognize you now."

"Is that good?"

"Growth is always good, if it's in the right direction." He studied her face. "You're stronger. More confident. More comfortable in your own skin. Those are all good directions."

"I still doubt myself."

"That's okay. Doubt isn't weakness—it's awareness. The people who never doubt are the ones who should worry us." He smiled. "You question yourself because you care about getting it right. That's a strength, not a flaw."

Bella let that settle. It was a reframe she hadn't considered—doubt as vigilance, not inadequacy.

"Thank you," she said finally. "For this year. For everything."

"Don't thank me. I just saw what was already there and helped it grow." Hawk's eyes were warm in the firelight. "The work was yours, daughter. The becoming was yours. I just... lit the fire."

She laughed at that. "Was that a pun?"

"Maybe." His grin was wolfish. "I'm allowed to be corny sometimes."

They sat together, watching the flames, comfortable in the silence between them.

One year. One transformation. One family, forged in fire and choice.

And this was just the beginning.
