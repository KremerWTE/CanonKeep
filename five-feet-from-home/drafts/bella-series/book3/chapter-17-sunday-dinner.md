# Chapter 17: Sunday Dinner

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Four: The New Normal

---

Sunday dinner at the compound was sacred.

Not in a religious sense—though there was that too, with grace said before meals. But sacred in the sense that it was inviolable. Phones went silent. Work stopped. The family gathered, regardless of whatever crises were brewing elsewhere.

"We protect this time," Addie had explained. "If we can't carve out space for each other, what's the point of everything else?"

---

Bella's first Sunday dinner as a full family member was different from all the ones she'd attended before.

She wasn't an observer anymore. She wasn't the new recruit watching from the edges, trying to learn the rhythms. She was seated at the table as a matter of course, her place marked by a napkin with her initial embroidered in the corner.

"When did—" She touched the fabric.

"Last week," Addie said, passing the salad. "Everyone has one. It's how we know where we belong."

---

The table was full that night.

Jasper sat at the head, but it was Hawk who led the conversation—asking questions, drawing out stories, making sure everyone was heard. Elena to Jasper's right, luminous as always, contributing with precise observations. Kendra and Chris across from each other, their easy partnership evident in the way they finished each other's sentences. Harper on video call from Miami, her children visible in the background.

And Grace, positioned next to Bella, chattering about her week.

"I got an A on the diorama," she reported, for perhaps the fifth time. "Mrs. Patterson said it was the best in the class."

"That's because you had excellent guidance," Hawk said, winking at Bella.

"No, it's because I understood the focal point." Grace was matter-of-fact. "Aunt Bella taught me that. The thing people should remember comes first, and everything else supports it."

"Solid marketing principle," Elena observed. "You're learning valuable skills."

"I'm learning how to make people look where I want them to look," Grace corrected. "That's different from marketing."

The table went quiet for a moment. Then Hawk laughed—a genuine, delighted sound.

"She's right. It is different." He shook his head. "Eleven years old and already understanding the game."

---

After dinner, the group scattered to various corners of the house.

Jasper and Elena disappeared into his office for what Bella had come to understand was their weekly "state of the union"—a private conversation about the balance of work and family. Kendra and Chris took Grace to the game room. Harper signed off, kids demanding attention.

Bella found herself in the kitchen with Addie, doing dishes.

"We have staff for this," Addie pointed out.

"I know. I like doing it." Bella scrubbed a pot. "Feels normal. Like the cafe."

"You miss it sometimes. Home."

"I miss versions of it. The rhythms. The simplicity." Bella rinsed the pot, set it aside. "But I don't miss feeling invisible. I don't miss being the one who held everything together while nobody noticed."

"You're still the one who holds things together."

"It's different now." Bella met Addie's eyes. "Now people notice. Now it counts."

---

Hawk found them later, after the dishes were done.

"Fire pit," he said. It wasn't a question.

Bella followed him into the cool evening air. The fire was already burning—low and steady, the way he liked it. Two chairs. Two glasses. The familiar ritual.

"How are you doing?" he asked.

"Good. Really good." Bella settled into her chair. "I feel like I finally understand my place here. Not just at BSS, but... in the family."

"It took time."

"It took believing." She accepted the whiskey he offered. "I kept waiting for someone to tell me I didn't belong. That it was all a mistake. That I'd wake up and realize I'd imagined the whole thing."

"And now?"

"Now I'm starting to believe it's real." She smiled. "The napkin helped."

Hawk chuckled. "Addie's idea. She said you needed something tangible. Proof you could touch."

"She was right."

---

They sat in silence for a while, watching the flames.

"You're ready for what comes next," Hawk said eventually.

"What comes next?"

"That's for you to decide." He glanced at her. "You've built your foundation here. Your skills, your relationships, your place in the family. Now you have to figure out what you want to build on it."

"What if I don't know yet?"

"Then you figure it out. You try things. You fail at some of them." His voice was matter-of-fact. "The point isn't to have all the answers. The point is to keep asking questions."

Bella thought about Vee. About the longing in her eyes. About the path she'd assumed would be hers that never materialized.

"What if what I want doesn't fit here? What if it takes me somewhere else?"

"Then you go somewhere else." Hawk's gaze was steady. "Family isn't a cage, daughter. It's a foundation. You can build on it wherever you go. The people who love you will still love you, even if you're on the other side of the world."

"What about BSS? The work?"

"The work continues. With you or without you." He shrugged. "We're building something that outlasts individuals. That's the point. You're part of it, but you're not shackled to it."

Bella felt something loosen in her chest—a fear she hadn't known she was carrying.

"I don't know what I want yet," she admitted.

"That's okay." Hawk raised his glass. "Take your time. Ask your questions. The answers will come when they're ready."

They clinked glasses and drank.

The fire crackled. The stars emerged. And somewhere in the house, Bella could hear Grace laughing at something Chris had said.

This was family. This was home.

And whatever came next, she'd have this to return to.
