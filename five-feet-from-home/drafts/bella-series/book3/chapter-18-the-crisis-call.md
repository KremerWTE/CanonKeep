# Chapter 18: The Crisis Call

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Four: The New Normal

---

The call came at 2 AM.

Bella was already awake—she'd been reviewing files for a case, the kind of late-night work that had become routine. When her phone buzzed with the red alert code, she was moving before her brain fully processed the message.

**PRIORITY: RED**
**CLIENT: CARDINAL-3**
**STATUS: ACTIVE THREAT**
**LOCATION: NYC**
**LEAD: KENDRA**
**SUPPORT: BELLA**

---

She was in the war room within eight minutes.

Kendra was already there, screens alive with data feeds. Maps, communications intercepts, real-time camera feeds from what looked like a hotel corridor.

"What do we have?"

"Corporate kidnapping." Kendra didn't look up. "Tech founder. His daughter was taken from a hotel in Manhattan three hours ago. Ransom demand just came in—not money, but proprietary data."

"His company's IP?"

"The core algorithm for their AI platform. Worth about four billion dollars." Kendra pointed to a secondary screen. "The kidnappers aren't professionals. They're ex-employees who got fired when the company restructured. This is a grudge play dressed up as corporate espionage."

"Amateurs with a hostage. That's worse than pros."

"Exactly." Kendra met her eyes. "Pros follow rules. Amateurs panic."

---

The next four hours were controlled chaos.

Bella coordinated with FBI contacts—BSS had relationships across every major agency, built through years of discreet cooperation. She tracked the ransom communications, analyzing the language for clues about the kidnappers' mental state. She monitored the daughter's last known movements, building a timeline of the abduction.

"They're communicating through a gaming platform," she reported. "Discord servers that get created, used once, and deleted. Smart, but traceable if you know where to look."

"Can we get ahead of them?"

"Jordan's on it. She says twenty minutes."

---

The break came at dawn.

Jordan found the kidnappers' base—a storage facility in Queens, rented three weeks ago under a false name. Satellite imagery showed vehicle activity consistent with the timeline. NYPD hostage response was already mobilizing.

But the father was breaking.

"I'll give them the algorithm," he said, voice cracking over the phone. "I'll give them whatever they want. Just get my daughter back."

"Sir." Bella's voice was calm, steady—the voice Kendra had trained her to use in crisis. "If you give them the algorithm, you destroy your company. You lose everything you built. And there's no guarantee they release your daughter afterward."

"Then what am I supposed to do?"

"You're supposed to trust us." Bella glanced at Kendra, who nodded. "We have their location. Tactical teams are moving now. Your daughter will be safe within the hour."

"How do you know?"

"Because that's what we do." Bella paused. "Sir, I know you're scared. I know every second feels like an eternity. But we need you to hold the line. Can you do that?"

Silence on the other end. Then, quietly: "Yes."

---

The rescue went smoothly.

The kidnappers—three of them, as Bella had predicted—were apprehended without resistance. The daughter was shaken but unharmed. By noon, the situation was contained, the media managed, and the father was hugging his daughter in a hospital room while Bella watched through a security camera feed.

"Clean work," Kendra said. "You kept him stable when he was ready to crack."

"I understood his fear." Bella turned away from the screen. "He thought he was going to lose the person who mattered most to him. That makes people do stupid things."

"And you talked him out of it."

"I gave him something to hold onto. Hope. Trust." Bella shrugged. "It's not magic. It's just knowing what people need to hear."

"That's not 'just' anything." Kendra's voice was serious. "That's a skill most people never develop. You have it naturally."

"I had good teachers."

---

Back at the compound, she found Hawk in the gym.

He was working the heavy bag—controlled strikes, rhythmic and precise. He didn't stop when she entered, just nodded at the bench against the wall.

She sat. Waited.

"I heard it went well," he said between strikes.

"It did."

"And the father? He held together?"

"Barely. I had to talk him down."

Hawk paused, catching the bag. "That's the hardest part of this job. Not the tactical work. Not the analysis. But keeping people stable when their world is falling apart."

"I didn't think. I just... knew what to say."

"That's instinct. Good instinct." He released the bag, turned to face her. "You're developing judgment, daughter. Real judgment, not just training. The kind that comes from doing the work."

"I was terrified the whole time."

"Good." He smiled slightly. "Fear keeps you sharp. The day you stop being scared is the day you start making mistakes."

---

That night, alone in her room, Bella wrote in her journal.

*Today I helped save a life. A young woman is home with her father because our team was good enough, and I was steady enough, to make it happen.*

*I don't know if this is what I'm supposed to be doing with my life. I don't know if this is forever. But I know that today, I mattered. I made a difference.*

*That feels like enough for now.*
