# Chapter 32: The White Queen

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Seven: Transition

---

Two years.

Two years since she'd walked into BSS headquarters, terrified and hopeful. Two years since she'd asked Addie to mentor her. Two years of fire pit lessons and chess metaphors and slowly, painfully, beautifully becoming someone new.

Hawk marked the anniversary at the fire pit.

---

"I have something for you."

He passed her a small box—velvet, the kind jewelry came in. Bella opened it and found a chess piece: a white queen, carved from ivory, polished to a soft glow.

"The queen," she said.

"The piece you've become." Hawk's voice was quiet. "When you first came here, you were a pawn. Useful. Expendable. Moving one square at a time."

"And now?"

"Now you move any direction. Any distance. You see the whole board." He met her eyes. "You're not just playing the game anymore, daughter. You're shaping it."

---

Bella turned the piece in her fingers.

The weight was satisfying. The craftsmanship was beautiful. But it was the symbolism that made her throat tighten.

"I don't feel like a queen."

"You don't have to feel it. You just have to be it." Hawk smiled. "Feelings are lagging indicators. They catch up eventually."

"What if I'm not ready?"

"Nobody's ever ready for the next level. That's what makes it the next level." He leaned back. "But you're more ready than you think. You've handled crises solo. You've built relationships across the network. You've found your niche and started claiming it."

"Human-centered crisis management."

"That's what Harper calls it. I call it 'seeing people.'" His voice was warm. "That's rare, Bella. Most fixers see problems. You see humans. That's a superpower."

---

They sat in silence for a while, watching the flames.

"I went to a ranch last month," Bella said finally. "In Colorado. Near Denver."

"I heard. Mandy Chen's place."

"You know her?"

"I know of her. Former Agency. Good reputation." Hawk's gaze was knowing. "What did you think?"

"I think..." Bella paused, organizing her thoughts. "I think I felt something I haven't felt in a long time. Quiet. Peace. Like I could breathe without worrying about the next crisis."

"And?"

"And there was someone there. The founder. Mandy." Bella felt heat rise in her cheeks. "I can't stop thinking about her."

---

Hawk was quiet for a moment.

Then: "Tell me about her."

So Bella did. The steel-green eyes. The matter-of-fact way she talked about her breakdown. The ranch she'd built from nothing. The way she looked at Bella like she was solving a puzzle.

"She sounds like someone worth knowing," Hawk said, when Bella finished.

"She scares me."

"Good." His smile was wolfish. "The ones who don't scare you aren't worth your time. Fear means something important is at stake."

"What if I'm not ready?"

"You asked that already." Hawk passed her the whiskey. "The answer hasn't changed. Nobody's ready. You try anyway."

---

"I still don't know what I want," Bella admitted. "Career. Love. Life. All of it."

"That's okay. You're thirty, not sixty." Hawk shrugged. "You've got time to figure it out. Just don't figure it out alone."

"Is that advice?"

"It's wisdom. Hard-won." He met her eyes. "I spent years figuring things out alone. It almost killed me. When I finally let people in—Addie, this family, this life—everything got easier."

"You're saying I should let people in."

"I'm saying you should consider it." His voice was gentle. "You've built walls, daughter. Good walls, necessary walls. But walls that keep danger out also keep love out. At some point, you have to decide which you want more."

---

Bella looked at the white queen in her hand.

Power. Protection. The ability to move anywhere, strike in any direction.

But alone on the board, even a queen could be cornered.

"I'm going to go back," she said. "To the ranch. To see her."

Hawk nodded. "When?"

"Soon. After I finish the current assignment."

"Good." He raised his glass. "To the white queen. May she find her king—or her equal. Or whoever she needs to find."

Bella clinked her glass against his.

"To the white queen," she echoed.

The fire crackled. The stars wheeled overhead. And somewhere in Colorado, a woman with steel-green eyes waited—without knowing she was waiting.

The game was changing.

And Bella was finally ready to play.
