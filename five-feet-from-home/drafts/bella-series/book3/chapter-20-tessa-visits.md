# Chapter 20: Tessa Visits

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part Five: Becoming

---

"I'm coming to Charlotte."

Tessa's announcement came via FaceTime, her face filling Bella's phone screen with typical Tessa exuberance.

"Wait, what?"

"I have a conference. Corporate event planning thing. And I figured, if I'm going to be in your city, I should actually see you. It's been, what, two years?"

"Almost three."

"Three years! Bella, that's insane. We need to fix that."

---

They'd kept in touch, but sporadic texts and occasional calls weren't the same as proximity.

Tessa had stayed in New York after graduation, building a career in corporate event management. She was good at it—her natural energy and meticulous organization made her a rising star. But their paths had diverged dramatically, and some days Bella barely recognized the girl she'd shared a dorm room with.

"I'll pick you up from your hotel," Bella offered. "We can have dinner."

"Dinner, drinks, staying up too late catching up on everything." Tessa's grin was the same as always. "Just like old times."

---

When Tessa arrived, she was exactly as Bella remembered: blonde, polished, talking before she'd fully exited the car.

"This city is gorgeous. Like, I thought Charlotte was just banking and NASCAR, but it's actually beautiful. And warm! It's January and I don't need a coat. This is amazing."

"Deep breaths, Tess."

"I've had three espressos since 6 AM. Deep breaths are not happening." But she paused long enough to pull Bella into a fierce hug. "God, I've missed you."

"I've missed you too."

---

They went to a restaurant Bella had discovered through Elena—upscale but unpretentious, the kind of place where the food was exceptional and the atmosphere was casual enough for real conversation.

Tessa noticed everything.

"This place is nice. Like, really nice." She studied the wine list. "And you just... know about it?"

"I work with people who know things."

"Your mysterious 'consulting' job." Tessa air-quoted the word. "Which you still haven't fully explained, by the way."

"It's complicated."

"I have time." Tessa set down the menu. "Bella, I was your roommate for four years. I know when you're hiding something. And whatever this is..." She gestured at Bella—her clothes, her confidence, the ease with which she inhabited the space. "This isn't just a job. This is a transformation."

---

Bella considered how much to share.

She couldn't tell Tessa about BSS specifically. About the crises they managed, the clients they protected, the operations that never made the news. But she could tell her about the other parts—the parts that mattered most.

"I found a family," she said finally. "People who chose me. Who mentored me. Who helped me become... this." She gestured at herself. "I know I seem different. I feel different. But I'm still me."

"No, you're not." Tessa's voice was soft, not accusatory. "You're better than you were. You were always good, Bella. Smart, capable, kind. But you never believed it. Now you do."

"Mostly. Sometimes."

"That's more than before." Tessa smiled. "I'm glad. Whatever these people are, wherever this job leads you, I'm glad you found something that made you believe in yourself."

---

After dinner, they walked through the city.

Charlotte at night was different from New York—quieter, calmer, with space to breathe. Tessa peppered her with questions, and Bella answered what she could.

"So this Addie woman. She's like your mentor?"

"She's like my mother. The chosen kind." Bella touched her pendant. "She gave me this. It means something important."

"The owl?"

"It's a long story."

"We have time."

So Bella told her. Not everything—but enough. The fire pit. The chess lessons. The book signed "Mom" and "Dad." The feeling of finally, finally belonging somewhere.

"You're crying," Tessa observed.

"I know." Bella wiped her eyes. "It still feels unreal sometimes. Like I'm going to wake up and find out it was a dream."

"It's not a dream. It's just... you finally got what you deserved." Tessa linked her arm through Bella's. "I always knew you were special. I just couldn't get you to believe it."

---

Later, in Bella's apartment, they stayed up talking.

About the old days. About the friends they'd lost touch with. About Tyler (married now, two kids) and David (still single, working in tech). About Sophie, briefly—the almost-something that had never happened.

"Did you ever think about reaching out to her?" Tessa asked.

"Sometimes. But what would I say?"

"I don't know. That you figured yourself out? That you're sorry you ran?"

Bella shook her head. "Sophie deserved better than me. The me I was then. Maybe if I met her now..."

"Maybe you should."

"Maybe someday." Bella stretched out on the couch. "Right now, I'm still figuring out what I want. Career-wise, life-wise, everything-wise."

"And love-wise?"

"That too." Bella smiled. "But I'm not in a rush. For the first time in my life, I feel like I have time. Like the universe isn't going to take everything away if I don't grab it immediately."

"That's growth."

"That's therapy and good mentors."

Tessa laughed. "Same thing, honestly."

---

When Tessa left the next day, they hugged at the airport.

"Don't be a stranger," Tessa said. "I mean it. Three years is too long."

"I'll be better about staying in touch."

"You'd better. I want updates. On the job, the family, all of it." Tessa stepped back, studied her. "You look happy, Bella. Really happy. I don't think I've ever seen you look like this."

"I'm getting there."

"You're already there." Tessa grinned. "You just need to stop waiting for permission to believe it."

She walked through security, waving over her shoulder. Bella watched until she disappeared into the crowd.

Some friendships faded with distance. Some grew stronger. She and Tessa had somehow managed the second—connected by years of shared history, by knowing each other's worst selves and choosing to stay anyway.

That was rare. That was valuable.

That was something worth protecting.
