# Chapter 1b: The Pendant

## Book 3: "I Could Have Danced All Night"
### Part One: The Ceremony

---

The pendant was everything.

Bella had seen the symbol before—glimpses on jewelry, on stationery, on the discreet pins some women wore at galas. She'd known it meant something, had pieced together enough to understand it represented a world she'd only dreamed of entering.

Now it rested in her palm, warm from Addie's hands.

---

The chain was delicate—fine gold links so small they seemed like woven thread. Eighteen inches long, designed to sit at the hollow of the throat where a pulse would make it dance.

The pendant itself was an oval about the size of a thumbnail. Gold, but not bright—a warm, aged tone that suggested it had been crafted by hands that understood legacy. The edges were smooth, worn to softness by generations of women who'd touched it the same way Bella was touching it now.

The front held the symbol.

It wasn't ornate—that would have been too obvious, too readable. Instead, it was elegant and understated: a stylized owl with wings partially spread, clutching what might have been a shield or might have been a book. The lines were clean, art deco in influence, designed to be beautiful whether or not you knew its meaning.

Below the owl, so small you'd miss it if you weren't looking, was a second symbol—the sorority subsection sigil that connected the Wives Club to its collegiate origins. A legacy within a legacy.

The back was smooth except for a small engraving: *Semper Vigilans*. Always Watching.

---

"This belonged to someone before you," Addie said, as Bella stared at the pendant. "Several someones, actually. It's been passed down through generations of women who earned their place."

"Who?"

"Does it matter?" Addie smiled. "What matters is that it's yours now. You've earned it."

---

Bella lifted the chain and settled it around her neck.

The weight was almost nothing—the pendant was delicate, designed to be worn without being noticed. But as it settled against her skin, Bella felt something profound.

Recognition.

Not from the room—though that was there too, in the raised glasses and the murmured congratulations. But recognition from herself. The moment when everything she'd worked for, everything she'd dreamed of, everything she'd never quite believed she deserved, became undeniably real.

She touched the pendant with her fingertips. The owl's wings were cool against her skin.

---

"The first time you wear it," Harper said later, catching Bella in a quiet moment by the windows, "it feels like a costume. Like you're playing a role."

"Does that go away?"

"Eventually. When you realize it's not a costume. It's a key." Harper's dark eyes were knowing. "That symbol opens doors, Bella. Introduces you to people you'd never meet otherwise. Creates opportunities that don't exist for anyone else."

"That sounds like responsibility."

"It is responsibility. But it's also power." Harper touched her own pendant—hidden beneath her neckline, but present. "Use it wisely."

---

That night, getting ready for bed, Bella looked at herself in the mirror.

The dress was hung carefully in her closet. The shoes were put away. The makeup was washed off. But the pendant remained, resting against her bare collarbone.

She didn't want to take it off.

She touched it again—the smooth gold, the etched owl, the weight of generations.

*You're not a pawn anymore,* Hawk's voice echoed in her head. *You're a queen. You just forgot.*

Maybe she was finally starting to remember.
