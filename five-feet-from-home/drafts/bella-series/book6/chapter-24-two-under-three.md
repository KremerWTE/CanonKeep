# Chapter 24: Two Under Three

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Nine: The Years of Little

---

The phrase "two under three" became a running joke.

Every time someone asked how she was doing, Bella would say it: "I have two under three. You do the math."

The math was chaos. Beautiful, exhausting, unrelenting chaos.

---

A typical day:

5:30 AM: James wakes for feeding.
6:00 AM: Rose wakes because James is awake.
6:30 AM: Everyone dressed, theoretically. Someone is crying.
7:00 AM: Breakfast. Most of it ends up on the floor.
7:30 AM: Nanny arrives. Brief reprieve.
8:00 AM: Work begins—Bella at her desk, one eye on the monitor.

The schedule continued in this fashion until bedtime, which itself was a two-hour process involving negotiations, tantrums, stories, songs, and the particular desperation of exhausted parents.

"This is temporary," Matt said, every night.

"So is a migraine. Doesn't make it fun."

---

Rose's jealousy emerged in waves.

Some days she was sweet—kissing James, bringing him toys, declaring him "my best friend." Other days she screamed if he so much as looked at her.

"It's developmental," the pediatrician said. "She's learning to share attention. It's a huge adjustment."

"What do we do?"

"Be patient. Make time for her alone. Remind her she's loved, no matter what."

---

The alone time was harder than expected.

Every moment with Rose meant time away from James. Every focus on James meant Rose felt neglected. Bella felt like she was constantly failing someone.

"You're not failing anyone," Matt said, after one particularly difficult day. "You're doing your best in an impossible situation."

"My best doesn't feel like enough."

"It's always enough. Even when it doesn't feel like it."

---

Month six with two kids: the equilibrium arrived.

Not perfect—nothing about early childhood was perfect. But sustainable. A rhythm that accommodated both children's needs without either parent collapsing.

Rose was in preschool three mornings a week. James had settled into predictable naps. Bella could actually work for consecutive hours.

"I feel almost human," she told Elena, during a lunch meeting.

"That's a low bar."

"You try having two under three and maintaining higher standards."

---

The business continued to evolve.

Rosewood was essentially self-running now—the systems Bella had built, the team she'd trained, all of it functioning without her constant oversight. She attended quarterly board meetings and occasional strategic sessions, but the day-to-day was handled by others.

"You've achieved what every entrepreneur dreams of," Hawk said. "A business that doesn't need you."

"Is that success or obsolescence?"

"Success. You built something that outlasts your involvement. That's the definition of legacy."

---

The POH work continued in modified form.

Page duties were shared now, with other members stepping up during Bella's busiest periods. The Herald had mentioned, more than once, that she was ready for more responsibility when she wanted it.

"Not yet," Bella said. "Maybe when the kids are older."

"The role will wait. Excellence isn't easily replaced."

---

One evening, Bella sat on the porch while both kids slept.

The ranch stretched out before her, quiet in the fading light. Horses in the distance. Mountains on the horizon. The life she'd built, visible in every direction.

"What are you thinking about?" Matt asked, joining her.

"Everything. Nothing." She leaned against him. "Mostly gratitude. For all of it."

"Even the hard parts?"

She smiled. "Especially those."
