# Chapter 25: Hawk's Legacy Talk

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Nine: The Years of Little

---

The call came on a Tuesday evening.

Hawk's voice was measured, controlled—the tone he used when something significant was coming.

"Daughter, I need to talk to you about something. In person. Can you come to Charlotte this weekend?"

"Is everything okay?"

"Everything is fine. This is about the future, not a crisis."

---

She flew out Friday morning.

Matt stayed with the kids—James was still too young for easy travel, and Rose was in a phase where any disruption to routine sparked meltdowns.

The compound was quiet when she arrived. Hawk was waiting in his study, whiskey already poured.

"Sit," he said. "We have a lot to discuss."

---

The conversation was about succession.

Not immediate—Hawk was healthy enough, his heart condition managed. But he was seventy now. The clock that had always seemed theoretical was becoming real.

"I've been thinking about BSS," he said. "About what happens when I'm not here to guide it."

"You're not going anywhere, Dad."

"Eventually, I am. That's not morbid—that's fact." His eyes were steady. "I want to be prepared. I want the company to be prepared."

"What are you asking me?"

"I'm asking if you want a role in the transition. Not as CEO—Jasper is groomed for that. But as a board member. A voice in strategic decisions. A guardian of the values we've built."

---

Bella listened as Hawk outlined his vision.

A board that included family members—her, Jasper, eventually Grace when she was ready. Outside advisors for expertise. A clear charter about mission and values.

"BSS isn't just a business," Hawk said. "It's a way of being in the world. Protecting people. Solving problems that others can't. I want that to continue, long after I'm gone."

"And you trust me to help ensure that?"

"I trust you with everything." His voice cracked, just slightly. "You've always understood what matters, Bella. From the first moment I saw you, struggling and fighting and becoming—I knew you had the vision."

---

She didn't answer immediately.

The weight of it—a seat at the table, a permanent role in the Barrett empire—was enormous. And she already had so much: the kids, Rosewood, the POH work.

"Can I think about it?"

"Of course. There's no rush." Hawk smiled. "This is a conversation we'll have many times, I hope. Many years to refine the plan."

"I hope so too."

---

That night, fire pit.

Just the two of them, the familiar ritual providing comfort.

"How are you doing, daughter?"

"Overwhelmed. Grateful. Processing."

"And how can I support you?"

"You already are." She looked at him across the flames. "By planning for the future instead of denying it exists. By including me in the conversation. By trusting me."

"Those aren't supports. Those are acknowledgments of reality."

"They're both."

---

She said yes two weeks later.

A board seat, effective when Hawk stepped back. Strategic input on major decisions. A voice in the legacy he was building.

"Welcome to the next chapter," Hawk said, when she called.

"Which chapter is this?"

"The one where you become the guardian. The one where you ensure what we've built continues." His voice was warm. "I'm proud of you, Bella. For everything you've become."

"I learned from the best."

"You learned from watching. And then you exceeded the teacher." He paused. "That's exactly as it should be."
