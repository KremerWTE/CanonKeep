# Chapter 11: Five Year Anniversary

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Four: The Growth

---

Five years of marriage.

It felt simultaneously like forever and like no time at all. Bella looked at her life—the business, the family, the home they'd built—and marveled at how much had changed.

"Do you feel different?" Matt asked, the morning of their anniversary.

"Different how?"

"Than you did five years ago. When we said the vows."

Bella considered.

"I feel more myself. If that makes sense. Like I've grown into who I was always supposed to be."

"That makes perfect sense."

---

They celebrated simply.

A weekend away—just the two of them—at a bed and breakfast in the mountains. No work, no family, no obligations beyond each other.

"This is nice," Bella said, on the first evening, stretched out on the cabin porch.

"'Nice' seems understated."

"Okay, it's wonderful. It's glorious. It's the most relaxing weekend of my entire life." She grinned. "Better?"

"Much."

---

**[ANNIVERSARY REFLECTION]**

Five years had taught her things.

That love wasn't a destination—it was a practice. Something you did every day, in small ways. A cup of coffee made without asking. A foot rub after a long day. The patience to listen, really listen, when words were hard.

That partnership meant compromise, but not surrender. They'd disagreed, sometimes fiercely. About business decisions, family priorities, where to spend holidays. Each disagreement had been an opportunity to understand each other better.

That she was worthy of being loved. Not because she'd earned it through achievement or sacrifice—just because she was. That was still the hardest lesson. The one she had to keep relearning.

---

"Tell me something I don't know about you," Matt said, that night, firelight dancing.

"After five years? You know everything."

"I know a lot. Not everything." He smiled. "There's always more. We could spend fifty years together and still be discovering new things."

Bella thought about it.

"When I was little, I used to imagine what my life would look like. I'd make up stories about the person I'd become. The places I'd go, the things I'd do."

"And?"

"And none of them—not a single story—looked anything like this." Her voice caught. "This is better. So much better than anything I could have imagined."

"Even the hard parts?"

"Especially the hard parts." She reached for him. "They made me ready for this. For you."

---

The conversation turned to the future.

"Where do you want to be in another five years?" Matt asked.

"Here. With you. Building whatever comes next."

"Be more specific."

Bella considered. Really considered.

"I want the business to be stable. Self-sustaining. Something I can step back from without it falling apart."

"Okay."

"I want us to still be us. Not stagnant—growing. But together. Always together."

"Of course."

She hesitated.

"And I want... more. More than just us."

Matt was quiet. Then, carefully: "You mean..."

"I mean I think I'm ready. To talk about it seriously. To make a plan."

His face transformed. Joy, barely contained.

"You're sure?"

"I'm sure I want to discuss it. That's a start."

"That's everything." He pulled her close. "We'll figure it out. Together."

"Together."
