# Chapter 10: The Fourth Venue

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Four: The Growth

---

The call came from Charleston.

An old plantation property—carefully renovated, sensitively contextualized—looking for new management. The owners had seen Rosewood's work. They wanted to talk.

"Charleston," Bella said, considering. "That's a different market."

"That's an opportunity market," Sara corrected. "Destination weddings. Corporate retreats. Historic tourism crossover."

"It's also further from everything else. Harder to manage."

"Which is why you'd need to hire someone to run it. Someone good." Sara smiled. "Someone who might be ready for their own leadership role."

---

The property was stunning.

Live oaks draped with Spanish moss. A main house that had been restored with exquisite care—honoring its history while acknowledging its complexity. Gardens that seemed designed for romance.

"This is beautiful," Bella admitted, walking the grounds with the current owners.

"It's been our life's work. But we're ready to step back." The owner, a woman named Catherine, studied her. "We've followed your career, Ms. Rossi—"

"Holloway, now."

"Holloway. Apologies." Catherine smiled. "We've watched what you've built. The care you bring. The way you create something special in each location instead of just stamping out replicas."

"That matters to you?"

"It matters deeply. This property has a story. We want whoever takes it to understand that story and tell it truthfully."

---

The acquisition took six months to negotiate.

Not just purchase price—terms. How the property would be used. What stories would be told about its past. Commitments to community engagement, historical education, responsible tourism.

Bella insisted on all of it.

"You're making this harder than it needs to be," their lawyer observed.

"I'm making it right. There's a difference."

---

Rosewood Oaks launched the following spring.

Sara's assistant—a young woman named Morgan who'd proven herself across three venues—took over as manager. Local staff. Local vendors. Deep integration with Charleston's existing event ecosystem.

"It doesn't feel like the others," Matt observed, during the soft opening.

"It's not supposed to." Bella looked at the property, at the guests exploring the grounds, at the story being told through every carefully considered detail. "Each one is unique. That's the point."

"The Rosewood point."

"Exactly."

---

By now, Bella's business had become something substantial.

Four venues across three states. A team of forty-two. Revenue that had grown exponentially each year. Industry recognition, media coverage, a reputation for excellence.

"You've built an empire," Elena said, reviewing the numbers.

"I've built something I'm proud of."

"Same thing."

"Not quite." Bella smiled. "An empire is about power. This is about joy. About creating spaces where people can celebrate the moments that matter."

"That's a good mission statement."

"It's not a statement. It's just... what I believe."

---

The success brought choices.

Investors interested in scaling faster. Franchise inquiries from across the country. Opportunities to grow in ways that would have been unimaginable when she started.

She said no to all of them.

"Why?" Matt asked, genuinely curious.

"Because bigger isn't better. Better is better." She thought about Hawk, about BSS, about the way he'd built something sustainable rather than explosive. "I want to do this well. Not just more."

"Some people would say that's leaving money on the table."

"Some people would be right." She shrugged. "Money isn't the point. Never has been."

"What is the point?"

She looked at him—her husband, her partner, her home.

"This. Everything this represents. The life we've built. The people we love." She smiled. "The money is just a tool. It's never the goal."

"I love you."

"I know. I'm lovable."

"And humble."

"And devastatingly humble."
