# Chapter 5: Recalibration

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Two: The Adjustment

---

The process took months.

Not a sudden shift—a gradual rebalancing. Letting go of tasks she didn't need to control. Training others to handle what she'd always done herself.

"You're delegating," Elena observed, impressed. "Actually delegating."

"I'm trying."

"How does it feel?"

"Terrifying." Bella smiled. "But also... lighter."

---

The first thing to go was the obsessive oversight at Rosewood.

She'd hired a venue manager—a capable woman named Sara who'd run events for a decade before deciding she wanted something smaller and more personal.

"You don't have to check my work," Sara said, gently, after Bella's third micromanaging incident. "I've got this."

"I know. It's just..."

"Hard to let go. I understand." Sara's smile was patient. "But you hired me for a reason. Trust that reason."

Bella tried. It got easier.

---

The second shift was at Maison Aurelia.

"I want to step back from COO," she told Elena. "Move to an advisory role. Strategic input without daily operations."

Elena didn't look surprised.

"I've been wondering when you'd get there."

"You expected this?"

"I expected you'd eventually realize you were doing five jobs and only getting paid for three." Elena squeezed her hand. "You've built something incredible with me. Now you need to focus on building something for yourself."

---

The new structure emerged:

BSS: Strategic involvement, major crisis management only. The operational work went to the growing team in Charlotte.

Maison Aurelia: Advisory board, quarterly strategy sessions. Sara handled day-to-day.

Rosewood Farm: Ownership, vision, special events. Management delegated.

POH: Continued Page duties, but with better boundaries. Not every evening. Not every task.

The schedule shifted. 5:30 AM starts. Midnight bedtimes. Still demanding, but survivable.

"You look better," Matt said, after a month.

"I feel better."

"Was it worth it? All those years of running flat out?"

Bella considered.

"Yes. It built the foundation. But now I'm ready to live in the house instead of constructing it."

---

The breathing room created space for other things.

Dinners that weren't rushed. Mornings that included coffee on the porch. Weekends that felt like weekends.

And conversations about the future.

"What do you want next?" Matt asked, one quiet evening.

"More of this. Building together. Growing together." She paused. "Maybe growing the family together."

His eyes widened. "You mean..."

"I mean I'm starting to think about it. For the first time. Really think about it."

"And?"

"And I'm not saying yes yet. But I'm not saying never anymore either."

---

That shift—from never to maybe—was its own kind of revolution.
