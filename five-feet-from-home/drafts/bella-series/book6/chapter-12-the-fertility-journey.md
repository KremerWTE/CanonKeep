# Chapter 12: The Fertility Journey

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Five: The Decision

---

They started trying in the spring.

Casual at first—not tracking, not stressing, just letting nature take its course. Bella had spent years preventing pregnancy; surely achieving it would be straightforward.

Three months passed. Then six.

"It's normal," the doctor said. "At your age, it can take a year or more."

"At my age" landed harder than expected.

---

The process changed their relationship in subtle ways.

Sex became less spontaneous, more scheduled. Bella found herself aware of her body in new ways—tracking cycles, monitoring symptoms, second-guessing every sensation.

"I don't want this to become an obsession," she told Matt, one evening when the pressure felt particularly heavy.

"Then let's step back. Take a break."

"I don't want to give up either."

"There's a middle ground. We keep trying, but we stop making it the center of everything." He took her hand. "If it happens, it happens. If it doesn't, we figure out alternatives."

"Alternatives."

"Adoption. Fostering. Being the cool aunt and uncle." He smiled gently. "There are many ways to have a family."

After a year, they consulted specialists.

Tests. Appointments. The uncomfortable intimacy of clinical discussions about the most private aspects of their lives.

The news was mixed.

Nothing was definitively wrong—that was the frustrating part. "Unexplained infertility," the doctor called it. Everything should work. It just wasn't working.

"We could try IVF," the doctor suggested. "It significantly improves odds."

"We could try patience," Bella countered. "Give it more time."

The doctor nodded. "That's also valid. Many couples conceive naturally after years of trying. There's no right answer here."

---

The emotional toll was heavier than Bella had anticipated.

She'd built an identity around achievement—work hard, get results. But this wasn't something she could power through. Couldn't optimize. Couldn't control.

"I feel helpless," she admitted to Addie, during a particularly difficult visit. "For the first time in years, I can't solve the problem."

"Some things aren't problems to be solved. They're mysteries to be lived."

"That's very philosophical."

"That's very true." Addie's eyes were kind. "Bella, you're allowed to grieve this. The timeline you expected. The ease you assumed. Let yourself feel it."

"I don't want to fall apart."

"Falling apart isn't the same as falling down. Sometimes we have to come undone to be remade into something stronger."

---

Matt was her anchor.

Through the disappointments, the negative tests, the well-meaning questions from family that felt like salt in wounds. He never lost hope. Never lost patience.

"We'll get through this," he said, on the hardest nights. "Whatever it looks like. We'll get through it together."

"How do you stay so positive?"

"I'm not positive. I'm committed." He pulled her close. "To you. To us. To whatever our family ends up being."

"Even if it's just us?"

"Then it's a family of two. And that's enough."

---

The decision came after eighteen months.

"I want to try IVF," Bella said. "One round. See what happens."

"Are you sure?"

"I'm sure I want to try everything before I accept that it's not meant to be." She looked at him. "Can you support that?"

"I can support anything that doesn't break you." His voice was gentle. "And I'll be there for the whole process. Whatever it takes."

"Whatever it takes," she repeated.

She wasn't sure what came next. But she knew she wouldn't face it alone.
