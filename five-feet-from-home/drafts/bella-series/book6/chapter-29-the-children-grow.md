# Chapter 29: The Children Grow

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Ten: A Decade

---

Rose was five now. James was three.

The years of little were transitioning into something new—still demanding, but different. More conversation. Less chaos. Actual personalities emerging.

Rose was fierce, like Bella had been at that age. Determined. Intense. Already reading at second-grade level, already asking questions that required real answers.

"Why do people get married?" she asked one evening.

"Because they love each other and want to build a life together."

"But you loved Daddy before you married him."

"Yes."

"So what changed?"

Bella considered how to explain commitment to a five-year-old.

"Marriage is a promise. To keep choosing each other, even when it's hard. To be a team, forever."

"Like you and me are a team?"

"Exactly like that. But with grown-up rules."

Rose nodded, satisfied. "Okay. Can I have more dessert?"

Profound questions and immediate needs. The essence of childhood.

---

**[CHILDREN DEVELOPMENT]**

James was gentler than his sister.

Thoughtful. Observant. Content to sit quietly while Rose commanded every room she entered.

"He's like Matt," Addie observed. "Steady. Patient. Watching before acting."

"He's also stubborn in his own way. You just don't see it until he's decided something."

"Also like Matt."

---

The sibling dynamic was complicated.

Rose adored James—possessively, protectively. She was the first to defend him, the first to claim him, the first to announce that no one else was allowed to criticize him.

James worshipped Rose. Followed her everywhere. Copied her words, her mannerisms, her preferences.

"They're going to be best friends," Matt predicted.

"Or mortal enemies."

"Or both, depending on the day."

"That's siblings for you."

---

Bella watched them develop with awe.

These two people she'd helped create, becoming themselves in ways she couldn't have predicted. Rose with her intensity. James with his depth. Both carrying pieces of her and Matt, combined into something entirely new.

"What do you think they'll become?" Matt asked, one evening.

"Whatever they want. That's the point."

"You don't have preferences?"

"I want them to be happy. I want them to be kind. I want them to know they're loved, no matter what choices they make." She paused. "Beyond that? It's their story to write."

---

The parenting challenges evolved.

No more diapers, no more midnight feedings. But new complexities: school decisions, social dynamics, the particular drama of young children navigating the world.

"Rose hit another kid today," the preschool teacher reported. "She says he was being mean to James."

"That's... not okay. But also kind of understandable."

"We need to talk to her about handling conflicts differently."

They talked. Rose listened. Rose agreed it was wrong. Rose also made clear she would do it again if anyone messed with her brother.

"She's going to be a handful," Matt said afterward.

"She's already a handful. She's going to be a force."

"Should we be worried?"

"We should be prepared."

---

The family dinners continued.

Charlotte compound trips, now with two kids in tow. Rosewood celebrations. Matt's mother's house at holidays.

Rose and James growing up surrounded by love, learning what family meant from people who'd chosen each other.

"They're going to have such a different childhood than I did," Bella said.

"Is that good?"

"That's everything. That's the whole point of breaking cycles." She watched Rose and James playing with Grace, the three of them laughing at something incomprehensible to adults. "They'll never wonder if they belong. They'll never doubt they're loved."

"Neither will you. Not anymore."

"Not anymore."
