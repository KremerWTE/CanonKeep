# Chapter 20: Returning to Work

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Seven: New Reality

---

The question came at four months.

When would she go back to work? Not "if"—Bella had never considered not returning. But the timing, the structure, the balance.

"I need to be involved," she told Matt. "The businesses, BSS, the POH work—it's part of who I am."

"But?"

"But I also can't leave her." She looked at Rose, happily gnawing a teething ring. "Not for long stretches. Not yet."

"Then we figure out a hybrid."

---

The hybrid took shape gradually.

Mornings at home, with Rose and the nanny they'd finally hired. Afternoons at her home office, with the baby monitor always in reach. Occasional trips to Rosewood or Charlotte, carefully planned around feeding schedules.

"You're reinventing work-life balance," Elena observed. "Most people just suffer through the traditional structure."

"Most people don't have my resources." Bella was pragmatic about this. "I have the privilege of flexibility. I should use it."

---

**[WORK DETAILS - POST-BABY]**

The Rosewood portfolio continued to grow.

With Sara and Morgan running operations, Bella could focus on strategy—new acquisitions, brand development, the vision that had started with a broken-down barn years ago.

"We're looking at a property in Georgia," Sara reported. "Historic mansion, enormous grounds. Perfect for Rosewood's aesthetic."

"Send me the details." Bella paused. "And add a nursery to any renovation plans going forward. Even if we don't use it, our guests might."

"Family-friendly events?"

"Family-inclusive everything. That's the future."

---

BSS had shifted during her absence.

Hawk had stepped back further, the health scare forcing a reassessment of his involvement. Jasper had taken on more leadership. The organization ran smoothly without Bella's constant input.

"Does that bother you?" Matt asked. "That it survived without you?"

"It's a relief, actually." She smiled. "I spent years trying to make myself indispensable. But healthy organizations shouldn't depend on any one person."

"Including you?"

"Especially me."

---

The POH work was the easiest to maintain.

Ceremonial coordination happened in bursts—intense periods of preparation followed by months of relative quiet. The Page role had grown to include mentorship of younger members, passing on what she'd learned.

"You're building a legacy there too," Addie observed.

"I'm trying to. It matters—the traditions, the rituals. The way we honor what came before while making space for what comes next."

"Sounds like motherhood."

Bella laughed. "Maybe everything is motherhood, in a way. Nurturing, guiding, eventually letting go."

---

Six months in, she found her stride.

Not the frantic pace of before—that woman was gone. But a sustainable rhythm. Work that mattered. Family that came first. A balance that felt, for the first time, genuinely balanced.

"You look different," Hawk said, during a visit. "Calmer."

"I feel different."

"Good different?"

"The best different." She watched Rose crawl across the floor, determined and unstoppable. "I'm not trying to prove anything anymore, Dad. I'm just... living."

"That's all I ever wanted for you."

"I know." She leaned against him. "Thank you for waiting while I figured it out."
