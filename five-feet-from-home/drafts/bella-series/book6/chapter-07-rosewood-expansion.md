# Chapter 7: Rosewood Expansion

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Three: The Business

---

Success bred demand.

Rosewood Farm was booked solid for eighteen months out. The waitlist grew weekly. Couples were offering premium prices for priority scheduling.

"We need more capacity," Sara said, reviewing the numbers. "Or we turn away business."

"I'm not comfortable with the venue getting bigger. That's not the vision."

"Then the vision needs to expand in a different direction."

---

The idea came from an unexpected source.

One of Matt's veteran contacts—a man named Derek who'd spent years in construction before his service—mentioned a property in Tennessee. Another barn, similar bones, owners looking to sell.

"It's a mess right now. But with work..." He shrugged. "Could be something."

Bella drove down to see it. Four hours each way, but she needed to understand.

The property was beautiful. Rolling hills. An old farmhouse that could be guest accommodation. A barn that, yes, was a disaster, but a disaster with potential.

"What do you think?" Matt asked, when she showed him the photos.

"I think I'm about to buy another barn."

---

**[BUSINESS EXPANSION DETAILS]**

Rosewood Tennessee launched eight months later.

Bella had learned from the first renovation. Better contractors. Smarter timelines. Systems that could be replicated.

The Tennessee property became a model—not just a second venue, but a proof of concept. The Rosewood brand could travel.

By year three of her venue business, she had:

- Rosewood Farm (North Carolina): The flagship. Consistently rated top five in the state.
- Rosewood Ridge (Tennessee): Smaller, more intimate. Positioned as boutique.
- Rosewood Pines (Virginia): Acquired but not yet developed. The next project.

"You're building a chain," Elena observed, during a strategy session.

"I'm building a brand. There's a difference."

"Explain it to me."

"A chain is about replication. Same experience everywhere. A brand is about standards. Same quality, but each location is unique to its place." Bella smiled. "I learned that from Maison Aurelia."

"Good student."

---

The team grew.

Not just Sara now—venue managers at each location, an operations coordinator, a marketing assistant. Bella had become what she'd always resisted: a boss with people to manage.

"How does it feel?" Matt asked. "Having employees?"

"Strange. Responsible." She paused. "Kind of wonderful, actually. Seeing people grow. Helping them become what they can be."

"Sounds familiar."

"Hawk's influence. Obviously."

"Obviously."

---

The success brought complications.

Media attention. Interview requests. A profile in Southern Living that led to a waiting list so long they had to hire someone just to manage inquiries.

"You're becoming a public figure," Addie warned. "That comes with scrutiny."

"I'm just running venues. It's not that interesting."

"It's a story. Young woman builds boutique empire through hustle and vision. People love that narrative." Addie's eyes were serious. "Just be prepared. The bigger you get, the more people will want to tear you down."

"I can handle criticism."

"I know you can. Just don't let it change you."

---

She didn't let it change her.

The core remained: beautiful spaces, flawless execution, personal touches that made each event feel special. Growth was a tool, not a goal. The goal was always the couples, the families, the moments that mattered.

"You're doing something good," Matt said, one night after a particularly beautiful wedding. "Something that makes people happy."

"Is that enough?"

"It's everything." He kissed her. "Joy is the most valuable thing there is. You're in the business of creating it."

She'd never thought of it that way.

But he wasn't wrong.
