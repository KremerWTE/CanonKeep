# Chapter 9: Hawk's Birthday

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Three: The Business

---

Hawk didn't celebrate birthdays.

Or rather, he hadn't, for decades. "Just another day," he'd always said. "The work doesn't stop for arbitrary calendar markers."

But this year was different. This year was sixty-five.

"We're doing something," Addie announced, in a tone that brooked no argument. "Whether he likes it or not."

---

The gathering was intimate by Barrett standards.

Family only—which still meant dozens of people, given the sprawl of the chosen clan. The compound fire pit, expanded for the occasion. Catering from Addie's favorite restaurant.

And Bella, coordinating from the shadows, ensuring everything flowed smoothly.

"You're working," Matt observed, finding her checking details on her phone.

"I'm helping."

"You're always helping. Tonight, try being present."

She put the phone away. He was right.

---

The evening unfolded in the warm chaos of family.

Grace organized the children into a performance—a song she'd written, surprisingly touching for an eight-year-old. Jasper gave a toast that balanced humor and sincerity. Kendra presented a photo album spanning Hawk's life, assembled in secret over months.

And Bella watched, feeling the weight of it.

This man—this impossible, demanding, brilliant man—had changed everything. Had looked at a lost young woman and seen a queen in waiting. Had invested when there was no logical reason to. Had loved her into becoming herself.

"Stop crying," Matt whispered, handing her a tissue.

"I'm not crying. It's allergies."

"You're allergic to feelings. I know."

---

Later, when the crowd had thinned and the fire had settled, Bella found Hawk alone at the edge of the property.

"Avoiding your own party?"

"Getting some air." He smiled, the expression softer than it used to be. "It's a lot. All of it."

"Good lot or bad lot?"

"Good. Overwhelmingly good." He looked at her. "I never expected this, you know. The family. The love. I built BSS because I thought that was all I'd have. A legacy of work."

"And instead?"

"Instead, I have you. All of you." His voice was quiet. "I have grandchildren who call me by silly nicknames. A wife who knows me better than I know myself. Daughters and sons who chose me."

He turned to face her fully.

"You were the first, Bella. The first one I called daughter and meant it. You taught me that family could be more than blood."

"You taught me too."

"We taught each other." He smiled. "That's how it works, when it works well."

---

The gift she'd brought him was simple.

A leather journal, similar to ones she knew he used. Inside, she'd written a letter—pages and pages of everything he'd meant to her. Everything she'd never quite said.

He read it standing by the fire, while she watched, while the stars emerged overhead.

When he finished, he didn't speak. He just pulled her into his arms and held on.

Some things didn't need words.

---

Later, much later, as she and Matt drove home:

"You okay?"

"More than okay." She watched the road unroll ahead. "I'm grateful. For all of it. Even the hard parts."

"Especially the hard parts?"

"You keep saying that."

"Because it keeps being true."

She reached for his hand.

"I love our life, Matt. I love what we've built. And I'm ready for what comes next."

"Whatever that is?"

"Whatever that is."
