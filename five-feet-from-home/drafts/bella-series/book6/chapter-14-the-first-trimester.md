# Chapter 14: The First Trimester

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Five: The Decision

---

They told no one.

Not Hawk. Not Addie. Not even Matt's mother, though she called weekly with increasingly pointed questions about grandchildren.

"It's too early," Bella said. "After everything—if something goes wrong—"

"I understand." Matt held her. "We tell them when you're ready. Not before."

---

The first trimester was terror masquerading as normalcy.

Every symptom was analyzed. Every twinge sent Bella into quiet panic. She took her progesterone supplements religiously, ate exactly what the doctors recommended, eliminated everything that might possibly pose a risk.

"You're not going to break the baby by having coffee," Matt observed gently.

"I might."

"You won't."

"You don't know that."

---

**[PREGNANCY DETAILS - FIRST TRIMESTER]**

Week six: heartbeat confirmation.

They sat in the ultrasound room, watching a tiny flutter on the screen. 120 beats per minute. Strong and steady.

"That's our baby," Matt whispered.

Bella couldn't speak. Could only stare at the proof of what they'd created, fought for, waited for.

Week eight: the nausea arrived.

Not morning sickness—all-day sickness. Bella discovered a new relationship with saltines and ginger ale. Work happened in brief windows between waves of misery.

"This is good," she told herself, head over the toilet. "This means the hormones are working."

The hormones were definitely working.

---

Week ten, they had a scare.

Spotting. Not much, but enough to send Bella into full panic mode. She called the clinic with shaking hands.

"Come in," the nurse said calmly. "We'll take a look."

The drive was silent. Matt's knuckles were white on the steering wheel.

The ultrasound showed a healthy baby. The heartbeat was strong. The spotting was, according to the doctor, "not uncommon and likely nothing to worry about."

"Likely?" Bella repeated.

"Nothing is certain in pregnancy. But everything looks good. Try to rest. Try to trust the process."

---

They told people at twelve weeks.

The risk of loss dropped significantly after the first trimester. The secret had become too heavy to carry.

Hawk cried.

Actually cried—tears running down his face, voice breaking. "A grandchild. A real grandchild."

"Grace calls you grandpa."

"That's different. This is..." He couldn't finish. Just pulled Bella into a hug that lasted long enough for her to feel his heartbeat slow.

Addie was more composed, but only just.

"I knew," she said. "I suspected, anyway. Something was different these last weeks."

"Was I that obvious?"

"Only to someone who's loved you for years." Addie smiled through tears. "I'm so happy for you, daughter. Both of you."

---

The relief of sharing was unexpected.

Bella hadn't realized how much energy she'd been spending on the secret. Now that it was out, she felt lighter. More present. More able to actually experience the pregnancy instead of just surviving it.

"Twelve weeks down," she told Matt, that night. "Twenty-eight to go."

"But who's counting?"

"I am. Very carefully." She laughed. "I've never been more aware of time in my life."

"Then let's make every week count."

"Deal."
