# Chapter 31: Carrying Forward

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Eleven: Transitions

---

Life continued, as it always does.

Mandy's absence was felt in small ways—the gap in the network, the voice missing from conversations, the particular kind of wisdom that couldn't be replicated.

But the work she'd started continued.

"We have sixty-three veterans in residence," the program director reported, during a board call. "With seventy on the waitlist. Demand has never been higher."

"Then we expand," Bella said. "That's what she would have wanted. Impact, not stagnation."

---

The expansion plans took shape over months.

A second property, acquired through the trust. Staff hired, systems established. The Mandy Barrett Veterans Ranch becoming a model, just as Rosewood had become a model.

"You're doing it again," Matt observed.

"Doing what?"

"Building something that outlasts you. That's your pattern."

"It's Hawk's pattern. It's Mandy's pattern." Bella smiled. "I'm just following good examples."

---

**[LEGACY DETAILS]**

The memorial bench was placed at the fire pit.

Bronze plaque, simple words: "Mandy Barrett. She saw potential. She made it real."

Bella sat there often, in quiet moments. Talking to someone who couldn't answer, but who still felt present.

"Rose is reading chapter books now. James is obsessed with horses—you would have loved that. The program is growing. The ranch is thriving."

She paused.

"I miss you. But I carry you forward. Every day."

---

The POH work had evolved too.

Bella had been elevated to Herald—a title she'd resisted at first, unsure if she was ready for the visibility.

"You've been ready for years," Hawk said. "Stop questioning yourself."

"That's rich, coming from you."

"I question strategy. Not worth." His eyes were serious. "You know your worth now, Bella. Act like it."

---

The Herald duties were more public.

Ceremonial leadership. Representative functions. A face of the Order, albeit not the highest one.

"You're good at this," Elena said, watching her navigate a particularly complex ceremony. "The diplomacy. The presence."

"I've had practice."

"You've had talent. Practice just revealed it."

---

Rose asked about death for the first time.

"Where did the lady go? The one with the ranch?"

Bella considered how to answer. Rose was six now—old enough for truth, young enough to need gentleness.

"Her body stopped working. That happens to everyone eventually. But the love she gave people—that doesn't stop. It stays forever."

"So she's still here?"

"In a way. She's in the ranch she built. In the people she helped. In the things she taught me, that I'm teaching you."

Rose thought about this.

"So when you stop working, you'll still be here?"

"Yes. In you. In James. In everything we've shared."

"Okay." Rose nodded, satisfied with this answer. "Can I have a snack?"

Profound and practical, in the same breath.

---

That night, Bella wrote in her journal.

Something she'd started years ago, on Hawk's suggestion. Processing through words, tracking the journey.

*Mandy is gone but not gone. That's the strange math of love—it multiplies even when it subtracts. Every person I touch carries a piece of her, whether they know it or not.*

*I am the product of everyone who invested in me. Hawk. Addie. Elena. Matt. Mandy. And now I invest in others. The cycle continues.*

*That's how legacy works. Not monuments or buildings. People, carrying each other forward.*
