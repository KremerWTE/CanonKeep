# Chapter 18: The Fourth Trimester

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Seven: New Reality

---

No one warned her about the fourth trimester.

The three months after birth when the baby was still adjusting to existence outside the womb. When sleep was measured in forty-minute increments. When Bella's body felt like a foreign country.

"Why didn't anyone tell me?" she asked Addie, during a tearful 3 AM call.

"We tried. You didn't believe us."

"I thought I was prepared."

"Everyone thinks that. No one is."

---

Rose was a good baby, by all accounts.

She ate well, gained weight appropriately, hit her developmental markers. But "good" didn't mean easy. Nothing about newborn care was easy.

The feeding schedule alone was relentless. Every two hours, around the clock. Bella's sense of time dissolved into a blur of nursing, burping, changing, and desperately trying to sleep.

"I love her," she told Matt, on day four. "But I'm also so tired I could cry."

"So cry. It's allowed."

She did.

Week two: The hormonal crash.

Bella had been warned about baby blues, but experiencing them was different. The sudden drops in mood. The crying jags that came from nowhere. The overwhelming sense that she was doing everything wrong.

"You're doing fine," Matt said, finding her sobbing in the nursery at midnight.

"I forgot to write down when she ate. What if I feed her too soon? Or not soon enough?"

"She'll tell you when she's hungry."

"What if I can't tell? What if I'm terrible at this?"

Matt took the baby monitor from her shaking hands.

"You're exhausted. You're hormonal. And you're harder on yourself than anyone else would ever be." He guided her back to bed. "Let me take this shift. Sleep."

"But—"

"Sleep."

She slept.

---

Week four: The rhythm emerged.

Not a schedule—nothing that structured—but patterns. Rose preferred sleeping on her left side. She was calmest after her evening feeding. She hated baths but loved being wrapped in the warm towel afterward.

Bella learned to read her. The different cries, the subtle cues. Language before language.

"You're getting it," the pediatrician said, at the one-month checkup. "She's thriving. Which means you're doing something right."

"Something" felt like an understatement. She was doing everything. All the time. Without pause.

---

Help arrived from unexpected quarters.

Matt's mother stayed for a week, handling meals and laundry and the thousand small tasks that fell through the cracks of new parenthood.

Addie flew out twice, bringing supplies and experience and the particular comfort of someone who'd done this before.

Even Mandy sent a care package: practical items, a handwritten note, and a blanket that she'd knitted herself.

"People love you," Matt said, surveying the accumulated support. "They want to help."

"I'm not used to accepting help."

"Maybe it's time to learn."

---

Week six: The fog lifted.

Not completely—she was still exhausted, still figuring it out—but enough to see clearly. Rose was real. The bond was real. This was her life now.

"I'm a mother," Bella said aloud, watching Rose sleep in her crib.

The words still felt strange. But also true.

She was a mother. And somehow, despite everything, she was surviving.
