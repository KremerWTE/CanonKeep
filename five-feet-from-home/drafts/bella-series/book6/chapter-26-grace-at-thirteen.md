# Chapter 26: Grace at Thirteen

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Nine: The Years of Little

---

Grace was thirteen now.

Teenage, technically. Full of opinions and moods and the particular intensity of a young woman discovering who she was.

"She's impossible," Elena said, during a video call. "One minute she's sweet, the next she's screaming about how no one understands her."

"That sounds like thirteen."

"That sounds like you at thirteen, according to your stories."

Bella laughed. "Fair point."

---

The relationship between Grace and Bella had evolved.

No longer the hero-worship of childhood—Grace was too old for that now. But something deeper: respect, trust, the kind of bond that survives adolescence.

"Can I ask you something?" Grace said, during a visit. They were sitting on the porch, away from the chaos of Rose and James.

"Anything."

"How did you know who you were supposed to be? When you were my age?"

Bella considered.

"I didn't. Not at thirteen. Not at eighteen. Not even at twenty-five." She looked at Grace. "I'm still figuring it out, honestly. It's not a destination—it's a process."

"That's terrifying."

"Or freeing. Depends on how you look at it."

---

**[GRACE DEVELOPMENT]**

Grace was struggling in ways Bella recognized.

Too smart for her classes. Too intense for most friendships. Feeling like an outsider in a world that valued conformity.

"I don't fit anywhere," Grace admitted, later that same visit. "School is boring. The kids there are boring. Everyone wants me to be smaller than I am."

"Never get smaller." Bella's voice was fierce. "Not for anyone. Not for anything."

"But it's lonely."

"Sometimes. But being authentically yourself, even when it's lonely, is better than being fake and surrounded." She took Grace's hand. "The right people will find you. I promise. It happened for me, and I was just as lost as you are."

"Who found you?"

"Your grandfather, in a way. The family you're part of. Matt." Bella smiled. "And now, these two little monsters who require all my energy."

On cue, Rose appeared at the door. "Aunt Bella, James is eating something weird!"

The moment was over, but the conversation stayed.

---

Grace wrote her a letter afterward.

Handwritten, on real stationery—a formality that felt intentional.

*Aunt Bella—*

*Thank you for what you said. About being authentic. About the right people finding you.*

*I've been thinking about it ever since. And I realized something: you're one of my right people. You've always understood me in a way that's different from how Mom and Dad do. Not better—just different.*

*I want you to know that I'm going to keep being myself. Even when it's hard. Because you showed me that it's possible.*

*Love,*
*Grace*

*P.S. I'm babysitting Rose and James anytime you need. They're chaos, but they're the best kind.*

---

Bella kept the letter in her jewelry box, next to the pendant.

Some gifts were more valuable than anything money could buy.
