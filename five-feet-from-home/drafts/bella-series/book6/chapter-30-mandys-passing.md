# Chapter 30: Mandy's Passing

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Eleven: Transitions

---

The call came on a Thursday morning.

Not unexpected—Mandy had been declining for months. But knowing something is coming doesn't make it easier when it arrives.

"She's gone," the voice on the phone said. "Peacefully. In her sleep."

Bella sat down heavily. The world tilted, just slightly.

Mandy. Gone.

---

She'd visited two weeks before.

Mandy was frail by then, the illness having taken most of her strength. But her eyes were still sharp. Still the steel-green that had first assessed Bella all those years ago.

"I don't have regrets," Mandy said. "That's what people always ask. 'Do you have regrets?' I don't."

"None?"

"None worth mentioning." Mandy smiled. "I lived fully. I loved deeply—even when I thought I couldn't. I built something that mattered." She looked at Bella. "And I helped you find your path. That might be the thing I'm most proud of."

"You gave me Matt."

"I positioned a piece. You made the move." The chess metaphor, one last time. "You always made your own moves, Bella. I just showed you the board."

---

The funeral was held at Mandy's ranch.

The property where everything had changed. The barn where veterans found healing. The trails where Bella had first let herself imagine a different future.

Hundreds of people came. Veterans she'd helped. Wives Club members who'd known her for decades. The extended Barrett family. Matt's people. Everyone who'd been touched by Mandy's particular brand of fierce love.

"She was a force," Hawk said, during his eulogy. "The kind of person who moved through the world changing it, whether people wanted to be changed or not."

He looked at the crowd.

"Most of us were changed for the better. Some of us wouldn't exist as we are without her influence." His eyes found Bella. "She saw potential where others saw nothing. She invested when there was no logical reason to. She loved without expecting return."

His voice cracked.

"The world is smaller without her. But the legacy she left—in every person she touched, every life she redirected—that will continue forever."

---

After the service, Bella walked the property alone.

The barn where she'd learned to ride. The trail where she'd fallen apart and been put back together. The fire pit where Mandy had told her about loss and love.

"Thank you," she whispered to the empty air. "For everything."

The wind stirred the trees. Nothing answered. But she felt heard anyway.

---

The will was straightforward.

The ranch went to a trust, managed by the board Mandy had assembled—which included Bella, as promised. The veteran program would continue, expanded now with resources Mandy had accumulated over a lifetime.

"She planned everything," Matt said, reviewing the documents.

"Of course she did. She was Mandy." Bella smiled through tears. "She never left anything to chance."

---

Grief arrived in waves.

Not the crushing kind—Bella had processed much of this during Mandy's illness. More like a gentle current, pulling at unexpected moments.

Seeing a sunset that Mandy would have loved. Hearing a song they'd listened to together. Finding a note Mandy had written years ago, tucked into a book Bella had borrowed and never returned.

"You're allowed to be sad," Matt said.

"I know. I'm also allowed to be grateful." Bella looked at him. "She gave me you. She gave me this life. The sadness and the gratitude exist together."

"They always do, with people we've loved."

"I guess they do."
