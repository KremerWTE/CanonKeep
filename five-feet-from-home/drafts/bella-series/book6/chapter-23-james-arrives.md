# Chapter 23: James Arrives

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Eight: Growing Together

---

James was born on a Sunday afternoon.

Calmer than Rose's arrival—a planned induction, a shorter labor, everything going smoothly. 8 pounds, 1 ounce. A big boy with Matt's eyes and Bella's determination.

"He's perfect," Matt said, holding his son for the first time.

"He's huge." Bella laughed, exhausted. "No wonder I felt like I was carrying a boulder."

---

Rose's introduction to her brother was carefully orchestrated.

Matt's mother brought her to the hospital the next day. Rose was uncertain at first—who was this creature stealing her parents' attention?

"This is James," Bella said, positioning Rose to see. "He's your brother."

Rose examined the baby with suspicion.

"Mine?" she asked.

"Yours. And Mama's. And Daddy's. He belongs to all of us."

Rose considered this. Then, with the decisive authority of a two-year-old, she kissed his forehead.

"My James."

Crisis averted. For now.

---

Coming home was different with a toddler.

No quiet recovery period. No gentle transition. Rose required attention, entertainment, reassurance that she hadn't been replaced.

"Divide and conquer," Matt suggested. He would handle Rose while Bella focused on newborn care. They would trade off as needed.

It worked. Mostly. The house was chaos—but organized chaos. Sustainable chaos.

---

The feeding schedule was brutal.

James ate voraciously, more than Rose had at the same age. Bella spent entire days on the couch, nursing, while Rose played nearby and Matt managed everything else.

"I feel useless," she admitted. "I'm just a milk dispenser."

"You're feeding our son. That's not useless—that's essential."

"It doesn't feel essential. It feels endless."

"Those can be the same thing."

---

Week two: James smiled.

Early for a smile—probably gas—but Bella chose to believe it was real. This tiny human, already finding joy in the world.

"He's going to be happy," she told Matt. "I can tell already."

"How can you tell?"

"He sleeps well. He eats well. He's not fussy." She stroked his cheek. "He's at peace with being here."

"Unlike Rose, who came out fighting."

"She still hasn't stopped."

---

The family descended again.

Hawk and Addie. The extended Barrett clan. Matt's relatives. Everyone wanting to meet James, to hold him, to welcome him to the village.

Rose tolerated the attention with the magnanimity of an eldest child who knew she was still the favorite. She showed James off like a prized possession: "My James. My brother."

"She's handling it well," Addie observed.

"She's possessive. I'm not sure that's the same as handling it well."

"At two? Possessive is exactly right." Addie smiled. "She'll learn to share. They all do."

---

That night, Bella stood in the nursery doorway.

James in the crib. Rose in her toddler bed across the hall. Matt asleep, finally, in their room.

Two children. A full house. A family she'd never dared to dream of.

"We did it," she whispered to no one. "We really did it."

The pendant at her throat caught the moonlight. A reminder of all the steps that had led here.

She was home.
