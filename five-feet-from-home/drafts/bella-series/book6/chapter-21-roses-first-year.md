# Chapter 21: Rose's First Year

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Eight: Growing Together

---

The year unfolded in milestones.

First solid food—sweet potatoes, met with suspicious examination and eventual enthusiasm. First tooth—a bottom one, emerging at 6 AM to the soundtrack of miserable crying. First word—"Da," which Matt claimed and Bella disputed.

"She was saying 'that,'" Bella insisted. "She was pointing at the dog."

"She was looking at me. And she said 'Da.'"

"We don't have a dog."

"Exactly. She was saying Dad."

---

Crawling came at seven months.

Walking at eleven. Rose was early, determined, refusing to be left behind by a world that was moving too fast.

"She's like you," Matt observed, watching Rose pull herself up on the coffee table for the hundredth time. "Stubborn. Driven. Doesn't know when to quit."

"Those are compliments?"

"Those are facts. Whether they're compliments depends on the day."

---

**[MILESTONE MONTAGE - FIRST YEAR]**

The photos accumulated.

Rose covered in first birthday cake, wearing more than she'd eaten. Rose on a horse—Bella's horse, carefully supervised, a moment of joy captured forever. Rose at the Charlotte compound, surrounded by the sprawling family that had claimed her before she was born.

Grace, now eleven, had become Rose's unofficial babysitter during visits. The hero worship was mutual.

"She follows me everywhere," Grace reported proudly. "Like a baby duck."

"That's because you're her favorite person."

"Obviously. I'm excellent with babies."

---

The first birthday party was held at the ranch.

Both families came. Matt's mother, his sisters, their children. Hawk and Addie. Jasper and Elena with Grace. Kendra and Chris. Harper, flown in from Miami.

"This is a lot of people," Bella said, surveying the chaos.

"This is love," Addie corrected. "Let her see it. Let her grow up knowing she has a village."

---

Hawk was different with Rose.

Softer. Gentler. The sharp edges that had defined him for decades seemed to dissolve in her presence.

"She's got you wrapped around her finger," Bella observed.

"Completely." He didn't seem bothered. "And I wouldn't have it any other way."

He sat with Rose for hours, reading books, playing simple games, talking to her in a voice Bella had never heard him use before. The patriarch becoming a grandfather, learning tenderness late in life.

"Thank you," he said to Bella, one evening.

"For what?"

"For this. For her. For letting me be part of it." His eyes were damp. "I never thought I'd have this, Bella. A grandchild I could love openly. A family that wasn't about business or legacy or strategic positioning."

"That's exactly what it's about, though." She smiled. "Just a different kind of legacy. A better kind."

---

Rose's first word that was definitely a word came at thirteen months.

"Mama," she said, clear as day, reaching for Bella across the room.

Bella dropped everything and scooped her up, tears streaming.

"You said it. You said Mama."

Rose, unaware of the significance, patted Bella's face and tried to grab her earring.

"That's me," Bella whispered. "I'm your mama. Forever."
