# Chapter 6: Grace Growing Up

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Part Two: The Adjustment

---

Grace was eight now.

Eight years old and already showing signs of who she'd become: sharp, observant, with a streak of mischief that Elena couldn't quite contain.

"Aunt Bella!" she shouted, launching herself at Bella during a Charlotte visit. "You're here! Finally! I have SO much to tell you!"

"I'm listening."

"Okay so first, Mom says I can't get a horse but that's UNFAIR because you have horses and I LOVE horses and second, I made a new friend at school named Marcus which is funny because you know someone named Marcus and third—"

"Grace." Elena's voice was patient but firm. "Breathe."

Grace did not breathe. She continued at high speed for another three minutes.

---

The relationship between Bella and Grace had deepened over the years.

Not quite aunt and niece—Grace had actual aunts. More like... chosen family. An adult who wasn't a parent, who could be trusted with secrets, who understood what it was like to feel different.

"Am I weird?" Grace asked, during a quiet moment away from the adults.

"What makes you ask that?"

"The kids at school. They say I'm too intense. Too much." Grace looked down. "Like I talk too loud or care too much about things."

Bella sat beside her.

"You know what? When I was your age, people said the same thing about me."

"Really?"

"Really. I was always too something. Too driven. Too focused. Too intense." Bella took her hand. "It took me a long time to realize that 'too much' is just another way of saying 'more than they can handle.'"

"So I'm not weird?"

"You're exactly who you're supposed to be. And the people who matter—the ones who get to be in your life—they'll love you for all that intensity."

Grace was quiet for a moment.

"Like Uncle Matt loves you?"

"Exactly like that."

---

**[OUTFIT DETAILS - CHARLOTTE VISIT]**

Bella had learned to dress for Grace visits.

Nothing too formal—Grace would immediately try to wrestle her into something more appropriate for their adventures. Today: comfortable jeans, a soft sweater, sneakers that could handle anything from hiking to impromptu dance parties.

The pendant stayed, always. Grace liked to touch it, tracing the design with her fingers.

"This is magic," she'd declared once. "I can tell."

"It's just jewelry."

"It's MAGIC jewelry. The kind that makes things happen."

Bella hadn't corrected her. Maybe she wasn't wrong.

---

That evening, Bella sat with Jasper and Elena after Grace was in bed.

"She's struggling a little," Elena admitted. "The social dynamics at school. She's too bright for her age group, too intense for the teachers."

"What are you considering?"

"Maybe a different school. Something that can challenge her better." Jasper looked thoughtful. "She reminds me of you, actually. That same restless energy. That same need to be doing, building, becoming."

"I take that as a compliment."

"It's meant as one." Elena smiled. "We're hoping you'll stay involved. As she grows. She looks up to you in a way that's different from how she sees us."

"I'm not going anywhere." Bella meant it. "She's family. All of you are."

---

The fire pit that night was smaller—just Bella and Hawk, the way it had been at the beginning.

"You're good with her," Hawk observed. "Grace. Natural."

"I love her."

"That much is obvious." He sipped his whiskey. "Ever think about having your own?"

Bella was quiet for a moment.

"More and more. Matt and I have been talking about it."

"And?"

"And I think I'm getting ready. Not yet—but the idea doesn't terrify me anymore." She looked at him. "Is that strange? After years of being certain I didn't want it?"

"People change. Circumstances change. Readiness comes when it comes." Hawk's eyes were warm. "For what it's worth, I think you'd be an exceptional mother."

"Based on what?"

"Based on everything you are. The way you love. The way you fight for your people." He smiled. "You have instincts, daughter. Trust them."
