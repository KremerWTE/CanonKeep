# Chapter 32: The Accustomed Life

## Book 6: "I've Grown Accustomed to Her Face"
### Conclusion

---

The face she'd grown accustomed to was her own.

Not just in the mirror—in the life. The routines that had become rhythm. The people who had become home. The identity that had, finally, fully formed.

Bella Holloway. Mother. Wife. Business owner. Board member. Herald. Daughter.

All of it true. All of it hers.

---

A typical day now:

Morning with the kids—breakfast chaos, school prep, the particular joy of young voices filling the house.

Work hours—Rosewood strategy, BSS board calls, POH coordination. All of it managed, none of it consuming.

Afternoon—sometimes at the ranch, sometimes in Charlotte, sometimes just being present for whatever the kids needed.

Evening—family dinner, homework help, the small rituals that built a life.

Night—Matt, the porch, the conversations that never ended.

"This is what I wanted," she told him, one ordinary evening. "Exactly this."

"It took a while to get here."

"It took exactly as long as it needed to."

---

**[REFLECTION - THE JOURNEY]**

She thought sometimes about the woman she'd been.

The young professional who didn't believe she was worth choosing. The protégé who wondered if she'd ever be more than potential. The crisis manager who was better at solving everyone else's problems than her own.

That woman felt like a stranger now. Or not a stranger—a relative. Someone connected to her by blood and experience, but fundamentally different.

"Do you ever miss who you were?" Matt asked.

"No." The answer came easily. "I'm grateful for her. She got me here. But I wouldn't go back."

"What would you tell her? If you could?"

Bella considered.

"That it works out. Not easily, not simply—but it works out. That the people who are supposed to find you will find you. That love isn't earned through achievement. That being enough isn't something you become—it's something you recognize you always were."

"That's beautiful."

"That's true. Which is better."

---

Rose came out to the porch, interrupting.

"Mommy, James is being weird."

"Weird how?"

"He says he wants to be a horse when he grows up."

"That's... ambitious."

"It's WEIRD."

Bella and Matt exchanged glances. The small crises of parenthood, endless and endearing.

"Let's go talk to him about career options," Matt suggested, rising.

"I'll handle it." Rose disappeared back inside, already taking charge.

"She's going to run the world," Bella said.

"She's going to try." Matt smiled. "And James will be right there, quietly figuring out how to make it work."

"They're us. But better."

"That's the goal, isn't it?"

---

Later, the house quiet, the kids asleep:

Bella sat with her journal. The practice that had become essential, the processing that never ended.

*I've grown accustomed to her face.*

*Not just Matt's—though yes, his. The face I wake up to, the face I'll grow old with.*

*Rose's face, so like mine at that age. Fierce and questioning and absolutely certain she's right about everything.*

*James's face, Matt's eyes looking back at me, patient and deep.*

*My own face, finally comfortable in its skin.*

*The accustomed life isn't about stagnation. It's about settling in. Finding where you fit. Recognizing that the search is over—you're home.*

*I'm home.*

---

The pendant caught the lamplight as she wrote.

Still worn, always worn. Not for what it represented—the Wives Club, the journey—but for what it reminded her.

She was chosen. She was loved. She belonged.

Always had.

Finally believed it.
