# Chapter 13: The Second Crash

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Five: The Reckoning

---

It happened again.

Not as dramatically as Europe. Not the complete breakdown. But close enough to scare her.

Three months after she'd returned to full duty. Three months of proving, achieving, running.

Her body simply... stopped.

---

**[THEME: THE BODY'S REBELLION]**

She was in a briefing when her hands started shaking.

At first she thought it was caffeine—too much coffee, not enough food. She'd been forgetting to eat again.

Then her vision narrowed. Tunnel vision. The room swimming at the edges.

"Bella?" Kendra's voice, from far away. "You okay?"

"I'm fine. Just—"

She didn't finish the sentence. She was on the floor.

---

They carried her to medical.

Not the field medic—the actual doctor Addie kept on retainer for the compound. A former trauma surgeon who'd seen everything.

"Exhaustion," he said, after running basic tests. "Dehydration. Probable adrenal fatigue. When did you last sleep more than four hours?"

Bella tried to remember.

"That long?" He shook his head. "You're running on cortisol and stubbornness. Neither is sustainable fuel."

---

Addie was waiting outside.

Not angry. Not disappointed. Just... sad.

"I thought you'd learned," she said quietly.

"I did. I know the pattern. I know what I'm doing."

"Knowing isn't the same as stopping." Addie sat beside her on the medical cot. "You can see the cliff edge and still walk toward it."

"Then how do I stop?"

"You choose to. Every day. Even when the fear is screaming."

"The fear never stops screaming."

"I know. That's the work."

---

This time, she stayed at the compound for a week.

Not optional. Doctor's orders, Addie's insistence. No arguments.

The voice was furious:

*See what happened? They think you're weak now. They're watching. Judging. All that work, and you're right back where you started.*

But another voice was growing. Quieter but persistent:

*Maybe this is the lesson. Maybe stopping isn't failure. Maybe it's the only way forward.*

---

Hawk sat with her at the fire pit every evening.

Same questions. Same patience. Same steady presence.

"How are you doing?"

"Scared. That I can't change. That I'll always be like this."

"That's honest." He paused. "Change is possible. But it's slower than you want. And harder than it looks."

"How do you know I can change?"

"Because you want to. That's the first step. Most people don't even get that far."

---

Journal entry:

*Second crash. Not as bad, but bad enough. The body doesn't lie—it just waits for the moment you can't ignore it anymore.*

*I'm scared. Really scared. Not of failing—I'm used to that fear. Scared that the pattern is stronger than I am. That no matter how hard I try, I'll keep running until there's nothing left.*

*But I'm also... tired. Tired of the running. Tired of the fear. Tired of never being enough.*

*Maybe tired is where change starts. When the old way stops working. When the cost finally exceeds the benefit.*

*I don't know how to change. But I'm ready to learn.*

*Please. Let me be ready to learn.*
