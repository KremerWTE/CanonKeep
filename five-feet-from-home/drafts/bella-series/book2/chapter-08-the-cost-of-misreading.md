# Chapter 8: The Cost of Misreading the Board

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Three: The Transformation

---

The fire pit glowed hot against the cool night air. Hawk poured their whiskey, lit a cigar, and gave Bella the ritual look.

"How are you doing?"

Bella pulled her knees up, balancing her glass. "Stronger. But still waiting for the moment I fall apart."

Hawk gave a short nod. "And how can I support you?"

She thought for a moment. "Tell me the story you promised. The one about misreading the board."

Hawk leaned back, eyes catching the firelight. He was quiet for a long time.

"Afghanistan," he said finally. "Years ago. I was running a team. Tier One. Good men—the kind you'd bet your life on."

Bella watched the fire dance in his eyes.

"We were supposed to take a compound. Simple on paper. I read the board wrong. Thought the enemy would hold the north wall. They didn't. They came through the east. We walked straight into it."

His voice was steady, but the weight beneath the words was unmistakable.

"I lost two men that day. The best shots in my unit. I thought I'd played ten moves ahead, but I hadn't even seen the first trap. And you know what haunted me most? Not the firefight. Not the blood. It was knowing they trusted me to see the board, and I didn't."

He puffed his cigar, exhaled smoke slowly.

"That's the cost of misreading the board, Bella. Sometimes you don't just lose the game—you lose the pieces that trusted you."

Bella's voice was small. "How did you keep going after that?"

"By learning I couldn't do it alone. Even a grandmaster needs other players to run with him. I had to build pillars around me—Addie, Elena, Harper, Jasper—each one holding part of the weight I couldn't carry."

He leaned forward, eyes sharp. "Which brings me to you."

Bella blinked. "Me?"

"I've got an additional question for you tonight. You asked me not to let you disappear. I won't. You asked me to show you the board. I will. But Bella—" He tapped his chest for emphasis. "—I can't be your only pillar. You need someone who can run with you. Someone who won't let you carry it alone. Because you deserve that. And because no player survives this game without their own support."

Bella stared into the fire. The thought had never occurred to her—that she deserved a pillar of her own.

Hawk sipped his whiskey. "Daughter, I'll always be here. But if I'm doing my job right, one day you'll lean on someone else too. That's not weakness. That's how you win the long game."

Her eyes glistened. She nodded slowly.

For the first time, she didn't just feel like a piece on the board. She felt like a player.
