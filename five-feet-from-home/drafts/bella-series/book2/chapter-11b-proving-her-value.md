# Chapter 11b: Proving Her Value

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Four: Finding Her Place

---

After the crisis, the pattern intensified.

She'd proven herself once. Now she had to do it again. And again. Every day.

*You're only as good as your last success. They'll forget what you did. You have to keep reminding them.*

The voice was relentless. And Bella listened.

---

**[THEME: THE NEVER-ENDING PROOF]**

Her new standard became: be the one they couldn't do without.

In meetings, she anticipated every question.
On reports, she added context no one asked for.
On timelines, she delivered early. Always.

"You're making us look bad," Kendra joked. Half-joked.

"I'm just doing my job."

"Your job doesn't require 14-hour days."

"Mine does."

---

The exhaustion was becoming permanent.

Not the kind you could sleep off. Something deeper. A fatigue that lived in her bones, that coffee couldn't touch.

But stopping was impossible. If she stopped, they'd see. They'd realize she wasn't special. Just a scared girl pretending to be competent.

*Keep moving. Keep producing. Keep proving.*

---

Harper pulled her aside one afternoon.

"Can I give you some feedback?"

Bella's heart sped up. *Here it comes. The criticism. The truth.*

"Of course."

"You're doing exceptional work. Everyone sees it." Harper paused. "But you're burning the candle at both ends, and I can see the wax pooling at your feet."

"I'm handling it."

"Are you? Or are you just postponing the crash?"

---

The word crashed through her defenses.

*Crash. Like Europe. Like the breakdown she'd barely survived.*

"That won't happen again."

"It will if you keep this pace. I've been in this business a long time, Bella. I know the signs."

"So what do I do?"

"You scale back. You ask for help. You trust that other people can carry some of the weight."

"I can't."

"Can't or won't?"

Bella didn't answer. She didn't know the difference anymore.

---

That night, alone in her apartment, she looked at her reflection.

Dark circles under her eyes. Weight she'd lost without noticing. A tension in her shoulders that never released.

*This is what it costs. This is the price of proving yourself.*

And underneath: *Is it worth it?*

---

The next morning, she was back at her desk at 6 AM.

Nothing had changed. Nothing could change—not until she believed she was enough without the performance.

And she didn't believe that. Couldn't believe that.

So the cycle continued.

---

Journal entry:

*Harper said I'm burning out. She's right. I know she's right.*

*But what's the alternative? Coast? Let things slip? Show them I'm not as good as they thought?*

*The voice says: you have to keep proving. There's no rest until you've earned it.*

*But I'm starting to wonder if "earned" is a lie. If the finish line doesn't exist. If I've been running toward something that was never there.*

*Maybe rest isn't something you earn. Maybe it's something you allow.*

*I don't know how to allow it. Not yet.*

*But I'm starting to want to learn.*
