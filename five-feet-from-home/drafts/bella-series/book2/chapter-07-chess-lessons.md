# Chapter 7: Chess Lessons

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Three: The Transformation

---

The fire pit was alive with flame, Ranger TV humming in the dark.

Hawk set down a small wooden travel chess set between them. Bella raised an eyebrow.

"Homework?" she teased.

"Lesson," Hawk corrected, sliding the pieces out with muscle memory, setting the board as naturally as others set a dinner table.

As always, he began: "How are you doing?"

Bella swirled her whiskey, thinking. "Better than last time. But still trying to figure out what piece I even am."

Hawk smirked. "That's the problem. You think you're one piece. You're not. You're the player."

He tapped the pawns. "Most people see these as expendable. But pawns win wars. They advance, they block, they force the other player to react. You've been living like a pawn, Bella—useful, quiet, never expecting to cross the board. But you've already got the instincts of a queen. You just don't believe it yet."

Bella blinked. "So what do I do?"

"You learn to see the board." Hawk leaned back, puffed his cigar, eyes glinting. "Everyone around you plays their piece. Addie burns like a rook—straight lines, fast, deadly. Elena plays like a knight—unexpected angles. Harper's a bishop, sliding through spaces people forget to guard. Jasper's the king—every move revolves around him, but he can't move far on his own. Me? I'm the board itself. I shape the game around the pieces."

He looked at her squarely. "Your mistake is thinking you're just moving along. No. You're the one who can see how the pieces work together. That's what makes a player."

Bella studied the board, tracing the lines with her eyes. For the first time, the metaphor clicked.

"Hawk," she said slowly, "can the fire pit rules apply to the range too?"

He cocked his head. "What do you mean?"

"I want this—the honesty, the questions, the lessons. But not just here. If you're going to teach me how to see the board, I want to learn how to move on it too. Out there. At the range."

Hawk chuckled low, a rough sound of approval. "You're asking to turn words into action."

She nodded.

He leaned forward, tapped her glass with his. "Deal. Fire pit rules apply to the range. But fair warning, daughter—Ranger TV is a lot gentler than Ranger lead."

Bella smiled for the first time that night, a spark of fire in her chest that matched the flames between them.
