# Chapter 3: Europe Assignment

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Two: Trial by Fire

---

The assignment came six months into Bella's time at BSS.

Europe. A multi-week operation that Addie framed as "experience building" but felt more like being thrown into the deep end to see if she'd swim.

Bella packed her bags with shaking hands. She'd been on domestic trips before—day flights to DC, overnights in New York—but nothing like this. Nothing that felt so far from everything safe.

The work itself was what she'd trained for. Analysis. Logistics. Reading situations and people, spotting patterns that others missed. She was good at it. Maybe even better than she'd allowed herself to believe.

But Europe was different.

The stakes were higher. The players more dangerous. The pressure constant and unrelenting in ways that ground against her mental health like sandpaper.

She worked eighteen-hour days. Slept in fragments. Lived on coffee and adrenaline and the stubborn refusal to fail.

By the third week, she was cracking.

It started small. Missed details. Slower reactions. A tremor in her hands during briefings. She tried to hide it—pushed harder, stayed later, told herself she just needed to get through the next day, the next hour, the next minute.

But she couldn't outrun herself.

One night, she found herself sitting on the floor of her hotel bathroom, the shower running cold, tears streaming down her face. The walls felt like they were closing in. Every breath was a fight.

She was alone. Far from Charlotte, far from Addie, far from the fire pit where Hawk would ask how she was doing.

Except she wasn't alone.

A knock on her door. A familiar voice—calm, measured, the kind of voice that suggested everything would be okay.

Doc.

She hadn't known he was in Europe. Hadn't known anyone was watching out for her.

But Hawk had positioned him there. Just in case.

"Open the door, Bella," Doc said. "We need to talk."
