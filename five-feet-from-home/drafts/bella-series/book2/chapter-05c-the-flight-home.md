# Chapter 5c: The Flight Home

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Two: Trial by Fire

---

The mission ended successfully.

Bella learned this from Kendra, who arrived at the hotel room with dark circles under her eyes and a faint smile on her face.

"It's done," she said. "The CFO is out. The evidence is documented. The client is satisfied."

"I didn't help at the end."

"You helped at the beginning. That's what mattered." Kendra sat on the edge of the bed. "The pattern you found—the shell company connection—that was the key. Without that, we'd still be chasing ghosts."

Bella didn't know what to say. She still felt the shame of her breakdown, hot and heavy in her chest.

"Stop," Kendra said quietly.

"Stop what?"

"Whatever you're telling yourself right now. I can see it on your face." Kendra's eyes were sharp. "You did your job. You did it well. And when you hit your limit, you asked for help. That's not weakness, Bella. That's wisdom."

---

They flew back to Charlotte together.

Bella spent most of the flight staring out the window, watching the clouds scroll past like an endless white ocean. Kendra worked on reports, occasionally glancing over to check on her.

"It gets easier," Kendra said, somewhere over the Atlantic.

"What does?"

"All of it. The pressure. The stakes. Learning to carry the weight without being crushed by it." She paused. "My first field assignment, I threw up three times. Couldn't keep food down for days. Addie had to pull me off and send me back early."

Bella looked at her. "Really?"

"Really." Kendra's smile was wry. "I thought my career was over before it started. Instead, Addie sat me down and told me the same thing I'm telling you: the best people struggle. The worst ones don't care enough to."

---

Charlotte appeared through the clouds—green and sprawling, so different from Boston's concrete density.

Bella felt something loosen in her chest. She was going home. Or what had become home, in the strange way that places could transform when the right people filled them.

"Addie will want a debrief," Kendra said as they collected their bags. "But not today. Today, you rest. Tomorrow, you talk."

"What do I say?"

"The truth. Whatever that is for you." Kendra's hand landed briefly on her shoulder. "She won't judge you, Bella. She'll help you understand what happened. And she'll help you make sure it doesn't happen the same way again."

---

Hawk was waiting at the compound.

Not in the parking lot—he wasn't that obvious—but at the fire pit, even though it was the middle of the afternoon and they hadn't arranged to meet.

Bella saw him as she walked toward her apartment and changed direction without thinking.

"Heard you had a rough one," Hawk said.

"Word travels fast."

"It's a small compound." The familiar response. The ritual comfort.

Bella sat in her chair. The fire wasn't lit—too early, too warm—but the space felt the same. Sacred. Safe.

"How are you doing?" Hawk asked.

The question she'd been dreading. The question she couldn't escape.

"Broken," she admitted. "I felt so strong coming in, and now I feel like I was fooling everyone. Including myself."

Hawk was quiet for a long moment.

"You know what happens to steel when it goes through fire?" he asked finally.

"It melts?"

"It tempers. It becomes stronger. More flexible. Better suited for the work it was made to do." He looked at her. "You went through fire, daughter. You didn't melt. You bent. And now you get to decide what shape you take when you cool."

Bella's eyes stung.

"How can I support you?" Hawk asked.

"Just... be here. For a while."

"I'm not going anywhere."

They sat together in the afternoon light, and slowly—slowly—Bella began to believe that maybe, just maybe, she was going to be okay.
