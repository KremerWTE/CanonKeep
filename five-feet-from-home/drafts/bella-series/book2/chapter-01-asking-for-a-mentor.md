# Chapter 1: Asking for a Mentor

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part One: The Mentorship

---

Bella stood outside Addie's office for three full minutes before she knocked.

---

She'd dressed carefully that morning—more carefully than usual—because she knew what she was going to do. The outfit was somewhere between her old Boston analyst uniform and something new: tailored navy trousers (not black, she was learning to be braver about color), a silk blouse in pale gray that felt more expensive than it was, and a blazer that she'd left in her office because the Charlotte warmth still surprised her.

Her heels were low—sensible for a day of walking between meetings—and her hair was down, soft waves she'd managed with the curling iron she'd bought the week before. Professional, but not stiff. Put-together, but not armored.

She wanted Addie to see her as capable. But also, for reasons she couldn't quite articulate, as herself.

---

The Charlotte headquarters of BSS was nothing like the Boston finance firm. There were no cubicle farms or sterile conference rooms. The space felt alive—tactical but elegant, filled with people who moved with purpose. Bella had been here for two weeks, and she still felt like an imposter every time she walked through the door.

The analyst work was familiar enough. Data collection. Pattern analysis. Report writing. She could do that in her sleep.

But this wasn't just analysis. She'd seen enough in her short time here to understand that BSS operated on a different level—crisis management, strategic intervention, problems that couldn't be solved with spreadsheets and client calls.

And at the center of it all was Addie Barrett.

Bella had watched her from the edges. The way Addie moved through rooms like she owned them. The way people deferred to her without being asked. The quiet authority in her voice, the certainty in her decisions.

She was everything Bella wasn't. Confident. Commanding. A leader who didn't apologize for taking up space.

And somehow, Addie had chosen her.

Bella still didn't understand why. But she knew she needed help understanding what she was supposed to become.

She knocked.

"Come in."

Addie looked up from her desk, a faint smile on her face. "Bella. Sit down."

Bella sat, her hands clasped tight in her lap. "I wanted to ask you something."

"Go ahead."

The words came out in a rush, unplanned and raw. "I don't know what I'm doing here. I mean—I know the work. The analysis. But this is different. This is bigger. And I look around at everyone here, and they all seem to know exactly who they are and what they're capable of, and I—I don't. I'm not like them. I'm not like you."

Addie was quiet for a moment. Then: "What are you asking?"

Bella swallowed hard. "Will you mentor me? I know that's probably—I don't know if that's something you do, or if I'm even worth your time, but I need someone to help me understand how to be... more. More than what I am."

Addie leaned back in her chair, studying her.

The silence stretched. Bella's heart pounded against her ribs.

Finally, Addie spoke. "You think you're not worth my time."

"I—"

"It wasn't a question." Addie's voice was gentle but firm. "You walked in here and immediately told me all the reasons I shouldn't help you. Do you do that a lot?"

Bella blinked. "Do what?"

"Talk yourself out of things before anyone else can."

The observation landed like a punch. Bella had no answer.

Addie leaned forward. "Here's what I see, Bella. A woman who saw a charity gala falling apart and fixed it without being asked. A woman who left a safe career because something bigger called to her. A woman who just walked into my office and asked for help, which takes more courage than you realize."

She smiled. "Yes. I'll mentor you. But we're going to work on that narrative in your head. Because the woman you think you are? She's not the woman I see."

Bella's eyes stung. She nodded, not trusting her voice.

"Good," Addie said. "Now let's get to work."
