# Chapter 2b: Second Fire Pit

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part One: The Mentorship

---

The second fire pit was different.

Bella came prepared this time—not physically, but mentally. She'd been thinking about Hawk's questions all week. *How are you doing? How can I support you?*

Nobody had ever asked her those things before. Not like that. Not with the expectation that she would actually answer.

---

She found Hawk in his usual spot, fire already crackling, whiskey already poured.

"You came back," he said. Not surprised. Just observing.

"You told me to."

"I suggested. You decided." He gestured to the empty chair. "Different things."

Bella sat. Accepted the glass. Let the warmth of the fire wash over her.

---

"How are you doing?" Hawk asked.

The ritual. The opening.

"Better," she said. "I'm starting to understand the work. Not all of it, but enough to feel less... lost."

"That's progress."

"Is it? I still feel like I'm pretending. Like everyone else knows something I don't."

Hawk was quiet for a moment. Then: "Let me tell you about chess."

Bella blinked. "Chess?"

"You know how to play?"

"The basics."

"Good enough." He stared into the flames. "Most people think chess is about attack. About capturing pieces. But the best players know it's really about position. About creating situations where your opponent has no good moves, even before they realize they're trapped."

Bella listened.

"The people at BSS—Addie, Harper, Kendra, Jasper—they're positional players. They don't react to problems. They shape the board so problems can't happen. Or if they do happen, the solution is already in place."

"And me?"

"You're learning the board." Hawk looked at her. "Right now, you're still figuring out which pieces are which. That's okay. That's where everyone starts. But here's what I want you to remember: you're not a pawn."

"What am I?"

"That's for you to decide. But you have more power than you think. You're just not using it yet."

---

The conversation drifted after that. Hawk told stories—carefully edited, probably, but vivid. Night missions in places he didn't name. Moments of decision that had determined whether men lived or died.

"Why are you telling me this?" Bella asked.

"Because you need to understand what's at stake." Hawk's voice was softer now. "We're not a regular company. We do things that matter. And sometimes, those things are dangerous."

"Addie never mentioned danger."

"Addie doesn't mention a lot of things until you're ready to hear them." Hawk smiled. "But you're ready. I can tell."

---

"How can I support you?" he asked as the fire burned low.

Bella thought about it. Really thought.

"Teach me," she said finally. "Not just the work. The thinking. The way you see situations before they become problems."

Hawk nodded slowly. "That's what the chess lessons are for. That's what the range days are for. That's what all of it is for."

"I don't understand."

"You will." He stood, stretching muscles that still moved like a much younger man's. "Same time next week?"

"Yes."

"Good." He paused at the edge of the firelight. "You're doing better than you know, daughter. Don't forget that."

He walked away, leaving Bella alone with the dying fire and a growing sense that something in her life was finally making sense.
