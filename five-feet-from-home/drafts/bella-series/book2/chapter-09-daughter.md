# Chapter 9: Daughter

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Three: The Transformation

---

The thank-you came a week later.

The compound was buzzing with its usual rhythm. Bella carried herself through the halls differently now—the pendant hidden under her blouse, the fire pit lessons settling into her bones. But the weight of Hawk's words still pressed on her chest.

She found him outside near the range, cleaning a rifle with calm, practiced motions. For a moment, she hesitated.

Then she stepped forward.

"Hawk?"

He looked up, eyebrow arched, waiting.

"I just... I needed to thank you. For everything. For seeing me when I didn't."

Hawk set the rifle down. "Don't thank me for doing what a father's supposed to do, Bella."

Her throat tightened.

Before she could think better of it, she moved in and wrapped her arms around him.

It was awkward at first—Hawk stiffened, more accustomed to battlefield grips than daughterly embraces. Then, slowly, he set a steady hand on her back. Not light, not tentative. Firm. Claiming.

"You're mine now," Hawk said quietly. "Daughter's daughter. That doesn't end."

Bella pressed her forehead against his chest, eyes closed. For the first time, she let herself believe it wasn't temporary.

When she pulled back, she managed a small smile. "I'll probably still get lost sometimes."

Hawk chuckled, shaking his head. "Good. Gives me an excuse to come find you."

---

That night at the fire pit, the ritual continued.

"How are you doing?"

"Missing my parents," Bella admitted. "My actual parents. And I feel guilty saying that here."

Hawk didn't flinch. He puffed his cigar. "Good. Say it again."

"What?"

"Say it. Out loud. You miss them."

Her voice wavered. "I miss them."

"You think saying that makes you less my daughter? Less Addie's? That missing them means you're letting us down?"

Tears prickled at her eyes. "Yeah. A little."

Hawk's gaze softened, the steel still there but warmer. "Daughter, listen to me. You will never let us down by loving the ones who raised you. Never. Family isn't a competition. It's layers. And you've got more layers than most."

He leaned back, tapping ash into the fire. "Here's what you're going to do. Once a month, you go home on the weekends. See them. Be with them. That's not weakness—that's rest. And rest is how you stay sharp for the long game."

"You mean that?"

"I don't waste words." His voice was firm. "You miss them, you go see them. And when you come back, you'll have a steadier hand on the board. You're not letting us down. You're keeping yourself whole."

For the first time, Bella didn't feel torn between two families.

She felt like both belonged to her.
