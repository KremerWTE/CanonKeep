# Chapter 3b: Preparing for Europe

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Two: Trial by Fire

---

The assignment came without warning.

"Europe," Addie said, dropping a file on Bella's desk. "You leave in three days."

Bella looked up from her analysis work, heart suddenly racing. "What?"

"A client situation. Sensitive. Kendra's team is handling the primary operation, but we need someone on-site for support." Addie's expression was unreadable. "I recommended you."

"I've only been here two months."

"I know."

"I don't know if I'm ready."

"I do." Addie sat on the edge of the desk. "Bella, you've been doing excellent work. Your analysis is sharp. Your instincts are developing. But you can't grow if you stay behind a desk forever."

---

The briefing lasted three hours.

Kendra led it, walking Bella through the operation with the clinical precision she brought to everything.

The client was a European bank—one with complicated connections to Vatican-adjacent interests, old-money families, and political figures who couldn't afford scandal. An internal audit had revealed irregularities. Someone was skimming. Someone high up.

"Our job," Kendra explained, "is to identify the problem, contain the damage, and extract the client without burning any bridges."

"Extract?"

"The CEO hired us. He suspects it's someone close to him. We need to confirm, document, and—if necessary—remove the threat without anyone outside the bank knowing what happened."

Bella felt her stomach tighten. "This sounds like espionage."

"This sounds like crisis management." Kendra's smile was sharp. "We're not spies, Bella. We're surgeons. We go in, we fix the problem, we get out. Clean."

---

She packed carefully.

Addie had provided a list: professional attire for meetings, casual clothes for fieldwork, a specific type of luggage that wouldn't draw attention at customs. Everything needed to fit in one carry-on.

Bella stood in her apartment, looking at her clothes—the Boston wardrobe that had followed her south, the new pieces she'd acquired in Charlotte—and felt the enormity of what was happening.

She was going to Europe. On a real mission. For BSS.

She'd dreamed about this. About being part of something that mattered. About proving she could do more than analyze spreadsheets and fix catering crises.

But dreams and reality were different things.

---

The night before she left, Hawk found her at the fire pit.

"Heard you're shipping out," he said, settling into his chair.

"Word travels fast."

"It's a small compound." He poured the whiskey. "How are you doing?"

Bella stared at the flames. "Terrified."

"Good."

"Good?"

"Fear is just awareness of the stakes." Hawk met her eyes. "The people who don't feel afraid are the ones who don't understand what they're risking. You understand. That's why you'll be careful."

"What if careful isn't enough?"

"Then you adapt. You improvise. You lean on your training and your team." He smiled. "And you remember that you're not alone. Even when it feels like it."

---

"How can I support you?" he asked.

Bella thought about it.

"Tell me I'll be okay."

Hawk was quiet for a moment. Then: "I can't promise that. Nobody can. The world doesn't work on guarantees." He paused. "But I can tell you this: I've watched you these past two months. Watched you learn, adapt, grow. You're stronger than you know. Smarter than you believe. And you have something most people never develop—the ability to see clearly when everything's falling apart."

He reached over and squeezed her shoulder.

"You're going to be okay, daughter. Not because the mission will be easy. But because you're the kind of person who figures things out. Trust that."

---

She left the next morning.

Kendra met her at the airport, already in travel mode—efficient, focused, carrying herself like someone who'd done this a hundred times.

"Ready?" Kendra asked.

"No."

"Perfect. Neither was I, my first time." Kendra smiled. "Let's go."

They boarded the plane. Bella watched Charlotte disappear beneath the clouds.

Whatever came next, there was no going back.
