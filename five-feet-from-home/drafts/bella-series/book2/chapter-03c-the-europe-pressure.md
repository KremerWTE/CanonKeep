# Chapter 3c: The Europe Pressure

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Two: The Assignment

---

The Europe assignment was supposed to be a test.

Hawk had said so directly: "This will push you. It's supposed to. That's how you find out what you're made of."

Bella heard: "This will determine if you deserve to be here."

They weren't the same thing. But she couldn't hear the difference.

---

The briefings started two weeks before departure.

Six-hour days of information. Intelligence protocols. Cover stories. Cultural considerations. Security procedures.

Bella took notes on everything. Reviewed them every night. Built flashcards. Memorized details that probably didn't matter.

"You don't have to know everything," the trainer said. "That's what the team is for."

But the team was made up of veterans. Operators with years of experience. People who belonged here.

She had to compensate for what she lacked.

---

**[THEME: PROVING THROUGH OVERPREPARATION]**

The preparation became obsessive.

She read intelligence reports until 2 AM. Studied maps of every location she might visit. Learned phrases in three languages she'd never speak fluently.

"You look exhausted," Harper observed.

"I'm prepared."

"Those aren't mutually exclusive. But they shouldn't be synonymous either."

Bella didn't understand what she meant. Preparation required sacrifice. That was the price of belonging.

---

The night before departure, she couldn't sleep.

Not from nerves—she'd prepared too thoroughly for simple nervousness. From the certainty that something would go wrong. That despite all her work, she'd fail.

*They're going to see you're not ready. They're going to realize you're not one of them.*

She got up at 3 AM and reviewed her materials again. If failure was coming, at least she'd have done everything possible to prevent it.

---

Addie found her in the office at 4 AM.

"Bella."

"I'm just—"

"I know what you're doing." Addie sat across from her. "You're trying to outwork your fear."

"Is that bad?"

"It's unsustainable." Addie's voice was gentle. "The fear doesn't go away with more preparation. It just shifts to the next thing. You have to learn to act despite the fear, not eliminate it."

"How?"

"Trust your training. Trust your team. Trust that we wouldn't send you if we didn't believe you were ready."

Bella nodded. But trust felt like a luxury she couldn't afford.

---

She flew out six hours later.

Exhausted. Over-prepared. Still convinced she'd find a way to fail.

The Europe assignment would test her. Just not in the ways she expected.
