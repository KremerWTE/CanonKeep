# Chapter 4b: The Spiral Begins

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Two: Trial by Fire

---

The first sign of trouble came on day three.

Bella was alone in the hotel room—a converted office, really, with secure communications equipment and stacks of files she was processing for the main team—when she realized her hands were shaking.

Not from cold. Not from caffeine.

From fear.

---

The operation was going badly.

Not catastrophically—not yet—but the careful plan Kendra had laid out was unraveling at the edges. The informant they'd relied on had gone silent. The bank's internal security was more sophisticated than expected. And the person they suspected—the CFO, a man with connections to half the powerful families in Europe—had started acting erratic.

"He knows something," Kendra said during the evening briefing. "Whether he knows about us specifically or just senses the pressure, he's making moves."

"What kind of moves?"

"The kind that suggest he's preparing to run. Or fight back."

Bella felt the ground shifting beneath her. This wasn't analysis anymore. This was real—real consequences, real danger, real stakes.

"What do you need from me?" she asked.

"Keep processing the data. Look for patterns we might have missed." Kendra's face was tense. "And stay in the room. No matter what happens, you stay in the room until I come get you."

---

Bella worked through the night.

The files blurred together—transaction records, communication logs, corporate filings. Her eyes burned. Her back ached. Her mind kept circling to dark places.

*What if we fail?*

*What if someone gets hurt?*

*What am I even doing here?*

The self-doubt was familiar. She'd lived with it her whole life. But this was different. This wasn't failing a test or disappointing a supervisor. This was failing people who were counting on her. People whose lives might be affected by her mistakes.

The weight of it pressed down on her chest until she could barely breathe.

---

By morning, she'd found something.

A pattern in the transaction data—subtle, almost invisible, but there. The CFO wasn't just skimming money. He was routing it through a shell company that connected to something larger. Something that went beyond simple embezzlement.

She documented everything. Cross-referenced. Verified.

Then she called Kendra.

"You found it," Kendra said, scanning the analysis. "This changes things."

"Is that good?"

"It's good for the mission. Not good for our timeline." Kendra's jaw tightened. "We need to move faster. Before he realizes we're onto him."

---

The next two days were a blur.

Bella barely slept. Barely ate. She processed data until her vision swam, then processed more. Kendra and the field team moved in and out of her consciousness, bringing updates, taking reports, never stopping.

She was operating on pure adrenaline and determination.

But underneath it all, something was cracking.

---

It happened on day five.

She was reviewing a surveillance transcript when suddenly she couldn't read the words. They blurred, scattered, refused to form meaning.

Her hands wouldn't stop shaking.

Her heart was pounding so hard she could hear it in her ears.

*I can't do this. I can't do this. I'm not good enough. I was never good enough.*

The spiral had begun.
