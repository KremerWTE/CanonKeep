# Chapter 11: The First Crisis Test

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Four: Finding Her Place

---

The call came at 2 AM.

Bella was awake anyway—reading reports, preparing for a meeting that wasn't until next week. When her phone lit up with Jasper's name, she answered instantly.

"We have a situation. Get to the office."

No details. No context. Just the urgency in his voice.

She was dressed and driving in under five minutes.

---

**[THEME: CRISIS AS PROVING GROUND]**

The war room was already full when she arrived.

Harper at the whiteboard, mapping something complex. Jasper on the phone, voice low and controlled. Kendra at a computer, pulling data.

And Addie. Standing back. Watching. The calm center of the storm.

"What do you need?" Bella asked, finding Harper.

"Everything you know about the Stratford account. Now."

Bella didn't have the file with her. She had it memorized.

---

For the next six hours, she worked.

Not frantically—there wasn't time for panic. But intensely. Every skill she'd developed, every hour of over-preparation, suddenly relevant.

She knew the players. The history. The weak points. She could answer questions before they were fully asked.

"How did you know that?" Jasper asked at one point, surprised by a detail she'd provided.

"I read the supplemental materials. All of them."

"No one reads those."

"I do."

A nod. Something like respect in his eyes.

---

The crisis resolved by noon.

Not perfectly—there would be cleanup. But the immediate threat contained. The client safe. The damage minimized.

"Good work," Harper said, exhausted but satisfied. "Everyone. That could have been much worse."

Bella waited for more. For specific praise. For acknowledgment that her contribution had mattered.

It didn't come. Harper moved on. Everyone did.

*Because that's how it works. You do the work, and then you do more work. There's no finish line.*

---

But something had shifted.

The way people looked at her now. The way they included her in conversations. The subtle change in status that crisis reveals.

She'd performed. Proved herself.

And the voice... the voice was quiet.

*For now. Until the next test. But this one, you passed.*

---

That night at the fire pit, Hawk asked the usual questions.

"How are you doing?"

"Tired. But good. I think I showed them something today."

"What did you show them?"

"That I can handle it. That I belong."

He was quiet for a long moment.

"Did you show yourself?"

The question caught her off guard.

"What do you mean?"

"You're looking for their approval. Did you get yours?"

---

She didn't have an answer.

The crisis had given her external validation. But inside, the worthiness wound was already healing over, preparing to doubt again.

*You did well. But can you do it again? Can you do it every time? What if the next crisis is the one where you fail?*

---

Journal entry:

*I proved myself today. Or I think I did. The crisis happened and I was useful. Valuable. Maybe indispensable.*

*It felt good. For about an hour. Then the voice came back.*

*"But what about next time?"*

*This is the trap, isn't it? No amount of proof is ever enough. Because the voice doesn't respond to evidence. It just moves the goalposts.*

*Hawk asked if I'd given myself approval. I don't know how to do that.*

*Maybe that's the actual lesson I need to learn.*
