# Chapter 14: Learning to Delegate

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Five: The Reckoning

---

Addie gave her an assignment.

"I want you to delegate three tasks today. Tasks you'd normally do yourself."

"Why?"

"Because the universe doesn't rest on your shoulders. And you need to learn that experientially, not just intellectually."

---

**[THEME: THE TERROR OF LETTING GO]**

The first task was small.

A research summary she'd normally spend hours perfecting. She handed it to a junior analyst with brief instructions.

"You want me to do this?" The analyst looked confused. "I thought you always did these."

"I'm... trying something new."

The task left her hands. And Bella felt physically ill.

---

The second task was harder.

A client briefing preparation. Her specialty. The thing she was known for.

She gave it to Harper's team instead.

"You sure?" Harper asked, one eyebrow raised.

"Addie's orders."

"Interesting." Harper didn't press. "We'll handle it."

---

The third task almost broke her.

Letting someone else run a meeting she'd scheduled. Sitting in the back, silent, while Kendra led the discussion.

Her hands were clenched the entire time. Every instinct screaming to jump in, to fix, to prove.

But she stayed quiet. Let it happen.

And the meeting went fine. Better than fine. Kendra had insights Bella wouldn't have thought of.

---

Afterward, she sat with the evidence.

Three tasks delegated. None had collapsed. No one had realized she was incompetent. Nothing fell apart.

*But they will. Eventually. You just got lucky this time.*

The voice, persistent as always.

But weaker. Slightly. The evidence was harder to ignore.

---

"How did it feel?" Addie asked that evening.

"Horrible. Like I was dying."

Addie laughed. "That's normal. You're killing the part of you that needs to control everything. Of course it feels like death."

"Will it get easier?"

"With practice. The first time you trust, it's agony. The hundredth time, it's just life."

---

Journal entry:

*Three tasks delegated. World didn't end. I'm still here. Still employed. Still valued.*

*The voice says I got lucky. That next time will be different. That I can't trust anyone to do things right.*

*But the evidence says otherwise. People handled it. Some of them handled it better than I would have.*

*Maybe I'm not as indispensable as I thought. Maybe that's... good? Maybe it means I can rest without everything collapsing?*

*New thought: what if my value isn't in what I do? What if it's in who I am?*

*I don't believe that yet. But I wrote it down. First step.*
