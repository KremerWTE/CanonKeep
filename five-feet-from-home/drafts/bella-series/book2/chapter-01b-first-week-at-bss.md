# Chapter 1b: First Week at BSS

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part One: The Mentorship

---

The first week was a blur of information.

Bella had thought she understood what BSS did from her conversation with Addie. Crisis management. Strategic consulting. The usual corporate language.

She was wrong.

---

Day One: Orientation.

They gave her credentials. A laptop. An employee handbook that was classified. A tour of the facility that somehow managed to show her everything and nothing.

"You'll have questions," the HR coordinator said—a woman in her fifties with silver hair and the demeanor of a librarian who'd seen things. "Ask them to Addie. Not to Google."

"I don't understand."

"You will."

---

Day Two: The Watch Desk.

Bella was assigned to the Watch Desk—a room full of screens displaying news feeds, social media trends, client notifications, and something called a "threat matrix" that looked like it belonged in a government facility.

"This is where we monitor," Kendra explained. Kendra Chen—early thirties, sharp-eyed, with the kind of intensity that came from being excellent at everything. "BSS clients have access to real-time support. When something goes wrong in their world, we see it here first."

"What kind of 'wrong'?"

Kendra smiled thinly. "You'll see."

She did. Within an hour, Bella watched the team mobilize on a case involving a CEO whose ex-wife had just leaked documents to a journalist. Within two hours, the crisis was contained—legal contacted, PR narrative developed, journalist redirected with a more interesting story.

It was elegant. It was terrifying.

Bella took notes until her hand cramped.

---

Day Three: The Analysis Team.

They put her with the analysts—a group of five, all women, all brilliant, all slightly suspicious of the newcomer.

"Fresh from Boston," one said. Maya. Harvard MBA, according to the whispered introduction. "Addie's latest project."

"I'm not anyone's project," Bella said, surprising herself with the firmness.

Maya's smile sharpened. "We'll see."

The work was familiar—data gathering, pattern recognition, report formatting. But the subjects were different. Not stock performances or client portfolios. People. Situations. Crises unfolding in real time.

Bella found her rhythm by the end of the day. She was good at this. She'd always been good at this.

But "good" felt like the bare minimum here.

---

Day Four: The Compound.

After work, Addie took her on a proper tour of the compound.

It wasn't just an office. It was a world.

Houses clustered around a central building—some for staff, some for guests, some for purposes Bella didn't ask about. A gym that looked military-grade. A pool. Training grounds she glimpsed but wasn't invited to explore.

"This is where the inner circle lives," Addie said, leading her past a row of elegant homes. "Kendra. Harper and her family when they're in town. Hawk and me."

"You all live together?"

"We live close. There's a difference." Addie's smile was knowing. "Family doesn't always mean blood. Sometimes it means choosing the people you'd die for—and who'd do the same for you."

---

Day Five: The Question.

Bella worked late, as she always did, trying to absorb as much as possible.

When she finally emerged, she found Hawk by the fire pit.

"Rough week?" he asked.

She sat without being invited. "Overwhelming. I don't know if I'm doing enough."

"You're doing exactly enough." Hawk poured her a whiskey. "Nobody masters this place in a week. Or a month. Or a year."

"How long did it take you?"

"Still working on it." He smiled, a rare thing. "How are you doing?"

The question was becoming familiar. Almost comfortable.

"Scared," she admitted. "But... hopeful? I think?"

"That's the right answer." Hawk clinked his glass against hers. "Welcome to BSS, daughter."
