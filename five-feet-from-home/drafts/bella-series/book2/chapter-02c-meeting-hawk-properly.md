# Chapter 2c: Meeting Hawk Properly

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part One: The Mentorship

---

Bella learned about Hawk in fragments.

The fire pit conversations told her some things—the Tier One background, the years of service, the quiet authority that came from having faced death and chosen differently. But the full picture assembled slowly, like a mosaic she was building from scattered tiles.

---

It was Addie who filled in the first gaps.

They were in her office, reviewing a case file, when Bella asked, "How did you and Hawk meet?"

Addie smiled—the soft one, the one that suggested memories she didn't share with everyone.

"He was consulting on a security assessment for a client. I was handling the crisis that made the assessment necessary." She leaned back. "We spent three days together in a safe house, reviewing protocols and watching cable news. By day two, I knew he was different."

"Different how?"

"Most men in that world—operators, soldiers, security types—they compartmentalize. Work is work. Life is life. Hawk doesn't compartmentalize. Everything he does is connected to who he is. When he protects someone, it's not professional. It's personal."

Bella thought about the fire pit. The two questions. The way Hawk looked at her like she mattered.

"He's like that with everyone?"

"No." Addie's smile sharpened. "He's like that with family. And he's decided you're family."

---

Bella asked Hawk about it the next week at the fire pit.

"Addie says you don't compartmentalize."

He laughed—a rare sound, low and genuine. "Addie knows me too well."

"Is that true? That everything's connected?"

Hawk stared into the flames, choosing his words.

"When I was downrange, we had a saying: 'You can't protect what you don't love.' People think professionalism means distance. Objectivity. But the best operators I knew—the ones who survived, who succeeded—they loved what they protected. The mission. Their team. Their country. Love made them fierce."

"That seems dangerous. Loving things you might lose."

"Everything worth having can be lost." Hawk's voice was quiet. "That's what makes it worth having."

---

He told her about his family that night.

His sister, Evie—the one who ran the catering company, who'd married young and lost her husband to cancer, who was raising two kids on her own with a smile that never faltered.

His parents—gone now, both of them, his father to a heart attack, his mother to the grief that followed.

His brothers-in-arms—some still alive, some in graves in places he couldn't name, some lost to their own demons in the years after coming home.

"I wasn't supposed to survive as long as I did," he said. "Statistically. The missions we ran, the places we went... the odds were against us. Every single time."

"But you did."

"I did." He smiled. "And now I'm here. By the fire. With you. Seems like a fair trade."

---

"Why did you choose me?" Bella asked. "As a daughter, I mean. You barely knew me."

Hawk was quiet for a long moment.

"The night we met—that first fire pit—you sat down without being invited. Most people don't do that. They wait. They hedge. They protect themselves from the risk of being unwanted."

"I was just tired. I wasn't thinking."

"Exactly." Hawk's eyes glinted in the firelight. "You weren't performing. You were real. And real people are rare." He paused. "I saw something in you that night. Something that reminded me of the best people I've known. The ones who serve without needing credit. Who love without needing guarantees. Who show up even when it's hard."

Bella's throat tightened.

"I didn't choose you because I knew who you were," Hawk said. "I chose you because I saw who you're becoming. And I wanted to be part of that."

---

The fire burned low. The stars came out. Neither of them moved to leave.

"Thank you," Bella said finally. "For seeing me."

"Thank you for being worth seeing."
