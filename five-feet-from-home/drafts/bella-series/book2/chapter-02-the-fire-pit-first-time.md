# Chapter 2: The Fire Pit (First Time)

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part One: The Mentorship

---

The compound was quiet at night.

Bella had been working late, the way she always did—pushing herself to prove she belonged, to justify Addie's faith in her. When she finally stepped outside, the cool air hit her like a relief.

That's when she saw the fire.

Hawk was sitting by the stone pit at the edge of the grounds, a tumbler of whiskey in one hand, a cigar glowing faintly in the other. The flames crackled and popped, casting long shadows across the grass.

Bella hesitated. She'd seen Hawk around the compound—Addie's husband, the quiet force who seemed to observe everything and say little. Former Tier One operator. The kind of man who made rooms feel smaller just by standing in them.

He looked up. "You going to stand there all night, or you going to sit down?"

She sat.

Hawk poured a second whiskey without asking and slid it across the stones toward her. For a long moment, neither of them spoke. Just the fire. The night. The silence.

"This is Ranger TV," Hawk said finally, nodding toward the flames. "Best thing we had downrange. Sit around the fire. Watch it burn. Let everything else go quiet."

Bella watched the flames dance. Something in her chest loosened.

"How are you doing?" Hawk asked. His voice was low, steady—not demanding, but present.

The question caught her off guard. People asked how she was doing all the time. They never meant it. It was just noise.

But Hawk waited. Really waited.

"Overwhelmed," she admitted. "I don't know if I belong here."

He nodded slowly. "And how can I support you?"

Bella blinked. "What?"

"Simple question. You're overwhelmed. What do you need?"

No one had ever asked her that. Not like that—direct, practical, without judgment.

"I don't know," she said. "I've never known what to ask for."

Hawk puffed his cigar, exhaled smoke into the night. "That's the first thing we're going to fix."

They sat together for another hour. Sometimes talking. Sometimes not. The fire burned low, and Bella felt something shift inside her—a door cracking open that she hadn't known was locked.

As she stood to leave, Hawk spoke again. "This pit is our space now. Fire pit rules apply."

"Fire pit rules?"

"No titles. No roles. Here, you're just Bella, and I'm just Hawk. Father and daughter, if you'll have me." His eyes glinted in the firelight. "Every time we sit here, I'll ask those two questions. How are you doing? How can I support you? That's our ritual."

Bella's throat tightened. She nodded.

"Good," Hawk said. "Now get some sleep. You look like hell."

She laughed—actually laughed—for the first time in weeks.
