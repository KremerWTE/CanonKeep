# Chapter 1d: The Schedule Begins

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part One: The Mentorship

---

Bella's first month at BSS, she built a routine.

5:00 AM: Wake up. Shower. Review the day's briefings.
6:00 AM: First coffee. Email triage. Preparation for morning meetings.
7:00 AM: Arrive at headquarters. Before anyone else. Always before.
7:00 AM - 7:00 PM: Work. Analysis. Training. Whatever was asked.
7:00 PM - 10:00 PM: Additional study. Background reading. Making up for what she didn't know.
11:00 PM: Sleep. If she was lucky.

She told herself it was temporary. Just until she got up to speed.

---

"You're here early," Harper observed, finding Bella at her desk at 6:30 AM.

"I like to be prepared."

"Prepared or obsessive?"

Bella didn't have an answer for that.

---

**[THEME: THE HUSTLE BEGINS]**

The pattern emerged without her noticing.

Every task became an opportunity to prove herself. Every meeting was a test she had to pass. Every interaction was data—did they respect her? Did they see her as competent? Was she earning her place?

She kept a mental tally of perceived failures:
- The question she couldn't answer in the morning briefing.
- The report that needed revisions.
- The moment she hesitated when Jasper asked for an opinion.

Each failure was evidence. Evidence that she didn't belong. Evidence that her presence here was a mistake.

The only antidote was more work. More hours. More proof.

---

Addie noticed first.

"You've been here until 9 PM every night this week."

"There's a lot to learn."

"There is. But burning out in your first month isn't the way to learn it."

"I'm fine."

"You're running on caffeine and adrenaline." Addie's voice was gentle but firm. "That's not sustainable."

Bella nodded, filed the advice away, and was back at her desk by 6 AM the next morning.

She couldn't slow down. If she slowed down, they'd see. They'd realize she wasn't enough.

---

The voice in her head was relentless.

*You're not as smart as them. You don't have their experience. You're lucky to be here—don't blow it.*

She knew it wasn't rational. She knew she'd been recruited specifically, that Addie had seen something in her.

But knowing and believing were different things.

So she worked. And worked. And worked.

And called it dedication.
