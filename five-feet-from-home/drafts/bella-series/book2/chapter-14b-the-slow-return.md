# Chapter 14b: The Slow Return

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Five: The Reckoning

---

Returning to work was different this time.

Not the frantic proving of before. Something more measured. Still intense—she didn't know how to be anything else—but with guardrails.

Addie had set clear boundaries:
- 50 hours maximum per week
- Weekends off except emergencies
- Fire pit sessions every Thursday
- Monthly check-ins with her

"You'll hate it at first," Addie warned. "The limits will feel like punishment."

She was right.

---

**[THEME: STRUCTURED RECOVERY]**

The first week, Bella checked the clock constantly.

*It's only 5 PM. I could get two more hours in. The deadline isn't until—*

No. The guardrail was clear. 50 hours. She was at 48. Time to go home.

Leaving at a normal hour felt shameful. Wrong. Like everyone was watching her fail.

But no one was watching. No one noticed. The work continued without her.

---

The weekends were harder.

What did normal people do with two consecutive days off? Bella had forgotten. Or maybe she'd never known.

She tried reading. Her mind wouldn't settle.
She tried exercise. Better, but not enough.
She tried cooking. Burned everything.
She tried just... sitting. That lasted approximately four minutes.

"You're detoxing from productivity addiction," Kendra told her. "It takes time. Your brain doesn't know how to idle."

"How long does it take?"

"Months. Sometimes years." Kendra smiled. "But it's worth it. I promise."

---

The fire pit helped.

Weekly now, not just when she was in crisis. A standing appointment with presence.

Hawk asked the same questions. Bella's answers slowly changed.

"How are you doing?"

Week one: "Struggling. Feel like I'm failing by not working."
Week three: "Better. Still uncomfortable, but the panic is fading."
Week six: "Something's shifting. I don't know what yet."

"That's progress," Hawk said. "Change doesn't announce itself. It just accumulates."

---

The work was still good.

Not as much of it—but better quality. More focused. Less scattered.

"You're actually more effective now," Harper observed. "Counter-intuitive, I know."

"How is that possible? I'm doing less."

"You're doing less, better. Rest makes the work sharper. Burnout makes it sloppy." She paused. "Took me years to learn that. You're learning faster."

---

Journal entry:

*Six weeks into the guardrails. It's getting easier. Not easy—but easier.*

*The voice still says I'm falling behind. That everyone else is working harder. That I'm going to be replaced.*

*But the evidence says otherwise. My work is better. People notice the difference. And I can almost—almost—remember what it feels like to have energy.*

*I'm not healed. Not even close. The worthiness wound is still there, whispering its lies.*

*But I'm learning to live with it differently. To hear it without obeying it. To choose rest even when fear says run.*

*Small victories. Baby steps. Forward motion.*

*I'll take it.*
