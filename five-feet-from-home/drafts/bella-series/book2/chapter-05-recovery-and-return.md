# Chapter 5: Recovery and Return

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Two: Trial by Fire

---

The rest of the Europe assignment passed in a blur.

Doc stayed close—not hovering, but present. He taught her to recognize the warning signs: the racing thoughts, the trembling hands, the way exhaustion could masquerade as determination. He made her eat meals she didn't want. Made her sleep hours she didn't think she could spare. Made her understand that the mission included taking care of herself.

By the final week, something had shifted.

Bella wasn't running at full speed anymore, but she was running steady. The analysis came easier when she wasn't fighting her own body. The decisions felt clearer when she wasn't drowning in cortisol and caffeine.

She finished the assignment. Not perfectly—there were mistakes, gaps, things she'd do differently next time. But she finished.

The flight back to Charlotte felt longer than the one that had taken her to Europe. She stared out the window at the clouds below, replaying every moment, every failure, every lesson.

Addie was waiting for her at the compound.

"You made it," Addie said, pulling her into a hug that was more maternal than professional.

"Barely," Bella admitted.

"That's how the first ones always go." Addie stepped back, studying her face. "Doc told me what happened. Not the details—that's between you and him—but enough."

Bella braced herself for disappointment.

"You know what I see?" Addie continued. "A woman who hit her limit and kept going anyway. A woman who accepted help when it was offered instead of drowning in pride. That's not weakness, Bella. That's exactly what I hoped you'd learn."

Bella's eyes stung. "I thought I'd let you down."

"Impossible." Addie's voice was firm. "You're mine now. You don't get to let me down by being human. You only let me down if you stop trying."

That night, Bella sat by the fire pit alone, staring into the flames.

When Hawk appeared beside her, pouring whiskey and settling into his chair, she didn't flinch.

"Welcome back," he said.

"Thank you," she whispered. "For Doc. For everything."

Hawk nodded once. "How are you doing?"

"Better," she said. "Still scared. But better."

"And how can I support you?"

Bella thought for a long moment. Then: "Teach me not to burn out. Teach me how to last."

Hawk smiled—the faint, wolfish smile she was learning to recognize as approval.

"Now you're asking the right questions."
