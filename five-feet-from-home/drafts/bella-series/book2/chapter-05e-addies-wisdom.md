# Chapter 5e: Addie's Wisdom

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Two: The Assignment

---

On the fifth day, Addie invited her to walk.

The compound property stretched for acres. Trails through woods. Views of distant mountains. Space to breathe.

"I want to tell you something," Addie said. "Something I recognized in you the first time we met."

Bella braced herself. For criticism. For the revelation of her flaws.

"You don't believe you deserve to be here."

---

The words hit like a physical blow.

"That's not—"

"It is. I can see it." Addie's voice was kind but unflinching. "You work twice as hard as anyone. You over-prepare. You never say no. You stay later, arrive earlier, and still feel like you're behind."

"That's just... being thorough."

"That's fear wearing a work ethic costume."

---

**[THEME: NAMING THE WOUND]**

They stopped at a clearing. A bench overlooking the valley.

"When I was young," Addie said, "I believed I had to be twice as good to be considered half as worthy. It was the world I lived in. And in some ways, I was right."

"So you understand."

"I understand the belief. I also understand it becomes a prison if you let it." She turned to face Bella. "You're not me. Your circumstances aren't mine. But the wound is similar. You don't feel like enough."

Bella felt tears coming. Fought them. Lost.

"I never have," she whispered. "For as long as I can remember."

---

Addie let her cry.

Didn't comfort with platitudes. Didn't try to fix it. Just sat with her in the pain.

When the tears slowed, she spoke again.

"That feeling is a liar. It's old—probably older than you can remember. And it will run your life if you let it."

"How do I stop it?"

"You don't stop it. You learn to recognize it. Name it when it shows up. Ask it what it's afraid of. And then—gently, always gently—you choose differently."

"Choose what?"

"To rest when you're tired. To ask for help when you need it. To trust that your worth isn't measured in output."

---

The voice in Bella's head was screaming now.

*She's wrong. If you stop working, they'll see. They'll realize you're nothing. You'll lose everything.*

"I can hear you arguing with yourself," Addie observed. "That voice that says I'm wrong—that's the wound talking."

"It's very loud."

"It gets quieter. Eventually. If you're willing to do the work."

"What work?"

"The internal kind. Harder than any operation. No external markers of success. Just you, facing yourself."

---

They walked back in silence.

But something had shifted. The wound had been named. Bella couldn't un-know it now.

*You don't believe you deserve to be here.*

She'd spent her whole life proving she did. Working for it. Earning it.

And it hadn't worked. Not really. The doubt was still there, demanding more.

What if there was another way?

---

That night, Addie gave her a journal.

"Write in it. Every day. Even if it's just a sentence. Especially when the voice is loud."

"What do I write?"

"The truth. Whatever that looks like that day."

Bella opened to the first page. Wrote the date.

Then: *I don't believe I deserve to be here. But maybe I want to learn how.*
