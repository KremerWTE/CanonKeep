# Chapter 7c: Hawk's Perspective

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Three: The Return

---

Hawk had seen her type before.

Not women like her, specifically. But people. People who ran. Who worked like their worth depended on it—because in their minds, it did.

He'd been one himself, once. Different reasons. Same pattern.

---

**[THEME: THE MENTOR'S VIEW]**

At the fire pit, he watched her more than talked.

Not because he didn't have things to say. But because she wasn't ready to hear them yet. And words given too early were worse than useless—they became walls to push against.

"She reminds me of you," Addie said one night, after Bella had gone back to her apartment.

"I know."

"Is that why you're so careful with her?"

"I made every mistake she's making. Burned out twice before I learned." He stared into the flames. "I don't want that for her. But I can't prevent it either."

---

The chess lessons were intentional.

Not really about chess—though she was getting better. About the pause. The moment between seeing the move and making it. The discipline of waiting.

"You rush," he told her once. "Even when you know better."

"I hate waiting."

"Waiting isn't passive. It's preparation without anxiety. There's a difference."

She didn't understand. He could see it in her eyes. But she was trying. That mattered.

---

What he saw in Bella:

Intelligence that sparkled like cut glass. Work ethic that would have impressed any operator he'd ever known. Emotional depth she tried desperately to hide.

And underneath it all—fear. The kind that never rested. That whispered constantly: *more, better, faster, or they'll see.*

He knew that fear intimately.

---

"How do I help her?" he asked Addie one night.

"You're already helping."

"It doesn't feel like enough."

"The fire pit. The questions. The consistency. That's what she needs. A place where she doesn't have to perform." Addie touched his arm. "You're giving her that. She's just not ready to accept it fully."

"She keeps going back to the hustle."

"She will for a while. Maybe years." Addie's voice was gentle. "But you're planting seeds. That's all anyone can do."

---

The questions Hawk asked weren't random.

*How are you doing?* forced presence. Forced her to stop and check.

*How can I support you?* offered help without conditions. Let her know she wasn't alone.

Simple questions. But for someone like Bella, revolutionary.

She'd probably never been asked how she was doing without an agenda attached. Never been offered support without strings.

---

One night, she surprised him.

"Hawk. Why do you do this? The fire pit. The questions. Caring about someone like me."

"Someone like you?"

"Someone who... can't stop. Who doesn't know how to be normal."

He let the silence stretch.

"Because someone did it for me, once. And I'm still alive because of it." He met her eyes. "That's what we do here. We pass it on."

---

She didn't respond. But she wrote it down later.

*We pass it on.*

Another seed, planted.
