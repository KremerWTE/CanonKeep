# Chapter 5b: Doc's Intervention

## Book 2: "The Rain in Spain"
### Part Two: Trial by Fire

---

Doc found her in the bathroom.

She was sitting on the cold tile floor, back against the wall, arms wrapped around her knees, trying to remember how to breathe.

"Bella." His voice was calm. Professional. The kind of voice that had talked people through worse. "I'm going to sit down across from you. Is that okay?"

She nodded. She thought she nodded.

---

Doc—she never learned his real name—was BSS's on-call physician. Former military medic. Now he traveled with high-risk operations, handling everything from gunshot wounds to mental health crises.

He was positioned in Europe for exactly this reason. Because Addie and Kendra knew that sometimes the body wasn't what broke first.

"Can you tell me what happened?" Doc asked. He'd settled onto the floor across from her, maintaining distance but present.

"I—" Bella's voice cracked. "I can't think. I can't—the words won't—"

"That's okay. You don't have to think right now. Just breathe."

"I can't breathe either."

"Then we'll practice." His voice was steady. "In for four. Hold for four. Out for four. With me, okay?"

They breathed together. Four counts in. Four counts held. Four counts out.

Slowly—so slowly—Bella's heart rate dropped.

---

"You're having a panic attack," Doc said, once her breathing had normalized. "Your nervous system got overwhelmed, and it triggered a fight-or-flight response. It's not weakness. It's biology."

"I failed."

"You didn't fail. You hit a wall. Those are different things."

Bella shook her head. "I'm supposed to be helping. Kendra needs me. And I'm sitting on a bathroom floor having a breakdown."

"You're sitting on a bathroom floor because you pushed yourself past your limits." Doc's eyes were kind but firm. "That's not failure. That's learning where your limits are."

---

He helped her back to the hotel room.

Made her drink water. Made her eat something—crackers, cheese, anything her stomach could handle. Made her lie down on the bed and close her eyes.

"You're going to rest," he said. "For real. Not laptop-rest. Not half-asleep-and-still-thinking rest. Actual, genuine rest."

"The mission—"

"The mission will continue. With or without you working yourself into the ground." Doc pulled the curtains closed. "Kendra knows. She understands. Right now, the best thing you can do for the team is recover."

---

Bella slept for fourteen hours.

When she woke, she felt different. Still exhausted. Still shaken. But the static in her head had quieted.

Doc was there, reading a medical journal in the chair by the window.

"Welcome back," he said.

"How long was I out?"

"Long enough." He set the journal aside. "How do you feel?"

Bella took inventory. "Like I got hit by a truck. But... clearer."

"That's the adrenaline finally metabolizing." Doc's smile was gentle. "Your body was running on stress hormones for days. It took time to crash. Now it's taken time to recover."

---

"Does this happen a lot?" Bella asked. "To people on missions?"

"More than anyone admits." Doc leaned forward. "The work you do—the work everyone at BSS does—it's intense. Mentally, emotionally, physically. Not everyone can handle it. And the ones who can, they still break sometimes. That's not shame. That's reality."

"Addie never seems to break."

"Addie's had decades to learn her limits. And she's got support systems you haven't built yet." He paused. "You will. But it takes time. And it takes experiences like this one."

Bella stared at the ceiling. "I thought I was stronger than this."

"You are. Strength isn't never falling. It's getting back up." Doc stood. "Kendra wants to see you when you're ready. No rush. Take the time you need."
