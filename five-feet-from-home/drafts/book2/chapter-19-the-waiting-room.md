# Chapter 19: The Waiting Room

*POV: Jasper Barrett*

---

The call came at 2:47 p.m., right in the middle of a client briefing.

Jasper felt his phone buzz in his pocket and ignored it—he'd learned the hard way that answering calls during critical meetings sent the wrong message, suggested that something else was always more important than the person in front of you.

Then it buzzed again. And again.

He pulled it out, saw Sara's name on the screen, and felt his stomach drop.

"Excuse me," he said to the clients—a CEO and her general counsel, both looking at him with the kind of polite irritation that suggested they'd noticed the interruption. "I need to take this."

He stepped into the hallway and answered. "Sara?"

"Elena's in labor." Sara's voice was calm, controlled, the kind of steady tone that only came from someone who'd learned to manage panic. "Her water broke about twenty minutes ago. We're at Presbyterian. You need to get here."

Jasper was already moving, his body operating on a kind of autopilot that had been honed through years of responding to crises. "How far apart are the contractions?"

"Five minutes. The doctor says it's moving fast—she thinks it might be quicker than it was with Grace."

"I'm on my way."

He ended the call and turned to find Harper standing in the conference room doorway, already holding his jacket and keys.

"Go," she said simply. "We'll handle the briefing."

"Harper—"

"Jasper." She stepped forward and pressed the jacket into his hands. "This is what we've been training for. You go be a husband and a father. Let us be the crisis managers."

He wanted to argue, wanted to list the seventeen things that needed his attention before five p.m. But Harper's expression left no room for negotiation, and somewhere beneath the work brain that never stopped calculating, Jasper knew she was right.

"Call me if anything goes red," he said.

"Call me when Lucas is born," she countered.

He nodded once, then turned and ran for the elevator.

---

The drive to Presbyterian took fourteen minutes in traffic that felt like it lasted hours.

Jasper gripped the steering wheel and tried to breathe through the adrenaline flooding his system. He'd been present for Grace's birth—barely. He'd made it to the hospital with thirty minutes to spare, still wearing his suit from a client meeting in Atlanta, his mind half-occupied with the acquisition deal he'd left hanging in order to be there.

He remembered the way Elena had looked at him when he'd walked into the delivery room—relief mixed with something that looked almost like surprise, as if she hadn't been entirely sure he'd make it.

That look had haunted him for years.

This time would be different. It had to be.

He pulled into the hospital parking garage, left the car in the first available spot, and ran for the elevator. His phone buzzed with texts—Harper confirming the briefing was handled, Addison asking if he needed anything, Cole sending a simple thumbs up emoji that somehow conveyed more support than words could.

Then another text, this one from Hawk:

*You've got this, brother. Elena's got this. We're all standing by.*

Jasper felt something loosen in his chest—gratitude, maybe, or the relief of knowing that he wasn't carrying everything alone. The team he'd built, the people he'd learned to trust, they had his back.

Which meant he could focus on the five feet in front of him: Elena, and the son they were about to meet.

---

The waiting room on the maternity floor was already full when Jasper arrived.

Sara met him at the nurses' station, her practical demeanor somehow more comforting than any amount of false reassurance. "She's in Room 314. They're checking her now—she's at six centimeters. The doctor thinks it'll be another two hours, maybe less."

"How is she?"

"Scared. Excited. Asking for you." Sara squeezed his arm. "Go. I've got Grace and Emma in the waiting room with coloring books and snacks. We're fine out here."

Jasper nodded and turned toward Room 314, but Sara's voice stopped him.

"Jasper?"

He looked back.

"You made it," she said, and there was something in her voice—approval, maybe, or the acknowledgment of how far he'd come from the man who'd almost missed Grace's birth entirely. "That matters."

He didn't trust himself to speak, so he just nodded again and walked down the hall.

---

Elena was sitting up in the hospital bed when he entered, her hair pulled back in a messy knot, an IV in her left arm. She looked tired and beautiful and terrified, and when she saw him, her entire face changed.

"You're here," she said, and it sounded like a prayer.

"I'm here." He crossed to her in three strides and took her hand, feeling the familiar weight of her wedding ring against his palm. "I'm not going anywhere."

She squeezed his hand hard, and he realized she was in the middle of a contraction—her breathing shallow, her jaw tight. He counted silently, the way the birthing class instructor had taught them seven years ago with Grace. Sixty seconds. Then the tension in her shoulders eased, and she exhaled slowly.

"That was a big one," she said.

"You're doing great."

"You don't know that. You just got here." But she was smiling, and Jasper felt the knot in his chest loosen a fraction.

"Then let me rephrase: you look great, and I'm in awe of you, and I'm exactly where I'm supposed to be."

Elena's eyes filled with tears. "Don't make me cry. I'm already leaking enough fluids."

He laughed, and the sound felt almost foreign—when was the last time he'd laughed in a hospital? But this wasn't the terrifying vigil of Elena's collapse last year. This was different. Scary, yes, but also full of anticipation, the kind of fear that came with waiting for something beautiful.

A nurse came in to check Elena's progress, and Jasper stepped back to give her space. His phone buzzed again—more texts from the team, updates he didn't need to read because Harper and Addison and the others had everything under control.

For the first time in his career, Jasper Barrett was not the one managing the crisis.

And it felt like freedom.

---

Two hours later, Elena was at nine centimeters, and the waiting room had filled with their entire inner circle.

Jasper stepped out to update everyone and found what looked like a small family reunion. Grace was sitting on Addison's lap, asking questions about babies and hospitals with the kind of innocent curiosity that made everything feel both ordinary and profound. Kendra sat beside them, braiding Grace's hair into an elaborate pattern that seemed to require intense concentration. Sara was talking quietly with Harper in the corner, both of them looking exhausted but present.

And in the chair by the window, Hawk sat with his arm around Addison's shoulders, his steady presence somehow grounding the entire room.

"How is she?" Addison asked when she saw Jasper.

"Strong. Stubborn. About to become a mother again." He looked at Grace. "Hey, sweetheart. You ready to meet your baby brother?"

Grace's eyes went wide. "Right now?"

"Soon. Maybe another hour."

"Can I see him when he's born?"

"As soon as the doctors say it's okay."

Grace thought about this for a moment, then nodded seriously. "Okay. I'll wait."

Jasper felt his throat tighten. She'd been waiting for so many things over the years—for him to come home, for him to keep promises, for him to be present instead of perpetually five feet away. But today, she was waiting for something they were all waiting for together.

That, he thought, was the difference.

Sara handed him a bottle of water. "You should eat something. It's going to be a long night."

"I'm fine."

"You're running on adrenaline and caffeine," Harper said from across the room. "Eat a granola bar, at least. You'll be useless to Elena if you pass out."

Jasper wanted to argue, but he was outnumbered and outmaneuvered. He took the granola bar Sara offered and forced himself to eat half of it, washing it down with water that tasted like plastic and hospital.

Kendra finished Grace's braid and looked up at Jasper. "Elena wanted me to tell you something."

"What?"

"She said to remind you that you're not in this alone. That all of us—" she gestured around the waiting room "—we're your team. Not just for work. For this."

Jasper stared at her, then at the assembled group. Addison, who'd built BSS with him and become the sister he'd never had. Harper, who'd been his right hand through every crisis and every triumph. Sara, who'd called him out when he needed it and supported Elena when he couldn't. Kendra, who'd somehow become part of their family through sheer force of loyalty and love. And Hawk, the operator who understood the weight of command and the cost of learning to let go.

They were all here. Not because he'd asked, but because this was what family did.

"Thank you," he said, and the words felt inadequate but true. "All of you."

"Save the speeches for after Lucas is born," Addison said, but her eyes were suspiciously bright. "Go be with your wife."

---

At 8:43 p.m., Lucas James Barrett was born.

Jasper held Elena's hand through the final contractions, coaching her breathing the way he'd learned from YouTube videos and half-remembered birthing classes. He watched the doctor guide their son into the world, watched the nurse clear his airways and wrap him in a blanket, watched Elena's face transform from pain to wonder as they placed Lucas on her chest.

"He's here," she whispered. "Jasper, he's really here."

"He's perfect," Jasper said, and his voice broke on the word.

Lucas was small and red and wrinkled, his tiny fists waving in the air as if he was already ready to fight the world. His eyes were closed, his mouth pursed in concentration, and when the nurse placed him fully in Elena's arms, he made a small sound that was half-cry, half-question.

"Hi, baby," Elena murmured, tears streaming down her face. "Hi, Lucas. We've been waiting for you."

Jasper leaned in and kissed Elena's temple, then touched his son's impossibly small hand. Lucas's fingers curled instinctively around his, and Jasper felt something crack open in his chest—not the breaking kind of crack, but the kind that let light in.

"You did it," he told Elena. "You were incredible."

"We did it," she corrected. "You were here."

Such simple words. But they meant everything.

---

Twenty minutes later, after the nurses had cleaned Lucas and run their tests, after Elena had been moved to a recovery room, Jasper stepped into the hallway to update the waiting room.

They all looked up when he appeared—Grace perched on the edge of her seat, Addison's hand clasped tight around Kendra's, Harper leaning forward with barely concealed anticipation.

"He's here," Jasper said. "Lucas James Barrett. Seven pounds, six ounces. Healthy and loud and perfect."

Grace launched herself at him, and Jasper caught her, lifting her into a hug that felt like an anchor. "Can I see him?"

"In a few minutes. Your mom wants to meet you first."

The waiting room erupted in quiet celebration—hugs and handshakes and Kendra wiping away tears she pretended not to be crying. Hawk clapped Jasper on the shoulder, his grip steady and grounding. Harper pulled him into a brief, fierce hug that spoke volumes.

And Addison stepped forward, her usual armor cracked just enough to show the woman underneath. "Congratulations, Jasper."

"Thank you." He met her eyes. "For everything. For holding down the fort today. For being here."

"Where else would I be?" She smiled—small and genuine and rare. "Now go introduce Grace to her baby brother. We'll hold the fort a little longer."

---

Inside the recovery room, Elena was sitting up in bed with Lucas cradled against her chest. She looked exhausted and radiant, the kind of beautiful that had nothing to do with makeup or styling and everything to do with the raw, fierce love of a mother for her child.

"Grace is here," Jasper said softly. "Are you ready?"

Elena nodded, and Jasper opened the door.

Grace walked in slowly, her eyes wide and cautious, as if she wasn't sure whether this moment was real. She stopped at the edge of the bed and stared at the tiny bundle in her mother's arms.

"This is your baby brother," Elena said gently. "Lucas. Do you want to meet him?"

Grace nodded, speechless for maybe the first time in her life.

Elena shifted carefully, making room, and Jasper helped Grace climb onto the bed. She settled next to Elena, her small hand reaching out to touch Lucas's head with the kind of reverence usually reserved for something sacred.

"He's so small," Grace whispered.

"You were this small once," Jasper said.

"Really?"

"Really. And now look at you—big sister, protector, teacher. Lucas is lucky to have you."

Grace looked up at him, her eyes shining. "I'm going to teach him everything. How to ride a bike and draw and make paper lighthouses."

"He's going to love that."

They sat together in the recovery room—Jasper, Elena, Grace, and Lucas—and for a moment, the world outside didn't exist. The crises at The Forge, the ownership disputes and supply chain nightmares, the endless demands of a life lived in perpetual motion—all of it faded into background noise.

This was the five feet that mattered.

---

Later, after Grace had been taken home by Sara with promises to return in the morning, after the nurses had checked on Elena one more time, Jasper sat in the chair beside her bed and held his son for the first time.

Lucas was impossibly light, his warmth seeping through the hospital blanket. His eyes were still closed, his breathing steady and soft. Jasper traced the curve of his son's cheek with one finger and felt the weight of responsibility settle onto his shoulders—not the crushing kind that came with crisis management, but the sacred kind that came with being trusted to protect something precious.

"I'm going to do better this time," he said quietly.

Elena reached for his hand. "You're already doing better."

"Am I?"

"You're here. You made it. You chose us."

Jasper looked at her—this woman who'd stood by him through his worst, who'd nearly died waiting for him to wake up, who'd given him the grace to learn how to be present even when it felt impossible.

"I'll keep choosing us," he promised. "Every day. Even when it's hard."

"Especially when it's hard," she said.

He leaned over and kissed her, careful not to jostle Lucas. "Especially then."

---

The waiting room had mostly cleared out by the time Addison and Kendra came back to say goodbye.

Jasper met them in the hallway, Lucas still sleeping in his arms. Addison stopped when she saw them, her usual composure slipping for just a moment.

"He's beautiful," she said softly.

"He is." Jasper shifted carefully, offering her a better view. "Thank you for being here."

"I wouldn't have missed it." Addison reached out and touched Lucas's tiny hand, her expression unreadable. "Elena asked me to come in. Is that okay?"

"Of course."

Inside, Elena was sitting up with pillows propped behind her, looking tired but content. When she saw Addison, she smiled and held out her hand.

"Come here," she said.

Addison crossed to the bed and took Elena's hand, and Elena squeezed it tight—the kind of grip that said more than words ever could.

"Thank you," Elena whispered. "For everything you've done. For Jasper. For our family."

"You don't need to thank me," Addison said, but her voice was thick with emotion.

"I do. Because you helped him learn how to be here. And that's the greatest gift you could have given us."

Addison's eyes filled with tears, and Kendra stepped forward, wrapping an arm around her shoulders. "We're all in this together," Kendra said gently. "That's what family means."

Elena nodded, still holding Addison's hand. "Exactly."

They stayed for another few minutes, the four of them—five, counting Lucas—sitting in the quiet comfort of shared love and hard-won grace. Then Addison and Kendra slipped out, leaving Jasper and Elena alone with their son.

Jasper settled back into the chair beside the bed, Lucas still cradled in his arms, and watched Elena's eyes drift closed.

"Get some rest," he murmured. "I've got him."

"You sure?"

"I'm sure."

She smiled without opening her eyes. "You're really here."

"I'm really here."

And for the first time in years, Jasper Barrett knew exactly where he belonged—not in the crisis, not in the strategy, not in the endless five-foot radius of the next emergency.

But here, in this room, with his wife and his son and the quiet certainty that this was what mattered most.

He held Lucas close and let the world outside wait.

Tomorrow, there would be new crises. New problems to solve, new fires to put out.

But tonight, there was only this: the weight of his son in his arms, the sound of Elena's breathing, and the knowledge that he'd finally learned what it meant to choose the five feet that mattered most.

---

*End of Chapter 19*
