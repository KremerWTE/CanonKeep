# Chapter 10: Three Fires at Once

*POV: Jasper Barrett*

---

The Forge felt like a command center under siege.

Jasper stood at the center of the main conference room, three monitors glowing behind him like warning lights in a cockpit seconds before impact. On the left: ESPN's morning show, where the NFL ownership battle had exploded into full media warfare overnight. In the middle: a live satellite feed from West Africa, red zones pulsing across the map like infected wounds. On the right: Bloomberg terminals tracking Asian markets, where someone was systematically manipulating a client's stock price with surgical precision.

Three fires. Three continents. One man trying to coordinate all of them without getting burned.

It was 6:47 a.m. on a Friday, and Jasper had been awake for thirty-two hours.

He took a sip of coffee—black, cold, his fifth cup since midnight—and let his eyes rest on the photo tucked into the corner of the whiteboard. Elena and Grace at the beach, the kite climbing behind them. Grace mid-laugh, Elena looking just off-frame at him.

*I promised I'd be home for dinner tonight,* he thought. *I promised.*

But promises felt fragile when the world was burning in three different time zones.

"Update," he said without turning around.

Harper appeared at his left shoulder, tablet in hand, her usual crisp professionalism showing the first cracks of exhaustion. Her dark hair was still perfectly styled, but her eyes had the hollow look of someone running on adrenaline and spite.

"NFL situation just went nuclear," she said. "Minority owner filed an injunction at six a.m. Eastern. He's trying to block the sale to the tech billionaire. Majority owner's legal team is countering with a motion to remove him from the ownership group entirely."

"Chris?"

"Caught in the crossfire. His agent called twice already. He wants to know if we can contain this before it destroys his career."

Jasper rubbed his face with both hands. "What about the league office?"

"Commissioner's trying to force mediation, but neither owner is budging. This is going to court unless we find leverage in the next twelve hours."

"Then we find leverage." He turned to the middle monitor, where the Africa map pulsed with instability. "Cole?"

Harper's expression tightened. "Coup attempt failed, but the fallout's destabilizing the entire region. Western partner's assets are at risk—oil infrastructure, supply chains, personnel. Cole and Dean are on the ground trying to secure extraction routes, but the window's closing fast."

"How fast?"

"Twenty-four hours. Maybe less. The interim government's losing control, and once that collapses, the whole thing goes red."

Jasper felt the familiar weight settle into his chest—the pressure of knowing that lives depended on decisions he'd make in the next few hours. This was the work he'd been born to do, the work that had nearly cost him his family.

*Not this time,* he told himself. *I can manage this. I have to.*

"And Asia?" he asked.

Harper swiped her tablet, pulling up trading data. "Someone's running a short campaign against Hammond Tech. They're dumping shares in coordinated waves, driving the price down, then buying back in at the bottom. It's textbook market manipulation, and if we don't identify who's behind it, Hammond's going to lose half their market cap by Monday."

"Dean's cyber team on it?"

"They've been tracking the trades for six hours. The money's moving through shell companies in Singapore and Hong Kong, layered so deep it'll take days to unravel."

"We don't have days."

"I know."

Jasper turned back to the monitors, his mind running through scenarios, contingencies, pressure points. The NFL battle required media strategy and legal leverage. Africa needed tactical coordination and diplomatic back-channels. Asia demanded forensic accounting and cyber intelligence.

Three different skill sets. Three different teams. And only one person holding it all together.

He was about to speak when Addison appeared in the doorway, her dark hair pulled back in a sharp bun, a stack of folders in her hands.

"Jasper," she said quietly. "You need to see this."

---

The folder Addison handed him contained the breakthrough they needed on the Asian manipulation case.

"Dean's team traced the shell companies back to a single source," she said, pointing to a name circled in red. "Victor Caine. He's a hedge fund manager based in Hong Kong. He's been shorting Hammond Tech for three months, but he escalated overnight because he knows we're onto him."

Jasper scanned the documents, his mind already assembling the strategy. "If Caine's coordinating the attack, he's vulnerable to exposure. We leak his name to the SEC, threaten him with an investigation, and force him to cover his positions."

"Already drafted the communication," Addison said, sliding another folder across the table. "We just need your authorization to send it."

Jasper looked at her—really looked at her. She'd been working just as hard as Harper, maybe harder, and it was showing. There were shadows under her eyes, a tightness in her jaw that spoke to too many hours, too much pressure, too little rest.

"When's the last time you slept?" he asked.

She blinked, caught off guard. "I'm fine."

"That wasn't the question."

"Jasper, we don't have time for—"

"When?"

She exhaled slowly. "Forty hours. Maybe more. I'm not sure."

He closed the folder and set it on the table. "Send the communication to Caine. Then go home."

"What? No. We're in the middle of—"

"And we'll still be in the middle of it when you get back. But if you collapse, we lose critical capacity we can't afford to lose. Go home. Sleep. Come back tomorrow."

Addison stared at him, her expression caught between frustration and something that looked almost like relief. Then she nodded, picked up the folder, and walked out without another word.

Harper watched her go, then turned to Jasper with raised eyebrows. "You just benched your best crisis manager in the middle of three active fires."

"I benched her before she burned out." He looked at Harper, seeing the same exhaustion he'd seen in Addison. "When's the last time you slept?"

"Don't start."

"Harper."

"I'm fine, Jasper. I've been doing this longer than Addison. I know my limits."

"Do you?" He stepped closer, lowering his voice. "Because from where I'm standing, you look like you're about two hours away from hitting the same wall she's heading for."

Harper's jaw tightened. "I can handle it."

"I'm not asking if you can handle it. I'm asking if you should have to."

For a moment, she looked like she was going to argue. Then her shoulders sagged, just slightly, and she turned away.

"I have to finish the NFL media strategy," she said quietly. "After that... maybe I'll take a break."

"Make it more than a break," Jasper said. "Make it a reset."

She didn't answer. But when she walked back to her laptop, he saw her pull out her phone and type a message.

To Ethan, he guessed. The architect who'd been waiting patiently while Harper poured everything into the work.

*Good,* Jasper thought. *At least someone's learning from my mistakes.*

---

By mid-morning, the Africa situation had escalated from critical to catastrophic.

Cole's encrypted message came through at 9:47 a.m., marked URGENT in the subject line.

"Interim government collapsed. New faction moving on Western assets. Need immediate extraction authorization for twelve personnel and their families. Window closing in six hours."

Jasper read it twice, his pulse steady despite the adrenaline flooding his system. This was the moment he'd been preparing for—the moment when all his training, all his experience, came down to a single decision.

He could authorize the extraction. Send Cole and Dean into the field with full support, coordinate with local assets, manage the operation from here while the team moved through hostile territory.

It was the right tactical decision. The professional decision.

But it was also the decision that would keep him at The Forge until midnight, maybe longer. It was the decision that would mean missing dinner with Elena and Grace. Again. It was the decision that would prove, once more, that when the choice came down to work or family, he'd choose work.

*Not this time.*

He picked up his phone and called Cole directly.

"Tell me you have options," Jasper said when Cole answered.

"One." Cole's voice was steady, calm despite the chaos Jasper could hear in the background. "Dean and I can run the extraction with local support. It's higher risk without you coordinating from stateside, but it's doable."

"Risk assessment?"

"Seventy percent success rate if we move now. Drops to fifty if we wait for you to set up full coordination."

Jasper closed his eyes, running the calculations. Seventy percent was acceptable. Not ideal, but acceptable.

And it meant he could delegate. He could trust his team to do what they'd been trained to do.

"You have authorization," Jasper said. "Full support from stateside. Dean coordinates cyber and logistics. You run point on the ground. I'll manage political cover and back-channel communications from here."

"Copy that." There was a pause. "You sure about this, boss? You usually run these operations yourself."

"I'm sure. You've got this, Cole. Both of you do."

"Roger. We'll check in at twelve-hour intervals."

The line went dead.

Jasper set down his phone and stared at the Africa monitor, watching the red zones pulse and shift. He'd just made the hardest decision of his career—not because it was tactically difficult, but because it required him to trust that someone else could do the work he'd always believed only he could do.

*Elena was right,* he thought. *I can't save everyone. But I can make sure the people I trust have what they need to save themselves.*

---

By early afternoon, the NFL situation had reached a breaking point.

Chris Cole arrived at The Forge at 2:00 p.m., looking every inch the franchise quarterback: tall, composed, dressed in a tailored suit that somehow made him look both professional and approachable. But his eyes were tight with stress, and when he shook Jasper's hand, his grip was harder than usual.

"They're tearing the team apart," Chris said without preamble. "Fans are picking sides. Players are getting asked about it in interviews. My wife's getting messages on social media from people telling her I need to speak up."

Harper appeared beside Jasper, tablet in hand. "The league office wants this contained by Monday. If it goes to court, it becomes a national story, and the NFL loses control of the narrative."

"Then we don't let it go to court," Jasper said.

Chris frowned. "How do you stop a legal filing that's already been submitted?"

"You don't stop the filing. You make it irrelevant." Jasper turned to the whiteboard, where he'd been mapping leverage points for the past hour. "The minority owner filed an injunction because he's afraid of losing control. The majority owner is countering because he's afraid of losing money. Neither one actually wants this fight—they just think it's the only way to protect their interests."

"So we give them another way," Harper said, catching on.

"Exactly." Jasper turned back to Chris. "You're the franchise. You're the face fans recognize, the player they trust. If you broker a deal between the owners—something that protects the minority owner's legacy while securing the majority owner's financial interests—they'll both look like statesmen instead of enemies."

Chris shook his head slowly. "I'm not a negotiator, Jasper. I'm a quarterback."

"You're a leader. And right now, your team needs you to lead off the field as much as on it."

Harper pulled up a document on her tablet. "We've drafted a framework. It's not a legal settlement—that's for the lawyers. But it's a public commitment from both owners to put the team first. If they sign it, the legal battle becomes moot."

"And if they don't sign?"

"Then you release a statement," Jasper said. "One that makes it clear you're ashamed of what's happening to the franchise, and you're calling on the league to intervene. You make them choose between their egos and their reputations."

Chris looked at the whiteboard, then at Jasper, then at Harper. "You really think this will work?"

"I think it's the only thing that might," Jasper said. "But it only works if you're willing to use your leverage."

Chris exhaled slowly, then nodded. "All right. Let's do it."

---

By the time Chris left The Forge at 4:30 p.m., the framework was in motion. Harper was coordinating with the league office. Dean's cyber team had sent the communication to Victor Caine, threatening SEC exposure if he didn't cover his short positions by Monday. And Cole had checked in from Africa with confirmation that the extraction was underway.

Three fires. Three strategies. Three teams executing without Jasper micromanaging every detail.

It should have felt like a victory.

Instead, it felt like standing on the edge of a cliff, trusting that the rope would hold.

Jasper looked at his watch: 4:47 p.m.

He'd promised Elena he'd be home by six. If he left now, he could make it.

But Harper was still at her desk, working on the NFL media strategy. The Africa operation wouldn't conclude for another six hours. And Victor Caine hadn't responded to Dean's communication yet.

*I should stay,* he thought. *Just another hour. Just to make sure everything holds.*

Then he heard Claire's voice in his head, from the session they'd had two days ago:

*"The work will never be done, Jasper. If you wait for the fires to go out, you'll wait forever. You have to decide when you've done enough—and then you have to leave."*

He closed his laptop.

Packed his bag.

And walked out of The Forge at 4:52 p.m.

---

Grace met him at the door of Oak Watch, her hair in a crooked ponytail, her eyes bright.

"Daddy! You're early!"

He swept her up into a hug, breathing in the familiar scent of strawberries and pencil shavings. "I promised, didn't I?"

"You always promise," she said, but she was grinning.

Inside, Elena was in the kitchen, Lucas in his bouncer, kicking at the mobile overhead. She looked up when Jasper entered, and for a moment, he saw the question in her eyes: *Did you really leave? Or are you about to tell me you have to go back?*

Then he set down his bag, crossed to her, and kissed her.

"I'm here," he said quietly. "And I'm staying."

She searched his face, then nodded. "Good. Because Grace has been working on a surprise for you all afternoon."

Grace tugged on his hand, pulling him toward the living room. "Come see! I made a poster for your office. It says 'DADDY'S TEAM' and it has all of us on it. Even Lucas, and he can't even draw yet."

Jasper followed her, feeling the weight of the day lift just slightly.

The fires were still burning. Africa was still unstable. The NFL battle was still unresolved. Asia was still vulnerable.

But for the next few hours, he was exactly where the five feet around him needed him to be.

And that, he was learning, was enough.

---

Across the city, Harper sat at her desk, staring at her laptop screen.

The NFL media strategy was complete. The framework for Chris's negotiation was solid. Everything was in place.

Her phone buzzed. A text from Ethan:

*Dinner tonight? I know you're swamped, but I miss you.*

She typed a response, hesitated, then deleted it.

Typed again: *Can't. Work emergency.*

Her finger hovered over the send button.

Then she thought about Jasper, walking out of the office at 4:52 p.m. despite three active crises.

She thought about Addison, sent home to sleep after forty hours awake.

She thought about Claire's words from the workshop two weeks ago: *"You can't be excellent at work and terrible at life. Eventually, one destroys the other."*

Harper deleted the message.

Typed a new one: *Yes. 7:00? I'll meet you there.*

She hit send before she could change her mind.

Then she closed her laptop, picked up her bag, and walked out of The Forge for the first time in two weeks before the sun had set.

It felt terrifying.

It felt like failure.

But as she stepped into the elevator and saw her reflection in the polished doors—tired, worn, but still standing—she realized it felt like something else, too.

It felt like a choice.

And maybe, just maybe, it was the right one.
