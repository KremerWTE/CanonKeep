# Chapter 22: The Threesome

*POV: Elena Barrett / Addison Price / Jasper Barrett (Rotating)*

---

**ELENA**

The dresses were finished. Grace was asleep. Lucas's baby monitor showed nothing but the gentle rise and fall of his chest.

And Elena Barrett stood in her own bedroom, heart racing, as Addison locked the door.

This was a threshold. She could feel it in the electric tension that filled the air, in the way Kendra's fingers trembled slightly as she set down her phone on the nightstand. They were about to cross into territory that couldn't be uncrossed—not with regret, not with rationalization, not with any of the defenses they'd all learned to build around their hearts.

Elena had spent weeks thinking about this moment. Not planning it, exactly—you couldn't plan intimacy like you could plan an event. But imagining it. Wondering what it would feel like to let herself want this. To admit that her love wasn't finite, carefully rationed out to the people who had first claim on it. That her heart was larger than she'd believed.

Addison turned from the door, her silk robe catching the lamplight. She looked at Elena with an expression that was equal parts desire and uncertainty.

"We can stop," Addison said. "Anytime. You just say the word."

Elena crossed to her, bare feet silent on hardwood. She reached up and traced the line of Addison's jaw, feeling the muscle jump beneath her fingertips.

"I don't want to stop," Elena said.

Kendra appeared at Elena's shoulder. "Neither do I."

Addison's eyes fluttered closed. For a woman who commanded boardrooms and managed crises with surgical precision, she looked utterly undone by the simple weight of being wanted.

Elena leaned in and kissed her.

It was different from the kiss in the hospital, different from the charged moments they'd shared over coffee or wine or late-night conversations about Grace. This kiss had permission behind it. Intention. The full weight of choice.

Addison's hands came up to frame Elena's face, gentle but sure. When they broke apart, Elena turned to Kendra and found her watching with dark, dilated eyes.

"Come here," Elena whispered.

Kendra closed the distance between them in two steps. Her kiss was different—hungrier, more urgent, like she'd been holding herself back for so long that now that she had permission, she couldn't quite control the flood of it.

Elena let herself sink into it, into the heat and the want and the simple joy of being desired by someone who saw her completely.

When she pulled back, breathless, she found Addison watching them both with something like reverence.

"God," Addison breathed. "You two are..."

"What?" Kendra asked, her lips curving into a smile.

"Everything."

---

They moved to the bed slowly, taking their time. There was no rush—Jasper wouldn't be home for another hour, and they had built this night specifically for themselves. For the exploration of what they could be together.

Kendra's robe slipped from her shoulders first. Beneath it, she wore nothing, and Elena couldn't help but stare at the lean muscle, the athletic grace of her body. Years of CrossFit had sculpted her into something powerful and beautiful.

"You're staring," Kendra said, but she was smiling.

"You're worth staring at."

Addison came up behind Kendra, her hands settling on Kendra's waist. "She's right," Addison murmured against Kendra's shoulder. "You're gorgeous."

Kendra's breath hitched. She turned in Addison's arms and kissed her—deep and searching, like she was trying to communicate everything she felt through the connection of their mouths.

Elena watched, her own desire building like a slow fire. There was something profound about witnessing this—two women who had loved each other, lost each other, and were finding their way back. She felt privileged to be here. To be part of it.

Addison's robe fell away next, pooling at her feet. She was all elegant lines and controlled strength, and when she turned to Elena, her eyes were dark with want.

"Your turn," Addison said softly.

Elena hesitated for only a heartbeat. Then she untied the sash of her blush robe and let it fall.

She was early in her pregnancy—barely showing, just a slight swell that most people wouldn't notice. But Addison noticed. Her gaze dropped to Elena's belly, and her expression softened into something achingly tender.

"You're beautiful," Addison whispered.

"I'm barely three months along. I'm bloated and exhausted and—"

Addison crossed to her and placed her hand gently on Elena's stomach. "You're carrying life. You're beautiful."

Kendra came up on Elena's other side, her own hand joining Addison's. "She's right."

Elena felt tears prick her eyes. This was what she hadn't known she needed—to be seen completely. Not just as Jasper's wife or Grace's mother or the woman who managed impossible events with unflappable grace. But as herself. Vulnerable and strong and worthy of being desired exactly as she was.

---

**ADDISON**

Addison Price had been with women before. She'd had lovers, partners, connections that burned bright and fast before fading into memory.

But this was different.

This was Elena—the woman who had anchored Jasper when he was spinning out of control, who had nearly died on a dining room floor and come back stronger. The woman who looked at Addison like she saw past the armor to the wounded, wanting person underneath.

And this was Kendra—her first love, her deepest regret, the person she'd walked away from because she'd been too afraid to admit what she wanted.

And now they were both here, in this bed, and Addison felt like she was standing on the edge of something vast and terrifying and utterly necessary.

Elena kissed her again, and Addison let herself fall into it. She felt Kendra's hands on her back, her lips on her shoulder, and the combination made her dizzy.

"I've got you," Elena murmured against her mouth.

Addison believed her.

They moved together—a choreography with no rehearsal, guided only by instinct and attention. Addison had always been someone who needed control, who mapped out every variable and prepared for every contingency. But here, in this bed, with these women, she let go.

She let Elena lead her down onto the mattress. She let Kendra's hands explore the places that made her breath catch. She let herself be vulnerable in ways she'd never allowed before.

And it felt like coming home.

---

**ELENA**

Elena had always been careful with her body. Careful with her heart. She'd given both to Jasper years ago and had spent a decade learning what it meant to love someone who was always leaving.

But tonight, she wasn't being careful.

Tonight, she was sprawled across her bed with Addison's head on her shoulder and Kendra's hand tracing lazy patterns on her hip, and she felt more herself than she had in years.

"Are you okay?" Kendra asked softly.

Elena turned to look at her. Kendra's hair had come loose from its knot, falling around her shoulders in dark waves. Her eyes were soft, sated, full of something that looked like wonder.

"I'm perfect," Elena said, and meant it.

Addison lifted her head. "No regrets?"

"None." Elena reached up and brushed a strand of hair from Addison's face. "You?"

"None," Addison echoed. She glanced at Kendra. "You?"

Kendra's smile was radiant. "Not a single one."

They lay there in comfortable silence for a while, skin against skin, heartbeats gradually slowing. Outside, the night pressed against the windows. Inside, Oak Watch held them safe.

Elena thought about Jasper, driving home from his dinner meeting. She wondered if he could sense the shift that had happened here tonight. If he would see it in her eyes when she kissed him hello.

She didn't think he would mind.

They'd talked about this—haltingly at first, and then with increasing honesty. Jasper had spent years married to his work, compartmentalizing his life into neat boxes that never touched. He understood the human capacity to love more than one person, to find connection in unexpected places.

And he trusted Elena. That was the foundation everything else was built on.

"What time is Jasper getting home?" Addison asked, as if reading Elena's mind.

Elena glanced at the clock on the nightstand. "Twenty minutes, give or take."

Kendra groaned. "I should probably put clothes on before I run into my future brother-in-law while naked in his bed."

Addison laughed—bright and unguarded. "That would make breakfast awkward."

They disentangled slowly, reluctantly. Kendra retrieved her robe from the floor. Addison found hers draped over the chair. Elena wrapped herself in the blush silk and felt the whisper of it against her skin.

"Tomorrow's going to be beautiful," Elena said, looking at Kendra. "Your wedding. I'm so happy for you."

Kendra's eyes shimmered. "Thank you. For this. For tonight. For... everything."

"Thank you for trusting us," Elena replied.

Addison came up behind Kendra and wrapped her arms around her waist, resting her chin on Kendra's shoulder. "We should probably go," she said quietly. "Give Elena and Jasper their space."

"You could stay," Elena offered. "If you want."

Addison met her eyes. "Are you sure?"

Elena thought about it. Thought about Jasper coming home to find all three of them here, wrapped in silk robes and the aftermath of intimacy. Thought about the conversation that would follow—the questions, the explanations, the renegotiation of boundaries.

"Actually," she said slowly, "maybe next time. Tonight... tonight I think I need to talk to my husband."

Understanding flickered across Addison's face. "Next time," she agreed.

They gathered their things—phones, the wedding dresses on their hangers, the scattered evidence of the night. At the door, they paused.

Kendra hugged Elena first, fierce and tight. "Love you," she whispered.

"Love you too."

Addison's hug was gentler, more tentative. "Thank you," she murmured. "For letting me in."

Elena pulled back and looked at her. "Thank you for being someone worth letting in."

Then they were gone, slipping down the hallway to the guest room where they'd left their overnight bags. Elena closed the bedroom door and leaned against it, her heart full.

She'd just made love to two women. Women she cared for deeply. Women who had become essential parts of her life.

And she didn't regret a single second of it.

**JASPER**

Jasper Barrett pulled into the driveway of Oak Watch at 10:47 p.m., his head still full of the NFL ownership dispute that had dominated dinner.

The house was mostly dark. Grace's bedroom window glowed with her nightlight—the star-shaped one she'd picked out herself. The living room was dim, lit only by the ambient light from the kitchen.

He let himself in quietly, set his briefcase by the door, and loosened his tie. The house smelled like home—lavender from Elena's diffuser, the faint scent of baby powder from Lucas's nursery.

And something else. Something he couldn't quite name.

He climbed the stairs, listening for any sound. Grace's door was closed. Lucas's nursery was silent except for the white noise machine. The guest room door was ajar, light spilling into the hallway.

He heard voices—soft, female, familiar.

Jasper paused. Addison and Kendra were here. That made sense—tomorrow was the wedding, and they'd been planning to prep together.

But the energy in the house felt different. Charged somehow.

He continued down the hall to the master bedroom and opened the door.

Elena was sitting on the edge of the bed, wearing her blush robe, her hair loose around her shoulders. She looked up when he entered, and the expression on her face stopped him cold.

She looked... luminous. Soft and content and utterly at peace.

"Hi," she said softly.

"Hi." He closed the door behind him. "How was the dress prep?"

"Good. Everything's ready for tomorrow."

He studied her. The flush on her cheeks. The slight swell of her lips. The way she was holding herself—open, vulnerable, waiting for him to understand.

Recognition clicked into place.

"They're still here," he said. Not a question.

"In the guest room," Elena confirmed.

Jasper crossed to her slowly, his mind working through the implications. He'd known this was a possibility. They'd talked about it in abstract terms—Elena's connection to Addison, the way Kendra had become part of their family. The idea that love didn't have to be scarce.

But theory was different from reality.

"Are you okay?" he asked.

Elena stood and came to him, her hands settling on his chest. "I'm more than okay. I'm happy."

He searched her face, looking for regret or doubt or any sign that she needed him to fix this. He found none.

"Did you...?"

"Yes."

The single word hung between them. Jasper felt a rush of emotions he couldn't quite name—surprise, curiosity, something that might have been jealousy but felt more like wonder.

"Do you want to talk about it?" he asked.

"Yes," Elena said again. "But not right now. Right now, I just want you to hold me."

Jasper pulled her into his arms, feeling the warmth of her body through the thin silk. She fit against him the way she always had—perfect, essential, home.

"I love you," he murmured into her hair.

"I love you too." She pulled back enough to look up at him. "That doesn't change. Nothing about us changes."

"Except that you have two more people who love you."

Elena's smile was radiant. "Yeah. Except that."

Jasper kissed her then—deep and claiming and full of all the things he couldn't put into words. She kissed him back with equal fervor, and when they finally broke apart, they were both breathing hard.

"Come to bed," Elena whispered.

"Are you sure? After—"

"I'm sure."

He let her lead him to their bed, the same bed where hours ago she'd been with Addison and Kendra. The sheets smelled faintly of lavender and something sweeter—perfume, maybe, or just the scent of skin.

They made love slowly, carefully, with the kind of attention that came from years of knowing each other. And afterward, as they lay tangled together in the dark, Jasper felt something settle in his chest.

His wife had expanded her heart to include others. She had found connection and joy and intimacy in places he couldn't reach.

And instead of feeling threatened, he felt grateful.

Grateful that she was loved. Grateful that she had people who saw her as clearly as he did. Grateful that she trusted him enough to share this with him.

"Jasper?" Elena's voice was drowsy.


"Yeah?"

"Thank you."

"For what?"

"For not making this complicated."

He kissed her forehead. "You've earned simple, El. You've earned joy. And if that joy includes Addison and Kendra, then I'm glad they're in your life."

She burrowed closer to him. "I don't deserve you."

"Yes, you do." He tightened his arms around her. "We've both earned each other. The hard way."

They fell asleep like that—wrapped around each other, the house quiet around them, tomorrow's wedding waiting just on the other side of dawn.

And in the guest room down the hall, two women who had become essential parts of their lives slept peacefully, knowing they were home.
