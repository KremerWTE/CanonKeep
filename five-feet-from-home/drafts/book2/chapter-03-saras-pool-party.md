# Chapter 3: Sara's Pool Party

*POV: Elena Barrett*

---

Sara's backyard was a study in controlled chaos.

The pool glittered in the late afternoon sun, inflatable flamingos bobbing near the deep end where Grace and Emma were already shrieking with laughter. The smell of charcoal and mesquite drifted from the grill where Sara's husband, Mike, stood with a beer in one hand and tongs in the other, looking like a man who knew his role and was content with it.

Elena stood on the patio with Lucas on her hip, taking in the scene with the particular awareness of a mother calculating exit strategies and nap schedules. Lucas was content for now, gnawing on a teething ring and watching everything with the wide-eyed fascination of a four-month-old discovering that the world extended beyond his immediate five feet.

"You made it!" Sara appeared at her elbow, hair damp from what looked like a recent swim, wearing board shorts and a navy tank top that showed off shoulders that could probably overhead press Elena without breaking a sweat. "I was starting to think you'd bail."

"Grace would have murdered me," Elena said. "She's been talking about this pool party for three days straight."

"Emma too. I think they've already planned out the next six hours down to the minute." Sara reached for Lucas, who went to her without protest. "Hey, buddy. You getting big. Your mama feeding you steroids?"

"Just breast milk and the occasional existential dread."

Sara laughed and bounced Lucas gently. "Come on. I'll introduce you to the crew. Fair warning—they're a lot."

She led Elena toward the far side of the pool, where a cluster of people were gathered around a cooler and a Bluetooth speaker playing something with a heavy bass line. They looked like they'd stepped out of a fitness magazine: lean muscle, easy confidence, the kind of bodies that came from years of disciplined training rather than vanity.

"Okay," Sara said, raising her voice to be heard over the music. "Listen up, animals. This is my sister, Elena. Be nice, or I'll program burpees for the rest of the month."

A chorus of groans and good-natured protests.

"Elena, this is the crew." Sara gestured to each person in turn. "Dylan—he's the one who thinks he can out-lift anyone here and is usually wrong."

Dylan was tall and broad-shouldered, with dark blond hair buzzed close and the kind of easy grin that probably got him out of trouble more often than it should. "Guilty as charged," he said, raising a beer in salute. "Nice to meet you."

"That's Tasha," Sara continued, pointing to a woman with rich brown skin and box braids pulled back in a high ponytail. "She holds the gym record for thrusters, which is both impressive and terrifying."

"Only terrifying if you're the one doing them," Tasha said, her voice warm and musical. She was wearing athletic shorts and a sports bra, completely unselfconscious about the sculpted muscle visible in her abs and arms. "Welcome to the madness."

"And that's Eli," Sara said, nodding toward a lean, wiry man with dark hair and forearms that looked like they'd been carved from stone. "He's our resident gymnast. Can do things on the rings that make grown men cry."

"Only because they're trying to keep up," Eli said, his grin crooked. "Sara exaggerates."

"Sara never exaggerates," came a voice from behind them.

Elena turned and found herself looking at the woman from Sara's video. Kendra.

In person, she was somehow both exactly what Elena had expected and completely different. The video had captured her intensity, the way she moved with precision and power. But it hadn't captured the warmth in her smile, the way her eyes crinkled at the corners when she laughed, the easy confidence that came from someone who knew exactly who they were and didn't apologize for it.

She was wearing a black bikini top and high-waisted athletic shorts, her dark hair loose around her shoulders, still damp from the pool. There was a small tattoo on her ribs—something geometric that Elena couldn't quite make out—and a silver bracelet on her wrist that caught the light.

"You must be Elena," Kendra said, extending a hand. Her grip was firm, her palm slightly calloused. "Sara's told me a lot about you."

"All lies," Elena said, surprising herself with the ease of the response.

"That's what I figured." Kendra's smile widened. "But I'm going to choose to believe the good ones anyway."

There was something magnetic about her—not in the way that demanded attention, but in the way that made you want to give it. Elena found herself smiling back, feeling the particular flutter of meeting someone who might become important.

"Kendra just got back from regionals," Sara said, the pride evident in her voice. "Third place in her division."

"Sara mentioned," Elena said. "Congratulations. That's incredible."

Kendra shrugged, but there was pleasure in the gesture. "It was a good day. The kind where everything clicks and your body does what you've been training it to do."

"She's being modest," Dylan interjected. "She beat people who've been training twice as long. It was insane."

"It was luck and timing," Kendra said, but her eyes were bright.

"It was skill and grit," Tasha corrected. "Own it, girl."

The conversation flowed easily after that—talk of training cycles and competition prep, stories about workouts gone wrong and PRs that felt impossible until they weren't. Elena listened, fascinated by the language they spoke, the shorthand of people who lived in their bodies in ways she'd never quite managed.

Then the patio door slid open, and Addison stepped out.

Elena felt something settle in her chest—the particular relief of seeing someone who understood the weight you were carrying, even if they never spoke of it directly.

Addison was wearing leggings in deep charcoal and a loose tank top in sage green, her dark hair pulled back in a messy bun. She looked more relaxed than Elena had seen her in months—no laptop, no phone pressed to her ear, just a woman showing up to a party.

"Sorry I'm late," Addison called. "Client call ran long."

"You're here now," Sara said. "That's what counts."

Addison crossed the patio and gave Elena a quick hug, then reached for Lucas, who immediately grabbed a fistful of her hair with the unerring instinct of babies everywhere.

"Ow. Okay. Hi to you too, buddy." Addison gently extracted her hair from Lucas's grip and settled him more comfortably against her shoulder. "How's he doing?"

"Good. Teething, but good."

"Poor kid." Addison looked around, taking in the gathering with the assessing eye of someone who was always reading the room. "This is quite the crew."

"Come meet them," Sara said. "You'll love them. Or find them exhausting. Possibly both."

She made the introductions again—Dylan, Tasha, Eli, Kendra. Elena watched as Addison shifted into the polished, professional version of herself, the one who could walk into any room and command it without raising her voice.

But something changed when she met Kendra.

It was subtle—a slight pause, a flicker of interest in her eyes, the way her posture shifted from polite to engaged. Kendra seemed to feel it too, her smile turning curious, her focus sharpening.

"Addison Price," Kendra said slowly, as if testing the name. "Sara's mentioned you. You work with Jasper, right?"

"Partner at BSS," Addison confirmed. "Crisis management."

"That sounds intense."

"It has its moments." Addison handed Lucas back to Elena and turned her full attention to Kendra. "Sara says you just placed third at regionals. That's impressive."

"Thanks. It's been a long road."

"How long have you been competing?"

"Five years seriously. Before that, I was just messing around in globo gyms, thinking I knew what fitness was." Kendra's laugh was self-deprecating. "Then I walked into Sara's gym and realized I didn't know anything."

"That's how I felt the first time I walked into BSS," Addison said. "Humbling and exhilarating at the same time."

They were talking about different things, but the subtext was the same—the recognition of someone who understood what it meant to be good at something, to chase excellence, to live in the space between ambition and achievement.

Elena watched them, filing away the way Addison leaned in slightly when Kendra spoke, the way Kendra's hand moved when she talked, the particular energy that hummed between them.

Not romantic. Not yet. But the potential was there, hovering in the space between words.

"You should come to the gym sometime," Kendra said. "We could do a session. I'm always looking for people who can push me."

"I might take you up on that," Addison said. "I've been looking for something to shake up my training."

"Fair warning—I'm a terrible coach. I expect too much and give too little sympathy."

"Good. I don't need sympathy. I need someone who won't let me quit when it gets hard."

Kendra's smile turned sharp and pleased. "Yeah. I can do that."

The conversation shifted as Dylan called Kendra over to settle a debate about pull-up technique, and Addison drifted toward the cooler to grab a water. Elena found herself standing alone for a moment, Lucas still on her hip, watching the way the group moved and laughed and challenged each other.

This was Sara's world—people who lived in their bodies, who pushed themselves to the edge and then kept going, who found joy in the struggle. It was so different from Elena's world of real estate showings and nonprofit galas, but there was something appealing about it, something she wanted to move closer to.

"Your sister knows how to throw a party," a voice said beside her.

Elena turned to find Tasha, who had apparently appointed herself the unofficial welcomer.

"She really does," Elena agreed.

"First time meeting the gym crew?"

"Is it that obvious?"

Tasha grinned. "You have the look of someone who's trying to figure out if we're all crazy or just really committed."

"Little bit of both?"

"Fair assessment." Tasha snagged a chip from the bowl on the table and popped it into her mouth. "For what it's worth, we're good people. Intense, but good. And Kendra's the best of us."

"Sara speaks highly of her."

"Sara's not wrong." Tasha glanced over to where Kendra was demonstrating something to Dylan, her hands moving in quick, precise gestures. "Kendra's the real deal. She'll push you harder than you think you can go, and then when you get there, she'll push you further. But she'll also be the first one to tell you when to rest, when to scale back, when to choose recovery over ego."

"That's a rare combination."

"It is. Which is why everyone at the gym would follow her into fire." Tasha's expression turned thoughtful. "Your sister-in-law seems interesting."

"Addison?"

"Yeah. She's got that same energy—like she's used to being the best in the room and isn't sure what to do when she meets someone who matches her."

Elena followed Tasha's gaze and saw Addison standing with Kendra again, the two of them talking with the focused intensity of people who had found something worth paying attention to.

"They just met," Elena said carefully.

"I know. But some people just click." Tasha shrugged. "Kendra's been looking for a training partner at her level. Someone who can keep up, who won't back down when things get hard. I think she might have just found one."

Elena felt something flicker in her chest—not quite jealousy, but close. The particular ache of watching someone you cared about find connection with someone else, someone who might understand parts of them you never could.

She pushed the feeling down, tucking it away to examine later.

"Come on," Tasha said, nodding toward the pool. "Your daughter's trying to drown mine in a pool noodle fight. We should probably supervise."

Elena laughed and followed, Lucas bouncing contentedly against her hip.

---

The afternoon stretched into evening, the sun dropping lower and painting everything in shades of gold and amber.

Mike had pulled the burgers and hot dogs off the grill, and people were scattered around the patio with paper plates and red Solo cups. Grace and Emma were still in the pool, their energy seemingly limitless, while the adults settled into lawn chairs and talked about everything and nothing.

Elena found herself sitting between Sara and Tasha, listening to a debate about the optimal protein intake for recovery.

"Bodyweight in grams is overkill," Dylan was saying, gesturing with a half-eaten burger. "You're just making expensive pee."

"Tell that to my deadlift PR," Tasha shot back. "Protein works."

"I'm not saying it doesn't work. I'm saying you don't need two hundred grams a day unless you're trying to become a superhero."

"Who says I'm not?"

The group laughed, and Elena found herself smiling despite not entirely following the conversation.

Across the patio, Addison and Kendra were sitting close together, Addison's phone out as Kendra showed her something—a training program, maybe, or a video from competition. Their heads were bent together, dark hair and darker hair, and there was an ease to their proximity that spoke of comfort rather than attraction.

But Elena knew how these things worked. Comfort could become attraction. Ease could become desire. And Addison had always been drawn to people who challenged her, who met her at her level rather than asking her to lower herself.

"They're going to be trouble together," Sara said quietly, following Elena's gaze.

"You think?"

"I know. I've seen that look before—two people realizing they speak the same language and wanting to see how far the conversation can go."

"It's just training talk."

"For now." Sara took a sip of her beer. "But Kendra's been single for six months, and Addison's been alone for longer than that. They're both driven, both used to being the best at what they do. And they're both looking for someone who can keep up."

"Addison's not looking for a relationship," Elena said. "She's married to her work."

"Maybe. But work doesn't keep you warm at night." Sara's voice was gentle. "And sometimes the best relationships start as partnerships. Shared goals, mutual respect, the slow build of realizing the person next to you in the gym is also the person you want next to you everywhere else."

Elena didn't answer. She couldn't quite name what she was feeling—a tangle of protectiveness and curiosity and something that might have been envy.

Not envy of Addison. Envy of the possibility. Of watching two people stand at the beginning of something and not knowing yet where it would lead.

"Come on," Sara said, standing and offering Elena a hand. "Grace is begging me to do a cannonball, and I need a witness to verify my technique."

Elena let herself be pulled up, Lucas still content on her hip, and followed her sister to the edge of the pool.

---

Later, as the party was winding down and parents were corralling tired children into cars, Elena found herself alone with Addison by the cooler.

"You looked like you were having a good time," Elena said, reaching for a bottle of water.

Addison glanced over, her expression unreadable. "I was. Sara's friends are good people."

"Kendra seems nice."

"She is." Addison hesitated, then added, "She offered to train me. I'm thinking about taking her up on it."

"You should. It might be good for you—having something outside of work."

"That's what Hawk keeps saying." Addison's smile was small and private. "He thinks I need hobbies."

"Do you?"

"Probably." Addison looked out at the pool, where Grace and Emma were doing one last lap before getting out. "I used to have things I did for myself. Before BSS consumed everything. I used to run, used to paint, used to have entire weekends where I didn't check my email."

"What changed?"

"I got good at something. And being good turned into being the best. And being the best turned into being indispensable." Addison's voice was matter-of-fact, not bitter. "It's a trap, you know. The indispensable trap. Because once people need you, it's really hard to let them down."

"But you're not indispensable to the gym," Elena said gently. "You're just someone who wants to get stronger."

"Yeah." Addison's smile turned real. "Maybe that's what I need. A place where I'm not the expert, not the one everyone's counting on. Just someone showing up to do the work."

"And Kendra seems like a good coach."

"She does." Addison paused, choosing her words carefully. "She reminds me of someone I used to know. Someone who pushed me to be better, who didn't let me hide behind excuses."

Elena heard the weight in those words, the history. She didn't press.

"Well," Elena said, "if you need a workout buddy, I'm happy to join you. Fair warning—I have no idea what I'm doing, and I'll probably embarrass myself."

Addison laughed, and the tension broke. "You'd do great. And besides, embarrassment builds character."

"That's what my mother used to say."

They stood there for a moment, two women at different stages of the same journey—learning to take up space, learning to want things for themselves, learning that love didn't have to mean surrender.

Then Grace called for Elena, and the moment passed.

---

On the drive home, Grace fell asleep in the backseat, her hair still damp from the pool, one hand clutching the stuffed dolphin Emma had given her.

Lucas was asleep too, his head lolling against the car seat, his breathing soft and even.

Elena drove through the quiet streets, the sun finally setting, painting the sky in shades of rose and violet.

She thought about Addison and Kendra, about the way they'd gravitated toward each other, about the possibility humming between them. She thought about Sara's words—*They're going to be trouble together.*

And she thought about herself, about the secrets she was carrying, about the new life growing inside her that Jasper still didn't know about.

So many beginnings. So many unknowns.

But for the first time in a long time, the unknowns didn't feel terrifying. They felt like possibility.

She pulled into the driveway of Oak Watch and saw the lights on in the kitchen—Jasper was home, probably working at the table, probably lost in whatever crisis was consuming him today.

But he was home. And that counted for something.

Elena unbuckled Lucas carefully, tucking him against her shoulder, and reached back to shake Grace gently awake.

"We're home, sweetheart."

Grace blinked sleepily, clutching her dolphin. "Did you have fun, Mama?"

"I did, baby. I really did."

And it was true.

---

*End of Chapter 3*
