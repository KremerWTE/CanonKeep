# Chapter 23: Morning After

*POV: Multiple (Elena / Addison / Jasper / Kendra)*

---

**ELENA**

The first thing Elena heard was Grace's voice, bright and insistent from the hallway.

"Mommy! Auntie Addie! It's wedding day!"

Elena opened her eyes to find herself alone in bed, the sheets cool on Jasper's side. Sunlight streamed through the windows, painting everything gold. She checked her phone: 7:23 a.m. The wedding wasn't until three, but Grace operated on her own schedule when it came to important events.

Elena pulled on her robe and opened the door to find Grace already dressed in leggings and a tunic, her hair sticking up in about seven different directions.

"Good morning, baby," Elena said, pulling her daughter into a hug.

"Can we make pancakes? Daddy said we could make pancakes for everyone. He said it's a special day so we should have a special breakfast."

"Where is Daddy?"

"Downstairs with Lucas. He's making coffee."

Elena followed Grace down the stairs, her heart doing something complicated in her chest. The last time she'd seen Jasper, they'd been wrapped around each other in the dark, and she'd fallen asleep feeling completely loved and completely safe.

Now it was morning, and she was going to have to face him—and Addison and Kendra—in the bright light of day.

She found Jasper in the kitchen, Lucas balanced on his hip while he worked the espresso machine one-handed. He looked up when she entered, and the smile he gave her was warm and uncomplicated.

"Morning," he said.

"Morning." She crossed to him and kissed him softly, tasting coffee and possibility. "You're up early."

"Grace woke me at six-thirty, demanding wedding pancakes." He shifted Lucas to his other hip. "I figured we could all use a good breakfast before the chaos."

"We?"

"Addie and Kendra are in the guest room. I haven't seen them yet, but I heard the shower running about ten minutes ago."

Elena studied his face, looking for any sign of discomfort or regret. She found only calm acceptance.

"Are you okay?" she asked quietly, aware of Grace rummaging in the pantry for pancake mix.

"I'm good." He transferred Lucas to her arms and cupped her face with his now-free hand. "Are you okay?"

"I'm good too."

They stood there for a moment, Grace's chatter filling the background, Lucas making happy gurgling sounds against Elena's shoulder. Then Jasper leaned in and kissed her forehead.

"I'm going to set the table," he said. "You wrangle the pancake chef."

---

**ADDISON**

Addison woke to the sound of water running and the realization that she was alone in the guest bed.

She sat up slowly, her body pleasantly sore in ways that made her face heat. Last night had been... she didn't have words for what last night had been. Transformative, maybe. Or just honest. The first time in her adult life that she'd let herself want something without planning three moves ahead.

The bathroom door opened, and Kendra appeared in a cloud of steam, wrapped in a towel, her hair damp.

"Morning," Kendra said softly.

"Morning." Addison patted the space beside her. "Come here."

Kendra crossed to the bed and sat, her damp skin cool against Addison's. "How are you feeling?"

"Like I spent the night doing something I should have done years ago," Addison said honestly.

Kendra's smile was radiant. "Yeah?"

"Yeah." Addison reached up and tucked a strand of wet hair behind Kendra's ear. "And how are you feeling? Because in about eight hours, you're getting married to someone who isn't me, and I need to know that you're okay with... all of this."

Kendra's expression turned serious. "I love Chris. I'm marrying Chris. That doesn't change."

"I know."

"But I also love you. And Elena. And I'm done pretending that love is a finite resource that I have to ration out to the people who got here first."

Addison felt something loosen in her chest. "Addition," she said.

"What?"

"That's what Elena called it last night. Addition. Not replacement. Just... more."

Kendra leaned in and kissed her—soft and sure. "I like that."

They sat there for a moment, foreheads touching, breathing the same air. Then Grace's voice echoed up the stairs, something about pancakes and weddings and hurry up.

Kendra laughed. "I think we're being summoned."

"Think we can face them? Elena and Jasper and Grace, all in one room, after last night?"

"I think we don't have a choice." Kendra stood and dropped her towel, reaching for her clothes with unselfconscious grace. "Besides, it's pancakes. Everything's better with pancakes."

Addison watched her dress—sports bra, leggings, an oversized sweatshirt that probably belonged to Chris. She looked strong and beautiful and utterly comfortable in her own skin.

"You're staring," Kendra said, echoing her words from last night.

"You're worth staring at," Addison replied.

They got dressed together, the silence comfortable. When they were both ready—or as ready as they'd ever be—they headed downstairs.

---

**JASPER**

Jasper was flipping pancakes when Addison and Kendra appeared in the kitchen doorway.

He'd known this moment would be awkward. You didn't spend a decade managing high-stakes crises without developing a sense for when tension was about to walk into a room.

But when he looked up and saw them standing there—Kendra with her damp hair and nervous smile, Addison with her carefully neutral expression—he felt no anger. No jealousy. Just a quiet sense of rightness.

"Morning," he said easily. "Hope you're hungry. Grace insisted on enough pancakes to feed a small army."

Addison's shoulders relaxed fractionally. "Morning."

Kendra moved first, crossing to the counter where Elena was cutting strawberries. "Can I help?"

"Sure. You can handle the whipped cream."

They worked side by side, their movements synchronized in that way that came from knowing someone well. Jasper watched them for a moment, then turned his attention back to the pancakes.

Grace appeared at his elbow, holding a bottle of syrup. "Daddy, do wedding pancakes need special syrup?"

"They need the fanciest syrup we have," Jasper said seriously. "Which is this one, with the real maple leaf on the label."

"Okay. I'll put it on the table with the fancy napkins."

She bounded off, a blur of energy and tulle (she'd already changed into her flower girl dress, despite the wedding being eight hours away).

Addison came to stand beside him, her voice low. "Thank you."

"For what?"

"For not making this weird."

Jasper flipped a pancake, considering his words carefully. "You love my wife. My wife loves you. And somehow, miraculously, that doesn't threaten what she and I have. So no, I'm not going to make it weird. I'm going to make pancakes and celebrate the fact that the people I care about are happy."

Addison stared at him. "You're a better man than most, Jasper Barrett."

"I'm a man who almost lost everything because I was too scared to be vulnerable," Jasper corrected. "I learned my lesson. I'm not going to waste time being threatened by love."

He plated the last pancake and carried the stack to the dining room, where Grace had indeed set out the fancy napkins—cloth ones with embroidered edges that Elena usually saved for holidays.

They gathered around the table—Jasper at the head, Elena to his right with Lucas in the bassinet beside her chair, Grace bouncing excitedly across from them. Addison and Kendra took the remaining seats, their hands briefly touching on the table before separating.

"Before we eat," Elena said, "I want to say something."

Everyone looked at her. She was glowing in the morning light, her hair loose around her shoulders, her hand resting on the small swell of her belly.

"Today, we're celebrating Kendra and Chris," Elena continued. "We're celebrating their love and their commitment and the life they're building together. But I also want to celebrate this—all of us, around this table. The family we've built. Not just by blood or marriage, but by choice."

Grace frowned. "I didn't choose you. You're my mommy. You came with me."

Jasper laughed. "That's true, Gracie. But the rest of us chose each other. And that's pretty special."

"Like when I choose to be friends with Emma instead of Madison who pushes people on the playground?"

"Exactly like that."

Grace nodded, satisfied. "Okay. Can we eat now?"

They ate with the easy rhythm of people who'd shared meals together before. Grace chattered about her flower girl responsibilities. Kendra talked about the final details for the ceremony. Addison fielded a work call (Harper, asking about the NFL situation) and Elena gently reminded her that she was off duty today.

Jasper watched them all and felt something settle in his chest. This was what he'd been fighting for when he'd turned down the global expansion role. This was what mattered.

Not the crisis in Hong Kong or the whistleblower in the supply chain or the NFL owners who couldn't see past their own egos.

This. The people in this room. The way they fit together, imperfect but honest.

---

**ADDISON**

After breakfast, Jasper disappeared into his study to finish the NFL report. Grace was upstairs "practicing" her flower girl walk. Lucas had fallen asleep, and Elena was washing dishes.

Addison found herself standing in the living room, holding a coffee cup and trying to figure out how she'd ended up here.

Six months ago, she'd been sleeping in her office, running on four hours of sleep and the kind of willpower that eventually burned you out. She'd been so focused on proving herself, on being indispensable to Jasper and BSS, that she'd forgotten what it felt like to be a person instead of a productivity machine.

Then Elena had collapsed. Jasper had nearly fallen apart. And somehow, in the aftermath of that crisis, Addison had found herself being pulled into this family's orbit in ways she hadn't expected.

She'd held Elena's hand in the hospital. She'd watched Frozen with Grace a dozen times. She'd let Kendra back into her life, and eventually into her heart, and last night into her bed.

And now she was standing in the Barretts' living room on a Saturday morning, drinking coffee and feeling more at home than she'd felt in her own apartment in months.

Jasper emerged from his study, laptop in hand. He saw her standing there and paused.

"Can we talk?" he asked.

Addison's heart rate kicked up. "Sure."

He gestured to the couch, and they sat. For a moment, neither of them spoke. Then Jasper set his laptop aside and turned to face her.

"I need to tell you something," he said.

Addison braced herself. Here it came—the boundaries, the conditions, the reminder that she was an outsider who'd been allowed in on a temporary basis.

"I'm promoting you," Jasper said.

Addison blinked. "What?"

"To junior strategist. Not just managing partner—a full strategist role. You'll be running your own division, handling high-level client relationships, making strategic decisions without having to clear them through me first."

"Jasper, I—"

"You've earned it," he continued. "Not because of last night. Not because of your relationship with Elena or Kendra. You've earned it through the work. The supply chain crisis, the NFL situation, the dozen other fires you've put out in the last six months while I was learning how to be home for dinner. You're one of the best crisis managers I've ever worked with, and it's time the title reflected that."

Addison felt tears prick her eyes. "Thank you."

"Don't thank me. This is business. You're good at what you do, and BSS needs you operating at the highest level." He paused, his expression softening. "But on a personal note—I'm grateful. For the way you've shown up for my family. For Elena. For Grace. For being someone I can trust when everything goes sideways."

"You're my family too," Addison said quietly. "All of you."

Jasper smiled. "I know. And that's why this works. Because it's not about jealousy or possession or any of that small, fearful stuff. It's about making sure the people we love are supported and safe and free to be exactly who they are."

He stood and held out his hand. "So. Junior strategist. Do you accept?"

Addison stood and shook his hand, her grip firm. "I accept."

"Good. We'll make it official Monday. But today is Kendra's wedding, and you have about six hours to help Elena and Grace finish whatever last-minute chaos they've cooked up."

"What about you?"

"I'm staying here with Lucas and finishing this report so I can actually be present at the ceremony." He picked up his laptop. "And Addie?"

"Yeah?"

"Thank you. For loving my wife the way she deserves to be loved."

Addison's throat tightened. "Thank you for letting me."

He nodded and disappeared back into his study. Addison stood in the living room for a moment longer, letting the weight of the morning settle.

She'd been promoted. She'd been accepted. She'd been given permission to be exactly who she was, to love who she loved, without apology or compromise.

For someone who'd spent her whole life wearing armor, it felt like freedom.

---

**KENDRA**

By noon, Oak Watch had transformed into controlled chaos.

The dresses were being steamed (again). Grace was practicing her flower girl walk (again). Elena was on the phone with the caterer, handling some last-minute gluten-free crisis with the calm efficiency of someone who'd managed a thousand events more complicated than this.

And Kendra was sitting in the guest room, alone for the first time all day, trying to quiet the riot of emotions in her chest.

She was getting married today. To Chris. The man who'd loved her through her worst moments, who'd supported her athletic dreams, who'd proposed to her with a ring that had belonged to his grandmother.

But last night, she'd slept with two women. Women she loved. Women who'd become essential parts of her life.

And somehow, miraculously, that didn't feel like a betrayal. It felt like expansion. Like her heart had finally grown large enough to hold all the love she'd been carrying.

A knock on the door interrupted her thoughts.

"Come in," she called.

Addison slipped inside, closing the door behind her. She was already dressed for the wedding, her emerald gown making her look like some kind of warrior goddess.

"You okay?" Addison asked.

"Nervous," Kendra admitted. "Happy. Scared. All of it."

Addison sat beside her on the bed. "That's allowed. Getting married is terrifying."

"Have you ever thought about it? Marriage?"

"Once." Addison's gaze turned distant. "But I was too scared to ask. Too scared to stay. Too scared to admit what I wanted."

Kendra reached for her hand. "And now?"

"Now I'm learning to be brave." Addison squeezed her fingers. "And you're part of that. What we have—what the three of us have—it's teaching me that love doesn't have to look like what everyone else says it should."

"I love Chris," Kendra said. "I need you to know that. I'm marrying him because I want to build a life with him."

"I know."

"But I also love you. And Elena. And I don't want to pretend that's not real."

"You don't have to pretend." Addison leaned in and kissed her forehead. "Chris knows. Elena knows. Jasper knows. Everyone who matters knows that love isn't a finite resource. It's just... more."

"Addition," Kendra whispered.

"Addition," Addison agreed.

They sat there for a moment, hands linked, breathing in sync. Then Grace's voice echoed from the hallway—something about needing help with her shoes—and they both laughed.

"Duty calls," Addison said.

"Yeah." Kendra stood, smoothing down her dress. "Thank you. For last night. For this morning. For being here."

"Always," Addison said. And Kendra believed her.

---

**ELENA**

At two-thirty, the house was quiet again.

Grace was dressed and perfected, her hair in the elaborate French-fishtail hybrid braid that Kendra had promised. Lucas was with the babysitter, a trusted college student who'd helped with Grace when she was small. Addison and Kendra had left for the venue to handle final preparations.

And Elena stood in front of her bedroom mirror, adjusting the sage wrap dress that accommodated her growing belly, and felt utterly at peace.

Last night had changed something in her. Not her love for Jasper—that was as deep and certain as ever. But her understanding of what love could be. How it could expand to hold more people, more connection, more joy.

She heard footsteps behind her and turned to find Jasper in the doorway, dressed in a charcoal suit, his tie perfectly knotted.

"You look beautiful," he said.

"You look pretty good yourself."

He crossed to her and wrapped his arms around her waist, his hands settling on her belly. "How's little one doing?"

"Active. I think they know something important is happening today."

Jasper rested his chin on her shoulder, and they stood there, watching their reflection in the mirror. A couple who'd nearly lost everything and fought their way back. A family that had expanded to include people they'd never expected.

"Are we crazy?" Elena asked softly. "For doing this? For letting our lives get so... complicated?"

"Probably," Jasper said. "But I've learned that the best things in life are usually complicated. You just have to decide if the complication is worth it."

"Is it?"

He turned her in his arms so she was facing him. "You're worth it. Grace and Lucas are worth it. And Addison and Kendra—they're worth it too. They love you. They love us. And I'm done being the kind of man who's threatened by that."

Elena kissed him then—deep and grateful and full of all the things she couldn't put into words.

When they broke apart, she rested her forehead against his. "Thank you."

"For what?"

"For trusting me. For trusting us. For being exactly who you are."

"Right back at you," Jasper murmured.

They stood there for another moment, wrapped in each other, before Grace's voice floated up the stairs: "Mommy! Daddy! We're going to be late!"

Elena laughed. "She's right. We should go."

"Yeah." But Jasper didn't let go immediately. "I love you, Elena Barrett."

"I love you too, Jasper Barrett."

They went downstairs together, hand in hand, and loaded Grace into the car. The drive to the venue was filled with Grace's chatter about flower petals and the proper way to walk down an aisle.

But beneath the noise, Elena felt a deep current of contentment. She'd spent years being the woman who held everything together, who managed crises and smoothed over conflicts and made sure everyone else was okay.

Today, she was the woman who'd expanded her heart to include more love. Who'd let herself be vulnerable and want things she'd been taught not to want. Who'd built a family not just through blood and marriage, but through choice.

And when they arrived at the venue and she saw Addison waiting at the entrance, stunning in her emerald gown, and Kendra inside, radiant in her navy halter, Elena felt her heart swell with something that felt a lot like joy.

This was her family. All of them. Complicated and unconventional and absolutely perfect.

And she wouldn't change a single thing.
