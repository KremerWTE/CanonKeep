# Chapter 15: Engagement Party Chaos

*POV: Multiple (Harper, Elena, Kendra, Jasper)*

---

## Harper

Harper had planned a thousand events in her career—corporate galas, crisis summits, media briefings that required the precision of a military operation. But planning her own engagement party felt different.

Maybe it was because Ethan had proposed three days ago, and she was still processing the fact that she'd said yes. Maybe it was because the same week she'd gotten engaged, Elena had quietly told the inner circle she was pregnant with baby number two. Or maybe it was because everything in Harper's life felt like it was shifting, and she wasn't sure if she was ready for the ground to move.

She stood in the living room of Ethan's loft—*their* loft now, she supposed—surveying the space with the assessing eye of someone who'd organized events in hotel ballrooms and corporate boardrooms. The exposed brick and industrial lighting were perfect. The open floor plan could easily accommodate fifty people. The rooftop terrace would be ideal for cocktails if the November weather cooperated.

"You're doing the thing," Ethan said from the kitchen, where he was unpacking groceries.

"What thing?"

"The thing where you turn into a logistics commander." His tone was amused, affectionate. "I can see you mentally mapping crowd flow and beverage stations."

Harper turned to him, unable to suppress a smile. "Is that a problem?"

"Not at all. I just want to make sure you're enjoying this and not just managing it."

She crossed to the kitchen, wrapping her arms around his waist from behind. "I enjoy managing things."

"I know." He turned in her arms, kissing her forehead. "But this is supposed to be a celebration, not a client event."

"Can't it be both?"

Ethan laughed, the sound low and warm. "Only you would ask that question."

Harper pulled back slightly, looking up at him. He was wearing a faded Duke t-shirt and jeans, his hair still damp from a morning run. He looked relaxed in a way Harper rarely felt. It was one of the things she loved about him—his ability to be present, to exist in the moment without constantly planning three steps ahead.

"Are you sure you want to do this?" she asked. "The party, I mean. We could keep it small. Just close friends."

"Harper." Ethan's hands settled on her hips, his gaze steady. "We're doing this because you deserve to celebrate. You work eighteen-hour days managing everyone else's crises. For once, let people celebrate you."

She wanted to argue. Wanted to deflect, to minimize, to do what she always did when the focus turned to her. But Ethan's expression was so sincere, so certain, that the words caught in her throat.

"Okay," she said finally. "But I'm still mapping crowd flow."

"I wouldn't expect anything less."

## Elena

Elena had spent the morning helping Sara set up for Harper's engagement party, which meant she'd spent the morning trying not to throw up.

Morning sickness was a misnomer. It should be called *all-day sickness that happens to be worse in the morning and also whenever you smell coffee, eggs, or literally anything cooking.*

She was twelve weeks pregnant now, just past the point where she'd told Jasper, just entering the phase where the secret was starting to feel too big to keep. Harper knew. Sara knew. Addison knew. Pretty soon, everyone would know, and then the questions would start: *How are you feeling? Are you excited? Is Jasper thrilled?*

The answers were: tired, terrified, and yes, respectively.

Jasper had been thrilled when she told him. He'd pulled her into his arms and held her for a full minute without saying anything, and when he finally pulled back, there were tears in his eyes. He'd promised to be present this time, to not let work consume him the way it had during her pregnancy with Grace.

Elena wanted to believe him. She was trying to believe him.

But she'd also seen the way his phone buzzed at dinner last night, the way his jaw tightened when he glanced at the screen, the way he'd excused himself to take a call that lasted twenty minutes.

Old habits died hard. Especially for men like Jasper Barrett.

"You okay?" Sara asked, appearing at Elena's elbow with a glass of ginger ale.

"I'm fine." Elena took the glass gratefully, sipping slowly. "Just trying not to ruin Harper's party by throwing up in her fiancé's loft."

Sara snorted. "That would definitely make an impression."

"Not the kind I'm going for."

They were in Ethan's loft, which was quickly filling with people. Harper had said she wanted something low-key, but Elena knew her well enough to know that "low-key" in Harper's world still meant perfectly curated guest list, catered appetizers, and a signature cocktail.

The engagement had happened fast—Harper and Ethan had only been dating a few months—but Elena could see why Harper had said yes. Ethan was steady. Grounded. The kind of man who didn't flinch when Harper worked late or took calls during dinner. He understood that her work wasn't a phase or a hobby—it was part of who she was.

Elena thought about Jasper, about the years she'd spent waiting for him to choose her over the next crisis. About the promise he'd made in the hospital room after her collapse. About the life they were trying to build now, one dinner at a time.

"You're thinking about him," Sara said.

"Is it that obvious?"

"Only to someone who knows you." Sara glanced around the room, then lowered her voice. "How's he doing? With the pregnancy news?"

"Good. He's... trying." Elena took another sip of ginger ale. "He's been home for dinner every night this week. He even turned down a client call yesterday because Grace had a school thing."

"That's progress."

"I know. I just keep waiting for the other shoe to drop."

Sara squeezed her shoulder. "Give him credit for the progress. And give yourself permission to enjoy this."

Elena nodded, though the knot in her stomach didn't loosen.

Across the room, Harper was laughing at something Ethan said, her hand resting on his arm in a way that looked effortless. Elena felt a pang of something—not jealousy, exactly, but a kind of wistful recognition. She remembered when things with Jasper had felt that easy, before the work and the distance and the weight of a decade had settled between them.

They were rebuilding. Slowly. But it was happening.

She just had to keep believing it.

## Kendra

Kendra hadn't expected to be invited to Harper's engagement party.

She'd only met Harper a handful of times—at the gym, at a few group dinners Sara had organized. But when the invitation had come, Sara had insisted she attend.

"You're part of the circle now," Sara had said, as if that settled it.

Kendra wasn't sure what "the circle" meant, but she'd learned not to argue with Sara when she had that look in her eyes.

So here she was, standing in a loft full of people she barely knew, holding a glass of wine she wasn't drinking, trying to look like she belonged.

She spotted Sara across the room, talking to Elena, who looked slightly pale but otherwise composed. Kendra had met Elena a few times now, and there was something about her that Kendra couldn't quite name—a quiet strength, a grace under pressure. She reminded Kendra of the women she'd trained with at regionals, the ones who could smile through pain and make it look easy.

"Kendra!"

She turned to see a woman approaching—tall, dark hair swept back in an elegant bun, wearing a blazer that probably cost more than Kendra's car payment. It took her a second to place the face: Addison Price, the woman Sara trained with twice a week.

"Addison," Kendra said, offering a smile. "Good to see you."

"You too." Addison's gaze was assessing, the kind of look Kendra recognized from coaches and judges. "Sara mentioned you placed third at regionals. That's incredible."

"Thanks. It was a tough field."

"I'm sure." Addison's expression softened slightly. "Sara also said you're looking to expand your coaching roster. I might have a lead for you."

Kendra's eyebrows rose. "Yeah?"

"There's a sports agency looking to bring in a performance coach for their NFL clients. Part-time, flexible hours, good pay. I can make an introduction if you're interested."

Kendra blinked. "That would be—yeah, I'm definitely interested."

"Good. I'll send you the details next week." Addison glanced around the room, then back at Kendra. "You should talk to Harper. She's been looking for a trainer who can keep up with her schedule. You might be a good fit."

Before Kendra could respond, Addison was gone, moving through the room with the kind of efficiency that suggested she had a mental checklist and limited time.

Kendra stood there for a moment, processing. A sports agency. NFL clients. Harper Caldwell, COO of Barrett Security Solutions, possibly as a client.

This was not what she'd expected when she'd walked into Sara's gym six months ago, looking for a place to train after moving to Charlotte.

"You look shell-shocked."

Kendra turned to see a man standing beside her—early thirties, fit, wearing a casual button-down and jeans. He had the kind of easy smile that suggested he was comfortable anywhere.

"I think I just got recruited," Kendra said.

"By Addison?" The man laughed. "That's her specialty. I'm Marcus, by the way. Friend of Ethan's."

"Kendra."

They shook hands, and Marcus gestured toward the bar. "Want another drink? You look like you could use one."

"I have one."

"You're not drinking it."

Kendra glanced at her glass, which was still full. "Fair point."

Marcus led her toward the bar, weaving through clusters of people. Kendra caught snippets of conversation as they passed—someone talking about a crisis in Lagos, someone else discussing a real estate deal, a group laughing about a story she didn't catch.

This was a different world than the one she was used to. The gym was her comfort zone—weights, chalk, the rhythm of lifts, the clarity of numbers. This world was all polish and connections, people who seemed to move through life with a kind of effortless competence she couldn't quite grasp.

But Sara had said she belonged here. And Addison had just offered to connect her with an NFL sports agency.

Maybe she did.

---

## Jasper

Jasper arrived at the party late, which wasn't surprising given that he'd spent the afternoon on a call with a client in London who couldn't seem to grasp the concept of time zones.

Elena had texted him twice: *Don't forget Harper's party* and *Seriously, Jasper. Don't be late.*

He'd made it with ten minutes to spare, which felt like a victory even if Elena's expression when he walked in suggested otherwise.

"You're here," she said, crossing to him with a glass of water in one hand.

"I'm here." He kissed her temple, then her lips. "I told you I wouldn't miss it."

"You also told me you'd be home by six last night."

Jasper winced. "That call ran long."

"They always do."

There was no heat in her voice, just resignation, and somehow that was worse. Jasper wanted to argue, to defend himself, to explain that the client had been on the verge of a meltdown and he'd needed to talk them down. But he also knew that Elena had heard every version of that excuse, and none of them changed the fact that he'd been late.

"I'm sorry," he said quietly.

Elena studied him for a moment, then sighed. "Just try, Jasper. That's all I'm asking. Just try."

"I am."

She didn't respond, just squeezed his hand and turned back toward the party.

Jasper followed, scanning the room. Harper was near the window, talking to Ethan and a man Jasper didn't recognize. Sara was by the bar, chatting with Kendra, the CrossFit athlete Sara had been raving about for weeks. Addison was in the corner, phone in hand, probably handling a crisis even at a social event.

And then there was the man standing near the appetizer table—tall, mid-thirties, athletic build, the kind of presence that suggested military or law enforcement background.

Jasper recognized him immediately: Derek Mills, an NFL agent he'd crossed paths with a few times over the years. Mills had a reputation for being ruthless in negotiations and fiercely loyal to his clients. He was also, according to Harper, considering a partnership with BSS to handle crisis management for his athlete roster.

Jasper made his way over.

"Derek," he said, extending a hand. "Good to see you."

Derek shook it, his grip firm. "Jasper. Harper mentioned you might be here."

"Wouldn't miss it. She's been talking about this partnership for weeks."

"Yeah, about that." Derek glanced around, then lowered his voice. "I wanted to run something by you. One of my clients—Charlotte quarterback—just got engaged. He's looking for someone to help manage the media side of things. I told him BSS might be a good fit."

Jasper nodded slowly. "We can definitely help with that. Have your client reach out to Harper. She'll set up a meeting."

"Appreciate it." Derek paused, then added, "I also wanted to mention—Kendra, the CrossFit coach Sara's been working with? I'm thinking about bringing her on as a performance coach for some of my guys. Harper said she's incredible."

"I haven't met her yet," Jasper admitted. "But if Sara vouches for her, that's good enough for me."

Derek grinned. "Sara doesn't vouch for anyone unless they're the real deal."

They talked for a few more minutes—logistics, timelines, potential clients—before Derek excused himself to grab another drink. Jasper stayed by the appetizer table, scanning the room again.

This was what success looked like, he supposed. A loft full of successful people, all connected by work and ambition and the shared understanding that excellence came at a cost. Harper had built this life through sheer will and competence. She deserved to celebrate it.

But as Jasper watched Elena across the room, one hand resting lightly on her stomach in a gesture she probably didn't realize she was making, he wondered if success was worth it when it meant missing the moments that mattered.

He thought about Claire's words from earlier that week: *Crisis management isn't about putting out fires. It's about seeing which sparks will catch.*

He'd spent a decade putting out fires. Maybe it was time to start seeing the sparks before they caught.

Maybe it was time to choose the five feet around him before the world pulled him away again.

## Harper

By nine PM, the party was in full swing.

Harper stood on the rooftop terrace, looking out at the Charlotte skyline, a glass of champagne in her hand. The November air was cool but not cold, the kind of evening that made her grateful for the city she'd chosen to call home.

Ethan appeared beside her, wrapping an arm around her waist.

"You did good," he said.

"We did good."

"Fair." He kissed her temple. "How are you feeling?"

Harper considered the question. She was tired—organizing an engagement party while managing a dozen client crises was not for the faint of heart. But she was also happy. Genuinely, unexpectedly happy.

"Good," she said finally. "Really good."

"Yeah?"

"Yeah."

They stood there for a moment, watching the city lights flicker in the distance. Harper thought about the life she'd built—the career, the friendships, the connections that had brought her to this moment. She thought about Elena, pregnant with her second child, trying to believe that Jasper would keep his promises this time. She thought about Addison, brilliant and exhausted, running herself into the ground because she didn't know how to stop. She thought about Kendra, the newcomer who was already being pulled into their orbit.

This was the circle. The wives, the friends, the people who understood that loving someone who lived in crisis mode meant learning to hold your ground while the world tried to pull them away.

Harper had spent years holding her ground alone. But now she had Ethan. And maybe that was the difference.

"Come on," Ethan said, tugging her hand. "People are asking for a toast."

Harper let him lead her back inside, where the party was louder, warmer, full of laughter and conversation. Sara was at the bar, pouring drinks with the confidence of someone who'd done this a thousand times. Elena was on the couch, talking to Addison, who looked slightly less tense than usual. Kendra was near the window, deep in conversation with Derek Mills about training regimens and performance metrics.

And Jasper was by the door, phone in hand, but not looking at it. Just holding it. Like he was trying to resist the pull.

Harper caught his eye, and he offered a small, apologetic smile. She nodded, a silent acknowledgment that she understood. That they all understood.

This was the life they'd chosen. High stakes, high rewards, and the constant tension between the work and the people who made the work worth doing.

Ethan handed her a fresh glass of champagne, then tapped his own against the side of a bottle to get everyone's attention.

"All right, everyone," he said, his voice cutting through the noise. "I know Harper's going to kill me for this, but I need to say a few words."

Harper groaned, but she was smiling.

Ethan turned to her, his expression soft. "Harper Caldwell is the most competent person I've ever met. She can manage a crisis in three time zones while planning a corporate event and still make time to bring me coffee at midnight. She's brilliant, she's terrifying, and she's the best thing that's ever happened to me."

The room erupted in applause and laughter.

Ethan raised his glass. "To Harper. The woman who makes chaos look easy."

"To Harper!" the room echoed.

Harper clinked her glass against Ethan's, then took a sip, her cheeks warm from the attention. She glanced around the room—at Sara, at Elena, at Addison, at Kendra, at Jasper, at all the people who had become her family over the years.

This was the circle. And for the first time in a long time, Harper felt like she was exactly where she was supposed to be.
