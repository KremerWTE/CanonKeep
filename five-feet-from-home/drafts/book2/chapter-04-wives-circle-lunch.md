# Chapter 4: Wives' Circle Lunch

*POV: Elena Barrett*

---

The Charlotte Rooftop was the kind of restaurant Elena loved: glass walls framing the skyline, exposed brick meeting polished steel, farm-to-table menus that changed with the seasons. It was a place where power lunches happened at noon and date nights unfolded at sunset.

Today, it was just the wives.

Elena arrived first, settling into the corner table Sara had reserved with a view of downtown Charlotte. The October air was crisp, the kind that made you want to order tea instead of wine. She was three months pregnant with baby number two, and though she hadn't made any announcements yet, the secret was starting to feel heavy in the best possible way.

Sara breezed in next, her dark hair pulled back in a low ponytail, wearing the kind of athleisure that looked effortless but probably cost more than most people's rent. She slid into the seat across from Elena with the easy grace of someone who spent her mornings coaching Olympic lifts.

"You're glowing again," Sara said, eyeing Elena with the assessing look of someone who noticed everything.

Elena felt the heat rise to her cheeks. "New skincare routine."

"Sure it is." Sara's smile was knowing, but she didn't push. That was the thing about Sara—she knew when to press and when to let things breathe.

Before Elena could deflect further, Harper swept in with a gust of energy that seemed to follow her everywhere. She was wearing a tailored blazer over a silk blouse, her hair swept back in a sleek bun that somehow looked both professional and effortless. She dropped her bag on the chair beside her and exhaled.

"Sorry I'm late," Harper said, already reaching for the water glass. "The NFL thing is a complete nightmare."

"Still?" Sara asked, raising an eyebrow.

"Still." Harper took a long sip of water, then seemed to remember she was among friends and visibly relaxed. "Let's not talk about work. I spend eighteen hours a day living it. Tell me something good."

Elena smiled. "Grace asked if we could get a kitten this morning."

"And what did you say?" Sara asked.

"I told her to ask her father." Elena's smile turned mischievous. "Who was already halfway out the door to The Forge."

Harper laughed. "Strategic parenting. I like it."

"I learned from the best," Elena said, glancing at Sara, who raised her water glass in mock salute.

The server appeared with menus, and they ordered—salads, grilled salmon, a flatbread to share. Elena requested decaf tea, which earned her another knowing look from Sara, but Harper was too distracted to notice.

"So," Sara said, leaning forward with a grin that suggested trouble, "is anyone going to address the elephant in the room?"

Harper froze with her wine glass halfway to her lips. "What elephant?"

"The one named Ethan."

Harper set down her wine glass and exhaled, a slow, measured breath that was part resignation, part relief. "His name is Ethan Cole. He's an architect. I met him at a gallery opening two months ago."

"An architect," Elena repeated, genuinely delighted. "That's perfect for you."

"Why is that perfect?" Harper asked, but she was smiling.

"Because architects build things from the ground up. They have vision. They think long-term." Elena paused, then added, "And they're used to working with complicated blueprints."

Sara snorted. "Subtle, Elena."

Harper laughed, but it was the kind of laugh that carried relief in it, like she'd been holding her breath for weeks and finally remembered how to exhale. "He's different. He doesn't care that I work eighteen-hour days. He brings me coffee at midnight and doesn't expect me to apologize for being busy."

"Does Jasper know?" Elena asked.

"Not yet. I wanted to make sure it was... real before I said anything."

"And is it?" Sara asked. "Real?"

Harper's expression softened in a way Elena had rarely seen. "Yeah. I think it is."

Elena reached across the table and squeezed Harper's hand. "I'm happy for you."

"Thanks." Harper squeezed back, then cleared her throat. "Now can we please talk about something other than my love life?"

"Sure," Sara said, her grin widening. "Let's talk about mine."

Elena raised an eyebrow. "You have news?"

"Not me. But I have someone I want you both to meet." Sara pulled out her phone, scrolling until she found what she was looking for. "Her name is Kendra. She just placed third in regionals. She's absolutely incredible."

Sara turned the phone so Elena and Harper could see. The video showed a woman in her late twenties, dark hair pulled back in a tight ponytail, performing a snatch complex with fluid precision. Her form was flawless, the kind that came from years of disciplined training. Elena watched the muscles in her arms and shoulders work, the focused intensity in her eyes.

"She's impressive," Elena admitted.

"She's more than impressive," Sara said, her enthusiasm spilling over. "She's also genuinely nice. She'll spend an hour coaching a beginner on their first pull-up, and then turn around and lift twice her body weight without breaking a sweat."

Harper leaned closer to the screen. "She trains at your gym?"

"Yep. And she's looking to expand her coaching roster." Sara's eyes flicked to Elena, then back to Harper. "I was thinking maybe we could bring her into the circle. You know, have her train with us."

Elena felt something flicker in her chest—a small, unexpected pinch of something she couldn't quite name. It wasn't jealousy, not exactly. But it was close.

"That sounds great," Elena said, and meant it. Mostly.

Sara seemed to sense the hesitation. "You'd love her, Elena. She's got this way of making you feel like you can do anything. Plus, she's great with kids. Grace would adore her."

"I'm sure she would," Elena said, taking a sip of her tea. The warmth of it settled her, pulled her back to the moment.

Harper was still looking at the video. "She's got great form. I've been trying to get my deadlift up for months."

"Kendra could fix that in a week," Sara said. "She's been working with Addison, too. They've been doing joint sessions."

Elena's eyebrows rose. "Addison's training with her?"

"Twice a week. She says it's the only thing keeping her sane with all the BSS chaos."

The server arrived with their food, and the conversation drifted to lighter topics—Grace's upcoming school play, Harper's latest Netflix binge, Sara's plans for Thanksgiving. But Elena couldn't quite shake the image of Kendra from her mind. The way she moved with such confidence, such certainty. The way Sara talked about her like she was already part of the family.

It wasn't that Elena didn't want new people in their circle. It was just that the circle had always been theirs—hers, Sara's, Harper's, Addison's. The wives who understood the cost of loving men who lived in crisis mode. Adding someone new felt like opening a door she hadn't realized was closed.

Halfway through lunch, Harper's phone buzzed on the table. She glanced at it, then frowned.

"Work?" Elena asked.

"Jasper." Harper picked up the phone, reading the message. "Two fires just popped. He's asking if I can take the NFL thing while he handles the other one."

"What's the other one?" Sara asked.

Harper's frown deepened. "European client tied to some kind of political mess in Africa. He's being vague, which means it's bad."

Elena's stomach tightened. She knew that look—the one that said Jasper was about to disappear into a crisis that would consume him for days. She'd seen it a hundred times before. The difference now was that he'd promised things would be different. That he'd be home. That he'd choose them.

"Tell him you've got it," Elena said quietly.

Harper looked at her, surprised. "You sure?"

"He needs to trust his team. That includes you."

Harper studied her for a moment, then nodded and typed a quick reply. When she set the phone down, she said, "You're good for him, you know that?"

Elena smiled, but it didn't quite reach her eyes. "I'm trying."

The conversation shifted again, this time to Addison, who apparently had her own crisis brewing. Harper mentioned something about a meeting that had gone sideways, a client who was threatening to pull out of a major contract. Sara asked if Addison needed backup, and Harper said she was handling it, but there was strain in her voice that suggested otherwise.

By the time dessert arrived—a shared chocolate tart that none of them really wanted but ordered anyway—Elena was feeling the weight of the afternoon settling over her. The secret of the pregnancy, the flicker of insecurity about Kendra, the worry about Jasper slipping back into old habits. It was a lot to carry.

"So," Harper said, breaking into Elena's thoughts. "When are you going to tell him?"

Elena blinked. "Tell who what?"

"Jasper. About the baby."

Elena stared at her, then at Sara, who was grinning like she'd just won the lottery.

"You told her?" Elena asked Sara.

"I didn't have to. She figured it out on her own." Sara leaned back in her chair, looking far too pleased with herself. "You're drinking decaf tea, you turned down wine, and you've been glowing like a damn lighthouse for the last three weeks."

Harper laughed. "Plus, you keep touching your stomach when you think no one's looking."

Elena felt the blush creep up her neck. "I was going to tell him tonight."

"Good," Harper said. "Because if you wait any longer, he's going to figure it out on his own, and you know how he gets when he feels like he's behind the curve."

Elena smiled despite herself. "He'll be thrilled."

"He'll be terrified," Sara corrected. "And then thrilled."

"Fair."

They finished lunch with plans to do it again in two weeks, this time with Addison if her schedule allowed. As they were getting ready to leave, Sara turned to Elena and said, "Bring Jasper to the gym next week. I want him to meet Kendra."

"Why?" Elena asked, genuinely curious.

"Because she's going to be part of this. And I want him to see why."

Elena nodded, though the flicker of unease was still there, small and persistent. She hugged Sara and Harper goodbye, then headed for her car, the October air cool against her skin.

On the drive home, she found her hand drifting to her stomach again. The pregnancy wasn't showing yet, but she could feel the changes already. The subtle shift in her body, the way her energy ebbed and flowed in patterns she remembered from Grace.

She pulled into the driveway of Oak Watch just as the sun was starting to dip below the trees. Inside, Grace's backpack was hanging on the fence, a sure sign she was home from school. Elena could hear her daughter's voice through the open window, chattering away to someone—probably Sara, who'd picked her up earlier.

Home.

Elena took a breath, then stepped out of the car. Tonight, she'd tell Jasper about the baby. Tonight, they'd savor the moment together before the world pulled him back into its chaos.

But first, she had to survive the rest of the day.

*End of Chapter 4*
