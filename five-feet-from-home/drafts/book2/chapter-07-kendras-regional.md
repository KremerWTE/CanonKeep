# Chapter 7: Kendra's Regional

*POV: Elena Barrett*

---

The CrossFit venue smelled like chalk dust, rubber mats, and the particular brand of determination that came with competitive athletics.

Elena stood in the bleachers, a water bottle in one hand and her phone in the other, scanning the crowd for a glimpse of Kendra. Around her, the energy was electric—coaches shouting encouragement, athletes pacing nervously, spectators cheering for competitors they'd never met but respected simply for being here.

The Mid-Atlantic Regional Championship was one of the toughest qualifying events in the CrossFit circuit. Only the top finishers would move on to the national stage, and everyone in this warehouse knew it.

Elena had never been to one of these before. Sara had invited her a dozen times over the years, but there had always been something else—a client dinner, a nonprofit board meeting, a family obligation. Today, though, the invitation had come from Kendra herself, in a text sent late last night:

*Competing tomorrow. Big one. Addison can't make it—she's traveling with Jasper. Would mean a lot if you came.*

Elena had texted back immediately: *I'll be there.*

Now, standing in the bleachers with Lucas strapped to her chest in a baby carrier, she felt a flutter of nerves that had nothing to do with her own performance. She barely knew Kendra—a few conversations at family gatherings, a handful of polite exchanges at Sara's gym. But the text had felt personal, almost vulnerable, and Elena had responded to that.

She spotted Kendra near the competition floor, stretching her shoulders and rolling her wrists. Even from a distance, she was striking: tall and athletic, with dark hair pulled back in a tight braid and a focus in her eyes that reminded Elena of soldiers preparing for deployment.

"She's in the third heat," Sara said, appearing beside Elena with two coffees. "She's been training for this for six months. Third place at Regionals last year. This time, she wants first."

"Does she have a shot?"

Sara grinned. "Oh, she has more than a shot. Watch."

---

The first event was a brutal combination of deadlifts, box jumps, and rowing intervals. Elena watched as Kendra moved through the workout with a kind of controlled ferocity, her form perfect, her transitions seamless. She wasn't the fastest in her heat, but she was efficient, and by the final round, she'd pulled ahead of two competitors who had started stronger.

When the buzzer sounded, Kendra dropped to her knees, chest heaving, hands braced on the floor. Her coach—a wiry man with a stopwatch and a clipboard—jogged over and said something Elena couldn't hear. Kendra nodded, then stood and walked to the sideline to cool down.

Elena caught her eye from the bleachers and waved. Kendra's face lit up in surprise, and she waved back, mouthing *Thank you.*

"She didn't think you'd actually come," Sara said quietly.

"Why not?"

"Because Addison didn't."

Elena frowned. "Addison's in Africa with Jasper. She couldn't—"

"I know," Sara interrupted. "And Kendra knows too. But it still stings."

Elena looked back at Kendra, who was now sitting on a bench, drinking water and staring at nothing in particular. There was something in her posture—shoulders slightly hunched, head tilted down—that spoke to more than physical exhaustion.

"They have a complicated history, don't they?" Elena asked.

Sara glanced at her, then nodded. "Complicated is one word for it. They were together, years ago. Before Kendra met Chris. Before Addison became... whatever she is now. It didn't end well, and they've been circling each other ever since."

"Does Chris know?"

"He knows enough," Sara said. "He's not stupid. But he loves Kendra, and he's willing to let her figure things out."

Elena didn't ask what "things" meant. She had a feeling she already knew.

---

The second event was worse: a chipper-style workout that combined thrusters, pull-ups, and a brutal finishing run. By the time Kendra crossed the line, her face was red, her breathing ragged, and her hands were bleeding from the barbell.

Elena met her at the medical tent, where a volunteer was wrapping her palms in athletic tape.

"You didn't have to come down here," Kendra said, but her voice was warm.

"I wanted to," Elena said. She shifted Lucas slightly, adjusting the carrier. "You're incredible, by the way. I had no idea how hard this was."

Kendra laughed—a breathless, exhausted sound. "It's harder than it looks. And it looks pretty hard."

"How are you feeling?"

"Like I got hit by a truck. But a truck I was expecting, so it's fine."

Elena smiled. "That's... oddly reassuring."

The medical volunteer finished taping Kendra's hands and stepped away. Kendra flexed her fingers experimentally, wincing slightly.

"One more event," she said. "Then I'm done. Or dead. Whichever comes first."

"You're going to do great," Elena said. And she meant it.

---

The final event was a partner workout—two athletes working in tandem to complete a series of synchronized movements. Kendra's partner was a woman named Jess, a former collegiate rower with shoulders like a linebacker and a grin that suggested she was born for this kind of suffering.

Elena watched from the bleachers as the two women moved through the workout with a rhythm that seemed almost choreographed. Wall balls, synchronized pull-ups, double-unders. They were in sync, feeding off each other's energy, pushing when the other faltered.

When they finished, they collapsed on the floor together, laughing and gasping for air. The crowd erupted in applause, and Elena found herself clapping along, a smile tugging at her lips.

Sara nudged her. "See? Told you she had a shot."

"She's amazing," Elena said.

"She is. And she doesn't believe it half the time."

---

By the time the scores were posted, Kendra had placed second overall—high enough to qualify for nationals, strong enough to prove she belonged.

Elena found her in the athlete area, wrapped in a towel and drinking a protein shake. She looked exhausted but happy, the kind of bone-deep satisfaction that came from pushing yourself to the edge and surviving.

"Second place," Elena said. "Congratulations."

"Thanks." Kendra grinned. "I was hoping for first, but I'll take it."

"Are you disappointed?"

"A little," Kendra admitted. "But mostly I'm just relieved I didn't fall apart on the last event. Jess carried me through the wall balls."

"I didn't see it that way. I saw two people who trusted each other."

Kendra looked at her for a long moment, something unreadable flickering across her face. "That's... really nice of you to say."

"It's true."

They stood there for a moment, the noise of the venue swirling around them. Then Kendra shifted her weight and glanced toward the exit.

"Hey," she said. "I don't know if you have plans, but... would you want to grab dinner? My treat. I'm starving, and I could use the company."

Elena hesitated, thinking of the evening ahead—Lucas's bedtime routine, Grace's homework, the laundry she'd been putting off. Then she looked at Kendra, at the hope in her eyes and the exhaustion in her posture, and made a decision.

"I'd love to," she said.

---

They ended up at a small Italian place a few miles from the venue, the kind of neighborhood restaurant with checkered tablecloths and a menu that hadn't changed in twenty years.

Kendra ordered pasta and a beer. Elena ordered soup and sparkling water, acutely aware of the baby sleeping against her chest in the carrier.

"I forgot you had him with you," Kendra said, glancing at Lucas. "Is he always this quiet?"

"Only when he's sleeping," Elena said with a smile. "Give him another hour and he'll remind you he exists."

"He's beautiful. He looks like Jasper."

"He does. Grace is convinced he's going to grow up to be just like his dad—serious, focused, always planning three steps ahead."

"And you?"

Elena paused, thinking. "I hope he grows up to be kinder than Jasper was at his age. More present. Less afraid of letting people in."

Kendra studied her. "Jasper's changed, hasn't he?"

"He's trying," Elena said carefully. "It's not easy for him. But he's trying."

"That's more than a lot of people do."

They ate in comfortable silence for a while, the kind of quiet that felt less like awkwardness and more like mutual understanding. Then Kendra set down her fork and leaned back in her chair.

"Can I ask you something?" she said.

"Of course."

"Do you ever feel like you don't belong somewhere, even when everyone tells you that you do?"

Elena blinked, surprised by the question. "What do you mean?"

Kendra exhaled slowly. "I've been training with Sara for three years. I've competed at Regionals twice. I'm dating a great guy, I have friends, I have a life that makes sense on paper. But sometimes I go to work—I help Addison with events, I coordinate logistics for BSS functions—and I feel like I'm playing dress-up. Like everyone can see I don't really fit."

"At the office?" Elena asked gently.

"Yeah. At the office, at the events, at the parties. I look around and see people like Addison and Harper—smart, polished, always three steps ahead—and I think, *What am I doing here?* I'm a CrossFit coach. I lift heavy things and yell at people to do burpees. That's what I'm good at."

Elena reached across the table and touched Kendra's hand. "Kendra, you just placed second at Regionals. You pushed yourself to the edge and came out stronger. That takes intelligence, discipline, and resilience. Those are the same qualities that make you valuable at the office."

"You really think so?"

"I know so. And I think the reason you feel like you don't belong is because you're surrounded by people who are so confident in their roles that they make it look easy. But it's not easy for anyone. Harper works seventeen-hour days because she's terrified of failing. Addison pushes herself until she collapses because she doesn't know how to stop. Even Jasper—he's brilliant at what he does, but he's constantly second-guessing whether he's doing enough."

Kendra looked at her, eyes wide. "I had no idea."

"Most people don't. We're all just trying to figure it out, Kendra. You're not playing dress-up. You're learning. And that's okay."

Kendra's eyes glistened, and she looked away quickly, blinking hard. "Thank you," she said quietly. "I needed to hear that."

"Anytime," Elena said.

---

By the time they finished dinner, it was nearly nine o'clock. Lucas had woken briefly, fussed, and settled again after Elena fed him. Kendra watched the whole process with a kind of quiet fascination, as if she were witnessing something sacred.

"You're really good at that," she said as Elena adjusted the carrier.

"At what?"

"At making it look easy. Being a mom, I mean."

Elena laughed softly. "It's not easy. I'm just good at hiding the chaos."

"Still. You make it look... I don't know. Natural."

"It's not natural," Elena said. "It's practice. And a lot of forgiveness—for yourself and everyone else."

Kendra nodded slowly. "I'll remember that."

They walked out to the parking lot together, the cool night air a welcome relief after the warmth of the restaurant. Kendra stopped beside her car and turned to Elena.

"Thank you for coming today," she said. "It really did mean a lot."

"I'm glad I was there," Elena said. And she meant it.

Kendra hesitated, then stepped forward and pulled Elena into a hug—careful of Lucas, but warm and genuine. "You're a good person, Elena Barrett. I'm glad Addison has you in her corner."

Elena hugged her back. "I'm glad you have all of us."

When they pulled apart, Kendra was smiling—the kind of smile that reached her eyes and softened the hard edges she usually wore.

"See you soon?" she asked.

"See you soon," Elena said.

---

On the drive home, Elena thought about the conversation they'd had over dinner. About belonging, and confidence, and the quiet fear that everyone carried but no one talked about.

She thought about Kendra—strong and vulnerable in equal measure, trying to find her place in a world that didn't always make room for people like her.

And she thought about Addison, halfway around the world with Jasper, chasing crises and proving herself in ways that came at a cost Elena was only beginning to understand.

When she pulled into the driveway at Oak Watch, the house was dark except for the porch light Jasper had left on before he'd flown to Africa. Grace was at Sara's for the night, and Lucas was asleep in his carrier.

Elena sat in the car for a moment, the engine ticking as it cooled, and felt the weight of the day settle over her.

She had gone to the competition to support Kendra. But somewhere along the way, she'd learned something about herself too—about the kind of person she wanted to be, and the kind of connections she wanted to build.

She wasn't just a wife or a mother or a nonprofit board member. She was Elena Barrett, and she was still figuring out what that meant.

But tonight, sitting in the driveway with her son sleeping against her chest, she felt like she was getting closer.

And that was enough.

*End of Chapter 7*
