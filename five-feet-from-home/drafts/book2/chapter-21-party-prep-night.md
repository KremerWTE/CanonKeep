# Chapter 21: Party Prep Night

*POV: Elena Barrett*

---

The night before a wedding should feel chaotic. Elena had learned this through years of managing events for Maison Aurelia—the last-minute alterations, the frantic phone calls, the inevitable crisis that required her to be three places at once.

But tonight, Oak Watch was quiet.

Upstairs in the master bedroom, steam rose from the bathroom where Addison had set up a makeshift pressing station. The silk robes they'd ordered weeks ago hung on the back of the door—midnight blue for Addison, champagne for Kendra, soft blush for Elena. The dresses for tomorrow's ceremony were laid out across the bed: Kendra's deep navy halter, Addison's emerald gown, and Elena's sage wrap dress that accommodated the early swell of her second pregnancy.

Elena stood at the window, watching the last light fade from the sky. Lucas was asleep downstairs in his bassinet, monitored by the baby camera on her phone. Grace was in her room, ostensibly doing homework but more likely building something elaborate with craft supplies.

Behind her, she heard the bathroom door open and the soft pad of bare feet on hardwood.

"Steam's ready," Addison said. Her voice carried that particular blend of efficiency and exhaustion that came from managing a week of wedding logistics while still running point on three active BSS crises. "We should get the dresses done before the fabric relaxes."

Elena turned. Addison had already changed into her robe, the silk falling just above her knees. Her dark hair was loose around her shoulders, still damp from the shower she'd taken after her evening run. There was something vulnerable about seeing her like this—no armor, no tailored blazer, just skin and silk and the kind of beauty that came from being completely yourself.

"Where's Kendra?" Elena asked.

"Finishing her call with the caterer. Apparently, someone panicked about the gluten-free options."

Elena smiled. "Of course they did."

Addison crossed to the bed and picked up her emerald gown, holding it up to catch the light. The fabric shimmered like deep water. "I still can't believe Chris agreed to this."

"To what? The wedding?"

"To all of it." Addison's fingers traced the neckline of the dress. "The fact that his fiancée is spending the night before their wedding with her... with us."

Elena heard the hesitation in that word. The uncertainty of what to call what they were building. Not quite a throuple—Chris was part of this too, in his own way. Not quite polyamory, though that word fit better than most. More like a constellation of people who loved each other in ways that didn't require labels to be real.

"He trusts you," Elena said simply. "He trusts Kendra. And he trusts that what happens tonight doesn't threaten what happens tomorrow."

"Does it threaten what you and Jasper have?"

The question was direct, the way all of Addison's important questions were. Elena had learned to appreciate that about her—the refusal to dance around difficult truths.

"No," Elena said. And she meant it. "Jasper and I have been through too much to be threatened by love. And what I feel for you—what I feel for Kendra—it's not a replacement. It's an addition."

Addison looked at her for a long moment, something shifting in her expression. Then she smiled—small, genuine, vulnerable in a way that Elena rarely saw.

"Addition," she repeated. "I like that."

The bathroom door opened again, and Kendra appeared in a cloud of steam, already wearing her champagne robe. Her hair was pinned up in a messy knot, and her face was flushed from the heat.

"The iron's ready," she announced. Then she saw them standing there, Addison holding her dress, Elena by the window, and her expression softened. "Are we doing this or just admiring the view?"

Addison's smile turned wicked. "Can't we do both?"

---

They worked in comfortable silence for a while, taking turns at the makeshift pressing station. Kendra went first, carefully steaming the navy halter while Addison supervised with the critical eye of someone who'd once managed a crisis involving a UN ambassador's wrinkled suit and a thirty-minute deadline.

Elena watched them together and felt something settle in her chest. This was what trust looked like—not grand gestures or dramatic declarations, but two women working side by side to prepare for a wedding, knowing that tomorrow would bring its own complications and choosing not to borrow trouble from the future.

"How's the supply chain situation?" Kendra asked, glancing at Addison.

"Contained," Addison said. "For now. Harper's handling the last of the internal interviews. We should have a final report by Monday."

"You're not allowed to work on your phone tonight," Kendra said firmly.

"I'm not working. I'm thinking about work. There's a difference."

"Not much of one."

Elena smiled. This was their dance—Kendra pushing Addison toward rest, Addison resisting with the stubbornness of someone who'd built her identity around never stopping. It was the same dynamic Jasper and Elena had navigated for years, though they'd needed a medical crisis to finally crack the pattern open.

"Turn around," Kendra said, and Addison did, letting Kendra inspect the back of the dress. Their bodies were close now, close enough that Elena could see the way Kendra's breath hitched when Addison's fingers brushed her wrist.

"It looks good," Kendra said softly.

"Yeah?" Addison's voice had dropped half an octave.

"Yeah."

The air in the room shifted. Elena felt it like a change in atmospheric pressure—the moment when something inevitable finally decided to happen.

Addison turned to face Kendra fully. For a heartbeat, they just stood there, silk robes and bare feet and the weight of everything unspoken between them. Then Addison lifted her hand and traced the line of Kendra's jaw with her fingertips.

Kendra's eyes fluttered closed.

"We have dresses to finish," she whispered, but there was no conviction in it.

"We do," Addison agreed. She leaned in slowly, giving Kendra every chance to pull away. "We should definitely finish the dresses."

"Definitely," Kendra breathed.

And then they were kissing.

It was slow. Unhurried. The kind of kiss that came from knowing you had time, that the world could wait, that this moment was exactly where you were supposed to be.

Elena watched from her place by the bed, her heart full. She should probably look away, give them privacy. But Addison's hand had moved to the back of Kendra's neck, and Kendra had made this small sound of surrender, and Elena couldn't look away because this was beautiful. This was two people she loved finding their way back to each other.

Addison's other hand found Kendra's waist, fingers spreading against silk. Kendra's arms wrapped around Addison's shoulders, pulling her closer. The kiss deepened, years of history and hurt and hope pouring into it.

Elena felt heat bloom in her own chest, spread through her limbs. She recognized this feeling—desire, yes, but also something deeper. Joy at witnessing love. Gratitude for being allowed into this space.

When they finally broke apart, both breathing hard, Kendra rested her forehead against Addison's.

"The dresses," she said weakly.

"Right. The dresses."

Neither of them moved.

Elena cleared her throat softly. Both women turned to look at her, and she saw the question in their eyes. Permission. Invitation. Hope.

She smiled. "Finish the dresses," she said gently. "Before the after-party."

Addison laughed—surprised and delighted. "After-party?"

"You didn't think I planned to spend the whole night ironing, did you?"

Kendra's eyes widened. She looked at Addison, then back at Elena. "You're sure?"

Elena crossed to them, letting herself feel the pull of this moment. She reached out and took one of Kendra's hands in her own, then one of Addison's. Their fingers intertwined, three women standing in a circle of silk and steam and possibility.

"I'm sure," Elena said. "But Grace gets out of the shower in about ten minutes, and she has a habit of barging in when she wants to show me something she's made. So let's get the dresses done, get her settled, and then..."

"After-party," Addison finished, her smile turning wicked again.

"After-party," Elena confirmed.

They broke apart reluctantly. Kendra returned to steaming her dress, though her hands were less steady now. Addison picked up Elena's sage wrap dress and held it up to the light, checking for wrinkles with exaggerated focus. Elena sat on the edge of the bed and watched them both, feeling the anticipation build like a slow burn in her belly.

This was right. However unconventional, however unexpected, this was exactly right.

---

Grace burst through the door seven minutes later, right on schedule.

She was wearing her unicorn pajamas, her hair still damp from the bath, and she was carrying what appeared to be an elaborate construction of pipe cleaners and construction paper.

"Mommy! Look what I made for Auntie Kendra's wedding!"

Elena held out her arms, and Grace bounded over, the construction wobbling precariously. "What is it, baby?"

"It's a flower arch! See? The purple pipe cleaners are wisteria, and the pink ones are roses, and the green paper is leaves."

It looked more like a abstract sculpture than a flower arch, but the pride in Grace's eyes was unmistakable.

"It's beautiful," Elena said, and meant it.

Grace beamed. Then she noticed Addison and Kendra in their matching robes and gasped. "Are you having a sleepover?"

"Something like that," Addison said, her voice carefully neutral.

"Can I sleep in here too? We can do face masks and paint our nails and stay up late!"

Elena caught Addison's eye and saw the flash of amusement there. "How about you sleep in your room tonight, and tomorrow after the wedding, we'll have a proper girls' night?"

Grace's face fell. "But I want to do it now."

"I know, sweetheart. But tomorrow's a big day. You need your rest."

"You're not resting. You're doing dresses."

"We're almost done with the dresses," Kendra said, hanging her navy halter on the back of the bathroom door. She crossed to Grace and knelt down to her level. "And then the grown-ups are going to have boring adult conversations about boring adult things. You'd be asleep in five minutes."

Grace considered this. "Will you braid my hair for the wedding?"

"Absolutely. French braid or fishtail?"

"Both!"

Kendra laughed. "Deal. Now give me a hug and get to bed."

Grace threw her arms around Kendra's neck, then scrambled over to hug Addison, then finally Elena. "Love you, Mommy."

"Love you too, baby."

Elena walked Grace to her room, tucked her in with Olaf the snowman plushie, and kissed her forehead. "Sweet dreams."

"Mommy?"

"Yeah?"

"Is Daddy on a trip?"

The question landed like a stone in Elena's chest. Even now, even after all the changes Jasper had made, Grace still associated nighttime with his absence.

"He's at a dinner meeting," Elena said carefully. "But he'll be home before you wake up. I promise."

Grace nodded, already half-asleep. "Okay. Love you."

"Love you too."

Elena closed the door softly and stood in the hallway for a moment, letting the rush of motherhood settle. Then she checked the baby monitor on her phone. Lucas was still asleep, his tiny chest rising and falling in the blue glow of the night light.

Her family was safe. Her children were cared for. And downstairs, her husband would be home within the hour, probably carrying his laptop and smelling of the bourbon that always accompanied late business dinners.

But upstairs, in the master bedroom, two women were waiting for her.

Elena took a breath, straightened her shoulders, and walked back down the hall.

When she opened the bedroom door, the dresses were all hung perfectly, steam rising from their freshly pressed fabric. Kendra was sitting on the edge of the bed, her robe loose around her shoulders. Addison was standing by the window, backlit by the moon, her silhouette a study in elegant tension.

They both turned when Elena entered.

"Grace settled?" Addison asked.

"Fast asleep."

"And Lucas?"

"Out cold."

Kendra stood slowly. "So it's just us."

Elena closed the door behind her and locked it. The sound of the bolt sliding home felt significant. Final.

"Just us," she confirmed.

Addison crossed to her in three strides. She didn't touch Elena, just stood close enough that Elena could feel the heat radiating from her skin.

"Are you sure?" Addison asked again. "Because once we start, I don't want to stop. And I need to know that you're not going to regret this in the morning."

Elena looked into those storm-gray eyes and saw the vulnerability there. Addison had been hurt before. Betrayed by people who'd promised her safety and then taken it away. She needed to know this was real.

Elena lifted her hand and cupped Addison's cheek. "I won't regret this," she said quietly. "I trust you. I trust Kendra. And I trust myself enough to know what I want."

"What do you want?"

Elena smiled. "You. Her. This."

Addison's breath hitched. Then she leaned in and kissed Elena—soft at first, questioning, and when Elena kissed back, it deepened into something warm and certain.

Kendra appeared at Elena's other side, her hand sliding around Elena's waist. Elena broke the kiss with Addison and turned to Kendra, who was looking at her with such open hope that it made Elena's chest ache.

"Hi," Kendra whispered.

"Hi," Elena whispered back.

And then they were all three of them together, hands and silk and heartbeats, the weight of everything that had led to this moment—hospital visits and midnight conversations, Grace's laughter and Lucas's first smile, years of loneliness and learning and finally, finally trusting enough to let someone else in.

Not to replace what they had.

To add to it.

Outside, the night settled over Oak Watch like a blanket. Inside, three women who had earned each other's trust began the slow, careful work of building something new.

The dresses hung on the bathroom door, perfect and ready for tomorrow.

But tonight was theirs.

*End of Chapter 21*
