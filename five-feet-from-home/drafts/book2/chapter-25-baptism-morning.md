# Chapter 25: Baptism Morning

*POV: Jasper Barrett*

---

The church was different on Sunday morning.

Yesterday, it had been filled with flowers and champagne and the electric energy of a First Wives Club wedding. Now, in the early light filtering through stained glass, it felt sacred. Quiet. Like the world had paused to take a breath.

Jasper Barrett stood in the narthex, Lucas sleeping in his arms, and tried to remember the last time he'd been in a church for something that wasn't a wedding or a funeral.

He couldn't.

Elena appeared beside him, Grace's hand in hers. Their daughter was wearing a white dress with delicate lace sleeves—something Elena had found at a boutique in Myers Park—and her hair was braided with small flowers woven through it.

"Ready?" Elena asked softly.

Jasper looked down at Lucas, at the tiny fist curled against his chest, at the impossibly small face that seemed to shift expressions even in sleep. Three months old. Barely twelve pounds. And yet this child had already changed everything.

"As ready as I'll ever be," Jasper said.

Grace tugged on Elena's hand. "Is Lucas going to cry?"

"Probably," Elena said with a gentle smile. "The water is usually cold."

"Can I hold him after?"

"Of course, sweetheart." Elena smoothed Grace's hair. "You're his big sister. That's the most important job in the world."

They walked into the sanctuary together—Jasper carrying Lucas, Elena holding Grace's hand—and the sight that greeted them made Jasper's throat tighten unexpectedly.

The church wasn't packed, but it was full. Full of the people who mattered.

Harper sat in the third row with Ethan beside her, both of them dressed in Sunday best. Addison and Kendra were a few rows back, flanking Chris like some kind of unconventional honor guard. Cole and Dean were near the back—tactical even in church—scanning the room out of habit.

Sara Whitaker sat with her daughter Emma in the front row, and Jessica was beside them, her hands folded in her lap with the kind of peace that came from decades of faith.

And there, standing near the baptismal font, was Father Michael—the priest who had married Jasper and Elena eleven years ago, who had blessed Grace as an infant, who had somehow remained a steady presence through all the chaos of their lives.

"Jasper." Father Michael's voice was warm, welcoming. "Elena. Grace. And this must be Lucas."

"This is Lucas," Elena confirmed, her voice soft with emotion.

Father Michael smiled and reached out to touch Lucas's head with gentle fingers. "He's beautiful. A blessing."

Jasper felt something shift in his chest—something he couldn't quite name. Pride, maybe. Or gratitude. Or the sudden, overwhelming awareness that he was standing at the intersection of everything that mattered.

---

The service began with hymns.

Jasper had never been particularly religious—his faith, such as it was, had been forged in foxholes and field hospitals, in moments when prayer felt less like a conversation with God and more like a last-ditch attempt to bargain with the universe.

But here, in this church, with Elena beside him and Grace singing softly and Lucas beginning to stir against his chest, something felt different. Purposeful. Right.

The readings were traditional—water and rebirth, promises and community, the responsibility of raising a child in faith.

"Baptism is not just about the child," Father Michael said from the pulpit, his voice carrying through the sanctuary. "It's about the community that surrounds that child. The village that promises to love and protect and guide. The family—biological and chosen—that says, 'We are here. We will not leave you.'"

Jasper glanced around the church. At Harper, who had flown in from a client meeting in Boston just to be here. At Addison, who had reorganized her entire weekend to make sure she didn't miss this. At Cole and Dean, who looked uncomfortable in their Sunday clothes but had shown up anyway because Jasper had asked.

This was his village. His chosen family. The people who had carried him through Elena's medical crisis and the NFL disaster and every other fire that had threatened to consume him.

And now they were here to carry Lucas.

---

When it was time for the baptism itself, Father Michael invited the family forward.

Jasper handed Lucas to Elena, and together they approached the font—an ancient marble basin that had held holy water for over a century. Grace stood beside them, her small hand gripping Jasper's.

"Who stands as godparents for this child?" Father Michael asked.

Addison and Hawk rose from their pew and joined them at the font.

Addison looked uncharacteristically emotional, her usual armor softened by something Jasper couldn't quite identify. Beside her, Hawk stood with his hands clasped, his operator's bearing giving way to something more human.

"We do," Addison said, her voice steady.

Father Michael nodded and began the ritual—ancient words spoken over Lucas's tiny form, promises made on his behalf, water poured three times over his head in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

Lucas woke at the first touch of water and let out a cry—sharp, indignant, utterly perfect.

Grace giggled. "I told you he'd cry."

Elena cradled Lucas close, soothing him with soft murmurs while Father Michael anointed him with oil and wrapped him in a white baptismal garment.

"Lucas James Barrett," Father Michael said, his voice carrying through the church. "I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. May you grow in wisdom and grace, surrounded by love and guided by faith."

"Amen," the congregation responded.

Jasper felt Elena's hand find his, their fingers interlacing. Grace leaned against his leg, her head resting just above his knee. And in that moment, standing at the font with his family and his village, Jasper Barrett felt something he hadn't felt in years.

Peace.

---

After the service, everyone gathered in the church hall for coffee and pastries.

Grace was holding Lucas now, sitting carefully in a chair while Elena hovered nearby. The baby had calmed after his baptismal outburst and was now gazing up at his sister with wide, unfocused eyes.

"He likes me," Grace said with absolute certainty.

"He loves you," Elena corrected. "You're his favorite person."

Jasper watched them from across the room, where he was accepting congratulations from Harper and Ethan.

"You clean up well," Harper said, eyeing his suit. "I wasn't sure you owned anything that wasn't charcoal gray."

"Navy blue exists in my wardrobe," Jasper said dryly. "Occasionally."

Ethan laughed. "Harper told me about the baptism planning. Said you color-coded a spreadsheet."

"Organization is not a character flaw."

"It is when you're tracking diaper changes in Excel," Harper said with a grin.

Before Jasper could respond, Addison appeared at his elbow, Kendra and Chris trailing behind her.

"Congratulations, boss," Addison said. She looked different this morning—softer, more open. The armor she usually wore was still there, but it had cracks in it now. Intentional ones.

"Thank you for being here," Jasper said. "For being godmother. It means—"

"Don't." Addison held up a hand, her eyes suspiciously bright. "If you get sentimental, I'll get sentimental, and then we'll both be disasters."

"Too late," Kendra said, wrapping an arm around Addison's waist. "You've been crying since the hymns."

"I was not crying. I had something in my eye."

"Sure you did." Chris kissed the top of Addison's head. "You're allowed to have feelings, you know."

"I have feelings all the time. I just keep them contained in a very small, manageable box."

Jasper smiled despite himself. "Well, thank you for letting them out long enough to stand up for Lucas."

Addison's expression softened. "He's lucky to have you, Jasper. All three of you—Elena, Grace, Lucas. You're building something real here. Don't forget that."

"I won't." Jasper glanced across the room to where Elena was laughing at something Sara had said, Lucas still cradled in Grace's arms. "I can't afford to."

---

Later, when the coffee had been drunk and the pastries eaten and the well-wishes exchanged, Jasper found himself alone in the sanctuary.

The rest of the family was packing up in the hall—Elena organizing leftover food, Grace helping Sara wipe down tables, Addison and Kendra managing logistics with the efficiency of military commanders.

Jasper sat in the front pew, his hands folded, staring at the baptismal font.

He wasn't a praying man. Not really. But here, in this quiet moment, he found himself speaking to whatever or whoever might be listening.

*Thank you.*

For Elena, who had survived when everything said she shouldn't. For Grace, who had asked the question that changed everything. For Lucas, who had arrived in the middle of chaos and somehow made it all make sense.

For Addison and Harper and Cole and all the others who had carried him when he couldn't carry himself.

For the second chance he didn't deserve but had been given anyway.

*I won't waste it,* he promised. *I won't go back to what I was.*

"Talking to God?"

Jasper turned to find Elena standing in the aisle, Lucas in her arms.

"Something like that."

She slid into the pew beside him, settling Lucas between them. "What are you telling Him?"

"That I'm grateful. And terrified. And completely out of my depth."

Elena laughed softly. "Welcome to parenthood."

"I've been a parent for seven years."

"You've been a father for seven years. You're just now learning to be a dad."

Jasper looked at her, at the gentle wisdom in her eyes. "Is there a difference?"

"All the difference in the world." She reached over and took his hand. "A father provides. A dad shows up. You're learning to do both."

Lucas made a soft cooing sound, his tiny hand reaching toward Jasper's finger. Jasper let him grab on, marveling at the strength of such a small grip.

"He's going to need you," Elena said quietly. "Not just your money or your protection. You. Present. Here. In the five feet around him."

"I know." Jasper's voice was rough. "I'm working on it."

"I know you are." Elena leaned her head against his shoulder. "And I love you for it."

They sat there in the quiet sanctuary, the three of them—soon joined by Grace, who climbed onto Jasper's lap and curled against him like she used to when she was small.

"Daddy?" Grace's voice was sleepy, content.

"Yeah, sweetheart?"

"Are you on our team now?"

Jasper felt his throat tighten. He thought about the question she'd asked months ago—the one that had cracked him open and forced him to see what he'd been missing.

"Yeah, Grace. I'm on your team. Always."

"Good." She yawned and closed her eyes. "I like when you're here."

"So do I," Jasper whispered. "So do I."

---

Outside, the Charlotte morning was clear and bright. The church bells rang the hour—ten chimes, each one echoing across the city.

The village gathered in the parking lot, saying their goodbyes and making plans for the brunch at Oak Watch.

Harper hugged Elena and whispered something in her ear that made Elena laugh. Addison lifted Grace into a spin, then handed her off to Kendra for another. Cole and Dean shook Jasper's hand with the kind of grip that spoke to respect and brotherhood.

And Claire Ashford—who had slipped into the back of the church just before the baptism began—caught Jasper's eye across the parking lot and gave him a small nod.

*You're doing it,* the nod seemed to say. *Keep going.*

Jasper nodded back.

Then he climbed into the car with his family—Elena in the passenger seat, Grace and Lucas buckled in the back—and drove toward home.

Toward the next chapter.

Toward whatever came next.

But this time, he wasn't running toward the crisis.

He was running toward the people who mattered.

And that made all the difference.

*End of Chapter 25*
