# Chapter 13: Elena's Push

*POV: Elena Barrett*

---

Oak Watch looked like a painting in the late afternoon light.

Elena stood at the kitchen window, watching Grace chase Lucas's soccer ball across the yard—though at four months old, Lucas was still too young to do anything but gurgle from his bouncer on the porch. The trees that lined the property were starting to turn, October painting them in shades of amber and rust. The air smelled like woodsmoke and the last remnants of summer.

It was the kind of afternoon that should have felt peaceful.

But Elena had a plan, and peace was going to have to wait.

She heard Jasper's car pull into the driveway before she saw it, the familiar sound of gravel crunching under tires. He'd left the office early—six o'clock, which counted as a minor miracle by his old standards—and the fact that he'd made it home before dark felt like a small victory.

He came through the door carrying his briefcase and a bouquet of sunflowers, which he handed to her with a kiss that lingered just long enough to remind her why she'd married him in the first place.

"For you," he said.

"You're getting soft, Barrett."

"Blame it on the second pregnancy." He grinned, then nodded toward Lucas on the porch. "How's the little man?"

"Perfect. Grace has decided he needs to learn soccer immediately."

Jasper looked out the window and laughed. "She's got her priorities straight."

Elena set the flowers in a vase, running water over the stems, arranging them with the kind of practiced ease that came from a lifetime of making things beautiful. But her mind was elsewhere, turning over the conversation she needed to have, testing the words like ingredients in a recipe.

"Jasper," she said, keeping her voice light. "I need to talk to you about something."

He stilled, the way he always did when she used that tone. "About what?"

"Harper."

---

Jasper leaned against the kitchen counter, arms crossed, his expression shifting into the one he used in crisis meetings—focused, assessing, ready to solve a problem.

"What about Harper?" he asked.

Elena dried her hands on a dish towel and turned to face him. "She's drowning."

"I know."

"Do you?" Elena's voice was gentle, but there was steel beneath it. "Because from where I'm standing, it looks like you know in theory, but you haven't actually done anything about it."

Jasper's jaw tightened. "I hired Claire. She's been working with the team for six weeks. Harper's part of that."

"Harper needs more than a performance coach, Jasper. She needs you."

"Elena—"

"She's your COO. She's been running the company with you for five years. And right now, she's carrying so much weight that if she stumbles, the whole operation stumbles with her." Elena stepped closer, looking up at him with the kind of calm intensity that made him listen even when he didn't want to. "You need to step up for her. The way you stepped up for me."

Jasper exhaled slowly, running a hand through his hair. "What do you want me to do?"

"I want you to mentor Addison."

He blinked. "Addie?"

"She's brilliant, Jasper. You know it. I know it. The entire firm knows it. But she's still acting like she has something to prove, and that's burning her out. If you mentor her—really mentor her, not just delegate tasks—she can take some of the load off Harper. And Harper can breathe."

Jasper was quiet for a long moment, his gaze drifting to the window where Grace was still chasing the soccer ball, her laughter carrying on the breeze.

"You're right," he said finally.

"I usually am."

He smiled despite himself. "I'll talk to Addie tomorrow."

"Good." Elena kissed his cheek. "Now go play soccer with your daughter before she decides you're only good for signing checks."

---

## Later That Evening

After Grace was in bed and Lucas was asleep in his crib, Elena found Jasper in his study, staring at his laptop with the kind of thousand-yard gaze that suggested he was looking at spreadsheets but seeing something else entirely.

She knocked lightly on the doorframe. "You okay?"

He looked up, blinking like he'd forgotten where he was. "Yeah. Just thinking."

"About Harper?"

"About all of it." He leaned back in his chair, rubbing his eyes. "You're right about Addie. She's got the skillset to be my second-in-command, but she's still operating like she's proving herself. If I can get her to step into that role, Harper can focus on operations instead of trying to do everything."

"So you'll start mentoring her?"

"I'll start Monday."

Elena smiled. "Good."

She moved into the room, perching on the edge of his desk. He reached for her hand automatically, threading his fingers through hers.

"There's something else," she said.

"What?"

"I'm going to tell people about the pregnancy."

Jasper straightened. "When?"

"Soon. This weekend, maybe. I'm starting to show, and I don't want to keep hiding it." She paused. "I was thinking I'd start with the close circle. Sara already knows. Harper knows. I want to tell Addison."

"Why Addison?"

Elena looked at him, her expression soft. "Because she's family. And because I think she needs to hear it from me."

Jasper studied her, the way he always did when he was trying to read between the lines. "This is about Kendra, isn't it?"

Elena didn't answer right away. She'd been thinking about Kendra and Addison for months, watching them orbit each other with a gravity that felt inevitable. She'd seen the way they looked at each other at the party last weekend, the way Kendra's hand had lingered on Addison's arm, the way Addison's eyes had softened when Kendra laughed.

She'd also seen the way they both looked at her sometimes, like they were asking a question they didn't know how to voice.

"It's about a lot of things," Elena said finally. "But yes. Kendra's part of it."

"And you're okay with that?"

Elena smiled. "I'm more than okay with it."

## POV: Elena Barrett

The next afternoon, Elena invited Addison to Oak Watch for tea.

Addison arrived at two o'clock, dressed in dark jeans and a fitted blazer, her hair pulled back in the kind of sleek bun that suggested she'd come straight from a meeting. She carried herself with the same controlled grace Elena had always admired, but there was something softer about her today, a lightness in her step that hadn't been there a month ago.

"Thanks for coming," Elena said, ushering her into the sunroom where she'd set up a small table with tea and pastries.

"Of course." Addison settled into one of the wicker chairs, glancing around. "Where's Grace?"

"Sara picked her up for a playdate with Emma. And Lucas is napping." Elena poured the tea, handing Addison a cup. "So we have the place to ourselves."

Addison accepted the tea, but her expression had shifted into something guarded. "Should I be worried?"

"No." Elena laughed. "I just wanted to talk."

"About?"

Elena took a sip of her own tea—decaf, naturally—and set the cup down. "About the pregnancy."

Addison blinked. "I—congratulations. I mean, I know Harper told me a few weeks ago, but I didn't know if I was supposed to say anything."

"You can say anything you want, Addie. You're family." Elena leaned forward slightly. "I wanted to tell you myself because I'm going to announce it this weekend. Close friends first, then the broader circle. And I wanted you to hear it from me before you heard it from anyone else."

"I appreciate that."

"Good." Elena paused, choosing her words carefully. "I also wanted to talk to you about Kendra."

The change in Addison's expression was subtle but unmistakable—a flicker of surprise, followed by the kind of defensive stillness that suggested she was preparing for impact.

"What about her?" Addison asked, her voice carefully neutral.

Elena smiled. "Addie, I'm not blind. I've seen the way you two look at each other. And I've seen the way you've been glowing for the last week."

Addison's cheeks flushed, just slightly. "Elena—"

"I'm not upset." Elena reached across the table, covering Addison's hand with her own. "I'm happy for you. Both of you."

Addison stared at her, something unreadable moving behind her eyes. "You're happy?"

"Of course I am. Kendra makes you smile, Addie. And you deserve to smile."

"It's not that simple."

"I know it's not." Elena squeezed her hand gently. "But it doesn't have to be complicated either. You love her. She loves you. That's a good place to start."

Addison was quiet for a long moment, her gaze dropping to their joined hands. When she spoke, her voice was softer than Elena had ever heard it.

"I've been in love with her for five years," Addison said. "Even when we weren't talking. Even when I convinced myself I was over it. And now she's back, and I don't know how to be anything except terrified."

"Terrified of what?"

"Of losing her again. Of not being enough. Of..." Addison trailed off, shaking her head. "Of all of it."

Elena understood that fear. She'd felt it herself, standing in a hospital room, wondering if Jasper would ever really come back to her. She'd felt it when she'd learned she was pregnant again, terrified that the demands of another child would pull him away.

But fear didn't have to be a wall. It could be a door.

"Addie," Elena said gently. "You are enough. You've always been enough. And Kendra knows that."

Addison looked up, her eyes bright with something that might have been tears. "How do you know?"

"Because I've watched her look at you the way Jasper looks at me. Like you're the answer to a question she's been asking her whole life."

Addison let out a shaky breath, and for a moment, Elena thought she might cry. But then she straightened, pulling herself back together with the kind of discipline Elena had seen her use in crisis after crisis.

"Thank you," Addison said quietly.

"For what?"

"For seeing me. For not making this harder than it has to be."

Elena smiled. "That's what family does."

## POV: Elena Barrett

Later that evening, after Addison had left and Grace was home from her playdate, Elena sat on the porch with a cup of tea, watching the sun set over the trees.

Jasper joined her, carrying Lucas in one arm and a glass of bourbon in the other. He settled into the chair beside her, Lucas gurgling happily against his chest.

"How'd it go with Addie?" he asked.

"Good. She's scared, but she's hopeful."

"That's progress."

Elena nodded, sipping her tea. "I told her about the pregnancy."

"And?"

"She was happy for us."

Jasper smiled, shifting Lucas to a more comfortable position. "You're good at this, you know."

"At what?"

"At holding people together. At making them feel seen." He looked at her, his expression soft. "You do it for me. For Grace. For Addie and Kendra. Hell, you even do it for Harper, and she's supposed to be untouchable."

Elena laughed. "Harper's not untouchable. She's just careful about who she lets in."

"Like Addie."

"Exactly like Addie."

They sat in comfortable silence for a while, the kind of quiet that came from years of learning how to be still together. Grace was inside, singing along to a Disney soundtrack at top volume. Lucas was starting to doze, his tiny fist curled against Jasper's chest.

"I love this," Jasper said quietly.

"Love what?"

"This. Right here." He gestured to the porch, the yard, the house. "The five feet around me. I spent so many years chasing fires on the other side of the world, and I almost missed the fact that everything I needed was right here."

Elena reached over and took his free hand. "You didn't miss it. You're here now."

"I know. And I'm not going anywhere."

"Good." She squeezed his hand. "Because we're going to need you when this one arrives." She patted her stomach, where the second pregnancy was just starting to show.

Jasper leaned over and kissed her, slow and sweet, Lucas nestled between them. "I'm ready."

## POV: Elena Barrett

On Saturday, Elena gathered the close circle at Oak Watch for what she billed as a "casual afternoon."

Sara arrived first, carrying a tray of cookies she'd stress-baked at three in the morning. Harper came next, looking polished as always in a cream sweater and tailored slacks. Kendra arrived with a bottle of wine and a bouquet of wildflowers, her smile bright and genuine. And Addison came last, her hair down and her expression softer than it had been in months.

They gathered in the sunroom, the late afternoon light streaming through the windows, glasses of wine and tea distributed according to who could and couldn't drink.

Elena stood at the head of the small table, her hands resting on the back of a chair, and took a breath.

"I wanted to gather you all here because you're the people who matter most to me," she said. "You're my family, in every way that counts. And I wanted to tell you something before I tell the rest of the world."

Sara was grinning already, because she knew. Harper's expression was warm and knowing, because she knew too. But Kendra and Addison were watching with curious, open faces.

"I'm pregnant," Elena said, her hand drifting to her stomach. "Sixteen weeks. We're due in March."

The room erupted.

Sara was on her feet first, pulling Elena into a hug that lifted her off the ground. Harper was next, her embrace warm and steady. Kendra hugged her with the kind of joy that felt like sunlight, and Addison held her last, whispering, "Congratulations," against her ear.

"Thank you," Elena said, her voice thick with emotion. "All of you. For being here. For being my family."

"We wouldn't be anywhere else," Harper said.

Kendra raised her glass. "To Elena. To new beginnings. To family."

They toasted, glasses clinking, laughter filling the room.

And Elena looked around at these women—her sister, her friends, her chosen family—and felt the weight of gratitude settle over her like a warm blanket.

This was what mattered. Not the galas or the board meetings or the empire she'd built with Maison Aurelia. This. These people. This moment.

The five feet around her.

## POV: Elena Barrett

After the others had left, Addison stayed behind to help clean up.

They worked in comfortable silence, washing dishes and putting away leftovers, the kind of easy choreography that came from years of knowing each other.

"Elena," Addison said quietly, drying a wine glass. "Can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

Addison set down the glass, turning to face her. "When you said you noticed my closeness with Kendra... did you mean that?"

Elena paused, setting down the dish she'd been washing. "I did."

"And you're really okay with it?"

Elena studied Addison's face, the vulnerability there, the hope. "Addie, I'm more than okay with it. I think you and Kendra are perfect for each other."

"Even though—" Addison hesitated. "Even though it's complicated?"

"Love is always complicated." Elena smiled. "But that doesn't make it any less worth it."

Addison exhaled, something loosening in her shoulders. "I've been so scared of what people would think. Of what you would think."

"I think you're brave," Elena said. "And I think Kendra's lucky to have you. Just like you're lucky to have her."

Addison's eyes were bright. "Thank you."

"You don't have to thank me, Addie. You're family. And family shows up."

Addison crossed the space between them and pulled Elena into a hug, holding her tightly. Elena hugged her back, feeling the tension drain out of Addison's frame, replaced by something that felt like relief.

"I love you," Addison whispered.

"I love you too," Elena said.

When they pulled apart, Addison was smiling, the kind of smile that reached her eyes and made her look younger, lighter, more herself.

"Go," Elena said, nodding toward the door. "Go find her."

Addison laughed. "She's probably at the gym."

"Then go to the gym."

"What if—"

"Addie." Elena's voice was firm but kind. "Go."

And Addison went.

## POV: Elena Barrett

Later that night, after Grace was asleep and Lucas was settled in his crib, Elena stood at the window of her bedroom, looking out at the darkened yard.

Jasper came up behind her, wrapping his arms around her waist, his hands settling over the small curve of her belly.

"You did good today," he murmured against her hair.

"I just told people I'm pregnant."

"You did more than that. You gave Addie permission to be happy."

Elena leaned back against him, closing her eyes. "She deserves to be happy."

"So do you."

"I am happy." She turned in his arms, looking up at him. "I have everything I need right here."

Jasper kissed her, slow and deep, the kind of kiss that promised more but didn't demand it. When they pulled apart, he rested his forehead against hers.

"I'm going to start mentoring Addie on Monday," he said. "Officially. Like you said."

"Good."

"And I'm going to make sure Harper gets the support she needs."

"Even better."

"And I'm going to be here. For you. For Grace. For Lucas. For this one." He patted her stomach gently. "I'm done chasing fires on the other side of the world. The five feet around me—that's where I belong."

Elena smiled, her heart full. "Yes, it is."

They stood there in the quiet, holding each other, the night settling around them like a benediction.

And Elena thought, not for the first time, that this was what love looked like. Not grand gestures or sweeping declarations. Just presence. Just showing up. Just choosing, every day, to stay.

The five feet around you.

That was home.
