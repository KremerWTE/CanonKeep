# Chapter 24: Wedding Weekend

*POV: Multiple*

---

The Charlotte church glowed in the late afternoon light, every detail perfect.

Harper stood in the bride's dressing room, adjusting the cathedral veil that cascaded down Madison Cole's back like a waterfall of silk and tulle. The dress—a custom Vera Wang with hand-sewn pearl details—caught the light with every breath Maddie took.

"You're sure about this?" Harper asked, her voice teasing but warm.

Maddie turned from the mirror, her eyes bright. "I've been sure since he asked me to marry him on a Tuesday morning in our kitchen wearing sweatpants."

"That's how you know it's real," said Vivienne Ross from her perch near the window, champagne flute in hand. She was wearing emerald silk that made her look like she'd stepped out of a Gatsby film. "When they propose in sweatpants instead of waiting for the grand gesture."

Natalia Cruz laughed from the vanity where she was touching up her lipstick. "Viv, your husband proposed to you at the Met Gala."

"That's why I know the difference." Viv raised her glass. "To Maddie. And to Chris, who had the good sense to lock you down before someone smarter came along."

The room erupted in laughter and the clink of champagne glasses.

Harper caught her reflection in the mirror—maid of honor, wearing a champagne-colored gown that Maddie had chosen specifically because it "brought out the gold in her eyes." Her hair was swept into an elegant updo, and for once, she wasn't checking her phone every thirty seconds.

The NFL crisis had resolved itself two days ago. The league had taken Harper's strategy—framing the ownership dispute around fan loyalty and franchise legacy—and run with it so successfully that both billionaires had backed down rather than look like the villains. The minority owner had agreed to stay local. The would-be mover had agreed to invest in stadium renovations instead.

It was over. And Harper had actually slept eight hours last night for the first time in three weeks.

"Harper." Maddie's voice pulled her back. "You look a million miles away."

"Just thinking about how far we've come since Columbia."

"We were idiots in college," Camille Whitmore said from the couch, where she was scrolling through her phone with the practiced ease of a lifestyle influencer. "Remember when we thought sleeping four hours and running on coffee was a personality trait?"

"Some of us still think that," Harper said dryly.

"Not anymore." The voice came from the doorway—Elena Barrett, radiant in a soft blush dress that draped elegantly over her post-pregnancy figure. In her arms, Lucas slept peacefully, his tiny fist curled against her shoulder.

The room went quiet for a beat, the kind of reverent hush that newborns commanded.

"Oh my God," Maddie whispered, crossing the room. "Can I?"

Elena smiled and carefully transferred Lucas into Maddie's arms. The bride-to-be looked down at the sleeping infant, her expression melting into something soft and wondering.

"He's perfect," Maddie said.

"He's also a terrible sleeper," Elena said with a laugh. "But we'll take what we can get."

Harper watched the scene—Maddie in her wedding dress holding Elena's three-month-old son, surrounded by women who had known each other for over a decade—and felt something shift in her chest. This was what mattered. Not the billable hours or the crisis calls or the seventeen-hour days. This. The people who showed up. The moments that couldn't be rescheduled.

Her phone buzzed in her clutch. She ignored it.

---

Downstairs, the church was filling with guests.

Jasper Barrett stood near the front, straightening his tie and scanning the room with the assessing eye of someone who had spent a lifetime evaluating threats and opportunities.

"You look nervous," said a voice beside him.

Jasper turned to find Ethan Cole—no relation to the groom—smiling at him with that easy architect's charm that had apparently won Harper's heart. He was wearing a navy suit that fit him like it had been tailored specifically for this occasion, and he carried himself with the quiet confidence of someone who didn't need to prove anything.

"I'm not nervous," Jasper said. "I'm observing."

"Same thing, different vocabulary." Ethan extended his hand. "Ethan. I've heard a lot about you."

"All of it accurate, I'm sure." Jasper shook his hand, noting the firm grip and the direct eye contact. Military? No. But something adjacent. "You're the architect."

"Guilty. I design buildings that don't fall down. Occasionally, I also date terrifying women who run crisis management firms."

Jasper found himself smiling despite himself. "How's that working out for you?"

"Terrifyingly well." Ethan glanced toward the front of the church, where the groomsmen were gathering. "Harper told me about the NFL thing. Said you pulled off a miracle."

"Harper pulled off the miracle. I just gave her the framework."

"That's not what she said." Ethan's gaze was steady, assessing. "She said you've been different lately. More present. More human."

Jasper didn't answer immediately. He thought about Elena in the hospital. About Grace asking if he was on their team. About Lucas sleeping in his arms at three in the morning while Elena showered and tried to remember what it felt like to be a person instead of just a mother.

"I'm working on it," he finally said.

"Good." Ethan's smile was genuine. "The world has enough workaholics. Not enough good fathers."

Before Jasper could respond, Elena appeared beside them, Lucas still asleep in her arms. She looked radiant—glowing in a way that had nothing to do with makeup and everything to do with the kind of peace that came from being exactly where you were supposed to be.

"Jasper, can you take him? I need to help Harper with something."

Jasper reached for his son, cradling the small, warm weight against his chest. Lucas stirred briefly, then settled back into sleep with a soft sigh.

"You're getting good at that," Ethan observed.

"Practice," Jasper said. "Lots of practice."

Elena kissed Lucas's head, then Jasper's cheek, and disappeared toward the bride's room.

Ethan watched her go, then turned back to Jasper. "You're a lucky man."

"I know."

---

The ceremony was everything a First Wives Club wedding should be: elegant, emotional, and just extravagant enough to make a statement without tipping into excess.

Chris Cole stood at the altar in a custom Tom Ford tux, his quarterback composure barely holding as Maddie walked down the aisle on her father's arm. When she reached him, he whispered something that made her laugh through her tears.

Harper stood to Maddie's right, holding her bouquet and watching with the kind of attention she usually reserved for boardroom negotiations. Across from her, Chris's best man—a teammate who looked like he could bench-press a truck—was trying not to cry.

The vows were traditional but personal, woven with inside jokes and promises that made the assembled guests laugh and cry in equal measure.

"I promise to love you even when you leave your cleats in the hallway," Maddie said.

"I promise to love you even when you reorganize my entire closet by color and season," Chris replied.

When the minister pronounced them husband and wife, the church erupted in applause. Chris kissed Maddie like they were the only two people in the room, and Harper felt her throat tighten with unexpected emotion.

She caught movement in the back row—Addison Price, stunning in a pastel yellow dress that brought out the gold in her skin, was wiping her eyes. Next to her, Hawk sat with his arm around her shoulders, looking like he'd been born to wear a suit.

And beside them, Kendra—glowing in a mint-green wrap dress—was holding hands with both Addison and a man Harper didn't recognize. The dynamic should have been strange, but somehow it just looked... right.

Harper's gaze drifted to the third row, where Claire Ashford sat with perfect posture, taking in everything with the calm, assessing gaze of someone who was always three steps ahead. She was wearing a simple navy sheath dress and looked completely at ease.

The new junior strategist. The woman Jasper had hired to keep everyone from burning out.

Harper caught Claire's eye across the church. Claire smiled—small, knowing—and gave a tiny nod.

*Message received,* Harper thought. *I see you. And I'm not threatened. Yet.*

---

The reception was held at The Mint Museum, with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the Charlotte skyline and enough champagne to float a small yacht.

Addison stood near the bar with Kendra and Chris—the man she'd met at Kendra's CrossFit competition, the one who had somehow become part of their unconventional circle without making it weird.

"This is beautiful," Kendra said, her eyes sweeping across the room. "Maddie has incredible taste."

"Maddie has an incredible budget," Addison said dryly. "There's a difference."

Chris laughed. "Spoken like someone who manages billionaires for a living."

"I contain crises. The billionaires just happen to be the ones creating them." Addison accepted a champagne flute from a passing server and took a sip. "But tonight, no crises. Just dancing and drinking and pretending we're normal people."

"Are we not normal people?" Kendra asked, leaning into Addison's side.

"Babe, you just placed third in regionals. I just saved an NFL franchise. And Chris here..." Addison turned to him with a raised eyebrow. "What do you do again?"

"I run a nonprofit," Chris said with a grin. "Very boring. Lots of paperwork."

"Liar," Kendra said affectionately. "You're building schools in Kenya."

"Still paperwork."

Across the room, Harper was introducing Ethan to the Tier 1 guys—Cole and Dean, who had arrived together and looked like they were attending a wedding under duress. Both men were in suits that somehow still managed to look tactical.

"So you're the architect," Cole said, shaking Ethan's hand with the kind of grip that tested a man's resolve.

"And you're the operator Harper refuses to fire even though you make her life difficult," Ethan replied smoothly.

Dean laughed. "I like him."

"Don't encourage him," Harper said, but she was smiling.

Cole released Ethan's hand and studied him for a moment. "You treat her right?"

"I bring her coffee at midnight and don't complain when she takes calls during dinner."

"Good man."

The conversation shifted to college football—apparently Ethan had played at UVA—and Harper found herself drifting away, drawn toward the windows where Jasper stood with Lucas in his arms, rocking the baby gently while Elena talked to Caroline Westbrook near the dessert table.

"He's changed," said a voice beside her.

Harper turned to find Claire Ashford, champagne in hand, watching Jasper with professional interest.

"He's trying," Harper said carefully.

"Trying is good. Succeeding is better." Claire took a sip of champagne. "How are you doing? With the resolution?"

"I'm good. Exhausted, but good."

"When's the last time you took a vacation?"

Harper laughed. "What year is it?"

"That's what I thought." Claire turned to face her fully. "I'm not here to fix you, Harper. But I am here to make sure you don't break. There's a difference."

"And what if I told you I'm fine?"

"I'd say that people who are actually fine don't need to announce it." Claire's gaze was direct but not unkind. "Look, I've worked with SEALs who insisted they were fine right up until they collapsed mid-mission. I've worked with CEOs who ran their companies into the ground because they couldn't admit they needed rest. You're brilliant, Harper. But brilliance doesn't make you invincible."

Harper wanted to argue. Wanted to prove that she was the exception, that she could handle anything. But then she thought about Ethan bringing her coffee at midnight. About the way he'd kissed her goodnight last Tuesday and told her he loved her even when she was cranky from lack of sleep.

"I hear you," Harper said finally. "I don't love it, but I hear you."

"That's all I ask." Claire raised her glass. "To not breaking."

"To not breaking," Harper echoed, and they clinked glasses.

---

The reception rolled on into the evening—toasts and dancing and the kind of laughter that only came when good people gathered to celebrate something worth celebrating.

Elena found herself at a table with Addison, Kendra, and Chris, watching as Jasper danced with Grace on the makeshift dance floor. Lucas was asleep in his carrier beside her, blissfully unaware of the music and lights.

"Your husband is full of surprises," Addison said, watching Jasper spin Grace with exaggerated care.

"He's learning," Elena said. "Slowly. But he's learning."

Kendra leaned forward, her eyes bright. "Can we talk about the fact that everyone is finally meeting Ethan? Harper's been hiding him for months."

"She hasn't been hiding him," Elena said. "She's been protecting him."

"From what?" Chris asked.

"From us." Addison gestured around the table. "From the circus. From the pressure of being Harper's person in a world where Harper doesn't do people."

"Except now she does," Kendra observed. "Look at them."

They all turned to watch Harper and Ethan on the dance floor. She was laughing at something he'd said, her head thrown back in genuine delight. He had his hand at the small of her back, guiding her through a turn with the kind of easy confidence that suggested he knew exactly how to handle her.

"He's good for her," Elena said softly.

"He's perfect for her," Addison corrected. "Which is terrifying, because Harper doesn't do perfect. She does complicated and messy and emotionally unavailable."

"People change," Chris said, his gaze finding Kendra's. "Sometimes they just need the right reason."

The music shifted—a slow song, something romantic and timeless—and Jasper appeared beside Elena, extending his hand.

"May I?"

Elena laughed. "You're asking permission now?"

"I'm practicing being civilized."

She took his hand and let him pull her onto the dance floor. His arms came around her waist, and she rested her head against his shoulder, breathing in the familiar scent of his cologne and the faint trace of baby powder from Lucas.

"You're good at this," she murmured.

"At dancing?"

"At being here. Present. Not thinking about work."

"I'm always thinking about work," Jasper admitted. "But I'm also thinking about you. And Grace. And Lucas. And how lucky I am that you haven't given up on me yet."

Elena pulled back enough to look at him. "I'm not going to give up on you, Jasper. But I need you to keep showing up. Not just tonight. Not just at the big moments. Every day. The small moments. That's where life happens."

"I know." He kissed her forehead. "I'm learning."

"Good." She settled back against his shoulder. "Because I really like this version of you."

---

Later, when the cake had been cut and the bouquet tossed (caught by Isabella Santoro, who looked equal parts delighted and terrified), Harper found herself on the museum balcony with Ethan, looking out over the Charlotte skyline.

"You survived," he said.

"I always survive."

"You did more than survive. You were present. You laughed. You danced. You didn't check your phone once during dinner."

Harper turned to him, surprised. "You were counting?"

"I'm always counting." He pulled her close, his arms warm around her waist. "I count how many times you smile. How many times you let yourself relax. How many times you choose to be here instead of somewhere else in your head."

"That's deeply neurotic."

"Says the woman who color-codes her calendar by crisis severity."

She laughed despite herself. "Fair point."

Ethan's expression turned serious. "I love you, Harper. All of you. The brilliant, terrifying, workaholic parts. But I also love this version—the one who lets herself enjoy a wedding. The one who's learning to breathe."

Harper felt her throat tighten. "I love you too. Even though you're too good at seeing through my armor."

"Someone has to." He kissed her, soft and slow. "And I'm not going anywhere."

Inside, the music swelled. Laughter echoed through the museum halls. Lucas woke up and started to fuss, and Elena scooped him up with practiced ease. Grace spun in circles until she was dizzy, her dress a blur of champagne silk.

And for one perfect evening, everyone was exactly where they were supposed to be.
