# Chapter 12: Kendra Wins

*POV: Multiple*

---

The convention center in Charlotte smelled like sweat and chalk dust and victory.

Kendra Ross stood on the competition floor, chest heaving, hands on her knees, a lifetime of effort distilled into one perfect moment. The scoreboard above her head told the story in glowing white numbers: first place in the Games division, women's 30-34 bracket.

From the bleachers, a roar went up that sounded like home.

Sara Whitaker was on her feet, hands cupped around her mouth, screaming loud enough to wake the dead. Elena Barrett stood beside her, clapping with the kind of grace that suggested she'd spent a lifetime at galas and fundraisers, but her smile was genuine. Grace was jumping up and down, tugging on Elena's sleeve, pointing at the scoreboard like she'd personally willed it into existence.

And Addison Price was there, standing a few rows back, arms crossed over her chest, wearing an expression that could have been carved from stone except for the wetness at the corners of her eyes.

Kendra looked up, found Addison's gaze across the distance, and mouthed the words: *I did it.*

Addison mouthed back: *I know.*

---

## POV: Kendra Ross

The victory platform felt surreal beneath Kendra's feet.

She'd competed in Regionals before, placed third last year, fought her way back from a shoulder injury that should have ended her career. She'd trained six days a week for eighteen months, eating chicken and rice until the sight of it made her nauseous, lifting weights until her hands bled, running sprints until her legs gave out and she had to crawl back to the starting line.

And now she stood here, gold medal heavy around her neck, photographers snapping pictures, the MC announcing her name like she'd just won the lottery.

But the only person she could see was Addison.

Addie stood at the edge of the crowd, hands still crossed, shoulders squared like she was bracing for impact. She wore dark jeans and a cream sweater that made her look softer than she had any right to be, hair pulled back in a messy braid that suggested she'd gotten ready in a hurry. The fluorescent lights caught the angles of her face, the sharp line of her jaw, the storm in her eyes.

Kendra had loved Addison Price for five years.

She'd loved her when they met at a charity 5K and Addie had outrun her by three minutes. She'd loved her when they started training together and Addie pushed her harder than any coach ever had. She'd loved her through the breakup, through the silence, through the slow, painful rebuild that had started six months ago when Sara dragged them both to the same CrossFit gym and forced them to be in the same room.

And she loved her now, standing on a platform with a medal around her neck, knowing that Addison had flown back from a work trip to be here.

The MC handed her a microphone.

"Kendra, how does it feel?"

She looked at Addison. "Like I'm exactly where I'm supposed to be."

---

## POV: Addison Price

Addison's hands were shaking.

She pressed them harder against her ribs, forcing them into stillness, the same way she'd learned to control her breathing during tactical operations, the same way she'd learned to bury emotion during client meetings that could make or break careers.

But this wasn't work. This was Kendra, standing on a platform in front of two hundred people, looking at Addison like she was the only person in the room.

*I can't do this,* Addison thought. *I can't feel this much and survive it.*

But she was doing it anyway.

"She looks happy," Elena said quietly, appearing at Addison's elbow.

Addison glanced over. Elena wore a soft blue dress that hid the early curve of her pregnancy, hair pinned up in a way that suggested effort but not vanity. She looked like the kind of woman who belonged at fundraisers and board meetings, not in a convention center that smelled like protein powder and ambition.

But she was here.

"She is happy," Addison said. "She worked for this."

"So did you."

Addison frowned. "I didn't do anything."

"You showed up." Elena's voice was gentle, the same tone she used when Grace asked difficult questions. "You've been showing up for six months. That's not nothing."

Addison didn't know how to respond to that. Showing up had always felt like the bare minimum, the thing you did because the alternative was cowardice. But Elena made it sound like a gift, like presence was the highest form of love.

Maybe it was.

Down on the floor, Kendra stepped off the platform and was immediately swarmed by other competitors, coaches, friends. She hugged a woman in a neon pink tank top, high-fived a guy with a beard and a CrossFit Games tattoo, laughed at something Sara shouted from the bleachers.

But then she looked up again, scanning the crowd until she found Addison.

Their eyes met.

And Addison felt something crack open in her chest, the same feeling she'd had in Miami when she'd woken up in a hospital bed and realized she'd almost died alone. The same feeling she'd had when Kendra had shown up at her apartment three months ago with a six-pack of beer and a willingness to sit in silence until Addison remembered how to speak.

*I love you,* she thought. *I never stopped.*

## POV: Jasper Barrett

Jasper arrived at the afterparty twenty minutes late, still wearing the suit he'd worn to a client meeting that morning.

The venue was a rooftop bar in Uptown Charlotte, string lights crisscrossing the open-air space, a DJ spinning something with a bass line that made the floor vibrate. The crowd was a mix of athletes, friends, and family, all of them glowing with the kind of energy that came from witnessing excellence.

Elena found him first, slipping her hand into his.

"You made it," she said.

"I made it." He kissed her temple, then her lips. "How's she doing?"

"Better than I've ever seen her." Elena nodded toward the far corner of the rooftop, where Kendra stood surrounded by a knot of people, still wearing her gold medal, laughing so hard she had to brace herself against Sara's shoulder.

"Good." Jasper scanned the crowd. "Where's Addie?"

Elena pointed to the bar, where Addison stood alone, nursing a whiskey neat, watching Kendra with the kind of focus Jasper recognized from their work together. It was the look she got when she was running scenarios, calculating outcomes, preparing for every possible future.

"She's scared," Elena said quietly.

"Of what?"

"Of this." Elena gestured to the party, the people, the joy. "Of letting herself have it."

Jasper knew that fear. He'd lived it for a decade, chasing crises across time zones, convinced that if he stopped moving, he'd have to face the fact that he was failing the people who mattered most.

He'd almost lost everything before he learned better.

"I should talk to her," he said.

Elena squeezed his hand. "Later. Let her sit with it."

---

## POV: Kendra Ross

By the time the party hit full swing, Kendra's voice was hoarse from shouting over the music.

She'd hugged what felt like a hundred people, posed for photos until her face hurt from smiling, answered the same questions about her training regimen and nutrition plan so many times she could recite them in her sleep. She'd danced with Sara, who spun her around until they were both dizzy, and with Chris, her boyfriend, who kissed her forehead and told her he was proud of her.

But the person she wanted was standing at the bar, alone, watching.

Kendra excused herself from the conversation she was pretending to follow and crossed the rooftop. The string lights cast everything in gold, making the night feel suspended, like a memory that hadn't finished forming yet.

Addison looked up as she approached, something unreadable flickering across her face.

"Hey," Kendra said.

"Hey." Addison took a sip of her whiskey. "Hell of a performance."

"Thanks." Kendra leaned against the bar, close enough to feel the warmth radiating off Addison's skin. "You didn't have to come."

"I wanted to."

"You flew back from Boston."

"I know."

Kendra studied her. Addison's armor was up, the same walls she'd built after the breakup, the same distance she maintained when things got too real. But there were cracks now, places where the light got through.

"Addie," Kendra said softly. "I'm glad you're here."

Addison's jaw tightened. For a moment, Kendra thought she might bolt, disappear into the crowd the way she always did when emotions ran too high. But then Addison set down her glass, turned to face Kendra fully, and said, "I'm glad I'm here too."

It wasn't a declaration. It wasn't a promise. But it was enough.

---

## POV: Elena Barrett

Elena watched from across the rooftop as Kendra and Addison stood together at the bar, not touching, but so close they might as well have been.

"They're going to figure it out," Sara said, appearing at Elena's side with two glasses of champagne. She handed one to Elena, who accepted it with a smile even though she couldn't drink it.

"I know," Elena said.

"You're not worried?"

Elena considered. She'd spent the last six months watching Addison and Kendra orbit each other like binary stars, pulled together by gravity, held apart by fear. She'd seen the way Addison's face softened when Kendra walked into a room. She'd seen the way Kendra lit up when Addison texted her.

And she'd seen the way both of them looked at her sometimes, like they were asking a question they didn't know how to voice.

"I'm not worried," Elena said. "I think they're exactly where they need to be."

Sara raised an eyebrow. "That sounds like you have a plan."

Elena smiled. "I always have a plan."

## POV: Jasper Barrett

Jasper found himself standing next to the DJ booth, holding a beer he hadn't asked for, watching his wife orchestrate the evening like a symphony conductor.

Elena moved through the crowd with the kind of grace that made people gravitate toward her, pausing to congratulate Kendra, to laugh at something Sara said, to pull Grace onto the makeshift dance floor and spin her around until she squealed.

She was radiant.

And she was pregnant.

Jasper was one of the few people who knew. Elena had told him two weeks ago, curled up beside him in bed, her hand pressed to his chest like she was feeling for his heartbeat. They'd agreed to wait before announcing it, to savor the secret a little longer, to let themselves adjust to the idea of another child before the world weighed in.

But watching her now, Jasper felt the weight of it settle over him like a coat. Another child. Another chance to get it right. Another opportunity to be present, to choose the five feet around him instead of the crises on the other side of the world.

He could do this.

He *would* do this.

Across the rooftop, Addison caught his eye and lifted her glass in a silent toast. Jasper raised his beer in response, and for a moment, they understood each other perfectly.

*We're stealing her for the office full-time,* Elena had said earlier, nodding toward Kendra. *She's too good to waste on just the gym.*

Jasper had agreed. Kendra had a mind for strategy, a work ethic that rivaled Addison's, and a network of athletes and influencers that could be invaluable for BSS's expanding portfolio. She'd been consulting part-time for six months, helping them build out their wellness and performance division.

But full-time meant something else. It meant Kendra and Addison in the same office, the same meetings, the same late nights.

It meant collision or combustion.

Jasper suspected it would be both.

---

## POV: Addison Price

The party lasted until midnight, but Addison left early, slipping out while Kendra was distracted by a group of competitors who wanted one more photo with the champion.

She didn't say goodbye. She just left.

The drive back to her apartment took fifteen minutes, the city sliding past in streaks of neon and shadow. Addison parked in the underground garage, took the elevator to the twelfth floor, unlocked her door, and stepped into the silence that had become her refuge.

But tonight, the silence felt wrong.

She poured herself another whiskey, neat, and stood at the floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the city. Charlotte glowed below her, a constellation of lights that never quite went dark.

Her phone buzzed.

**Kendra:** *You left.*

Addison stared at the message, thumb hovering over the keyboard.

**Addison:** *Needed to clear my head.*

**Kendra:** *Did it work?*

**Addison:** *Not yet.*

Three dots appeared, disappeared, appeared again.

**Kendra:** *Can I come over?*

Addison closed her eyes. She could say no. She could protect herself, keep the walls up, maintain the distance that had kept her safe for five years.

Or she could say yes.

**Addison:** *Yes.*

---

## POV: Kendra Ross

Kendra arrived at Addison's apartment twenty-three minutes later, still wearing the gold medal because she'd forgotten to take it off.

Addison opened the door in bare feet and the same cream sweater from earlier, hair down now, falling in waves past her shoulders. She looked softer like this, less armored, more human.

"Hey," Kendra said.

"Hey." Addison stepped back. "Come in."

The apartment was exactly as Kendra remembered it: minimalist, elegant, everything in its place. Floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the city, a sleek kitchen that looked like it had never been used, furniture that suggested money but not warmth.

It looked like a place someone lived but didn't call home.

"You want a drink?" Addison asked.

"Sure."

Addison poured two whiskeys, handed one to Kendra, and they stood there in the living room, not quite looking at each other.

"Congratulations," Addison said finally. "I didn't get to say it properly."

"You said it at the bar."

"I know. But I wanted to say it again." Addison took a sip of her drink. "You were incredible today. I've watched you train for eighteen months, and I knew you'd win. But seeing it happen..."

"What?"

Addison's eyes met hers. "It reminded me why I fell in love with you."

The words hung in the air between them, heavy and bright and terrifying.

Kendra set down her glass. "Addie—"

"I know," Addison said quickly. "I know you're with Chris. I know we said we'd take it slow. I know all of that. But tonight, watching you up there, I just..." She exhaled. "I needed you to know."

Kendra crossed the space between them, closing the distance in three steps. She reached up, cupped Addison's face in both hands, and kissed her.

Addison froze for half a second, then melted into it, hands coming up to grip Kendra's waist, pulling her closer. The kiss was soft at first, tentative, like they were remembering the shape of each other. Then it deepened, became something urgent and necessary, five years of longing compressed into a single moment.

When they finally broke apart, both of them breathing hard, Kendra rested her forehead against Addison's.

"I love you," Kendra whispered. "I never stopped."

"I know," Addison said. "Me neither."
