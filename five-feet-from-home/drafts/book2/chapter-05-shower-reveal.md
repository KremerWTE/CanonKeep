# Chapter 5: Shower Reveal

*POV: Jasper/Elena Shared*

---

## JASPER

The drive home from The Forge took longer than usual. Traffic on I-77 was snarled by construction, and Jasper found himself sitting in the kind of gridlock that used to make him want to throw his phone out the window. Now, he just turned up the podcast he wasn't really listening to and let his mind drift.

Two crises in one day. The European client situation was worse than he'd initially thought—a tech firm with ties to a mining operation in Central Africa, allegations of bribery, whispers of a coup attempt. Harper had the NFL mess under control, which meant he could focus on the international piece without splitting his attention.

But the cost of that focus was time. Time away from home. Time away from Elena and Grace.

He thought about the look on Elena's face this morning when he'd kissed her goodbye. She'd smiled, but there was something underneath it—a wariness, maybe, or a question she wasn't ready to ask. He couldn't blame her. Three months of being present didn't erase a decade of absences. Trust was rebuilt in moments, but it was tested in patterns.

By the time he pulled into the driveway of Oak Watch, the sun was setting, painting the trees in shades of amber and gold. Grace's bike was leaning against the garage, crooked and unbothered. Inside, he could hear laughter—Grace's high-pitched giggle and Elena's lower, warmer laugh.

Home.

He stepped through the front door and was immediately ambushed by Grace, who launched herself at him with the kind of enthusiasm that suggested she'd been waiting by the window.

"Daddy! You're home!"

He caught her mid-leap, lifting her up and spinning her around. "I told you I would be."

"Before dark," she said, pointing at the window where the last rays of sunlight were still visible. "You made it."

"I did." He set her down, then ruffled her hair. "Where's your mom?"

"Upstairs. She said she needed to 'decompress,' which I think means take a bath."

Jasper smiled. "Probably."

Grace was already bounding toward the kitchen, something about homework and a snack, and Jasper let her go. He climbed the stairs, loosening his tie as he went. The house was quiet in the way that only happened when Grace was occupied and Lucas was napping. It was the kind of quiet that felt like a gift.

He found Elena in their bedroom, standing by the window with a cup of tea in her hands. She was wearing one of his old college sweatshirts and leggings, her hair damp and pinned up in a messy knot. She looked beautiful in the way that still caught him off guard sometimes—not the polished, put-together version of herself she wore for the world, but the softer, more vulnerable version she saved for him.

"Hey," he said softly.

She turned, and the smile that crossed her face was the real one. The one that reached her eyes. "Hey yourself. How was the drive?"

"Long. Traffic was awful." He crossed to her, wrapping his arms around her waist and pulling her close. "How was lunch?"

"Good. Sara's on a mission to introduce us to her new trainer. Harper has a mystery man she's trying to keep secret. The usual."

Jasper raised an eyebrow. "Mystery man?"

"His name is Ethan. He's an architect. And before you go into protective mode, she's happy. Let her have this."

"I wasn't going to go into protective mode."

Elena gave him a look.

"Okay, maybe a little." He kissed her temple, breathing in the scent of her shampoo. "But only because I care."

"I know." She set down her tea and turned in his arms, looping her hands around his neck. "You smell like stress and coffee."

"That bad?"

"Not bad. Just... you." She studied his face, her eyes searching. "How bad is it? The European thing?"

"Bad enough that I'll need to make some calls tonight. But not bad enough that I'm getting on a plane."

"Yet."

"Yet," he admitted. "But I'm trying, Elena. I really am."

"I know you are." She reached up and traced the line of his jaw with her thumb. "And I see it. Grace sees it. We're not asking for perfect, Jasper. We're just asking for present."

"I can do present."

"Good." She kissed him, slow and soft, the kind of kiss that felt like a promise. When she pulled back, there was something in her eyes—something he couldn't quite name. "I need to tell you something."

His stomach tightened. "Okay."

"But first, I need you to take a shower with me."

He blinked. "Now?"

"Now." Her smile was mischievous, the kind that reminded him of the woman he'd fallen in love with a decade ago. "Grace is doing homework. Lucas is asleep. We have maybe twenty minutes before chaos descends. Let's not waste it."

---

## ELENA

The shower was one of the few luxuries they'd splurged on when they renovated Oak Watch—a walk-in with rain showerheads and enough space for two people to move without bumping elbows. Elena had insisted on it, over Jasper's protests that it was "unnecessarily extravagant." Now, standing under the hot water with her husband's hands on her waist, she was pretty sure he'd changed his mind.

"This is nice," Jasper murmured, his lips brushing her shoulder.

"Mm-hmm." Elena leaned back against him, letting the water cascade over both of them. "We should do this more often."

"Agreed."

They stood like that for a long moment, the steam rising around them, the world outside the bathroom door feeling impossibly far away. Elena's heart was pounding in her chest, not from the heat of the water but from the weight of what she was about to say.

She'd known for two weeks. Two weeks of carrying the secret like a treasure she wasn't quite ready to share. She'd told Sara first, in a whispered confession at the gym. Then Harper, during a quiet moment at a charity event. But Jasper—Jasper deserved to hear it in a moment like this. Private, intimate, theirs.

"Jasper," she said softly.

"Yeah?"

"I need to tell you something."

His hands stilled on her waist. "You're scaring me."

"Don't be scared." She turned in his arms, water streaming down her face, and looked up at him. "It's good news. I promise."

He studied her, his eyes searching hers, and she could see the moment he started to piece it together. The decaf tea. The way she'd been glowing, according to Sara. The fact that she'd pulled him into the shower instead of waiting until after dinner to have this conversation.

"Elena," he said slowly, his voice thick with something between hope and disbelief. "Are you—"

"Pregnant," she finished. "Three months. We're having another baby."

For a moment, he just stared at her, his expression unreadable. Then, slowly, a smile broke across his face—the kind of smile that was rare for Jasper Barrett, the kind that reached all the way to his eyes and made him look ten years younger.

"We're having another baby," he repeated, as if saying it out loud would make it more real.

"We are."

He pulled her close, his arms wrapping around her so tightly she could barely breathe. "Elena. God, Elena. This is—"

"Good?" she offered, her voice muffled against his chest.

"This is everything."

She felt the tension in her chest release, the worry she hadn't realized she'd been carrying. She'd known he'd be happy—of course he'd be happy—but there was a part of her that had worried he'd see it as another complication, another thing pulling him away from work.

But the man holding her now wasn't the man who would have thought that way. This was the Jasper who'd chosen to come home before dark. The Jasper who'd turned down the global expansion. The Jasper who was learning, slowly and imperfectly, how to be present.

"We've been trying," she said, pulling back just enough to look at him. "Quietly. For a few months now."

"I know." He cupped her face in his hands, his thumbs brushing away the water on her cheeks. "I was starting to think maybe it wasn't going to happen."

"Me too." She smiled, feeling the tears mix with the shower water. "But it did. And now we get to do this all over again."

"Grace is going to lose her mind."

Elena laughed. "She's going to demand a baby sister. You know that, right?"

"I don't care if it's a sister or a brother. I just care that it's ours."

They stood there under the water, holding each other, and Elena felt something settle in her chest. This was what she'd been waiting for. Not just the pregnancy, but this moment—this connection, this certainty that they were building something together.

"I love you," she whispered.

"I love you too." He kissed her, slow and deep, and for a moment, the rest of the world disappeared.

---

## JASPER

They made it out of the shower before Grace came looking for them, which Jasper considered a minor miracle. Elena was wrapped in a towel, her hair dripping onto the bathroom floor, and he was pulling on sweatpants when his phone buzzed on the nightstand.

He ignored it.

It buzzed again.

"You should check that," Elena said, toweling off her hair.

"It can wait."

"Jasper."

He sighed and picked up the phone. Three texts from Harper, two from Cole, and a missed call from a number he didn't recognize but knew was connected to the European client. He scanned the messages, his jaw tightening with each one.

The situation in Africa had escalated. The mining company's CEO had been detained at the airport in Kinshasa. The allegations of bribery were now public, splashed across international news. And the coup attempt—previously a whisper—was now being treated as a credible threat by the State Department.

"Jasper?" Elena's voice was quiet, careful.

He looked up at her, standing there in their bedroom with her towel wrapped around her and her eyes full of worry, and he felt the familiar pull. The crisis was calling. The fire needed to be put out. His team needed him.

But so did she.

"I need to make some calls," he said slowly. "But I'm not leaving. Not tonight."

"What if you have to?"

"Then I'll deal with it tomorrow. Tonight, I'm here. With you and Grace." He set the phone down, face-down on the nightstand, and crossed to her. "We just found out we're having a baby, Elena. I'm not walking away from that."

She searched his face, and he could see the war happening behind her eyes—the part of her that wanted to believe him and the part of her that was waiting for the other shoe to drop.

"Okay," she said finally.

"Okay?"

"Okay." She kissed him, quick and firm. "But you're telling Grace about the baby tonight. At dinner. Because if you wait, I'm going to burst."

He laughed. "Deal."

They got dressed—Elena in the leggings and sweatshirt she'd been wearing earlier, Jasper in jeans and a t-shirt—and headed downstairs. Grace was at the kitchen table, her homework spread out in front of her, a half-eaten granola bar beside her notebook.

"Did you finish decompressing?" Grace asked without looking up.

Elena glanced at Jasper, her lips twitching. "We did."

"Good. Because I need help with my math."

"Let me see." Jasper slid into the chair beside her, looking at the worksheet. It was multiplication tables, the kind Grace had been struggling with all month. He walked her through the first few problems, his hand over hers, guiding her pencil.

Elena started dinner—something simple, pasta with marinara and a salad—and Lucas woke from his nap with a cry that echoed through the baby monitor. Grace abandoned her homework to help, bounding up the stairs with the kind of energy that made Jasper tired just watching her.

For the next hour, the house was chaos in the best possible way. Grace chattered about school. Lucas giggled in his bouncer. Elena moved through the kitchen with the kind of practiced ease that came from years of juggling a thousand things at once.

And Jasper—Jasper sat at the table, his phone still face-down on the nightstand upstairs, and let himself be exactly where he was.

Dinner was served at six-thirty. They ate at the kitchen table, the four of them crowded together in a way that felt both chaotic and perfect. Grace talked about her upcoming school play, where she'd been cast as a tree. Elena asked if she was excited or nervous, and Grace said both, which Jasper figured was about right.

Halfway through the meal, Jasper caught Elena's eye. She nodded, just once, and he knew it was time.

"Grace," he said, setting down his fork. "Your mom and I have some news."

Grace looked up, her eyes wide. "Are we getting a kitten?"

Elena laughed. "No. Better."

"Better than a kitten?" Grace's skepticism was evident.

Jasper smiled. "You're going to be a big sister again."

For a moment, Grace just stared at them. Then, slowly, her face broke into the kind of grin that could light up a room.

"Really?"

"Really," Elena said.

"Can it be a girl?"

"We don't get to pick," Jasper said. "But we'll love them no matter what."

Grace launched herself at Elena, wrapping her arms around her in a hug that was all joy and no restraint. "This is the best news ever."

Elena hugged her back, tears streaming down her face, and Jasper felt something crack open in his chest. This was it. This was what mattered. Not the crises burning halfway around the world. Not the clients demanding his attention. This.

His phone buzzed upstairs, three sharp vibrations that he could almost feel through the floor. He ignored it.

Grace pulled back, her eyes shining. "When do we find out if it's a boy or a girl?"

"Not for a few more months," Elena said. "But we'll tell you as soon as we know."

"Can I help pick the name?"

"We'll see."

Grace turned to Jasper, her expression suddenly serious. "Daddy, are you going to be here? For the baby?"

The question hit him like a punch to the gut. It was the same question she'd asked months ago, the one that had started all of this. *Is Daddy on our team?*

"I'm going to be here," he said, his voice steady. "I promise."

"Even if there's a fire?"

He thought about the calls waiting for him upstairs. The crisis in Africa. The client who needed him. The team that was depending on him.

Then he looked at Grace, at Elena, at Lucas in his bouncer, and he knew the answer.

"Even if there's a fire."

Grace nodded, satisfied, and went back to her pasta. Elena reached across the table and squeezed his hand, her eyes saying everything her words didn't need to.

They finished dinner in relative peace. Grace helped clear the table, chattering about baby names and nursery colors. Elena loaded the dishwasher while Jasper gave Lucas a bottle. By the time they got Grace to bed, it was almost nine, and Jasper could feel the weight of the day settling over him.

He climbed the stairs to their bedroom, where his phone was still waiting on the nightstand. He picked it up, scrolling through the messages. Harper had handled the NFL situation. Cole had contained the European client's public relations nightmare. The Africa situation was still a mess, but it wasn't an emergency that required him to get on a plane tonight.

He typed a quick reply to Harper: *Good work. Let's regroup in the morning.*

Then he set the phone down, face-up this time, and climbed into bed beside Elena.

"You didn't check your phone all night," she said softly.

"I did just now."

"And?"

"And it can wait until tomorrow."

She turned to face him, her eyes searching his in the dim light. "You're really doing this, aren't you?"

"I'm really doing this." He pulled her close, his hand resting on her stomach where their second child was growing. "We're really doing this."

She kissed him, slow and sweet, and for the first time in years, Jasper Barrett felt like he was exactly where he was supposed to be.

But even as he drifted off to sleep, he knew the crises wouldn't stay quiet forever. The fire in Africa was still burning. The client was still in custody. The world was still demanding his attention.

Tomorrow, he'd have to face it. Tomorrow, the choice between work and family would test him again.

But tonight—tonight, he chose them.

And that, he told himself, was enough.
