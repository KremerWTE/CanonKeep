## Background

Grace Barrett is seven years old and has already learned more about adult relationships than most kids her age. She's watched her mother hold everything together while her father existed mostly as a voice on the phone and a figure who showed up late to birthday parties—if he showed up at all.

But Grace isn't bitter. She's observant. She notices when her dad checks his phone during her soccer games. She notices when her mom pretends everything is fine. She notices the way conversations stop when she walks into a room.

Despite growing up with an often-absent father, Grace has retained an almost preternatural sweetness and wisdom. She asks the questions adults are afraid to ask, cuts through pretense with innocent honesty, and somehow becomes the moral compass for a family of high-achieving adults.

Her broken foot early in Book 1 sets off a chain of events that culminates in her mother's hospitalization—and ultimately, her family's healing. Grace doesn't know she's the catalyst. She just knows she wants her daddy on her team.

---

## Role in the Story

Grace is the emotional anchor of the story. Her simple, honest observations carry more weight than any boardroom strategy. She represents what Jasper is really fighting for—and what he's at risk of losing.

**Book 1 (Five Feet From Home):** Her question—"Is Daddy on our team?"—becomes the thematic center of the narrative. Her broken foot, her worry about her mother, and her quiet hope for her father's presence drive Jasper's transformation.

**Book 2+ (Royal Phoenix Series):** Ages into a mentor role, helping younger children in swim programs. Continues to be wise beyond her years while maintaining childlike joy.

Grace dresses like a seven-year-old whose mother picks her clothes but who has strong opinions about accessories. Practical school wear meets unicorn leggings meets glitter sneakers. Always something slightly askew—a crooked ribbon, a stained shirt, evidence of a day fully lived.

## Personality

### Core Traits
- **Perceptive:** Sees what adults try to hide
- **Direct:** Asks the questions no one else will
- **Brave:** Faces fears with quiet courage
- **Playful:** Still very much a kid despite her wisdom
- **Protective:** Fiercely loyal to family
- **Loving:** Leads with her heart

### Strengths
- Cuts through adult pretense with innocent honesty
- Natural peacemaker
- Brings levity to tense situations
- Observes everything; remembers everything
- Can comfort adults with surprising insight

### Weaknesses
- Carries worries too big for her age
- Sometimes too aware of family tensions
- Anxious about being abandoned
- Takes on emotional weight she shouldn't have to

## Quirk

**The Drawings:** Grace expresses herself through art. Her fridge drawings include crooked houses, three-figure families holding hands, backward letters, and big yellow suns touching everything. Her drawings tell the story she can't articulate.

**"You Can't Spell Team Without Me":** Her phrase to Jasper, delivered with a pencil between her teeth—becomes a touchstone he carries with him.

## Character Arc Summary

**Beginning:** A seven-year-old who has learned to expect her father's absence. Brave but anxious. Watches adults carefully. Asks the hard questions with innocent directness.

**Middle:** Becomes the unexpected catalyst for family healing. Her broken foot, her hospital vigil for Elena, and her simple question ("Is Daddy on our team?") force Jasper to confront his priorities.

**End:** Gets what she wanted—a father who shows up. Maintains her wisdom while getting to just be a kid.

**Core Role:** The heart of the story. The stakes personified. The reason everything matters.
