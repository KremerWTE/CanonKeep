# Chapter 21: Nico's Adventures

## Harper Series - Book 1: "The Shadow's Ledger"
### Part Ten: The Troublemaker

---

Nico Barrett was eight years old and constitutionally incapable of sitting still.

This was not a figure of speech. The boy vibrated with energy from the moment he woke up until the moment he crashed—which often happened mid-sentence, in strange places, like he'd simply run out of battery.

Harper loved him desperately and worried about him constantly.

---

## The Report Card

"He's not struggling academically," the teacher explained, a woman named Ms. Peterson whose patience seemed as bottomless as her coffee mug. "His test scores are excellent. His reading level is two years ahead. The problem is..."

"Everything else?" Harper offered.

"He has difficulty with structure. Following directions. Staying in his seat." Ms. Peterson smiled ruefully. "Yesterday he organized an impromptu science experiment in the cafeteria. Something about testing the aerodynamic properties of different food items."

"Was anyone hurt?"

"Only the custodian's morale."

Harper sighed. This was familiar territory. Nico's teachers had been having variations of this conversation since kindergarten.

"He's not trying to cause trouble," she said. "He just..."

"Sees the world differently. I know." Ms. Peterson leaned forward. "Honestly, Mrs. Barrett? I've been teaching for fifteen years. Most kids are either bright or energetic. Your son is both, which means he gets bored faster than I can engage him." She paused. "Have you considered advanced enrichment programs?"

"We've tried three. He got kicked out of two and the third one bored him."

"Of course it did."

---

## The Aunt

Two weeks later, Selene arrived in Miami.

She swept into the Barrett house like a storm—all sharp edges and expensive perfume and the kind of beauty that made people forget to breathe.

"Auntie Selene!" Nico launched himself at her with the force of a small missile. She caught him, laughing, and spun him around.

"Little monster. You've grown."

"Three inches! Chiara measured."

Harper watched from the doorway, calculating. Selene's visits always came with complications—she was brilliant and dangerous in equal measure, and Harper had never fully trusted her. But she was also, inexplicably, Nico's favorite.

"What brings you to Miami?" Harper asked, keeping her voice neutral.

"Business in the Caribbean. I thought I'd stop by." Selene set Nico down, meeting Harper's eyes with a smile that said she knew exactly what Harper was thinking. "Don't worry. I'm not corrupting him."

"Yet."

"Give me time."

---

## The Day Out

Against her better judgment, Harper let Selene take Nico for an afternoon.

They returned seven hours later, both sunburned and grinning, with stories about sailing lessons, a tour of a marine research center, and what Nico described as "the best lunch ever" at a waterfront restaurant Harper had never heard of.

"What exactly did you do today?" Harper asked Selene privately while Nico chattered at Daniel about stingrays.

"Wore him out." Selene shrugged. "Kids like Nico don't need more discipline. They need more stimulation. Everything he did today—the sailing, the fish talk, the restaurant—required him to focus, learn, engage. By the time we finished, he was too interested to be restless."

Harper studied her. "That's surprisingly insightful."

"I was Nico, once. Before I learned to channel it into..." Selene gestured vaguely at herself. "Whatever this is."

"And what is this, exactly?"

Selene's smile flickered. "Someone who learned to use the chaos instead of fighting it. Not all of us find the right kind of structure, Harper. Some of us just get really good at surviving without it."

---

## The Conversation

That night, Harper sat with Nico during his bedtime routine—a negotiation involving three books, two glasses of water, and a detailed discussion of whether hamsters dream.

"Did you have fun today?" she asked.

"The best." Nico bounced slightly even while lying down. "Aunt Selene is so cool. She knows everything about boats and fish and she let me steer even though the captain said I was too small."

"She let you steer a boat?"

"Just for a minute. She held on too." Nico's eyes were already growing heavy. "Mom? Why doesn't Aunt Selene visit more?"

Harper thought about how to answer. Because I don't fully trust her. Because she makes choices I don't agree with. Because she's the kind of person who's wonderful in small doses and dangerous in large ones.

"She travels a lot for work," Harper said instead. "Like me."

"But you come home."

"I always come home."

Nico was quiet for a moment. Then: "Aunt Selene seems sad sometimes. Even when she's smiling."

Harper's heart clenched. Out of the mouths of babes.

"Some people carry things that are hard to put down," she said carefully. "Even when they're having fun, the weight is still there."

"Like a backpack?"

"Something like that."

Nico considered this with the seriousness of an eight-year-old philosopher. "Maybe she needs someone to help her carry it."

Harper kissed his forehead. "Maybe she does, buddy. Maybe she does."

---

## The Insight

After Nico finally fell asleep, Harper found Selene on the back patio, nursing a glass of wine and staring at the city lights.

"He sees you," Harper said, settling into the chair beside her.

"Who?"

"Nico. He sees past the performance."

Selene was quiet for a long moment. "He's special. All your kids are, but Nico... he feels things differently. He's going to drive you crazy for the next ten years, but he's also going to surprise you."

"He already does."

"Good." Selene set down her wine. "Harper, I know what you think of me. I know I'm on your list of people to watch."

Harper didn't deny it.

"But I would never hurt him. Any of them. Whatever else I am, I'm not someone who damages children."

"I believe you."

"Do you?"

Harper thought about it. Really thought. The Index in her office. The careful calculations. The way she categorized everyone she knew.

"I believe you love my children," she said finally. "I believe you'd protect them if it came to it. What I'm less sure about is whether you'd protect yourself."

Selene's expression shifted—something raw, quickly hidden.

"That's fair."

They sat in silence for a while, watching the lights of Miami shimmer against the dark.

"Thank you for today," Harper said. "For seeing him the way he needs to be seen."

"Thank you for letting me." Selene stood, gathering her things. "I should go. Early flight."

"Where this time?"

"Singapore. London. The usual chaos."

"Be careful."

Selene paused at the door, looking back. "Always."

She disappeared into the night, and Harper was left wondering—not for the first time—whether Selene was someone who could be saved, or someone who'd already made peace with being lost.

---

## The Morning After

Nico woke up with a new obsession: marine biology.

He talked about it through breakfast, during the drive to school, and in a rapid-fire text message to Chiara that was mostly emojis and exclamation points.

"Can we go to the aquarium this weekend?" he asked Harper.

"If you get through the week without any cafeteria experiments."

"I'll try. But no promises."

Harper laughed, ruffling his hair. "That's my boy."

As she watched him race into school—already chattering to a friend about stingrays—she thought about what Selene had said. Stimulation, not discipline. Channeling the chaos instead of fighting it.

Maybe that was the key to Nico. Maybe it was the key to a lot of things.

Her phone buzzed. Jasper: *Singapore situation escalating. Need you on a call in an hour.*

Back to reality. Back to the work.

But she'd remember this morning—her son's enthusiasm, his aunt's unexpected wisdom, the small miracle of a difficult kid finding something that lit him up.

These were the moments that made the rest of it worthwhile.
