## The Boston Collapse

The mahogany-paneled boardroom of a Back Bay firm smelled of cigars and old money. Jasper leaned forward, selling the client on an aggressive expansion into biotech. The executives—gray-haired, cufflinks gleaming—nodded eagerly.

At the back of the room, Harper sipped her coffee. She wasn't here to pitch. She was here to watch.

Halfway through the meeting, she saw it: the CFO's left hand twitching against his cufflink every time Jasper mentioned projected revenue. Not once, not twice—every single time.

When the group broke for lunch, Harper pulled Jasper into the hallway.

"Shut it down," she said.

He frowned. "We're this close to closing."

"They're cooked. The CFO's already moving money offshore. I'd bet my last dollar their books collapse in sixty days. If we hitch BSS to them, we go down too."

Jasper blinked, incredulous. "You got that from a cufflink twitch?"

"I got that from everything he's not saying," Harper snapped. She shoved a flash drive into his hand. "I pulled their SEC filings last night. Cooked. The twitch just confirmed it."

By the time dessert was served, BSS had quietly exited the deal. Two months later, the firm declared bankruptcy. Jasper sent Harper flowers with a single note: *You're the reason we're still alive.*

## The Victory/Failure Board

Harper's office in Miami became the "second HQ"—where finance, HR, compliance, and structure flowed from. But what set it apart was the wall behind her desk.

She called it the "victory/failure board"—color-coded dossiers of every case, every team, and where the next fire would ignite.

Green tabs meant wins. Red tabs meant losses. Yellow tabs meant brewing trouble.

Every morning, she walked the wall like a general surveying her battlefield. Every evening, she updated it with precision. The younger analysts whispered that Harper could see the future. The truth was simpler: she paid attention when everyone else celebrated too early.

"You're obsessed with this thing," Jasper said one night, finding her still at the board at midnight.

"No," Harper replied, not looking up. "I'm prepared. There's a difference."

She moved a yellow tab to red. "The Philadelphia deal is about to implode. Call the team. We have forty-eight hours."

Jasper didn't ask how she knew. He just made the call.
