# Chapter 16: Harper's Traveling COO Life

## Harper Series - Book 1: "The Shadow's Ledger"
### Part Eight: The Road Warrior

---

Monday: Miami.
Tuesday: London.
Wednesday: London.
Thursday: Singapore.
Friday: Charlotte.
Saturday: Home.
Sunday: Home.

Harper kept the rhythm in her head like a heartbeat. Three time zones. Four flights. Seventeen meetings. One soccer game she absolutely would not miss.

"Mrs. Barrett?" The flight attendant appeared with her usual sparkling water. "We'll be landing in Heathrow in approximately two hours."

"Thank you, Catherine."

Harper knew all the flight crews by name. She'd logged enough miles to circumnavigate the globe several times over. The Gulfstream was practically a second office—satellite wifi, secure comms, and a fold-out desk that had seen more crisis management than most war rooms.

She reviewed the London briefing materials while the Atlantic passed beneath her. A banking client with exposure to a regulatory investigation. Nothing flashy, but the kind of quiet disaster that could unravel a career if mishandled.

Her phone buzzed with messages from home.

Izzy: *Made the debate team. Thought you should know.*

Harper smiled, typing back: *That's my girl. Crush them.*

Nico: *Can I get a snake?*

Harper: *Ask your father.*

Daniel: *Don't you dare put this on me.*

Harper laughed, the sound strange in the quiet cabin.

---

## The London Hub

BSS's London office occupied the top floor of a Mayfair townhouse—discreet, elegant, and close enough to the financial district that panicked executives could arrive within minutes.

Harper stepped out of the car into a gray drizzle, her assistant Marcus already waiting with an umbrella.

"Sir Richard is in the conference room," Marcus reported, falling into step beside her. "He's brought his general counsel and two board members. They're... concerned."

"They should be." Harper climbed the stairs without slowing. "What's the read on the room?"

"Defensive. They want this to go away quietly."

"Don't they all."

Sir Richard Pemberly—no relation to the Pembertons in Manhattan, though Harper sometimes marveled at how small the world of the ultra-wealthy actually was—rose as she entered. He was seventy-three, silver-haired, and had built his banking empire through a combination of ruthlessness and charm.

Right now, he looked like neither.

"Harper." He clasped her hand with both of his. "Thank God you're here."

"Tell me everything," she said. "And Richard—I mean everything."

---

## The Singapore Pivot

Twenty-six hours later, Harper was on another plane, this time heading east.

The London situation was contained—a carefully worded statement, a proactive disclosure to regulators, and a quiet resignation of the executive whose creative accounting had started the whole mess. Sir Richard's empire would survive. His reputation would heal.

Singapore was a different beast.

A tech consortium was attempting a hostile takeover of a client's shipping company. The target was small by global standards but strategically vital—a chokepoint in Pacific supply chains that certain parties desperately wanted to control.

Harper read the intelligence briefing while the sun rose over the Indian Ocean.

"They're using shell companies layered through four jurisdictions," she muttered to herself, making notes. "Classic misdirection. But the money trail leads back to..."

She stopped. Reread the paragraph.

Then she called Jasper.

"We have a problem," she said. "The Singapore acquisition isn't just hostile. It's connected to the Dubai consortium we pushed out last month. Al-Farsi's people."

A pause. "They're escalating."

"They're probing. Testing our response across multiple fronts." Harper stared out at clouds painted gold by sunrise. "This isn't about one shipping company. It's about whether we can protect our clients globally."

"What do you need?"

"Full intel workup on Al-Farsi's network. Every connection, every asset, every vulnerability." Harper's voice hardened. "If they want a war, we need to understand the battlefield."

---

## The Family Thread

Singapore was sixteen hours ahead of Miami. Harper had learned to live in the gaps—calling home during her morning (their evening), texting during her afternoon (their midnight silence), video chatting during her night (their morning chaos).

"Mom!" Sofia's face filled the screen, gap-toothed and beaming. "I lost another tooth! The tooth fairy owes me like a million dollars by now."

"That's a lot of teeth, baby. You'll be eating through a straw."

"Ew, Mom."

Behind Sofia, Nico was attempting to build something that defied physics. Izzy sat at the kitchen table, homework spread before her, pretending not to listen.

"How's the trip?" Daniel appeared, coffee in hand, looking handsomely rumpled.

"Long. But productive." Harper wished she could reach through the screen. "I'll be home Saturday night. Don't let them forget me."

"As if that's possible." He lowered his voice. "Izzy's been asking about what you do. More seriously this time."

"What did you tell her?"

"That you help people who are in trouble. That you're very good at it." A pause. "She wants to know if she can come with you sometime."

Harper's heart clenched. Pride and fear and love all tangled together.

"Someday," she said. "When she's ready."

"When she's ready or when you're ready?"

Trust Daniel to ask the question she couldn't answer.

---

## The Charlotte Debrief

Friday meant Charlotte. The compound. Home base.

Harper landed at 6 a.m. and went straight to the war room, where the team had assembled the full Al-Farsi dossier.

"Seventeen shell companies," Kendra reported, pointing to the web of connections on the screen. "Three sovereign wealth funds we suspect are fronts. Business interests in real estate, shipping, tech, and—this is new—media."

"Media?"

"They've been quietly acquiring stakes in news outlets across the Gulf and Southeast Asia." Hawk stepped forward, his presence commanding attention even in stillness. "Classic soft power play. Control the narrative before the crisis hits."

Harper studied the web. It was vast. Sophisticated. And aimed, she realized, not just at BSS's clients but at the kind of world order that made BSS possible.

"This is bigger than crisis management," she said slowly. "This is geopolitics."

Jasper nodded. "Which is why we're not going to fight it alone. I've been building relationships with partner firms—London, Singapore, Tokyo. If Al-Farsi wants to test us everywhere, we need to be strong everywhere."

"A network to counter a network."

"Exactly."

Harper thought of the flight hours ahead, the calls, the meetings, the endless chess game of global influence. Then she thought of Sofia's tooth, of Nico's snake campaign, of Izzy wanting to understand her mother's world.

"Then we build it," she said. "But I'm still making Nico's soccer game tomorrow."

Jasper smiled. "I wouldn't expect anything less."

---

## The Return

Saturday afternoon. Miami. The sidelines of a youth soccer field.

Harper sat on a folding chair, coffee in hand, watching Nico chase a ball with more enthusiasm than skill. Daniel sat beside her, their shoulders touching.

"You look tired," he said.

"I am tired."

"When was the last time you slept for eight hours straight?"

Harper had to think about it. "Izzy's birthday?"

"That was three months ago."

"Then probably not since then."

Chiara appeared with snacks, distributing orange slices to the team with practiced efficiency. She caught Harper's eye and smiled—the understanding of two women who kept the same family running through sheer force of will.

On the field, Nico scored a goal. He looked immediately to the sidelines, finding Harper, his face splitting into a grin.

She waved, throat tight.

This was why. The flights, the crises, the endless calculation of risk and response. It all came back to this: a boy with grass-stained knees, looking for his mother in the crowd.

"Worth it?" Daniel asked, reading her mind.

Harper leaned against him, letting herself feel the exhaustion she usually kept at bay.

"Every mile," she said. "Every single mile."
