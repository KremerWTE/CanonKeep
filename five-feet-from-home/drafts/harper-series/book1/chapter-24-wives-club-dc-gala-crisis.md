# Chapter 24: Wives Club - D.C. Gala Crisis

## Harper Series - Book 1: "The Shadow's Ledger"
### Part Eleven: The Dress Code

---

The Kennedy Center Honors Gala was one of Washington's most prestigious events. Black tie. Secret Service everywhere. The kind of guest list that made security teams nervous and tabloid editors salivate.

Harper was attending as Daniel's plus-one, a rare reversal—Barrett Maritime was being honored for their hurricane relief work in the Caribbean. She'd been looking forward to a night of blending in, observing, and not being in charge of anything.

She should have known better.

---

## The Arrival

The gown was midnight blue, floor-length, elegant without being ostentatious. Daniel looked devastating in his tuxedo, and for a moment as they stepped out of the car, Harper let herself imagine they were just a couple at a party.

"You're allowed to relax tonight," Daniel murmured, guiding her up the steps. "This is my event. My crisis if anything goes wrong."

"Have you ever actually handled a crisis at one of these?"

"There was a champagne shortage in 2019. I managed."

Harper laughed, and for a brief, beautiful moment, everything was normal.

Then her phone buzzed.

Elena: *Emergency. Find me. East gallery. Now.*

---

## The Crisis

Harper found Elena in a small antechamber off the main gallery, pale beneath her flawless makeup.

"What happened?"

Elena's voice was tight. "Senator Morrison's wife just cornered me. She's heard something—rumors about Jasper's involvement in that D.C. scandal we handled last year. She's planning to make a scene."

"What kind of scene?"

"The kind that involves confronting Rebecca Thornton in front of two hundred of the most connected people in Washington. She claims she has proof that BSS manufactured evidence."

Harper's blood went cold. The Caldwell affair. The disinformation campaign they'd turned around. Someone had apparently decided to weaponize it.

"Where's Rebecca now?"

"Main ballroom. She doesn't know it's coming."

Harper was already moving. "Who else from our circle is here tonight?"

"Addie's at table six. Kendra and Chris are near the bar. Catherine Pemberly is somewhere—I saw her earlier."

"Get them. Private meeting. Now."

---

## The Response

The Wives Club assembled in a borrowed conference room, gowns rustling, faces tense.

"Someone's been feeding Senator Morrison's wife information," Harper said without preamble. "Incomplete information designed to create maximum damage at maximum visibility."

"The consortium?" Addie asked.

"Almost certainly. This is their playbook—use social connections to create pressure we can't counter through normal channels."

Catherine Pemberly—whose own family had been saved by BSS—looked stricken. "Patricia Morrison is a friend of mine. Was a friend. I had no idea she was involved in anything like this."

"She's probably not. Not knowingly. She's being manipulated, same as everyone else." Harper pulled out her phone. "But we can use that."

"How?"

"Catherine, I need you to talk to her. Now, before she makes her move. Tell her you've heard what she's planning. Tell her you have concerns about her source."

"What if she doesn't listen?"

"Then we go to Plan B." Harper looked around the room. "Kendra, I need you to find Rebecca and keep her occupied. Dance with her if you have to. Just keep her away from the Morrison table until we've resolved this."

"On it."

"Addie—you're with me. We're going to have a conversation with Senator Morrison himself."

---

## The Conversation

Senator James Morrison was a twenty-year veteran of Washington politics—smooth, calculating, and currently looking very uncomfortable as his wife approached Rebecca Thornton's table.

Harper intercepted her first.

"Mrs. Morrison." Her voice was warm, social, completely at odds with the steel in her eyes. "I don't believe we've met properly. I'm Harper Barrett. I understand you have concerns about some recent events."

Patricia Morrison froze mid-stride. "I—yes. I have information that suggests—"

"Information from an anonymous source, provided through channels you can't verify, designed to damage someone's reputation at a public event where the damage would be maximized?" Harper tilted her head. "That must have seemed very convenient."

"I don't know what you're implying."

"I'm not implying anything. I'm observing." Harper lowered her voice, stepping closer. "Mrs. Morrison, I understand the impulse. Someone gives you what looks like proof of wrongdoing. Your sense of justice demands action. But I would strongly encourage you to verify that proof before you act on it."

"It's verified."

"Is it? By whom?" Harper produced a document from her clutch—she always carried relevant materials, even to galas. "Because I have documentation showing that your source has ties to foreign interests currently under investigation for election interference. Documentation that I would hate to have to share with your husband's opponents."

Patricia Morrison's face went white. Then red. Then settled into a careful neutrality.

"I think," she said slowly, "I may have been... hasty."

"We're all hasty sometimes. The wise among us recognize it before the damage is done." Harper smiled graciously. "Now. May I get you some champagne? I believe this is a celebration, after all."

---

## The Aftermath

An hour later, Harper found Daniel at the bar, looking bemused.

"Did you just defuse a political assassination attempt while I was discussing hurricane logistics with the Coast Guard commandant?"

"Something like that."

"And here I thought tonight was going to be boring."

Harper accepted the glass of wine he offered, letting herself lean against him for a moment.

"The consortium tried to use Senator Morrison's wife as a weapon. She didn't know she was being used—she genuinely believed she was exposing wrongdoing."

"But you stopped it."

"The Wives Club stopped it. Catherine convinced her to hesitate. Kendra kept Rebecca occupied. Addie provided the Senator with enough information to understand he was being targeted." Harper sipped her wine. "It takes a village."

"To fight a shadow war?"

"To protect what matters."

Daniel set down his glass and wrapped an arm around her waist. "Have I mentioned lately how terrifying you are?"

"Not lately."

"You're terrifying. In the best way possible."

"Thank you." Harper smiled. "Now can we please dance? I've been handling crises all night and I'd like to pretend to be a normal person for at least one song."

"Your wish is my command."

---

## The Dance

The band was playing something slow and classic. Harper let Daniel lead, her head resting against his shoulder, the chaos of the evening finally settling.

Around them, Washington's elite glittered and schemed and performed their endless dance of power. Rebecca Thornton was laughing at something her husband said, unaware of how close she'd come to destruction. Senator Morrison was watching his wife with concern. The consortium's plan had failed—for now.

"They're going to try again," Harper murmured.

"I know."

"And again, and again, until they find a weakness we can't cover."

"Probably."

"You're not worried?"

Daniel's arms tightened around her. "I'm terrified. But I'm also married to the most brilliant crisis manager in the country. So I figure the odds are in our favor."

"Flattery."

"Truth."

The song ended. Another began. They kept dancing.

Tomorrow there would be debriefs and damage assessment and planning for the next attack. Tomorrow the war would continue.

But tonight, in this room full of power and danger, Harper Barrett allowed herself one small victory:

She was still standing.

And so was everyone she'd sworn to protect.
