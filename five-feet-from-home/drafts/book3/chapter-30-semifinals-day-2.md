# Chapter 30: Semifinals Day 2

**POV:** Kendra
**Location:** Knoxville, TN
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

Kendra wakes up at 5:00 AM and her body is already screaming.

Her quads are concrete. Her shoulders feel like they've been beaten with hammers. When she tries to roll over in the hotel bed, her hip flexors seize up so badly she has to breathe through the pain for thirty seconds before she can move.

Yesterday was Event 1 and Event 2 and Event 3. A marathon row, a legless rope climb complex, and a sprint that nearly killed her. She finished 12th, 8th, and 5th respectively. Good enough to put her in 7th place overall going into Day 2.

Not good enough to relax.

Chris is already awake, sitting in the chair by the window with his laptop open. He's been tracking every heat, every score, every movement on the leaderboard since they arrived in Knoxville three days ago.

"Morning," he says quietly when he sees her stir. "How do you feel?"

"Like I got hit by a truck," Kendra admits. She sits up slowly, testing her range of motion. Everything hurts, but nothing's injured. That's good. That's manageable.

Chris closes his laptop and comes to sit on the edge of the bed. His hand finds her shoulder, his thumb working into the knot of muscle at the base of her neck.

"Events 4 and 5 today," he says. "Event 6 tonight. You ready?"

"I don't know yet." Kendra leans into the pressure of his thumb. "Ask me after I've had coffee and moved around."

"Event 4 is legless rope climbs and heavy cleans. Event 5 is a sprint—box jumps and snatches. Event 6 is still a mystery, but rumors are it's a chipper."

Kendra groans. A chipper. That means volume—high reps across multiple movements, designed to test your ability to keep moving when your entire body is begging you to stop.

"Of course it is," she mutters.

Chris smiles. "You've got this."

"You don't know that."

"I do." His voice is calm, certain. "You've trained for this. Your engine's stronger than most of the field. And you're smart enough to pace yourself."

Kendra looks at him. Chris has been her anchor through this entire season—quiet, steady, never doubting her even when she doubted herself. He's taken time off work to be here. He's sat through every heat, every briefing, every moment of waiting.

"Thank you," she says quietly. "For being here."

"Nowhere else I'd rather be." He kisses her forehead, then stands. "I'm going to grab breakfast from downstairs. Eggs, oatmeal, and fruit?"

"And coffee. Lots of coffee."

"Done."

After he leaves, Kendra forces herself out of bed and into the bathroom. She stands under the shower and lets hot water pound into her shoulders, her back, her legs. She does gentle mobility work—arm circles, hip rotations, ankle rolls—trying to coax her body back to something resembling functional.

By the time Chris returns with breakfast, she's dressed in her competition gear—black leggings, sports bra, BSS tank top that Jasper sent her as a good luck gift. Her wrists are taped. Her hair is pulled back in a tight braid.

She looks ready. She doesn't feel ready. But that's the point of competition, isn't it? You show up anyway.

---

The venue is chaos.

Jacob's Pavilion, Knoxville's massive indoor sports complex, has been converted into a CrossFit battleground. Eight competition lanes, each with a full rig, barbells, rowers, assault bikes, climbing ropes. Stands packed with spectators—athletes' families, CrossFit junkies, people who just want to watch other people suffer.

Kendra stands in the athlete warm-up area, watching the first heat of Event 4 unfold on the big screens. Legless rope climbs and heavy cleans. Three rounds. It's brutal.

The rope climbs are exactly what they sound like—climb a fifteen-foot rope using only your arms, no legs for assistance. Then drop down and immediately hit three cleans at 185 pounds for men, 135 for women.

Kendra's done legless rope climbs before. She's strong enough. But three rounds, after yesterday's volume? Her shoulders are already compromised. Her grip is questionable.

And 135-pound cleans aren't heavy for her in isolation. But after rope climbs, when her arms are shaking and her core is fried? That's a different story.

"Heat 7, you're up in fifteen minutes," a volunteer calls.

Kendra's in Heat 7. She has fifteen minutes to get her mind right.

She moves to an empty pull-up bar and does some dead hangs, trying to open up her shoulders. Then she grabs a barbell and works through some light cleans—45 pounds, 65 pounds, 95 pounds. The movement feels okay. Not great, but okay.

Sara appears at her elbow. She flew in yesterday to coach Kendra through Day 2 and Day 3. Elena offered to pay for the flight, which Kendra initially refused, then accepted when she realized she actually needed the support.

"You good?" Sara asks.

"Nervous," Kendra admits.

"Good. Nerves mean you care." Sara crosses her arms, watching Kendra move through another clean. "Here's the strategy. Don't try to go unbroken on the rope climbs. You won't. Nobody's going unbroken after yesterday. Three climbs, take a breath between each one. Then hit the barbell fast. Unbroken if you can, but don't fail a rep. Failed reps cost you more time than a quick break."

Kendra nods. It's the same advice she'd given herself, but hearing it from Sara makes it feel real.

"What if I can't finish?" Kendra asks quietly.

Sara looks at her. "Then you don't finish. But that's not going to happen. You know why?"

"Why?"

"Because you didn't come all this way to quit in the middle." Sara's voice is fierce. "You came here to prove you belong. And you do. So get out there and show them."

---

Heat 7 is called.

Kendra walks to Lane 4, her assigned lane. The rope hangs in front of her, thick and intimidating. The barbell is loaded—135 pounds, the plates stacked in a way that looks heavier than it is.

Around her, seven other women are doing the same mental prep. Some are bouncing on their toes, shaking out their arms. Others are standing still, eyes closed, breathing.

Kendra does neither. She just stares at the rope and reminds herself that she's done this a thousand times in training. This is just one more rep.

The judge assigned to her lane—a woman in her forties with a clipboard and a no-nonsense expression—steps forward.

"Kendra Hale?"

"That's me."

"I'm Judge Amy. I'll be counting your reps. Rope climbs are to the top, you have to touch the beam. Cleans are full squat, bar has to touch your shoulders, then you have to stand to full lockout. Any questions?"

"No, ma'am."

"Good luck."

The clock counts down. Sixty seconds until start. Kendra chalks her hands, shakes them out. Her heart is hammering, but her breathing is steady.

Thirty seconds.

She looks up into the stands. Chris is there, in the third row, watching her. He gives her a small nod. She nods back.

Fifteen seconds.

The arena goes quiet. Eight women. Eight ropes. Eight barbells.

"Three… two… one… GO!"

Kendra jumps to the rope.

The first climb is hard but manageable. She locks her hands around the rope and pulls, arms burning, core engaged. No legs. Just arms and lats and sheer will. She reaches the top, slaps the beam, and drops.

Her hands are already raw.

She moves to the barbell. Plants her feet. Grabs the bar. Pulls it to her shoulders in one smooth clean, drops into a squat, then drives up to standing. One. She drops the bar and resets. Two. Three.

First round done.

Back to the rope.

This time, it's harder. Her grip is slipping. Her shoulders are on fire. She makes it halfway up and has to pause, hanging on the rope, trying to find the strength to keep going.

"Come on, Kendra!" Sara's voice cuts through the noise. "You've got this! Pull!"

Kendra pulls. Inches upward. Slaps the beam. Drops.

Her hands are shaking.

Back to the barbell. The weight feels heavier now. She hits the first clean, but it's slower. The second clean almost fails—she catches it low, has to fight to stand it up. The third clean is ugly, but she gets it.

Second round done.

One more.

The third rope climb nearly breaks her. She's halfway up and her arms are gone. Completely gone. She hangs there, feet dangling, and for a second she thinks she's going to have to drop and try again.

But dropping costs time. And time is everything.

She screams—actually screams—and pulls. One hand over the other. Inches. Inches. She slaps the beam and drops.

The arena is roaring. Or maybe that's just the blood rushing in her ears.

She staggers to the barbell. Her vision is narrowing. She grabs the bar and pulls. The first clean almost fails, but muscle memory takes over. She stands it. Drops it. Grabs it again.

The second clean does fail. She catches it, tries to stand, and her legs give out. She drops the bar and steps back, gasping.

"Reset!" Judge Amy calls. "You've got this!"

Kendra chalks her hands again, even though they're too torn up for chalk to matter. She grabs the bar. Pulls. Catches it. Stands.

Two down. One to go.

The last clean is pure survival. She doesn't think. She just moves. Pull. Catch. Stand.

The bar hits the floor and the buzzer sounds.

"Time!" Judge Amy calls. "Good work, Kendra."

Kendra collapses forward, hands on her knees, trying not to throw up. Her whole body is shaking. Her hands are bleeding. Her shoulders feel like they've been ripped off and sewn back on wrong.

But she finished.

Sara is there immediately, hand on her back. "That was brutal. But you did it."

"What was my time?" Kendra gasps.

Sara checks her phone. "Eight minutes, forty-two seconds."

Kendra straightens up slowly. Eight forty-two. That's not great. But it's not terrible either. In an event this brutal, surviving is half the battle.

"What place?" she asks.

"Won't know until all the heats finish. But you didn't fail any reps. That matters."

---

By the time Event 4 is over, Kendra's dropped to 9th place overall.

She sits in the athlete recovery area with ice packs on her shoulders and her hands wrapped in gauze. Chris is beside her, quiet, steady. He doesn't try to fix anything. He just sits.

"Event 5 in ninety minutes," she says.

"You need food," Chris says. "And water. And maybe some ibuprofen."

"I need a miracle," Kendra mutters.

Chris smiles. "You need to stop doubting yourself. You're still in the top ten. Event 5 is a sprint. You're good at sprints."

He's right. Event 5 is a seven-minute AMRAP—box jumps and snatches. It's the kind of workout that rewards pure speed and aggression. Kendra's engine is built for that.

She eats a protein bar and drinks two bottles of water. She takes four ibuprofen and tries not to think about how much her hands hurt. Then she moves to the warm-up area and starts prepping.

Box jumps. Snatches. Box jumps. Snatches.

Her legs feel okay. Her shoulders are wrecked, but snatches don't require the same kind of grinding strength that cleans do. It's more about timing and technique.

She can do this.

---

Event 5 is a blur.

Seven minutes of pure output. Kendra jumps on the box, jumps off. Grabs the barbell and snatches it overhead. Drops it. Repeats.

Her lungs are on fire. Her quads are screaming. But she doesn't stop. She doesn't slow down. She just moves.

When the buzzer sounds, she's finished six full rounds plus ten reps. It's the best score in her heat. By far.

Sara is grinning when Kendra collapses onto the floor. "That's what I'm talking about. That's how you race."

"Did I win?" Kendra gasps.

"Your heat, yes. Overall?" Sara checks the leaderboard on her phone. Her eyes go wide. "Holy shit. You're second. Second in the entire event."

Kendra sits up. "Second?"

"Second. Out of forty women." Sara's voice is fierce. "You just climbed the leaderboard, Kendra. You're in fifth place overall."

Fifth place.

Kendra stares at the number on the screen. Fifth place. Going into Day 3. Top five go to the Games.

She's right on the edge.

Chris pulls her into a hug, and she lets herself collapse against him, shaking. Not from exhaustion. From the terrifying, exhilarating reality that this might actually happen.

"One more day," Chris murmurs into her hair. "One more day, and you're going to Madison."

Kendra doesn't respond. She can't. She just holds on and tries to believe him.

---

Event 6 is at 7:00 PM.

By the time Kendra steps into the arena for the final event of Day 2, her body is a wreck. But her mind is clear.

Event 6 is exactly what she expected—a chipper. Twenty burpees, thirty wall balls, forty kettlebell swings, fifty double-unders. For time.

It's going to hurt. But it's also straightforward. No weird technicalities. No failed reps. Just volume and grit.

Kendra lines up in Lane 4 again. Same judge. Same setup.

When the clock starts, she moves.

The burpees are terrible. Her chest hits the ground and it feels like concrete. But she grinds through them.

The wall balls are worse. Her shoulders are so fried that every throw feels like lifting a truck. But she keeps moving.

The kettlebell swings are brutal. The double-unders are a blur.

And then it's over.

She finishes in 11 minutes, 18 seconds. Middle of the pack. Not great. Not terrible.

When the final scores post, she's still in 5th place overall.

Fifth place. Going into Day 3. One day away from the CrossFit Games.

Kendra sits in the stands with Chris after the event, too exhausted to move. The arena is clearing out. Athletes are heading back to hotels. The energy is electric and exhausted all at once.

"Tomorrow," Chris says quietly. "Top five go to Madison. You're in fifth."

"I could drop," Kendra says. "One bad event and I'm out."

"Or one good event and you move up." Chris takes her hand. "You've earned this, Kendra. Now go finish it."

Kendra looks at him. At the man who's believed in her when she didn't believe in herself. Who's flown across the country to watch her suffer through workouts that most people can't even comprehend. Who's sitting here, holding her hand, telling her she can do this.

"Okay," she whispers. "Okay. I'll finish it."

And somewhere, deep in the part of her that's been fighting for this dream for years, she believes him.

---

**End of Chapter 30**
