# Chapter 57: Kendra's Promise Ring

**POV:** Kendra
**Location:** Charlotte
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

A week after the cabin, Chris asks her to dinner.

Not just dinner—*dinner*. The kind with a reservation at the French place downtown that requires a dress code and has a wine list longer than the menu. The kind of dinner that means something.

Kendra knows before they even get there.

She wears the black dress Chris loves, the one that makes her feel elegant instead of athletic. She does her makeup carefully, curls her hair. She looks at herself in the mirror and tries to feel excited.

She feels terrified instead.

Chris picks her up at seven. He's in slacks and a button-down, clean-shaven, looking nervous in a way that's not like him. He's usually steady. Confident. The kind of guy who knows what he wants and goes after it.

Tonight, he looks like he's about to jump out of his skin.

"You look beautiful," he says when she climbs into the car.

"Thanks." Kendra forces a smile. "You look great too."

The drive downtown is quiet. Chris keeps glancing at her, like he wants to say something but can't find the words. Kendra stares out the window and tries to breathe.

She knows what's coming.

She's known since he made the reservation.

And she has no idea what she's going to do about it.

---

The restaurant is exactly as pretentious as she expected.

White tablecloths. Soft lighting. A string quartet playing in the corner. The kind of place where people propose and celebrate anniversaries and mark Important Life Moments.

Kendra's stomach churns.

They're seated at a corner table with a view of the city. The server brings water and a wine menu. Chris orders a bottle of something French and expensive without consulting her.

"Big night?" the server asks, smiling knowingly.

"Something like that," Chris says.

The server winks and disappears.

Kendra picks up her menu and pretends to read it. The words blur together. She has no idea what any of it says.

"Ken," Chris says softly.

She looks up.

He's watching her with those steady brown eyes, the ones that have always made her feel safe. Grounded. Like no matter what happens, he's there.

"You okay?" he asks.

"Yeah. Just... this is a really nice place."

"You deserve nice places."

Kendra's throat tightens. She does not deserve this. Does not deserve his kindness, his patience, the way he looks at her like she's the best thing that ever happened to him.

Not when she's been thinking about someone else.

Not when she kissed someone else.

Not when she can't stop thinking about Addison's eyes in the firelight, saying *it meant everything*.

The wine arrives. Chris tastes it, nods, and the server pours. Kendra takes a long sip. It's good. Complex. Wasted on her.

They order—Kendra picks something at random, Chris gets the steak—and then they're alone again.

Chris reaches across the table and takes her hand.

"I've been thinking a lot lately," he says.

Kendra's heart is pounding. "About what?"

"About us. About the future." He squeezes her hand. "About what I want."

"Chris—"

"Let me finish." His smile is gentle. "I know we've been together less than a year. I know that's not a long time in the grand scheme of things. But I also know that I've never felt this way about anyone. You make me better, Ken. You make me want to be better."

Kendra's eyes are stinging. "Chris, you don't have to—"

"I want to." He pulls a small box from his jacket pocket and sets it on the table between them.

Kendra stares at it.

It's not the size of an engagement ring box. It's flatter. Wider.

But it's still a box.

"I'm not proposing," Chris says quickly, reading her expression. "Not yet. But I wanted to give you something. To show you I'm serious about this. About us."

He opens the box.

Inside is a ring. Not a diamond. Not an engagement ring. But a band—delicate, silver, with a small stone that catches the light.

A promise ring.

"I want to build something that lasts," Chris says. His voice is steady, but his hands are shaking slightly. "I want to wake up next to you. I want to know your training schedule better than my own. I want to be the person you call when you hit a PR or when everything falls apart. I want all of it, Ken. And I know it's going to take time. But I'm patient. I can wait."

Kendra can't breathe.

This is everything she should want. A good man. A stable relationship. Someone who loves her without conditions, without complications.

Someone who isn't Addison.

"This is a promise," Chris continues. "That I'm in this. That I'm not going anywhere. And when you're ready—when we're both ready—I'm going to ask you to marry me. But for now, I just want you to know I'm here."

He takes the ring out of the box and holds it up.

Kendra looks at it. Looks at Chris. Looks at the future he's offering her.

And thinks about the cabin. About Addison by the fire. About the way her voice cracked when she said *I don't know how to go back*.

Kendra should say no. Should tell Chris the truth. Should admit that she's confused and conflicted and not sure what she wants.

But she doesn't.

Because Chris is good. Chris is steady. Chris is safe.

And Addison is... Addison is a maybe. A possibility. A risk Kendra's not sure she's brave enough to take.

"Yes," Kendra hears herself say.

Chris's face breaks into the biggest smile she's ever seen. "Yeah?"

"Yeah." Kendra forces herself to smile back. "Yes."

Chris slips the ring onto her finger. It fits perfectly. Of course it does.

Then he's standing, pulling her up, kissing her in the middle of the restaurant while the people at nearby tables clap and cheer.

Kendra kisses him back.

And hates herself a little bit for it.

---

Dinner is a blur.

Kendra eats without tasting. Drinks without feeling it. Smiles and nods and lets Chris talk about the future—where they might live, what kind of house they'd want, whether she'd keep competing or focus on coaching.

She says all the right things. Agrees in all the right places.

But inside, she's screaming.

When they finally leave the restaurant, Kendra's exhausted. Not from the food or the wine, but from the effort of pretending.

Chris drives her home, one hand on the wheel, the other holding hers. He keeps glancing at the ring on her finger, grinning like he can't believe his luck.

Kendra stares out the window and tries not to cry.

When they pull up in front of her building, Chris walks her to the door.

"Tonight was perfect," he says, pulling her into his arms.

"Yeah," Kendra manages. "It was."

He kisses her—slow and sweet and full of promise. Kendra kisses him back, but her mind is elsewhere.

In a cabin in the mountains. By a fire. With a woman who said *it meant everything*.

"I love you," Chris says against her lips.

"I love you too," Kendra whispers.

And the worst part is, she means it.

She does love him.

She's just not sure it's enough.

Chris leaves, and Kendra goes inside. She climbs the stairs to her apartment, unlocks the door, and steps into the silence.

She stands in the middle of her living room and looks down at the ring on her finger.

It's beautiful. Delicate. A symbol of everything she should want.

She takes it off and sets it on the coffee table.

Then she sits on the couch and stares at it for a long time.

---

Her phone buzzes. A text from Addison.

**Addison:** How are you?

Kendra stares at the message. Three words. Simple. Loaded.

She could ignore it. Could pretend she didn't see it. Could go to bed and deal with it tomorrow.

But she's so tired of pretending.

**Kendra:** Chris gave me a promise ring tonight.

The typing bubbles appear immediately. Then disappear. Then appear again.

Finally: **Addison:** Oh.

**Kendra:** Yeah.

**Addison:** Are you okay?

Kendra looks at the ring on the coffee table. At her empty apartment. At the life she's supposed to want.

**Kendra:** I don't know.

The bubbles appear again. Stay for a long time. Then:

**Addison:** Do you want to talk?

Kendra does. God, she does. But she's also terrified of what might happen if they do. Terrified of what she might say. What she might admit.

**Kendra:** Not tonight. I need to think.

**Addison:** Okay. I'm here if you need me.

**Kendra:** I know. Thank you.

Kendra sets the phone down and picks up the ring again. She turns it over in her hands, watching the light catch on the stone.

Chris loves her. He wants to marry her. He's offering her a future.

And all Kendra can think about is Addison.

Addison's smile. Addison's laugh. The way she looked at Kendra in New York like she was the only person in the room.

The way she said *I don't know how to go back*.

Kendra doesn't know how to go back either.

But she also doesn't know how to move forward.

So she sits on her couch, holding a ring that should make her happy, and lets herself cry.

For Chris, who deserves better.

For Addison, who deserves an answer.

For herself, stuck between two futures and terrified to choose.

---

The next morning, Kendra puts the ring back on.

She looks at it in the mirror. It's pretty. Subtle. The kind of thing people will notice but not make a big deal about.

She tells herself it's fine. That she can do this. That she'll figure it out.

But when she goes to the gym for her morning workout, the ring feels like a weight.

Sara's there, coaching the early class. She spots the ring immediately.

"Oh my God," Sara says, grabbing Kendra's hand. "Is that—did Chris—?"

"It's a promise ring," Kendra says quickly. "Not an engagement ring."

"Still!" Sara's grinning. "That's huge! Congratulations!"

"Thanks."

Sara studies her. "You don't look happy."

"I am," Kendra lies. "I'm just tired."

Sara doesn't look convinced, but she doesn't push. "Well, I'm happy for you. Chris is a great guy."

"He is," Kendra agrees.

And he is. Chris is great. Perfect, even.

So why does Kendra feel like she's drowning?

---

The rest of the day is a blur.

People notice the ring. People congratulate her. Kendra smiles and says thank you and tries to mean it.

By the time she gets home that night, she's exhausted.

She makes dinner—pasta with marinara, nothing fancy—and eats it on the couch, scrolling mindlessly through her phone.

There's a text from Harper: **Saw the ring! SO HAPPY FOR YOU!!! Dinner soon to celebrate?**

There's a text from Elena: **Chris told Jasper. Congratulations, Kendra. You deserve happiness.**

There's nothing from Addison.

Kendra sets the phone down and stares at the ring again.

She thinks about the cabin. About the firelight. About the way Addison looked at her when she said *it meant everything*.

She thinks about Chris. About the way he smiled when she said yes. About the future he's offering her.

She thinks about the choice she's going to have to make.

And she realizes, with a sinking feeling in her chest, that she's already made it.

She chose safety. Chose certainty. Chose the path that makes sense.

But sitting here, alone in her apartment, wearing a ring that should make her happy, Kendra wonders if she chose wrong.

---

Book 3 closes with Kendra sitting on her couch, the promise ring on her finger, staring at her phone.

At Addison's name in her contacts.

At the message she wants to send but can't.

At the life she's building and the life she's leaving behind.

She thinks about New York. About the cabin. About all the moments in between when she and Addison looked at each other and saw possibility.

And she thinks about what she just promised away.

Chris deserves the truth.

Addison deserves an answer.

And Kendra deserves to figure out what she actually wants.

But tonight, all she has is a ring on her finger and a choice she's not sure she can live with.

So she sits in the silence and lets herself feel it.

The weight of the promise.

The ache of what might have been.

The terrifying certainty that everything is about to change.

Whether she's ready or not.
