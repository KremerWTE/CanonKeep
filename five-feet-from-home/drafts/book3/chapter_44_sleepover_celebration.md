# Chapter 44: Sleepover Celebration

**POV: Addison**
**Location: Oak Watch**

"Please, Addie? Please please please?"

Grace has been asking for the past hour, ever since the last guest left and Elena started cleaning up cake plates. Now she's deployed the ultimate weapon: the puppy dog eyes, combined with hands clasped in prayer position.

"Grace, Addie probably has things to do—" Elena starts, but I'm already caving.

"I can stay," I hear myself say.

Grace's squeal could shatter glass. She launches herself at me, wrapping her arms around my waist. "Yes! This is the best First Communion ever!"

"You only get one First Communion, kiddo," Jasper points out from the couch, but he's smiling at me. "You sure? She's going to keep you up all night."

"I'm sure." And surprisingly, I am. I hadn't planned on a sleepover when I got dressed this morning, but spending more time with Grace sounds infinitely better than going home to my empty apartment.

"Okay, but you're in charge of getting her to sleep at a reasonable hour," Elena says, pointing at me with a dish towel. "And by reasonable, I mean before midnight."

"No promises," I say.

Grace is already dragging me toward the living room. "We have to build a blanket fort! It's tradition!"

"Since when is it tradition?"

"Since right now. Come on!"

---

Twenty minutes later, the Barrett living room looks like a fabric store exploded.

Grace has pulled every blanket, sheet, and pillow from the upstairs linen closet. We've commandeered dining room chairs, the coat rack, and a decorative ladder Elena usually uses for hanging plants. The architectural integrity is questionable at best, but Grace is in heaven.

"No, that corner needs to be higher," she directs, pointing at a sagging section. "We need room to sit up."

"You're very demanding for someone who's supposed to be celebrating a holy sacrament today," I tell her, but I'm already repositioning the chair.

"Jesus would want a good blanket fort," Grace says with absolute certainty.

"I don't think that's in the Bible."

"It's in spirit." She crawls inside, testing the space. "Perfect! Now you get in."

I duck under the entrance — constructed from a flowered bedsheet and what I'm pretty sure is Elena's good tablecloth — and settle into the nest of pillows Grace has arranged. She's strung up a strand of battery-powered fairy lights somewhere, and they cast everything in a warm, golden glow.

"This is actually pretty impressive," I admit.

"I know. I'm very good at forts." She flops down next to me, still in her white Communion dress because she refused to change. "Now we watch Moana."

"Of course. The sleepover isn't complete without Moana."

"Three times," she specifies.

"Grace—"

"It's my First Communion sleepover. I make the rules."

I look at her determined little face and can't help but laugh. "Okay. Three times. But your mom is going to kill me."

"She won't. She loves you."

The simple certainty in her voice makes my chest tight. Grace states it like an obvious fact, like the sky is blue and water is wet and Elena Barrett loves Addison Wells.

And maybe she's right. Maybe over the past year, through RCIA classes and family dinners and moments like this, I've been adopted into this family in a way that still surprises me.

Jasper pokes his head into the fort. "You girls need anything? Snacks? Emotional support?"

"Popcorn!" Grace demands. "And juice boxes!"

"Say please," I remind her.

"Please!" she adds, grinning at Jasper.

He returns a few minutes later with popcorn, juice boxes, and — because he's Jasper — a plate of leftover cake.

"Don't tell Elena about the cake," he whispers conspiratorially.

"Our secret," I promise.

He leaves us to it, and Grace immediately queues up Moana on the tablet she's propped against a pillow tower. We settle in, sharing the popcorn bowl, and I realize I'm completely content.

This is nice. Easy. Uncomplicated.

Everything the rest of my life isn't right now.

---

We're fifteen minutes into Moana — Grace singing along to "Where You Are" with enthusiasm that makes up for questionable pitch — when my phone buzzes.

Sara: *How's the sleepover going?*

Me: *Currently in a blanket fort. About to watch Moana for the first time of three.*

Sara: *Three times? Grace is a mastermind.*

Me: *It's her First Communion sleepover. Apparently she makes the rules.*

Sara: *You're a good godmother.*

I smile at the phone, then set it aside as Grace starts narrating the movie to me despite the fact that we're both watching it.

"This part is so sad," she says as Moana's grandmother gives her the heart of Te Fiti. "But also beautiful. Do you think it's okay for things to be sad and beautiful at the same time?"

"Yeah," I say softly. "I think that's most of the best things, actually."

She considers this, then nods solemnly. "Like First Communion. It was beautiful, but also I was kind of scared before."

"Scared of what?"

"What if I wasn't ready? What if I did it wrong?"

I shift to look at her better. "Grace, you can't do it wrong. It's not about being perfect. It's about showing up and being open to grace — which, by the way, is your name, so you're basically built for it."

She giggles. "That's what Father Michael said. That my name is a reminder."

"Smart man."

We watch in silence for a while. Grace makes it through maybe ten minutes before she starts talking again.

"Addie?"

"Hmm?"

"Do you think Moana and Maui are in love?"

I blink at the sudden topic change. "Uh... I think they're friends. Good friends who go on adventures together."

"But they could fall in love later, right? After the movie ends?"

"Maybe. Why?"

She shrugs, eyes still on the screen. "I just think sometimes people who are friends first make the best couples. That's what Mom says about her and Daddy. That they were friends first."

"Your mom is right. Being friends first is a good foundation."

"Are you friends with Kendra?"

And there it is. My chest tightens.

"Yeah," I say carefully. "Kendra and I are friends."

"But you used to be more than friends, right?"

I nearly choke on a piece of popcorn. "What? Who told you that?"

"Nobody told me. I just know." She looks at me with those too-wise eyes. "You look at each other different sometimes. Like you're trying to figure something out."

I have no idea how to respond to that. Grace is seven. She just had her First Communion. She should not be this perceptive.

"It's complicated," I finally say.

"That's what adults always say when they don't want to explain things."

"Sometimes things actually are complicated, though."

She thinks about this, then nods. "Okay. But Addie?"

"Yeah?"

"Even when things are complicated, you should probably talk about them. That's what Mom makes me and Lucas do when we're fighting."

Out of the mouths of babes.

"That's good advice," I tell her.

"I know. I'm very wise." She snuggles closer to my side. "Now shh, this is the good part."

---

We make it through Moana once. Grace immediately starts it again.

"Maybe we should take a break?" I suggest. "Do something else for a bit?"

"Like what?"

"I don't know. Do you have games? Books?"

Her face lights up. "Face masks! Mom has the really good ones that smell like strawberries!"

Before I can protest, she's scrambling out of the fort and thundering up the stairs. I hear muffled conversation, then Elena's laugh, and then Grace is back with an entire spa treatment's worth of supplies.

"Mom says we can use all of this," she announces, dumping everything on the fort floor. "She said to take pictures."

"Of course she did."

Grace is already opening packets. "This one is for hydration. This one is for brightening. This one has charcoal in it."

"How do you know all this?"

"Mom and I do spa days sometimes. It's very relaxing." She hands me a headband. "Here, you have to put your hair back."

I obey, pulling my hair into the headband while Grace does the same. Then she's carefully applying a pink face mask to my cheeks, her tongue sticking out in concentration.

"Hold still," she commands.

"Yes, ma'am."

When she's satisfied with my face, she does her own, smearing the pink cream with abandon. We're both laughing by the time she's done, looking ridiculous with matching pink faces and our hair pushed back.

"Cucumber slices!" Grace remembers, pulling them from a small container. "For our eyes. It's very sophisticated."

She places them carefully over her closed eyes, then lies back on the pillows like she's at an actual spa. I do the same, cucumber slices in place, trying not to laugh.

"This is very relaxing," Grace says in a faux-serene voice.

"Very."

We lie there for maybe thirty seconds before Grace starts giggling, and then we're both cracking up, cucumber slices sliding off our faces.

"We're terrible at this," I say.

"The worst," she agrees.

Jasper chooses that moment to check on us, taking one look at our pink-masked faces and laughing so hard he has to lean against the fort entrance for support.

"Don't you dare collapse our fort," Grace warns.

"I'm getting photographic evidence of this," he says, pulling out his phone. "Say cheese!"

"Cucumbers!" Grace shouts instead.

The picture he takes is ridiculous — me and Grace with pink face masks, cucumber slices in hand, grinning like maniacs in our blanket fort. He shows us the result, and Grace immediately demands he send it to her mom.

"Also send it to Kendra!" she adds. "She'll think it's funny."

I see Jasper hesitate, glance at me. I shrug, trying to look casual.

"Sure thing, Gracie."

He leaves, and Grace goes back to the tablet, queuing up Moana for the third time. But I'm thinking about that photo going to Kendra, wondering what she'll think when she sees it.

Wondering if she'll wish she was here instead of in Madison.

Wondering if I wish she was here too.

---

It's nearly eleven when Grace finally starts yawning. We've made it through Moana twice and are halfway through the third viewing. The face masks are long washed off, the popcorn is gone, and Grace is curled into my side, fighting to keep her eyes open.

"You can sleep, you know," I tell her.

"Not sleepy," she mumbles, even as she yawns again.

"Grace—"

"Just five more minutes."

I smile, running my fingers through her hair. The fort is warm and cozy, fairy lights still glowing, and I can hear Elena and Jasper talking quietly in the kitchen.

This is nice. This is good.

This is the kind of moment I didn't know I needed.

"Addie?" Grace's voice is small, drowsy.

"Yeah?"

"Do you have secrets?"

The question catches me off guard. "Everyone does."

"I'll keep yours if you keep mine." She holds up her pinky. "Promise?"

I link my pinky with hers, this tiny, sacred gesture. "Promise."

"Good." She yawns again. "'Cause I have a secret."

"You do?"

"Mm-hmm. But I'm too sleepy to tell you right now. Tomorrow."

"Okay. Tomorrow."

She's quiet for so long I think she's fallen asleep. Then: "Addie?"

"Yeah, Gracie?"

"I'm really glad you're my godmother."

My throat gets tight. "I'm really glad too."

"Even though you're still figuring out the God stuff?"

I have to smile at that. "Even though I'm still figuring out the God stuff."

"That's okay. We can figure it out together."

"Deal."

This time when she goes quiet, she really is asleep. I can tell by the way her breathing evens out, the way she goes heavy against my side. I shift carefully, making sure she's comfortable, and pull one of the blankets over both of us.

My phone buzzes. A text from Kendra — her response to the photo Jasper sent.

*You guys look ridiculous. Also adorable. Tell Grace I said goodnight.*

I stare at the message, trying to decode it. Is it friendly? Wistful? Matter-of-fact? With Kendra, I can never quite tell anymore.

I type back: *She's asleep. Today was big.*

Three dots appear. Disappear. Appear again.

Kendra: *I wish I could have been there.*

Me: *I know. She understands though. Games are important.*

Kendra: *Yeah. Still.*

I want to ask what "still" means. Want to ask if she's okay, if Madison is everything she hoped, if she's thinking about Charlotte the way I'm thinking about her.

Instead, I type: *How are you doing? Really?*

The dots appear and disappear several times. Finally:

Kendra: *Tired. Sore. Overwhelmed. But good. I think I'm good.*

Me: *You're doing amazing. 11th place at the Games is incredible.*

Kendra: *Thanks. How was the rest of the day?*

Me: *Perfect. Grace is perfect. Your family is perfect.*

Kendra: *They're your family too.*

The message sits on my screen, and I don't know what to do with it. Because she's right — somewhere along the way, the Barretts became my family too. But where does that leave us? What are we to each other in this constellation of relationships?

Before I can figure out how to respond, another message comes through:

Kendra: *I should sleep. Big day tomorrow. But Addie? I'm glad you were there today. For Grace. It meant a lot.*

Me: *Wouldn't have missed it.*

And that's true. Even if it meant seeing Kendra through a screen instead of in person. Even if it meant feeling the complicated ache of missing someone who's choosing to be somewhere else.

I set my phone down and look at Grace, peaceful in sleep, still in her white Communion dress in our ridiculous blanket fort.

"Your secrets are safe with me," I whisper.

Mine, though? I'm not so sure about those.

Because the truth is, I do have secrets. Like the fact that watching Kendra compete makes my heart race. Like the fact that I think about her more than I should, more than makes sense for someone who's supposedly just a friend.

Like the fact that I might be falling for someone who's already chosen someone else.

---

Elena finds us the next morning, still asleep in the fort.

I wake to the smell of coffee and the soft sound of a camera shutter. Blinking awake, I see Elena in the fort entrance, phone in hand, smiling.

"Don't mind me," she whispers. "Just capturing the moment."

Grace is draped across me, drooling slightly on my shoulder. One of her hands is still linked with mine from our pinky promise. We're surrounded by the detritus of our sleepover — empty popcorn bowl, face mask packets, the tablet frozen on the Moana title screen.

"What time is it?" I mumble.

"Almost eight. You can sleep more if you want. I'm making pancakes when you're ready."

"Pancakes?" Grace stirs, eyes still closed. "With chocolate chips?"

"If you say please."

"Please," Grace says automatically, then burrows back into my side.

Elena laughs quietly and disappears, leaving us in our fort cocoon.

"Gracie, you awake?"

"No," she mumbles.

"You just asked about pancakes."

"Sleep-talking."

I smile, shifting slightly. "Come on. Your mom's making breakfast."

"Five more minutes."

It's her favorite phrase. Five more minutes in bed. Five more minutes of playtime. Five more minutes of whatever good thing is happening before we have to move on to the next thing.

Sometimes I wish life worked that way — that you could just ask for five more minutes and the world would give it to you.

Five more minutes before things get complicated. Five more minutes before people make choices that change everything. Five more minutes in the easy, simple warmth before reality intrudes.

But time doesn't work that way. It just keeps moving forward whether we're ready or not.

"Come on," I say again, gently extracting myself from Grace's octopus grip. "Chocolate chip pancakes wait for no one."

That gets her moving. We emerge from the fort, blinking in the morning light, and make our way to the kitchen where Jasper is reading the paper and Elena is indeed making pancakes.

It's domestic and lovely and everything I didn't grow up with.

It's also temporary. In a few hours, I'll go home to my apartment. Grace will go back to being a regular seven-year-old instead of a sleepover conspirator. Life will resume its normal pace.

But for now, there are pancakes. There's laughter. There's Grace telling her parents about every single moment of last night like they weren't in the next room the whole time.

There's family, chosen and claimed, gathered around a breakfast table on a Sunday morning.

And there's a pinky promise between me and a seven-year-old about secrets we're keeping.

Everyone has secrets, I told her.

Mine are getting harder to keep.

But I'll hold onto them a little longer. For Grace. For the Barretts. For whatever fragile peace we've all built.

Even if that peace is as unstable as our blanket fort, held together by faith and fairy lights and the hope that if we just stay still enough, it won't come crashing down.
