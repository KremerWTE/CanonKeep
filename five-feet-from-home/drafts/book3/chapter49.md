# CHAPTER 49: Games Final Day

**POV: Kendra**
**Location: Madison, Wisconsin**

The morning of the final day starts with silence.

Kendra wakes at 5:47 AM in the athlete hotel, three minutes before her alarm. Her body knows. This is it. The last day of the CrossFit Games. Two events left. Everything she's trained for, everything she's sacrificed, everything she's fought through comes down to the next twelve hours.

She lies still for a moment, staring at the ceiling. Her legs are wrecked. Her shoulders are on fire. Her hands are torn to shreds despite the tape jobs Sara has been doing every night. She's in tenth place on the leaderboard. Ten points out of ninth. Fifteen points out of eighth.

Top ten. She's so close.

Her phone buzzes. A text from Chris: *You're going to crush today. I can feel it.*

Then one from Addison: *Final push. Leave it all out there.*

Then Jasper: *Show them what Barrett Standard means.*

She closes her eyes. They're all here. Chris flew in yesterday. Addison and Jasper came for the weekend. Her parents are somewhere in the stands. Sara's in the athlete area. Elena texted last night: *Make us proud. You already have.*

Kendra gets up. Showers. The water stings her hands but she doesn't care. She braids her hair tight, the way she always does for competition. Game face. War paint. Whatever you want to call it.

By 7:30 AM she's at the competition floor. Athletes are scattered everywhere, warming up, stretching, trying to stay loose. The energy is different today. Everyone's tired. Everyone's hurting. But there's something else too — a kind of electric desperation. This is it. No tomorrow. No second chances.

Sara finds her by the rig. "How are you feeling?"

"Like I got hit by a truck."

"Good. Everyone else feels worse." Sara hands her a protein shake. "Event nine is posted. You're going to love it."

Kendra pulls out her phone and opens the Games app. The workout pops up:

**EVENT 9: "GRACE AND PULL"**
*For time:*
*30 Thrusters (95 lbs)*
*30 Chest-to-Bar Pull-ups*
*Time cap: 6 minutes*

Kendra reads it twice. Then a third time.

"It's a sprint," Sara says.

"It's a couplet."

"Classic CrossFit. No tricks. Just you and the bar."

Kendra nods slowly. Thrusters and chest-to-bar. She can do this. She's done this a thousand times. The weight is moderate. The volume is high but manageable. The question is: can she go unbroken?

"What are you thinking?" Sara asks.

"I'm thinking I either go for it or I don't."

"Go for it."

"If I blow up—"

"You won't." Sara grabs her shoulders. "Kendra. You've done thrusters at twice this weight. You've done fifties on chest-to-bar. This is your wheelhouse. Trust your engine."

Kendra meets her eyes. Sara's right. This is her event. Power and pulling. If she's going to make a move up the leaderboard, it's now.

"Unbroken," Kendra says.

"Unbroken," Sara confirms.

---

Event nine starts at 10 AM. Kendra is in heat three, lane seven. She stands behind her barbell, chalk on her hands, heart hammering in her chest. The crowd is massive. The noise is deafening. She can't see Chris or Addison or Jasper but she knows they're out there.

The countdown begins. Ten seconds. Five. Three. Two. One.

Beep.

Kendra grabs the bar and cleans it in one smooth motion. The weight settles on her shoulders. She drops into the first thruster. Explodes up. Locks out overhead. Drops into the second. Explodes up. Locks out.

Her breath is steady. Her rhythm is perfect. Don't think. Just move.

Ten thrusters. Fifteen. Twenty.

Her quads are screaming. Her shoulders are burning. Keep going.

Twenty-five. Twenty-six. Twenty-seven.

The crowd is roaring but she doesn't hear them. It's just her and the bar.

Twenty-eight. Twenty-nine. Thirty.

She drops the barbell and sprints to the pull-up bar. Three steps. Jump. Catch.

First chest-to-bar. Clean. Second. Third.

She finds her kip. Fast and efficient. Chest touches the bar every rep. Her hands are on fire but she doesn't care.

Ten reps. Fifteen. Twenty.

She can feel herself slowing. Her grip is slipping. Her lats are screaming.

Don't you dare let go.

Twenty-five. Twenty-six.

Four more. Just four more.

Twenty-seven. Twenty-eight. Twenty-nine.

One more.

She pulls with everything she has. Her chest hits the bar. The judge calls it good.

Kendra drops to the ground and sprints to the finish mat.

Time: 3:47.

She collapses. Can't breathe. Can't see. Her whole body is on fire.

Sara is there instantly. "You did it. You went unbroken. Holy shit, Kendra, you went unbroken."

Kendra rolls onto her back, gasping. The screen above the floor updates with times. She's fourth in her heat. But the final standings won't be posted until all heats finish.

She closes her eyes and waits.

---

Forty-five minutes later, the leaderboard updates.

**Event 9 Results:**
**4th Place: Kendra Wright — 3:47**

Kendra stares at the screen. Fourth. She got fourth in an event at the CrossFit Games.

Her overall standing jumps from tenth to eighth.

Sara grabs her. "Eighth! You're eighth in the world!"

Kendra can't speak. She just nods. Eighth. Top ten. She did it.

But there's still one more event.

---

The final event is announced at 2 PM. It's called "Last One Standing" and it's exactly what it sounds like: an elimination ladder.

**EVENT 10: "LAST ONE STANDING"**
*Round 1: 30 Double-Unders, 15 Box Jump-Overs (24"), 10 Dumbbell Snatches (50 lbs)*
*Round 2: 40 Double-Unders, 20 Box Jump-Overs, 15 Dumbbell Snatches*
*Round 3: 50 Double-Unders, 25 Box Jump-Overs, 20 Dumbbell Snatches*
*Rounds continue with increasing volume until one athlete remains.*
*Last 5 finishers in each round are eliminated.*

Kendra reads it and feels her stomach drop. An elimination event. One by one, they'll cut athletes until there's only one left. It's brutal. It's strategic. And it's completely unpredictable.

"Don't overthink it," Sara says. "Just don't be in the bottom five."

"Easier said than done."

"You're eighth. You just need to survive."

The event starts at 4 PM. All remaining athletes line up on the floor. Forty women. By the end, thirty-five will be eliminated.

The horn sounds.

Round one is chaos. Everyone sprints. Double-unders, box jump-overs, dumbbell snatches. Kendra moves fast but controlled. She's not trying to win the round. She's trying to survive.

She finishes twelfth. Safe.

Five athletes are cut. Thirty-five remain.

Round two. The volume increases. Kendra's legs are dead but she keeps moving. Double-unders are clean. Box jump-overs are smooth. Dumbbell snatches are heavy but manageable.

She finishes fifteenth. Safe again.

Five more cut. Thirty remain.

Round three. Fifty double-unders. Twenty-five box jump-overs. Twenty dumbbell snatches.

Kendra is struggling now. Her shoulders are done. Her grip is failing. She fights through the double-unders, grinds through the box jump-overs, but the dumbbell snatches are brutal.

She finishes twenty-third.

Five more cut.

She's safe. Barely.

Twenty-five athletes remain.

Round four starts. Sixty double-unders. Thirty box jump-overs. Twenty-five dumbbell snatches.

Kendra gets through the double-unders but on the box jump-overs, her legs give out. She misses a jump. Steps down. Jumps again. She's losing time.

By the time she gets to the dumbbells, she's in trouble. Athletes are finishing around her. She powers through ten snatches. Fifteen. Twenty.

Five more.

She's in the bottom five. She knows it. But she doesn't stop.

Twenty-one. Twenty-two. Twenty-three. Twenty-four. Twenty-five.

She hits the finish mat.

The screen updates.

She's twenty-first.

Last place to survive.

Five more athletes are cut. Twenty remain.

Kendra stands there, shaking. She made it. One more round.

But when the judge calls for round five, Kendra looks at the workout on the board and knows she's done. Seventy double-unders. Thirty-five box jump-overs. Thirty dumbbell snatches.

She can't. Her body has nothing left.

Still, she starts. Double-unders are sloppy but she gets through them. Box jump-overs are agony. Every jump feels like her quads are tearing. The dumbbells might as well weigh a thousand pounds.

She finishes in the bottom five.

It's over.

The judge taps her shoulder. "You're out. Great work."

Kendra steps off the competition floor. She's eliminated. But when she looks up at the leaderboard, her name is still there.

**Overall Standings:**
**8th Place: Kendra Wright**

Eighth in the world.

Top ten at the CrossFit Games.

She did it.

---

Chris finds her first. He wraps her in his arms and she collapses into him, shaking and crying and laughing all at once.

"You did it," he whispers. "You did it."

Addison and Jasper are there next. Addison hugs her so tight she can't breathe. "Eighth in the world, Kendra. Eighth."

Jasper just nods, that quiet pride in his eyes. "Barrett Standard."

Sara brings ice and water and a towel. Her parents push through the crowd. Her mom is crying. Her dad can't stop smiling.

Elena calls. Kendra can barely hear her over the noise but the words come through: "I'm so proud of you."

Kendra stands there, surrounded by everyone who got her here, and lets it sink in.

She came to Madison as a long shot. A regional athlete trying to prove she belonged. And she's leaving as a top-ten finisher at the CrossFit Games.

Eighth in the world.

She looks out at the competition floor one last time. Athletes are still grinding through the elimination rounds. The crowd is still roaring. The energy is still electric.

But for Kendra, it's over.

And it's perfect.

---

That night, they all go to dinner. Chris, Addison, Jasper, Sara, Kendra's parents. They take over a corner of a steakhouse downtown and order everything on the menu.

"To Kendra," Jasper says, raising his glass. "Eighth in the world. Top ten at the Games. You earned every bit of it."

Everyone cheers. Kendra's face is red but she's smiling so hard her cheeks hurt.

"Speech," Addison says.

"Oh God, no—"

"Speech!"

Kendra stands, reluctantly. "Okay. Fine. Um. I don't really know what to say except thank you. To all of you. Chris, you've been my rock through all of this. Sara, you believed in me when I didn't believe in myself. Addison, Jasper, you gave me the resources and the support to chase this dream. Mom, Dad, you've been there since day one. And Elena—" She pauses. "Elena showed me what it means to be part of something bigger than yourself."

She raises her glass. "To Barrett Standard Solutions. To POH. To family. To all of us."

They drink.

The rest of the night is a blur of laughter and stories and exhaustion. Kendra barely eats. She's too tired. Too overwhelmed. Too happy.

At some point, Chris leans over and whispers, "I'm so proud of you."

She kisses him. "I couldn't have done it without you."

Later, when they're back at the hotel, Kendra lies in bed and stares at the ceiling. Her body is destroyed. Her hands are wrecked. But her heart is full.

Eighth in the world.

She did it.

And this is just the beginning.
