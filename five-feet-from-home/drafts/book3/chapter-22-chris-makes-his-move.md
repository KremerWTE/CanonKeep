# Chapter 22: Chris Makes His Move

*POV: Kendra*

---

The text came through at 2:47 p.m., right as Kendra finished her second training session of the day.

*Dinner tonight? I know a place. Nothing fancy. Just... let me take you to dinner. —Chris*

Kendra sat on the bench outside the gym, her quads still burning from the squats she'd programmed for herself. Her phone screen was smudged with chalk dust, and she had to wipe it on her shorts to read the message clearly.

Chris Donnelly had been circling for weeks—respectfully, carefully, like he knew she was still figuring something out but wasn't quite sure what. He'd shown up at her regional competition in Charleston, watching from the stands without making a big deal about it. He'd texted her congratulations after she'd placed third. He'd been... present. Not pushy. Not demanding. Just there.

And now he was asking her to dinner.

Kendra's first instinct was to say no. To make an excuse. To keep him at the safe distance she'd been maintaining since—well, since Addison had walked back into her life and complicated everything in ways she was still trying to name.

But she'd been thinking about that distance. About the fact that Addison was undefined, unspoken, a thing that existed in the five feet between them when they were together and dissolved into uncertainty the moment they were apart. About the fact that Kendra was tired of waiting for something that might never solidify.

She typed back: *What time?*

His response came through almost immediately. *7? I'll pick you up.*

*OK.*

She stared at the phone for a long moment, waiting to feel guilty. Waiting to feel like she was betraying something.

Instead, she just felt curious.

---

Chris pulled up at exactly seven o'clock in a dark gray truck that was clean but not obsessively so—the kind of vehicle that suggested someone who had their life together but didn't need to prove it. He got out to open her door, which Kendra found both charming and slightly old-fashioned in a way that made her smile.

"You clean up nice," he said, giving her an appraising look.

Kendra had opted for dark jeans and a fitted green blouse—casual enough to not feel like a big deal, put-together enough that she didn't look like she'd just rolled out of the gym. "You too."

He was wearing a button-down with the sleeves rolled to his forearms and dark jeans that fit well. Not trying too hard. Just... right.

He drove them to a small Italian place on the east side of Charlotte, the kind of restaurant that didn't have a website and got all its business from word-of-mouth. The inside was dimly lit, with checkered tablecloths and the smell of garlic and fresh bread heavy in the air.

"How'd you find this place?" Kendra asked as they slid into a booth in the back corner.

"Friend of a friend. I've been coming here for years." Chris handed her a menu. "The lasagna's incredible. So is the chicken marsala. Honestly, I don't think they know how to make anything bad."

Kendra scanned the menu, but her attention kept drifting to him. He was relaxed, leaning back against the booth, one arm draped casually along the top of the seat. No nerves. No agenda. Just... ease.

"What?" he asked, catching her looking.

"Nothing. You're just... calm."

"Is that a bad thing?"

"No. It's different."

"Different from what?"

Kendra hesitated. Different from Marcus, who'd been all intensity and competition, who'd treated their relationship like another event to win. Different from Tyler, who'd been safe but boring, who'd never challenged her to be anything more than what she already was. Different from Addison, who was brilliant and magnetic and utterly terrifying in her ability to make Kendra feel seen and invisible at the same time.

"Different from what I'm used to," she said finally.

Chris nodded, like that was answer enough. "I'll take different."

---

They ordered—lasagna for her, chicken marsala for him, a bottle of red wine to share. The conversation started easy: her training, his work managing athletic performance programs for a sports training facility, the weird overlap between CrossFit culture and professional athletics.

"You ever think about going pro?" Chris asked, twirling pasta on his fork.

Kendra laughed. "Pro CrossFit? That's like five people in the world who make a living at it. Everyone else is just really fit and really broke."

"You're better than most people I've worked with."

"You're biased."

"I'm observant." He set down his fork, his gaze steady on her. "I've worked with a lot of athletes, Kendra. Division I, Olympic hopefuls, NFL guys rehabbing injuries. None of them have what you have."

"And what's that?"

"Fire." He said it simply, like it was the most obvious thing in the world. "Most people train because they have to. You train because it's the only thing that makes sense. Like if you stopped, you wouldn't know who you were anymore."

Kendra felt something tighten in her chest. She thought about the hours she spent in the gym, the way she pushed herself until her body screamed, the way physical pain was easier to process than emotional confusion.

"You think that's a good thing?" she asked quietly.

"I think it's honest. And honesty is rare."

Kendra took a sip of wine, using the moment to collect herself. "What about you? What's your fire?"

"That's easy. Helping people find theirs." Chris leaned forward, elbows on the table. "I spent years chasing other people's definitions of success. College ball, trying to make it pro, all of it. When I didn't make the cut, I felt like I'd failed. Took me a long time to realize I hadn't failed—I'd just been chasing the wrong thing."

"So what's the right thing?"

"Building something that lasts. Helping athletes figure out who they are when the game's over. Creating programs that actually make people better, not just faster or stronger."

"That sounds... fulfilling."

"It is. Most days." He smiled, and it was warm and genuine and completely disarming. "And some days it's just exhausting and I eat too much pasta and wonder if I should have been an accountant."

Kendra laughed—a real laugh that surprised her. "You'd be a terrible accountant."

"You're probably right."

---

They talked through dessert—tiramisu they split between them, trading bites and stories. Chris told her about growing up in South Carolina, about his family, about the faith that had grounded him through the years when he'd been lost.

"Do you still go to church?" Kendra asked.

"Most Sundays. You?"

"Not since I was a kid. My parents were... complicated about it. Lots of rules, not a lot of grace."

"That's the worst kind." Chris's expression softened. "Faith's supposed to be about freedom, not fear. If it's just fear, you're doing it wrong."

"You sound like you've thought about this."

"I've lived it. Made a lot of mistakes, hurt a lot of people, had to figure out what forgiveness actually meant." He paused. "I'm still figuring it out, honestly. But I'm trying."

Kendra studied him. There was no performance here. No pretense. Just a man who'd been broken and put himself back together and was willing to admit he was still working on the seams.

"Why me?" she asked.

Chris blinked. "What?"

"Why ask me to dinner? You could date anyone. Why me?"

He didn't answer right away. He set down his fork, took a sip of wine, and looked at her with an intensity that wasn't aggressive, just... present.

"Because when I watch you compete, I see someone who's fighting for something. I don't know what it is yet. Maybe you don't either. But I see it. And I want to know what happens when you figure it out."

Kendra's throat tightened. "That's not an answer."

"Sure it is. I'm interested in the person you're becoming, not just the person you are right now." He leaned back. "And before you ask—no, I'm not trying to fix you. I don't think you're broken. I just think you're in the middle of something, and I want to be around when you get to the other side."

Kendra didn't know what to say to that. She thought about Addison, about the unspoken thing between them that never quite solidified. About the way Addison looked at her sometimes, like Kendra was both the answer and the question. About the way that felt exhilarating and exhausting in equal measure.

Chris wasn't asking her to be anything. He was just asking to be there.

"This scares me," she admitted.

"Good. Means it matters."

---

He drove her home just before ten. The truck cab was quiet, the radio playing something soft and unobtrusive. Kendra watched the streetlights blur past and tried to sort through the tangle of feelings in her chest.

When they pulled up outside her apartment, Chris put the truck in park but didn't turn off the engine.

"I had a good time," he said.

"Yeah. Me too."

"Can I ask you something?"

Kendra turned to look at him. "Sure."

"Is there someone else?"

The question hung in the air between them. Kendra could lie. Could deflect. Could say it was complicated and leave it at that.

But Chris had been honest with her all night. She owed him the same.

"I don't know," she said slowly. "There might be. Or there was. Or there could be. I don't... I don't know what it is."

Chris nodded, his expression unreadable. "Is it Addison?"

Kendra's breath caught. "How—"

"I've seen the way you look at her. At Elena's party. At the gym when she showed up that one time." He said it without judgment, just observation. "I'm not blind, Kendra. And I'm not trying to compete with something that might be real."

"I don't know if it's real," Kendra said, and the admission felt like a crack splitting open. "I don't know what it is. I don't know if she knows what it is. We're just... undefined."

"That sounds hard."

"It is."

Chris was quiet for a long moment. Then he shifted in his seat to face her more fully. "Here's what I know. I like you. I think you're extraordinary. And I'm willing to be patient while you figure out what you need to figure out. But I'm not going to be a placeholder. If you're in, I need you to be in. Not halfway. Not 'maybe.' In."

"And if I'm not ready for that?"

"Then I'll walk away. Not because I don't care, but because I care too much to settle for less than what we both deserve."

Kendra stared at him. This man who'd shown up at her competition. Who'd watched her from the sidelines without demanding anything. Who was now telling her exactly what he needed with a clarity that was both terrifying and refreshing.

"I don't know what to say," she whispered.

"You don't have to say anything tonight." He reached over and squeezed her hand—a brief, warm pressure that felt like both a promise and a release. "Think about it. Figure it out. And when you know, tell me."

Kendra nodded. She got out of the truck, her legs feeling unsteady. Chris waited until she was inside before pulling away, and she watched his taillights disappear around the corner.

Inside her apartment, she sat on the couch in the dark, her phone in her hand.

She thought about texting Addison. Thought about calling her. Thought about asking the question she'd been avoiding for months: *What are we doing?*

But she didn't.

Instead, she sat in the silence and thought about Chris. About the way he'd looked at her. About the way he'd asked for honesty instead of half-measures.

About the way that scared her more than anything Addison had ever made her feel.

Because Addison was undefined. Addison was possibility and potential and all the things that could be.

Chris was asking for something real.

And Kendra wasn't sure she knew how to choose between the dream and the man standing in front of her, asking her to be brave.

---

*End of Chapter 22*
