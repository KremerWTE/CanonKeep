# Chapter 4: The Expansion Offer

**POV: Jasper Barrett**

The call came at two PM on a Thursday.

Jasper was in a client meeting when his phone buzzed with a message from his assistant: *Board needs you. Conference call in 10 minutes. Priority.*

He excused himself, walked back to his office, closed the door. Ten minutes to mentally shift from client work to whatever crisis or opportunity the board had cooked up.

He made coffee. Reviewed his calendar. Tried to guess what this was about.

The board didn't do surprise calls unless something significant was happening.

At 2:10 exactly, his phone rang.

"Jasper Barrett."

"Jasper, it's Richard." Richard Caldwell, chairman of the BSS board. Former CEO of a Fortune 500, current advisor to three private equity firms, and the closest thing Jasper had to a mentor. "Thanks for jumping on. I've got the full board here."

"What's going on?"

"We want to talk about the future of BSS. Specifically, international expansion."

Jasper's pulse quickened. They'd discussed international expansion before—blue sky thinking, five-year plans, hypothetical scenarios. But the urgency in Richard's voice suggested this was no longer hypothetical.

"I'm listening."

"We've been approached by three separate clients in the past month asking if we have presence in Europe or Asia. All three are major accounts—two are current clients looking to expand the relationship, one is a potential new client worth eight figures in annual revenue. We're leaving money on the table by staying regional."

"Agreed," Jasper said. "But international expansion isn't simple. It's not just opening an office—it's dealing with different regulatory environments, hiring local talent, building new client networks from scratch."

"We know. Which is why we need someone who can do it right. Someone who understands the BSS culture, who has the strategic vision, and who has the operational experience to build offices in new markets."

Jasper felt the weight of where this was going.

"You want me to lead it," he said.

"We want you to *be* it," Richard corrected. "Global Expansion Director. You'd have full authority over international operations. Budget for four offices in the first eighteen months—London, Singapore, Dubai, and São Paulo. You pick the locations, the team, the strategy. You build it."

Four offices. Four continents. The kind of opportunity people spent their entire careers chasing.

"What's the timeline?" Jasper asked.

"We want to launch London by Q3. Singapore by Q4. Dubai and São Paulo in the first half of next year."

Six months to launch London. That meant scouting locations, hiring staff, establishing client relationships, building operations. All while maintaining oversight of Charlotte.

"That's aggressive," Jasper said.

"It has to be. We're not the only firm thinking about this. If we don't move now, someone else will claim the territory."

Jasper walked to his window, looked out at Charlotte. The city where he'd built BSS. Where he'd grown from a single consultant to a firm of thirty strategists. Where his wife worked, where his son was growing up, where his life was rooted.

"I need details," Jasper said. "Compensation, structure, support."

"Of course. We'll send over a full package by Monday. But I want to be clear about what we're offering, Jasper. This isn't just a promotion. This is a chance to build something global. To take everything you've done in Charlotte and scale it worldwide. This is legacy-level work."

Legacy. The word hung in the air.

"Can I think about it?" Jasper asked.

"Take the weekend. Review the package. Talk to Claire. But we need an answer by Tuesday. We're meeting with potential London office locations next week, and we need to know if you're leading this or if we need to look elsewhere."

The implied threat was gentle but clear: Say yes or watch someone else build the thing you're meant to build.

"I'll have an answer Tuesday," Jasper said.

After the call ended, he sat in silence, staring at his phone.

Everything he'd worked for. Everything he'd built. The board was offering to take it global, to transform BSS from a regional player to an international force.

And all it would cost was his presence at home.

---

He didn't tell Claire right away.

Not because he was hiding it, but because he needed to process it himself first. To understand what he was feeling before trying to explain it to someone else.

He went home at six, earlier than usual. Claire was in the kitchen making dinner while Lucas played on the floor with his blocks. The scene was so normal, so beautifully mundane, that Jasper felt something tighten in his chest.

"You're home early," Claire said, looking up from the cutting board. "Everything okay?"

"Yeah. Just wanted to see my family."

She smiled, but he could see the question in her eyes. Jasper didn't come home early without a reason.

He picked up Lucas, who immediately grabbed his tie and tried to put it in his mouth.

"How was your day?" Jasper asked.

"Good. Marcus and I finalized the plans for the new training program. Enrollment starts next month." Claire was the operations director at a CrossFit gym, managing everything from class schedules to coach training. She loved it—the problem-solving, the community building, the tangible impact.

"That's great."

"Lucas took four steps today. Four! He's going to be running by next month."

"He's going to be trouble."

"He already is." Claire took Lucas from him, set him back down with his blocks. "What's going on, Jas? You have your thinking face."

"I had a call with the board."

"And?"

He told her. The international expansion, the four offices, the timeline. He laid it out factually, trying not to color it with his own confusion.

Claire listened without interrupting. When he finished, she was quiet for a long moment.

"That's a big opportunity," she said finally.

"It is."

"But?"

"But it means I'm gone. A lot. London for three months to set up the office. Then Singapore, Dubai, São Paulo. Even after they're established, I'd be traveling constantly—putting out fires, managing operations, building client relationships."

"How much travel are we talking about?"

"Two weeks a month, minimum. Probably more in the first year."

Claire nodded slowly. "So Lucas's first birthday. His first words. His first day of daycare. You'd miss most of it."

"Probably."

She turned back to the cutting board, started chopping vegetables. Jasper recognized the movement—it was what she did when she needed to think, when she needed her hands busy while her mind worked.

"What do you want to do?" she asked.

"I don't know."

"Yes, you do. You just don't want to say it."

She was right. He did know. Part of him, the ambitious part that had built BSS from nothing, wanted this desperately. Wanted to prove he could operate on a global scale. Wanted to build something that would outlast him.

But another part—the part that had watched Lucas learn to crawl, that had held Claire after long days, that had built a life in Charlotte—wanted to stay. Wanted to be present. Wanted to be five feet from home.

"I want both," Jasper admitted. "I want the expansion and the family. I want to build something global and still make it to Lucas's first birthday. I want everything."

"You can't have everything."

"I know."

Claire set the knife down, turned to face him. "Here's what I know about you, Jasper Barrett. You're the most driven person I've ever met. When you see an opportunity, you take it. When you see a challenge, you tackle it. It's who you are. It's why you built this firm in the first place."

"But?"

"But you're also the man who came home from the hospital after Lucas was born and cried because you were so happy. You're the man who schedules client calls around dinner time because family meals matter to you. You're not just your ambition. You're also your presence."

"So what do I do?"

"You figure out which version of yourself you want to be right now. Because you can't be both. Not with this decision."

---

That night, after Lucas was asleep and Claire had gone to bed, Jasper sat in his home office reviewing the expansion package the board had sent over.

The numbers were staggering. Projected revenue growth of 300% over three years. Market penetration in four key regions. Salary package that would triple his current compensation plus equity incentives.

The strategic plan was solid. The board had done their homework—identified target markets, analyzed competition, built financial models. This wasn't a pipe dream. This was a real, executable plan for global expansion.

And they wanted him to lead it.

He thought about what Richard had said: *Legacy-level work.*

Was that what he wanted? To be remembered as the man who took BSS global? To build something that would last decades, that would employ hundreds of people, that would impact clients worldwide?

Or did he want to be remembered as the man who was present? Who didn't miss his son's milestones? Who built a successful company without sacrificing his family?

His phone buzzed. A text from Addison: *Just landed in New York. Heading to the hotel. Wish me luck tomorrow.*

Jasper smiled, typed back: *You don't need luck. You've got skill and preparation. Trust yourself.*

*Thanks, Dad. That means a lot.*

Dad. The word hit him differently now. He was Lucas's dad too. And being a dad meant more than providing financially. It meant being there. Being present. Being consistent.

He thought about his own father, who'd traveled constantly for work. Who'd built a successful career but missed most of Jasper's childhood. Who'd retired wealthy but distant, a stranger to his own children.

Jasper had promised himself he'd be different. He'd built BSS in Charlotte specifically so he could be present. So he could have dinner with his family, coach Lucas's future soccer team, be at school plays and parent-teacher conferences.

The global expansion would undo all of that.

But it would also fulfill a different promise—the one he'd made to himself about building something significant. About not settling for good when great was possible. About pushing beyond limits.

He couldn't have both promises.

He had to choose.

---

Friday morning, Jasper called Harper into his office.

She arrived with her expanded Miami proposal—now 120 pages, spiral-bound, with detailed appendices and financial models that would make a CFO weep with joy.

"This is impressive," Jasper said, flipping through it. "Really impressive."

"I did what you asked. Full implementation plan, org structure, ninety-day metrics, contingencies."

"You did more than I asked. This is partner-level work."

Harper sat up straighter. "Does that mean—"

"It means I'm taking it to the board next week. I can't promise anything, but if they approve it, the Miami office is yours to build."

"Jasper, I—thank you. Thank you for believing in me."

"You made it easy." He set the proposal down. "Can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

"Why are you doing this? Really. Not the professional answer about building something. The real answer."

Harper thought for a moment. "Because I'm afraid," she said finally. "I'm afraid that if I don't push myself now, I'll wake up at forty and realize I played it safe my whole life. I'll have a good career and good stability and nothing that's truly mine. I want to know what I'm capable of when I'm not playing it safe."

Jasper nodded. He understood that fear intimately.

"What about you?" Harper asked. "I heard rumors about a board offer. Global expansion?"

"Where'd you hear that?"

"People talk. Is it true?"

"It's true."

"Are you taking it?"

Jasper looked out his window at Charlotte. "I don't know yet."

"What's holding you back?"

"Lucas. Claire. The life I've built here."

Harper was quiet for a moment. "Can I tell you something my mom told me once? She said the hardest decisions aren't between good and bad. They're between good and good. Between two things you want, two paths that both matter."

"And how do you choose?"

"You figure out which one you can live without. Which regret you can carry."

---

That night, Claire found him on the back porch long after midnight.

She wrapped a blanket around both of them, sat next to him in the quiet darkness.

"Big decision?" she asked.

"The biggest," Jasper said.

"Tell me what you're thinking."

So he did. He told her about the expansion, about the opportunity, about the legacy. He told her about his fears—of missing Lucas's childhood, of becoming his own father, of choosing ambition over presence.

But he also told her about Harper's proposal, about Addison's New York case, about Kendra pushing herself in two different worlds. About the people he'd mentored who were now ready to lead.

"Maybe that's the real legacy," Jasper said. "Not the offices I build, but the people I develop. Not where BSS expands to, but who I help become their best selves."

"You can do both," Claire said. "Develop people and expand globally."

"Not well. Not the way I want to. Something suffers."

"So what's the answer?"

Jasper took a deep breath. "I think I need to say no to the global expansion. And I think I need to offer it to someone else."

"Who?"

"I don't know yet. Maybe we hire someone. Maybe we promote from within. But whoever it is, it won't be me." He turned to look at Claire. "Is that crazy? Turning down the opportunity of a lifetime?"

"Is it the opportunity of a lifetime? Or is this the life?" She gestured around them—at their home, at the baby monitor on the table, at the quiet Charlotte night. "Because this feels pretty significant to me."

Jasper pulled her closer. "What if I regret it?"

"What if you regret saying yes?"

They sat in silence, holding each other, weighing futures that would never exist against the present that did.

Finally, Jasper spoke. "I'm going to call Richard tomorrow. I'm going to tell him I'm honored, but I can't do it. I'm going to recommend we hire an external candidate to lead global expansion while I focus on developing the next generation of leadership here."

"You're sure?"

"No. But I'm choosing anyway."

Claire kissed him. "Then we'll make it the right choice."

---

Saturday morning, Jasper called Richard.

The conversation was harder than he'd expected. Richard pushed back, tried to negotiate—what if Jasper did just London? What if he limited travel to one week a month? What if they hired a deputy?

But Jasper held firm. "I can't do this the way it deserves to be done, not while being the father and husband I want to be. It's not fair to BSS, and it's not fair to my family. You need someone who can go all in."

"You're making a mistake," Richard said. "Opportunities like this don't come twice."

"Maybe not. But I'd rather make this mistake than the other one."

After he hung up, Jasper felt a strange mix of relief and loss. Relief that the decision was made. Loss for the path not taken.

He found Claire in the nursery, changing Lucas's diaper.

"I did it," he said. "I called Richard. I said no."

Claire looked up, studied his face. "How do you feel?"

"Terrified. Relieved. Sad. Certain." He picked up Lucas, held him close. "Mostly certain."

"That's enough."

Lucas grabbed Jasper's nose, giggled. Such a simple moment. Such a profound moment.

This was what he'd chosen. Not conference rooms in London or client dinners in Singapore. This. Right here. Five feet from home.

"I need to figure out the Miami situation with Harper," Jasper said. "If I'm staying in Charlotte, I can support her expansion better. Maybe that becomes our growth strategy—regional expansion instead of global. Miami first, then Atlanta, then DC."

"Building the team instead of building the empire."

"Something like that."

Claire took Lucas back, set him down in his crib. "You know what I love about you? You could have built the empire. You have the skill, the drive, the opportunity. But you're choosing something else. Something harder to measure but more important."

"Being present."

"Being present."

Jasper looked at his son, already drifting off to sleep. In a few years, Lucas would be playing soccer, starting school, asking questions about the world. And Jasper would be there. Not perfectly—he'd still travel, still work long hours, still struggle with balance.

But he'd be there. Present. Engaged. Five feet from home.

That was the legacy he wanted.

Not offices in four continents, but moments in one house. Not global expansion, but deep presence. Not everything, but enough.

He put his arm around Claire, and together they watched their son sleep, choosing this moment over all the others that might have been.

"No regrets?" Claire whispered.

Jasper smiled. "Ask me in twenty years."

"I will."

"And I'll tell you the same thing I'm telling you now: I chose what mattered most."

Outside, Charlotte hummed with weekend life. Inside, the Barrett family stood together, choosing presence over ambition, home over empire, enough over everything.

It was the hardest decision Jasper had ever made.

It was also the easiest.

Because when he looked at Lucas sleeping peacefully, at Claire standing beside him, at the life they'd built together, he knew one thing with absolute certainty:

He was already living his legacy.

The expansion could wait.

This moment couldn't.
