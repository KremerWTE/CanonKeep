# CHAPTER 51: NYC Just Us

**POV: Addison**
**Location: New York City**

The client meeting goes exactly as planned.

Addison, Jasper, and Kendra arrive in Manhattan on a Tuesday morning. The client is a hedge fund manager worried about industrial espionage. Routine case. BSS has handled dozens like it.

They spend day one in meetings. Jasper leads, Addison handles the technical details, and Kendra provides the security assessment. By 6 PM, they've outlined a comprehensive protection plan. The client is happy. They schedule a follow-up for tomorrow.

That night, the three of them grab dinner at a steakhouse near their hotel. The conversation is easy. Work talk, mostly. Jasper mentions the POH alliance. Kendra talks about her training plan for next season. Addison is quiet, tired from the day.

They're back at the hotel by 10 PM. Early night. Professional.

Addison is brushing her teeth when her phone buzzes. A text from Jasper: *Can you come to my room?*

She rinses and walks down the hall. Jasper's door is open. He's on the phone, looking stressed.

He waves her in and finishes the call. When he hangs up, he says, "Chicago."

"What about it?"

"Situation with a client. Major breach. They need someone on site immediately."

Addison's stomach sinks. "When?"

"Tonight. I'm booking a flight now."

"Do you need me to come?"

"No. You and Kendra can handle the follow-up tomorrow. It's just a formality at this point."

Addison nods. "Okay. We've got it."

Jasper grabs his bag and starts packing. "I'll be back by Thursday. Friday at the latest. You two finish up here, then head home."

"Got it."

He pauses. "You're sure you're good with this?"

"Jasper, I've been doing this for five years. I can handle a follow-up meeting."

"I know. I just—" He shakes his head. "Never mind. You've got this."

Thirty minutes later, Jasper is in an Uber to the airport.

Addison stands in the hotel hallway, staring at Kendra's door.

It's just the two of them now.

Just us.

She goes back to her room and tries to sleep.

---

Day two starts normal.

Addison and Kendra meet for breakfast in the hotel restaurant. Coffee, eggs, professional small talk. They review the follow-up presentation. Everything is ready.

The meeting is at 2 PM. It goes smoothly. The client signs off on the plan. Handshakes all around. They're done by 3:30.

"That's it?" Kendra says as they walk out of the building.

"That's it."

"We flew to New York for a ninety-minute meeting."

"Welcome to consulting."

Kendra laughs. "What do we do now?"

"Technically? Nothing. We're not flying back until tomorrow morning."

"So we have the rest of the day."

"And the evening."

They stand on the sidewalk, the city buzzing around them. It's a beautiful October afternoon. The kind of day that makes New York feel electric.

"Want to explore?" Kendra asks.

Addison hesitates. This is dangerous. She knows it. Being alone with Kendra in New York with nothing to do and no agenda.

But she says, "Sure."

---

They spend the afternoon wandering.

Central Park. The Met. A coffee shop in the West Village. They don't talk about work. They talk about everything else. Books. Movies. Childhood memories. Kendra tells a story about her dad teaching her to change a tire when she was twelve. Addison talks about the first time she realized she wanted to work in security.

It's easy. Too easy.

By the time the sun starts to set, they're in SoHo, walking past art galleries and boutiques.

"I'm starving," Kendra says.

"Me too."

They find a small Italian place tucked down a side street. Candles on the tables. Exposed brick walls. The kind of place that feels intimate without trying.

They order pasta and wine. One glass turns into two. Two turns into a bottle.

"Can I ask you something?" Kendra says, halfway through her third glass.

"Sure."

"Do you ever feel like you're living two lives?"

Addison looks at her. "What do you mean?"

"I don't know. Like, there's the version of you that everyone sees. The professional. The leader. The person who has it all together. And then there's the version that's just... you. The one that doesn't have it all figured out."

Addison is quiet for a long moment. "Yeah. I feel like that all the time."

"Which one is real?"

"Both. Neither. I don't know."

Kendra nods slowly. "I feel that way about Chris."

Addison's chest tightens. "What do you mean?"

"I love him. I do. But sometimes I feel like I'm playing a role. The girlfriend. The supportive partner. And I'm good at it. But it's not... I don't know. It's not all of me."

"Does he know that?"

"I don't think so. I don't think I know it most of the time." Kendra drains her glass. "God, I shouldn't be saying this."

"It's okay."

"Is it?"

Addison doesn't answer.

They finish dinner. The bill comes. Addison pays before Kendra can argue.

Outside, it's started to rain. Not heavy, just a light drizzle. They don't have an umbrella.

"Should we get a cab?" Kendra asks.

"Hotel's only ten blocks. We could walk."

"In the rain?"

"Why not?"

Kendra grins. "Okay."

They walk.

The rain picks up. By the time they're halfway back, they're both soaked. Kendra's hair is plastered to her face. Addison's jacket is dripping.

They're laughing. Addison doesn't know why. It's not that funny. But they can't stop.

Three blocks from the hotel, Kendra grabs Addison's arm and pulls her into a doorway.

"Wait," Kendra says, breathless. "I need to—"

She doesn't finish the sentence.

She kisses Addison.

For a moment, Addison freezes. Her brain screams at her to stop. To pull away. To think about Chris, about consequences, about all the reasons this is a terrible idea.

But her body doesn't listen.

She kisses Kendra back.

It's not gentle. It's desperate and messy and everything they've been avoiding for months. Maybe years.

When they finally pull apart, they're both breathing hard.

"We can't," Addison whispers.

"I know."

"This is—"

"I know."

But neither of them moves.

The rain pounds on the awning above them. The city roars around them. And for one perfect, terrible moment, nothing else exists.

"My room," Kendra says.

Addison should say no. She should walk away. She should be the responsible one.

Instead, she nods.

---

They barely make it through the door.

Addison has been with women before. She's not new to this. But this — with Kendra — is different. It's not just physical. It's everything. All the tension. All the unspoken words. All the moments they've carefully avoided.

It's reckless and stupid and perfect.

Afterward, they lie in the dark, not speaking.

Addison stares at the ceiling, her mind racing. What did they just do? What happens now? What about Chris? What about work? What about everything?

Kendra breaks the silence. "Are you okay?"

"I don't know."

"Me neither."

"We should talk about this."

"I know."

But they don't. They just lie there, tangled in hotel sheets, listening to the rain.

Eventually, Addison falls asleep.

When she wakes up at 6 AM, Kendra is still asleep beside her. Her face is peaceful. Vulnerable.

Addison's chest aches.

She slips out of bed as quietly as she can. Gets dressed. Writes a note: *Went to grab coffee. See you at breakfast.*

She leaves it on the nightstand and escapes to her own room.

---

Day three is agony.

They meet for breakfast like nothing happened. Professional. Polite. Kendra doesn't bring it up. Addison doesn't either.

They check out of the hotel. Grab a cab to the airport. Go through security. Wait at the gate.

Kendra reads a book. Addison stares at her laptop without seeing it.

On the plane, Kendra takes the window seat. Addison takes the aisle. There's an empty seat between them. It feels like a canyon.

Three hours to Charlotte. Addison spends the entire flight pretending to work.

When they land, they collect their bags in silence.

"Do you need a ride?" Kendra asks.

"No. I've got my car here."

"Okay."

They stand there in baggage claim, people flowing around them.

"Addison—"

"Don't." Addison's voice is sharper than she intends. "Not here. Not now."

Kendra nods. "Okay."

"I'll see you at the office."

"Yeah. See you."

Addison walks away. She doesn't look back.

In her car, she grips the steering wheel and tries to breathe.

What did she just do?

She slept with Kendra. Her colleague. Her friend. Chris's girlfriend.

She cheated. Or helped Kendra cheat. Or both.

Her phone buzzes. A text from Jasper: *Made it back to Chicago. How'd the follow-up go?*

Addison types back: *Fine. Client signed off. Heading home now.*

Another buzz. This time from Chris: *Hey! How was NYC? Can't wait to hear about it.*

Addison stares at the message. Her stomach twists.

She doesn't respond.

She drives home in silence, the weight of what she's done pressing down on her like a physical thing.

---

That night, Addison lies in her own bed and stares at the ceiling.

Her phone is on the nightstand. She hasn't checked it in hours.

She knows she should call Kendra. They need to talk. They need to figure out what this means. What they're going to do.

But she can't. Not yet.

Instead, she closes her eyes and tries to sleep.

It doesn't work.

All she can see is Kendra. In the doorway. In the rain. In the dark.

All she can feel is the kiss. The moment everything changed.

She tells herself it was a mistake. A moment of weakness. Too much wine and too much proximity and too much of whatever has been building between them for months.

But deep down, she knows the truth.

It wasn't a mistake.

It was everything they've been avoiding.

And now there's no going back.

---

The next morning, Addison gets a text from Kendra: *We need to talk.*

Addison stares at it for five minutes before responding: *I know. Not yet. I need time.*

Kendra replies immediately: *Okay. But we can't avoid this forever.*

*I know.*

Addison puts her phone down and gets ready for work.

She showers. Gets dressed. Drinks coffee. Goes through the motions.

But inside, she's falling apart.

Because she knows Kendra is right.

They can't avoid this forever.

And when they do talk, everything is going to change.

Again.
