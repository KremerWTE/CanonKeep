# Chapter 11: The RCIA Study Buddy

**POV:** Addison Price
**Location:** Barrett Home, Oak Watch, Charlotte, NC
**Timeline:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

Addison's phone buzzed with a text from Grace at 3:47 PM on a Wednesday.

*Addie we're doing communion study tonight!!! Mom says you need help. I'm your helper. Come over after your boring work!!!*

Addison stared at the message, caught between amusement and terror. She'd been to exactly two RCIA classes—both of which had left her brain buzzing with theological concepts she only half understood—and now Grace Barrett, age eight, had apparently decided she was qualified to be a teaching assistant.

She typed back: *Your mom put you up to this?*

Three dots appeared immediately, then: *No I put ME up to this. Mom said ok tho. Please please please???*

Addison sighed, glancing at the spreadsheet on her laptop. She had a crisis debrief with Jasper in twenty minutes, three client emails that needed responses by end of day, and a site assessment report she'd promised Harper by Friday.

She also had a small girl who thought studying saints was the most exciting thing in the world and who'd apparently appointed herself as Addie's personal RCIA coach.

*I'll be there at 6,* she typed.

The response was immediate: *YESSSSSS!!!! I'm making flashcards!!!!*

Addison closed her laptop, shaking her head. Flashcards. Of course there would be flashcards.

---

The Barrett house smelled like garlic and tomatoes when Addison arrived.

Elena opened the door with Lucas on her hip, both of them dusted with what looked like flour. "Hey! You're early."

"Crisis debrief ran short," Addison said, stepping inside. "What's the flour situation?"

"Grace decided we needed homemade breadsticks to go with study time." Elena gestured vaguely toward the kitchen, where Addison could hear Grace's voice narrating something about "the perfect twist."

"Should I be worried?"

"Only moderately," Elena said. "She's been watching cooking videos on YouTube. It's a whole thing."

Addison followed Elena into the kitchen, where Grace stood on a step stool at the counter, hands covered in dough, tongue poking out in concentration as she attempted to twist a rope of bread into something that vaguely resembled a breadstick.

"Addie!" Grace's face lit up. "Look! I'm making study snacks!"

"I see that," Addison said, dropping her bag on a chair. "Very professional."

"Sister Margaret says studying is easier with snacks." Grace held up a misshapen breadstick. "This one's the Apostles' Creed. See how it's all twisted? That's because the Creed has lots of parts that all connect."

Addison blinked. "That's... actually a pretty good metaphor."

"I know." Grace set the breadstick on a baking sheet with the others. "Mom, can we put them in now?"

"Five more minutes," Elena said, wiping Lucas's hands. "Let Addie settle in first."

Grace nodded seriously, climbing down from the stool and grabbing Addison's hand with her flour-covered one. "Come on. I set up the study space."

The study space, it turned out, was the living room floor. Grace had spread out a blanket, arranged throw pillows in a circle, and laid out what appeared to be an entire craft store's worth of supplies: markers, construction paper, glitter glue, stickers, and a stack of index cards already filled with Grace's careful printing.

"Okay," Grace said, settling cross-legged on the blanket and patting the space beside her. "Sit here. We're gonna start with saints."

Addison lowered herself onto a pillow, feeling oddly like she was back in elementary school. "Grace, I'm pretty sure the saints aren't covered until later in RCIA."

"But they're the BEST part," Grace said, undeterred. She picked up the first flashcard, which had a crayon drawing of what looked like a bird. A very sparkly, very blue bird. "This is the Holy Spirit."

Addison squinted at the drawing. "Is that... a chicken?"

"It's a DOVE," Grace said, deeply offended. "The Holy Spirit is a dove. But I made it blue and sparkly because that's more interesting."

"Can't argue with that logic."

Grace flipped to the next card. This one showed a crude drawing of a piece of bread with a smiley face. "And this is the Eucharist. It's Jesus but also bread but also Jesus. It's confusing, but Sister Margaret says that's okay because it's a mystery."

"The Eucharist has a smiley face," Addison observed.

"Because Jesus wants us to be happy," Grace said matter-of-factly. "Duh."

Addison felt something in her chest loosen—the tight, controlled thing that had been wound up since she'd walked into Father Thomas's classroom and realized she'd signed up for something she didn't fully understand.

This. This she could do.

Theology via eight-year-old crayon drawings and smiley-face bread.

"Alright," Addison said, settling in more comfortably. "Hit me with the saints."

Grace beamed.

---

For the next hour, Grace quizzed Addison on everything she'd learned in her First Communion prep class—which, as it turned out, was a chaotic blend of actual theology and pure childhood imagination.

"Okay, this one," Grace said, holding up a card with a stick figure surrounded by what appeared to be dogs. "Who is it?"

Addison studied the drawing. "Saint Francis of Assisi?"

"YES!" Grace pumped her fist. "He talked to animals."

"Right. Animals."

Grace grinned mischievously. "And dragons."

Addison paused. "I'm pretty sure Saint Francis didn't talk to dragons."

"But he COULD have," Grace insisted. "Dragons are animals."

"Dragons aren't real, Gracie."

"You don't know that. Maybe they were real in Italy back then."

"I'm confident they weren't."

"Well, I'm confident they were," Grace said stubbornly. "And Saint Francis would've talked to them if they were because he loved ALL animals. Even scary ones."

Addison opened her mouth to argue, then stopped. Because honestly? The idea of Saint Francis calmly negotiating with a dragon was kind of perfect.

"Fine," she said. "Saint Francis talked to dragons."

Grace's face split into a triumphant grin. "I KNEW you'd see it my way."

Elena appeared in the doorway, leaning against the frame with her arms crossed and an expression that was pure maternal amusement. "How's it going?"

"Addie agrees that Saint Francis talked to dragons," Grace announced.

"Does she now."

"It's a theological gray area," Addison said seriously.

Elena's mouth twitched. "I'm sure Father Thomas will appreciate that interpretation."

Grace was already moving on to the next card—this one featuring what looked like a woman with a sword. "Saint Joan of Arc. She was a warrior."

"I know this one," Addison said. "She led armies, heard voices, got burned at the stake."

"And she wore ARMOR," Grace added. "Sister Margaret showed us a picture. She looked so cool."

"She was pretty cool," Addison agreed.

Grace studied her for a moment, head tilted. "You'd be good at being a warrior saint."

"Oh yeah?"

"Yeah. You're strong like Joan. And you fight bad guys. Not with swords, but with words and plans and stuff." Grace set the card down. "That's even better than swords sometimes."

Addison felt her throat tighten unexpectedly. She glanced at Elena, who was watching with soft eyes, then back at Grace.

"Thanks, Gracie," she managed.

"You're welcome." Grace pulled out another card. "Okay, next one. This is Saint Cecilia. She's the patron saint of music."

They went through a dozen more saints—some accurate, some wildly creative interpretations that would probably make Sister Margaret's eye twitch. Grace mixed in questions about sacraments, prayers, and random bits of Bible trivia, all delivered with the solemn importance of a child who took her teaching assistant role Very Seriously.

At some point, the breadsticks came out of the oven—golden and uneven and smelling amazing. Grace insisted they take a "study snack break," so they moved to the kitchen table, where Jasper had appeared with Lucas and was attempting to feed the baby pureed sweet potatoes.

"How's the studying?" Jasper asked, glancing between Addison and Grace.

"Addie's doing really good," Grace reported. "She only got three wrong."

"What did you get wrong?" Jasper asked, amused.

"Apparently I didn't know that Saint Patrick drove snakes out of Ireland using a magic stick," Addison said dryly.

"Staff," Grace corrected. "It was a staff, not a stick. And it was probably magic. How else would the snakes leave?"

"I'm not touching that one," Jasper said, wisely returning his attention to Lucas.

Elena set glasses of water on the table, then settled into the chair beside Addison. "Grace, why don't you show Addie your favorite prayer?"

Grace's whole face lit up. "Ooh! Yes!" She scrambled off her chair and ran to the living room, returning moments later with a laminated card that looked professionally made—gold edges, ornate script, an image of Mary at the top.

"This is the Hail Mary," Grace said, handing it to Addison. "We say it every night before bed. It's Mommy's favorite."

Addison looked at the card, then at Elena, who smiled softly.

"My grandmother taught it to me," Elena said. "Her grandmother taught it to her. It's been in our family for generations."

Grace leaned against Addison's chair, pointing at the words. "Can you read it?"

Addison cleared her throat. "*Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou amongst women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus.*"

"Now the second part," Grace prompted.

"*Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.*"

The room went quiet. Lucas made a small sound, and Jasper murmured something soothing. Elena's hand found Addison's under the table, squeezing gently.

"That's really pretty," Grace said. "Don't you think it's pretty, Addie?"

"Yeah," Addison said, voice rough. "It's really pretty."

"When you get baptized, you'll say it with us every night," Grace said confidently. "And we can be prayer buddies."

"Prayer buddies," Addison repeated.

"Yeah. Like study buddies, but for praying." Grace took the card back, tracing the image of Mary with one finger. "Sister Margaret says Mary watches over everyone. Especially people who are scared or sad."

Addison felt something crack open in her chest—something she'd kept locked down for years, buried under work and walls and the carefully constructed armor of competence.

"Gracie," she said quietly. "What if I mess this up?"

Grace looked up at her, eyes wide and serious. "Mess up what?"

"All of it. RCIA. Being your sponsor. Being good enough for this."

Grace set the prayer card down, then climbed into Addison's lap—all knobby knees and bony elbows and fierce, uncomplicated love.

"Addie," she said patiently, like she was explaining something obvious to a very slow adult. "You can't mess it up. You're already family. This just makes it official."

Addison wrapped her arms around Grace, holding on tighter than she probably should. Over Grace's head, she met Elena's eyes—saw understanding there, and encouragement, and something that looked a lot like pride.

"Okay," Addison whispered. "Okay."

Grace pulled back just enough to look at her. "Plus, I'm your helper. And I'm REALLY good at helping."

Addison laughed, wet and shaky. "You really are."

"I know." Grace kissed Addison's cheek—sticky with breadstick crumbs and complete confidence—then scrambled down. "Okay! Now we do the fun part. We're gonna make a poster about your favorite saint."

"Grace—"

"We have glitter glue," Grace said, already pulling out construction paper. "And stickers. You can pick anyone you want. Even dragon-talking Francis."

Addison looked helplessly at Jasper, who was grinning. "Don't look at me. You signed up for this."

"I signed up for RCIA," Addison said. "Not an eight-year-old craft party."

"They're basically the same thing," Elena said, completely unhelpful.

Grace was already spreading supplies across the table, humming the tune to some hymn Addison half-recognized. Lucas babbled along, waving a spoon. Jasper leaned back in his chair, watching his family with that soft expression he'd been wearing more lately—the one that said he was exactly where he wanted to be.

And Addison, sitting in the middle of it all with glitter glue and prayer cards and a small girl who thought she hung the moon, felt something she hadn't let herself feel in a very long time.

Hope.

Not the strategic, calculated kind she used at work. The real kind. The terrifying kind that meant you believed things could actually be good, that people could actually stay, that you might actually be worth loving.

Grace pushed a piece of purple construction paper toward her. "This one's your color. Now pick your saint."

Addison picked up a gold marker, considering. "What about Joan of Arc?"

"YES," Grace said, punching the air. "Warrior saint for warrior Addie."

"But—" Addison added, starting to sketch. "We're giving her a dragon."

Grace's eyes went wide with delight. "REALLY?"

"Really. Because if Saint Francis can talk to dragons, Saint Joan can definitely ride one into battle."

Grace dissolved into giggles, and Addison found herself laughing too—deep and genuine and completely free.

For three hours, they made the most theologically questionable saint poster in the history of Catholic education. Joan of Arc riding a purple dragon, surrounded by sparkly stars and inspirational quotes that Grace made up on the spot. ("Be brave! Fight bad guys! Trust Jesus!")

By the time Addison left, Grace was half-asleep on the couch, covered in glitter, the poster carefully set aside to dry. Elena walked Addison to the door, Lucas drowsing on her shoulder.

"Thank you," Elena said quietly.

"For what? Grace did all the teaching."

"For letting her." Elena shifted Lucas, smiling. "For showing up. For being willing to try this even though it scares you."

Addison looked back at the living room—at Grace curled up with her prayer card, at the chaos of craft supplies and breadstick crumbs, at the life Elena and Jasper had built that was messy and loud and so full of love it hurt to look at sometimes.

"I'm not good at this," Addison said.

"At what?"

"Family. Faith. Believing in things I can't control or measure or fix."

Elena reached out, squeezing her arm. "You're better at it than you think. And you're getting better every day."

Addison nodded, not trusting her voice.

"Same time next week?" Elena asked.

"Yeah," Addison managed. "Same time."

She made it to her car before the tears came—quiet and overwhelming and somehow not sad at all.

Grace had given her flashcards. Sparkly, ridiculous, theologically suspect flashcards featuring the Holy Spirit as a blue chicken and the Eucharist with a smiley face.

And somehow, those flashcards felt like the most sacred thing Addison had ever held.

She pulled out her phone, snapping a picture of the stack Grace had sent home with her. The top card showed a simple cross, drawn in crayon, with words in Grace's careful handwriting:

*God loves you. Even when you don't know how to love yourself yet.*

Addison stared at it for a long time, then carefully tucked the cards into her bag.

Next Wednesday, she'd go back to RCIA. She'd listen to Father Thomas explain transubstantiation and sacramental theology. She'd take notes and ask questions and probably argue about at least three different doctrinal points.

But first, she'd come back here. To this house, this family, this small girl who believed Addison was worthy of being a warrior saint.

And maybe, slowly, Addison would start to believe it too.

**End of Chapter 11**
