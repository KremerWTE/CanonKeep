# Chapter 2: Harper's Miami Pitch

**POV: Harper Steele**

Harper had been working on the proposal for three weeks.

Not during work hours—she was too smart to let her regular caseload slip. But late at night in her apartment, early mornings before the gym, weekends when everyone else was unwinding. She'd built spreadsheets, mapped market opportunities, researched competitors. She'd called contacts in Florida, talked to commercial real estate brokers, even flown down to Miami for a weekend to scout locations.

Now it all came down to this: a sixty-page proposal document, spiral-bound, sitting in the center of Jasper Barrett's conference table.

Harper sat across from him, hands folded, trying to project confidence she didn't entirely feel.

"Miami," Jasper said, picking up the proposal. He didn't open it yet, just held it, weighing it like he was weighing the entire idea.

"Miami," Harper confirmed.

"Walk me through it."

This was it. The moment she'd been rehearsing for three weeks. Harper took a breath and began.

"BSS is a Charlotte firm. We're strong here—great reputation, solid client base, consistent growth. But we're regional. If we want to compete with the big strategy firms, we need presence beyond North Carolina."

"We have clients in other states," Jasper pointed out.

"We have clients we serve remotely. That's different than having boots on the ground. Physical presence signals commitment. It signals permanence." Harper leaned forward. "Miami is the perfect first expansion market. It's a major financial center with Caribbean and Latin American money flowing through it. Hedge funds, family offices, multinational corporations. The wealthy client base is there. The strategic need is there. But the competition is mostly focused on New York or international."

Jasper opened the proposal, began flipping through pages. "You've done your homework."

"Page twelve has the market analysis. Miami-Dade County has over two hundred firms managing assets above fifty million. That's two hundred potential clients within a twenty-mile radius. Page eighteen shows comparable firm expansion case studies. Page twenty-four is the financial model—startup costs, revenue projections, break-even timeline."

Jasper read in silence. Harper watched his face for reactions, but he was unreadable. This was Jasper at his most analytical, processing information, running scenarios.

After what felt like an eternity, he looked up.

"This is ambitious."

"It's necessary," Harper said. "BSS is at an inflection point. We can stay regional and do well, or we can expand and become something bigger. This is how we become something bigger."

"And you want to run it."

It wasn't a question, but Harper answered anyway. "Yes."

"Why?"

The question hung in the air. Harper had expected it, but it still made her pause. This was the real test—not the numbers or the market analysis, but the why.

"Because I want to build something," she said finally. "I've spent two years executing other people's strategies. Learning, growing, getting better. But I can see the path from here, and it's good but it's... limited. Analyst to strategist to senior strategist. Ten years, maybe fifteen, to make partner. And that's fine. That's a great career."

"But?"

"But I don't want fine. I don't want to just climb someone else's ladder. I want to build my own." Harper met Jasper's eyes. "You understand that. You built this firm from nothing. You took the risk. I'm asking for the chance to do the same thing—not on my own, but as part of BSS. Let me build the Miami office. Let me prove I can do more than execute your vision. Let me create my own."

Jasper set the proposal down. "You know what you're asking for."

"I do."

"You'd be leaving Charlotte. Leaving the support system here. Moving to a city where you don't have relationships, don't have established networks. You'd be building from scratch."

"I've already started building." Harper pulled out her phone, opened her notes. "I've identified three potential office locations. I've had preliminary conversations with four target clients. I've researched hiring—there are at least a dozen qualified strategists in Miami who might be interested in joining a growing firm."

"You've been busy."

"I told you. I'm ready for this."

Jasper stood, walked to the window. Outside, Charlotte stretched out beneath them—the city they'd both chosen, the city where BSS had grown. Harper wondered what he was thinking. If he saw her request as ambition or arrogance. If he saw potential or risk.

"You know I'm getting another offer," Jasper said quietly.

Harper's stomach dropped. "What kind of offer?"

"The board wants me to lead global expansion. London, Singapore, Dubai, São Paulo. They want BSS to become a truly international firm."

"That's incredible."

"It's complicated." Jasper turned back to face her. "If I take it, I'm gone more than I'm home. I'm building offices in four different countries while trying to raise a seven-month-old. Claire and I are still figuring out what that looks like."

Harper processed this. "You're thinking about saying no."

"I'm thinking about what matters." Jasper returned to the table, picked up her proposal again. "This is good work, Harper. Really good. The analysis is solid, the financial model is realistic, the strategy is sound. You've thought through the details."

"But?"

"But I need you to understand what you're signing up for. This isn't just opening an office and watching it grow. You'll be working eighty-hour weeks. You'll be pitching clients who've never heard of us. You'll be hiring people, managing operations, dealing with problems I can't help you with because I'm a thousand miles away. And if it fails—if the Miami office doesn't hit projections, if clients don't materialize, if the market doesn't respond—that's on you."

"I understand."

"Do you?" Jasper's expression was serious now. "Because I've been there. I've built something from nothing. And it's exhilarating, but it's also terrifying. There are nights when you can't sleep because you're thinking about payroll. Days when you question every decision. Moments when you wonder if you've made a massive mistake."

"Did you?" Harper asked. "Make a mistake?"

Jasper smiled. "No. But I also knew what I was getting into. So I'm asking you—really asking you—are you ready for this? Not just professionally. Personally. Are you ready to sacrifice everything else for the next two years while you build this thing?"

Harper thought about her apartment in Charlotte. Her routine. Her friends. The comfort of knowing how things worked, where things were, who to call when she needed help.

Then she thought about the alternative. About staying in Charlotte, following the path, doing good work but never taking the leap. About being sixty years old and wondering what would have happened if she'd just tried.

"I'm ready," she said.

Jasper studied her for a long moment. Then he nodded.

"Write me a full proposal."

Harper blinked. "That is a full proposal. Sixty pages. Market analysis, financial models, implementation timeline—"

"That's a feasibility study," Jasper corrected. "I need a proposal. What's the org structure? Who are the first three hires? What's the ninety-day plan? What metrics are you committing to? What happens if you miss them? How are you going to handle HR, legal, accounting in a new state? What's the marketing strategy? The client acquisition plan?"

Harper's mind raced. "How detailed?"

"Detailed enough that if I drop it on the board's desk, they can make a decision without asking questions. Detailed enough that you've thought through every contingency. Detailed enough that I know you're serious."

"How long do I have?"

"Two weeks."

"Two weeks?" Harper tried to keep the panic out of her voice. "Jasper, that's—"

"Ambitious. But so is opening an office in Miami." He handed her the proposal back. "This is good foundational work. Now take it to the next level. Show me you can think like a leader, not just a strategist."

Harper took the document, her mind already spinning with everything she'd need to add. Org structure. Hiring plan. Ninety-day metrics. Contingencies.

"What if I nail this?" she asked. "What if I give you exactly what you need?"

"Then we have a serious conversation about Miami." Jasper's expression softened. "I believe you can do this, Harper. But belief isn't enough. You have to prove it. To me, to the board, and most importantly, to yourself."

---

Harper spent the rest of the day in a daze.

She sat through client meetings, nodded at appropriate times, took notes she barely registered. Her mind was elsewhere, building org charts, mapping out hiring timelines, calculating metrics.

By five PM, she couldn't take it anymore. She packed up her laptop and headed out, ignoring the surprised looks from colleagues who were used to her staying until seven or eight.

She needed space. She needed air. She needed to think.

She ended up at a coffee shop near her apartment, a local place with mismatched furniture and local art on the walls. She ordered an Americano, claimed a corner table, and opened her laptop.

The enhanced proposal stared back at her—sixty pages that now felt woefully inadequate.

She started a new document. Titled it: *BSS Miami Expansion - Full Implementation Proposal.*

Then she stared at the blank page.

Where did she even start? Org structure seemed logical. She'd need a local team—at least two junior strategists to start, maybe an operations coordinator. But who? She didn't know the Miami talent pool beyond what she'd seen on LinkedIn.

Maybe hiring plan should come first. Define the roles, then figure out structure.

Or maybe client acquisition. Without clients, nothing else mattered.

Harper rubbed her temples. This was different than building a case study or strategy document. This was building a business. A whole functioning operation that would either succeed or fail based on her decisions.

Her phone buzzed. A text from Kendra: *Drinks tonight? I'm dying from deadlifts and need someone to complain to.*

Harper smiled despite her stress. She typed back: *Can't. Buried in work. Rain check?*

Kendra's response came immediately: *You're always buried in work. Come anyway. One drink.*

Harper looked at her blank document, at the blinking cursor mocking her uncertainty.

One drink wouldn't hurt.

---

They met at a wine bar in South End, a trendy spot with exposed brick and an impressive selection. Kendra was already there when Harper arrived, sitting at the bar in workout clothes, hair still damp from a shower.

"You look stressed," Kendra said by way of greeting.

"Observant as always." Harper slid onto the stool next to her, ordered a Pinot Noir.

"Big case?"

"Big opportunity." Harper filled her in on the Miami proposal, Jasper's challenge, the two-week deadline.

Kendra listened, sipping her beer. When Harper finished, she was quiet for a moment.

"So you're leaving Charlotte," Kendra said finally.

"If I pull this off, yeah."

"When. When you pull this off."

Harper smiled. "Optimistic."

"Realistic. You don't do anything half-way." Kendra set her beer down. "But are you sure about this? Miami's a long way from everyone."

"That's kind of the point. Build something that's mine."

"Your own office. Your own team. Your own client list." Kendra nodded slowly. "I get it. But you're also leaving behind everything you've built here. The relationships, the support system, the people who know you."

"I can come back. It's a two-hour flight."

"It's not the same. You know it's not the same."

Harper did know. She'd been trying not to think about it—about leaving Charlotte, leaving the Forge, leaving the people who'd become her family over the past two years.

"What would you do?" Harper asked. "If it were you?"

Kendra considered this. "I'd probably do exactly what you're doing. Chase the thing that scares me. Push myself beyond what's comfortable." She paused. "But I'd also make sure I knew what I was giving up. So when it gets hard—and it will get hard—I don't have regrets."

"You think I'll regret it?"

"I think you'll regret it if you don't try."

They sat in silence for a moment, watching the other patrons drift in and out of the bar. Young professionals, couples on dates, friends meeting after work. Life happening all around them.

"You're doing analyst work for BSS too, right?" Harper asked, changing the subject. "How's that going?"

Kendra's expression shifted—part pride, part exhaustion. "It's good. Challenging. I'm learning a lot about markets I never thought about."

"But?"

"But I'm also training for the CrossFit Open. Which means I'm at the gym at five AM, at the office by eight, working until six, then back to the gym for evening sessions. I'm running on fumes most days."

"That's not sustainable."

"Tell me about it." Kendra finished her beer. "But neither is giving up either thing. So I just keep going."

Harper studied her friend. Kendra had always been driven, but there was something different now. An intensity that bordered on obsession.

"You okay?" Harper asked.

"I'm great. Tired, but great." Kendra smiled, but it didn't quite reach her eyes. "We're all chasing something, right? You're chasing Miami. Addison's chasing New York. I'm chasing... whatever comes next."

"The Games?"

"Maybe. Or maybe just proof that I can do both. That I don't have to choose between the athlete and the analyst."

"You don't have to prove anything to anyone."

"Don't I?" Kendra signaled the bartender for another beer. "Everyone sees me as the athlete. The girl who does CrossFit. Even at BSS, even when I'm doing good work—that's still how people introduce me. 'This is Kendra, she's an athlete.' Like that's the only thing that matters."

"Is that what you want to change?"

"I want to be both. Fully. Not compartmentalized, not choosing, not compromising. Both."

Harper understood that desire more than Kendra probably realized. The need to be whole, not divided. To integrate all the parts of yourself instead of leaving pieces behind.

"We're ambitious idiots, aren't we?" Harper said.

Kendra laughed, a real laugh this time. "The most ambitious idiots in Charlotte."

They clinked glasses.

---

Harper left the wine bar an hour later feeling lighter. The conversation with Kendra had helped crystallize something she'd been circling around for weeks.

This wasn't just about opening an office or building a client list. It was about proving she could be more than what people saw. More than the analyst in the bullpen, more than the person executing someone else's vision.

She went home, made coffee even though it was nine PM, and opened her laptop.

The blank document was still waiting.

This time, Harper started typing.

*ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE*

*The BSS Miami office will launch with a lean, efficient team focused on rapid client acquisition and service excellence. Initial team composition:*

*1. Managing Director (Harper Steele) - Client relationships, strategic oversight, business development*
*2. Junior Strategist - Client work, market research, analytical support*
*3. Operations Coordinator - Administrative support, client services, office management*

She kept going, the words flowing now. Hiring criteria. Compensation structure. Reporting relationships. She built it all out, referencing what she knew from BSS Charlotte but adapting it for a startup environment.

By midnight, she had twenty new pages.

By two AM, she had forty.

At three-thirty, she crashed on her couch, laptop still open, document still growing.

When she woke at six, the first thing she did was keep writing.

Two weeks. She had two weeks to build a proposal so complete, so detailed, so undeniably solid that Jasper would have no choice but to say yes.

Harper looked at her progress—sixty pages had become ninety, with sections still to add. Financial models to refine. Client acquisition strategies to map. Contingency plans to develop.

It was overwhelming. It was terrifying.

It was exactly what she wanted.

She made more coffee and got back to work.
