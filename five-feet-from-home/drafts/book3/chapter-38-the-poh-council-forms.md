# Chapter 38: The POH Council Forms

**POV: Elena Barrett**

---

The Charlotte Compound looked like something out of a period drama—sprawling Georgian architecture set on forty acres of manicured grounds, with formal gardens that had been tended for three generations and an interior that managed to feel both stately and lived-in.

Elena had been here dozens of times over the years, mostly for the annual Palace of Honor galas that drew philanthropists and power players from across the Southeast. But today was different. Today, she was here not as a guest or even as an event planner, but as something she still didn't have adequate words for.

Architect. Leader. Builder of something new.

She walked through the main entrance, past the portrait gallery where centuries of Southern aristocracy stared down with varying degrees of judgment, and found her way to the library—a two-story room with floor-to-ceiling bookshelves, leather furniture arranged around a fireplace, and windows that overlooked the rose garden.

Lady Margaret Ashford stood near the fireplace, her silver hair caught in an elegant chignon, wearing a pale blue dress that probably cost more than most people's cars. She was in her early seventies, with the kind of presence that commanded attention without demanding it.

She turned when Elena entered, her expression warm. "Elena. Thank you for coming."

"Of course." Elena crossed to her, accepting the light embrace that was Lady Margaret's version of a hug. "Though I have to admit, I'm not entirely sure why I'm here."

Lady Margaret's eyes gleamed with something that might have been amusement. "Because, my dear, we're about to build something important. And I wanted you to help shape it from the beginning."

Before Elena could respond, the door opened and three more women entered.

Victoria Chen—Harper's mother, though the two couldn't have been more different in temperament. Where Harper was all sharp edges and controlled intensity, Victoria was graceful fluidity, a retired diplomat who'd spent thirty years navigating international crises with nothing but charm and strategic brilliance.

Catherine Laurent—wife of the Palace of Honor's treasurer, a former federal prosecutor turned nonprofit legal counsel, with the kind of quiet competence that made powerful people nervous.

And Sarah Whitmore—the youngest of the group at forty-eight, a former investment banker who'd walked away from Goldman Sachs to run a foundation supporting women's education across the developing world.

Elena knew all of them, had worked with each of them on various projects over the years. But she'd never seen them gathered in one place quite like this.

"Ladies," Lady Margaret said, gesturing to the seating arrangement. "Shall we?"

They settled into the comfortable chairs arranged in a loose circle. A tea service waited on the side table—fine china, tiny sandwiches, the kind of civilized presentation that felt like a callback to a different era.

Lady Margaret poured tea with practiced ease, then settled into her chair with the air of someone calling a meeting to order.

"Thank you all for coming on short notice," she said. "I wanted to gather you here today because the Palace of Honor is at a crossroads. For the better part of a century, we've operated as we always have—board meetings, annual galas, charitable giving structured around the same families who've supported us for generations."

She paused, looking at each woman in turn. "But the world has changed. The threats we face have changed. And if we're going to remain relevant—if we're going to actually make a difference rather than just throwing elegant parties—we need to change too."

Victoria leaned forward slightly. "You're talking about operational structure."

"I'm talking about leadership succession." Lady Margaret's gaze was steady. "The current board is aging. Half of our members are over seventy, and while I like to think we're all sharp as tacks, the truth is we're not the ones who will lead this organization into the next generation."

"So you want to identify successors," Catherine said.

"I want to formalize a leadership council. Not just advisors or committee members, but actual decision-makers who can shape the direction of the organization. Women who understand both the traditional values we represent and the modern challenges we need to address."

Sarah smiled slightly. "Women like us."

"Exactly like you." Lady Margaret set down her teacup. "I've already spoken to the board about this. They've agreed to create a formal POH Leadership Council—five seats, rotating three-year terms, with authority over programming, partnerships, and strategic direction. The existing board will maintain fiduciary oversight, but the operational vision will belong to the council."

Elena felt her heart rate kick up. This was bigger than she'd expected. Not just a committee or an advisory role, but actual institutional power.

"Who did you have in mind for the other seats?" Victoria asked.

Lady Margaret pulled out a leather portfolio and opened it to reveal a list of names, each one annotated with notes in her elegant handwriting.

"The four of you are my first choices," she said. "Victoria for your diplomatic experience and international networks. Catherine for your legal expertise and understanding of regulatory frameworks. Sarah for your foundation experience and fundraising acumen. And Elena..."

She looked at Elena with an expression that was difficult to read. "Elena because you understand how to build systems that actually work. Because you can move between worlds—the old guard and the new generation, the traditional and the innovative. And because you have vision."

Elena felt heat rise to her cheeks. "I'm honored. But I'm not even a formal member of the Palace. I've done event planning, some consulting work, but I'm not—"

"You're exactly what we need," Lady Margaret interrupted gently. "Which is why I want to propose something that may seem unconventional."

She pulled out another sheet of paper—this one showing an organizational chart with five names at the top.

Four of them were the women sitting in this room.

The fifth was Addison Price.

Elena's breath caught.

"Addison?" Victoria's eyebrows rose. "Jasper's business partner?"

"The same." Lady Margaret's voice was calm, matter-of-fact. "I know it's unusual. She's not Catholic. She's not married. She's not part of the traditional social circles that have historically led this organization. But she has something the rest of us don't."

"What's that?" Catherine asked, her tone curious rather than challenging.

"She has the ability to see systems clearly and rebuild them from scratch. She understands crisis management, strategic communications, operational efficiency. And she's not beholden to the way things have always been done because she's never been part of that system in the first place."

Lady Margaret looked at Elena. "I understand she's been attending RCIA. That she's exploring conversion."

Elena nodded slowly. "Yes. She started seven weeks ago. I'm her sponsor."

"Then she's on a journey toward the faith. That's enough for me." Lady Margaret set down the organizational chart. "But I want to be honest about what this will trigger. There will be resistance. Some of the old guard will object to having someone who isn't already Catholic in a leadership position. They'll say she doesn't understand our values, our traditions, our mission."

"And what do we say to that?" Sarah asked.

"We say that our mission is to support communities in crisis, to build institutions that serve rather than extract, to create pathways for flourishing in places where hope has been stripped away. And we say that Addison Price has spent her entire career doing exactly that." Lady Margaret's expression was fierce now, the Southern gentility giving way to something sharper. "We say that faith is important, but so is competence. And we need both."

The room was quiet for a long moment.

Then Victoria spoke. "I've worked with Addison on the BSS international projects. She's exceptional. Brilliant strategist, understands stakeholder management better than most diplomats I've met. If we're serious about operational transformation, she's exactly the kind of person we need."

"I agree," Catherine said. "Though I share Lady Margaret's concern about resistance. We'll need to manage that carefully."

Sarah looked at Elena. "What do you think? You know her better than any of us."

Elena took a breath, gathering her thoughts. "Addison is one of the most capable people I've ever met. She's also one of the most guarded. This kind of leadership role—particularly in an organization rooted in faith and community—would push her far outside her comfort zone."

"Is that a concern?" Lady Margaret asked.

"No. It's an asset." Elena met her gaze. "Addison is at her best when she's building something new. When she's taking chaos and creating structure. And she's also on a personal journey right now—toward faith, toward letting herself be part of something bigger than her work. This could be exactly what she needs."

"Then you support the nomination?" Victoria asked.

"I do. Completely." Elena felt certainty settle in her chest. "But I think we need to be clear about what we're asking of her. This isn't just a professional role. The POH is built on relationships, on trust, on a shared commitment to values that go beyond strategy. If we bring her in, we need to be prepared to support her through that transition."

"Agreed," Lady Margaret said. "Which is why I wanted you on the council, Elena. You'll be her advocate, her translator between worlds."

She looked around the circle. "So. Are we in agreement? Do we formally propose a five-member council with these names?"

One by one, they nodded.

"Then we have our founding council," Lady Margaret said. "Now comes the harder part—actually doing the work."

---

Over the next hour, they mapped out the vision.

The POH Leadership Council would meet monthly, alternating between in-person gatherings at the Compound and virtual sessions. They would have authority over three main areas: program development, strategic partnerships, and operational oversight.

Catherine outlined the legal framework, ensuring they had appropriate liability protection and governance structures. Sarah talked about fundraising strategy—how to maintain the traditional donor base while expanding to new demographics. Victoria described the international partnerships she'd been cultivating, opportunities for the POH to expand its mission beyond the Southeast.

And Elena found herself talking about integration—how to honor the organization's history while building something that could adapt to changing needs, how to maintain the values that made the POH unique while opening the doors to people who didn't fit the traditional mold.

"The tension," she said, thinking out loud, "is between preservation and transformation. We want to keep what's good about the Palace—the commitment to service, the focus on building institutions rather than just giving handouts, the understanding that sustainable change requires long-term investment. But we also need to acknowledge that the old model is too insular, too dependent on the same families and the same power structures."

"So how do we resolve that tension?" Sarah asked.

Elena thought about Addison sitting in that RCIA circle, asking hard questions about faith and belonging. Thought about Grace learning to navigate between her mother's world and her father's work. Thought about her own journey, building Maison Aurelia while raising children, learning to hold multiple identities at once.

"We don't resolve it," she said finally. "We live in it. We acknowledge that transformation is uncomfortable, that growth requires loss, that becoming something new means letting go of some parts of what we were. But we do it anyway, because the alternative is irrelevance."

Lady Margaret smiled—genuine and warm. "You see? This is exactly why I wanted you here."

The conversation shifted to practical matters. Timeline for the formal announcement. Communication strategy for the existing board. Plans for the first official council meeting.

But as they talked, Elena felt the weight of what they were building. This wasn't just another committee or advisory board. This was a fundamental restructuring of an institution that had operated the same way for nearly a century.

It was ambitious. It was risky. It was necessary.

---

After the meeting, Lady Margaret asked Elena to stay behind.

They stood in the library as the afternoon light slanted through the tall windows, casting geometric patterns across the Persian rugs.

"I want to talk to you about Addison," Lady Margaret said.

Elena tensed slightly. "Is there a concern?"

"Not a concern. A curiosity." Lady Margaret moved to the window, looking out at the rose garden. "You care about her. More than just professionally."

It wasn't a question, but Elena answered anyway. "Yes. She's become important to me. To our whole family."

"And this nomination—you're certain it's what she needs? Not just what the organization needs?"

Elena considered the question carefully. "I think they're connected. Addison has spent her whole life building walls, protecting herself through competence and control. This role would require her to be vulnerable, to trust other people, to invest in something that can't be managed through strategy alone. That terrifies her. But I also think it's exactly what she needs to grow."

Lady Margaret nodded slowly. "You're guiding her toward faith. Toward community. Toward belonging in a way she's never allowed herself."

"I'm trying to create space for her to choose it herself."

"That's good. That's the right approach." Lady Margaret turned from the window. "But I need you to be honest with me, Elena. Is there more to your relationship with Addison than mentorship?"

The question landed carefully, without judgment, but Elena felt her heart rate spike anyway.

She thought about deflecting, about giving a safe answer that wouldn't complicate things.

But Lady Margaret had been navigating complex relationships for seven decades. There was no point in lying to someone who could read people the way others read books.

"There might be," Elena said quietly. "It's complicated. I'm married to Jasper, and I love him completely. But Addison..." She paused, searching for words. "She fills spaces in my life that I didn't know were empty. She challenges me, supports me, sees me in ways that feel different from how anyone else sees me. And I think she's starting to feel the same way about me."

"Does Jasper know?"

"We haven't talked about it explicitly. But Jasper is many things, and oblivious isn't one of them. I think he knows something is shifting. I think he's... waiting to see what shape it takes before deciding how to respond."

Lady Margaret was quiet for a long moment. "The Palace of Honor has a complicated history with unconventional relationships. On one hand, we're rooted in traditional Catholic values—marriage, family, clear roles and expectations. On the other hand, we've always understood that human hearts are messy, that love doesn't follow neat categories, that grace means making space for complexity."

She moved closer to Elena. "I'm not going to tell you what to do with your personal life. That's between you, Jasper, and Addison—and God, if you're inclined toward that framework. But I will say this: if you bring Addison onto this council, her relationship with you will become part of the landscape. People will notice. They'll talk. And some of them will use it to undermine her authority."

"I know," Elena said. "I've thought about that."

"And you still want to move forward?"

"I do. Because Addison deserves this opportunity regardless of what's happening between us personally. She deserves to be seen for her gifts, her vision, her capacity to build something meaningful. I won't let my own complications limit what she can become."

Lady Margaret studied her for a long moment, then smiled. "You're stronger than you give yourself credit for, Elena. And wiser. I think you'll navigate this well—both the council work and the personal complexity."

"I hope so."

"Hope is good. But so is preparation." Lady Margaret moved toward the door. "Think about what you need to be successful in this role. What boundaries you need to set, what support you need to ask for, what conversations you need to have with the people who matter most. This council is going to demand a lot from all of us. Make sure you're building from a foundation that can sustain the weight."

Elena nodded, feeling the truth of the advice settle into her bones.

---

On the drive home, Elena called Jasper.

He answered on the second ring. "Hey. How'd it go?"

"Interesting. Complicated. Potentially transformative." She merged onto the highway, the late afternoon sun glinting off the windshield. "Lady Margaret wants to form a formal POH leadership council. She asked me to be part of it."

"Of course she did. You'd be perfect for that." His voice was warm, proud. "Who else is on the council?"

"Victoria Chen, Catherine Laurent, Sarah Whitmore. And..." She took a breath. "She wants to nominate Addison for the fifth seat."

The silence on the other end of the line stretched for several seconds.

"Addison," Jasper said finally. "Who isn't Catholic."

"Not yet. But she's in RCIA. She's exploring conversion."

"Because of you."

Elena heard something in his voice—not jealousy, exactly, but awareness. Recognition that the dynamics between the three of them were shifting in ways that couldn't be ignored much longer.

"Partly because of me," Elena admitted. "But also because she's searching for something. Meaning, community, a framework for understanding the world that goes beyond crisis management. This role could give her that. It could give her a place to belong."

"And what does it give you?" Jasper's voice was gentle, genuinely curious.

Elena thought about the question. "A partner in building something important. A way to use the gifts I've been developing through Maison Aurelia in service of something with deeper purpose. And..." She paused. "A way to help Addison become who she's meant to be. Which matters to me. A lot."

"I know it does." Another pause. "Elena, we need to talk. About what's happening with you and Addison. About what it means for us, for our family, for the life we're building."

"I know. You're right." Her hands tightened on the steering wheel. "But not on the phone. This is a conversation we need to have face to face."

"Agreed. This weekend? After Grace goes to bed?"

"Yes. This weekend."

They talked for a few more minutes about mundane things—Lucas's pediatrician appointment, Grace's upcoming school play, the BSS case that was consuming Harper's attention. The kind of normal conversation that felt like solid ground beneath shifting plates.

When they hung up, Elena felt the weight of what was coming. The conversation she'd been avoiding, the truth she'd been circling around for months.

She loved Jasper. Completely, genuinely, with the kind of deep commitment that came from choosing someone again and again even after seeing all the ways they could fail you.

But she also loved Addison. Differently, but no less truly.

And somehow, impossibly, she needed to find a way to honor both truths without destroying either relationship.

The POH Council was going to be demanding, complex, transformative work.

But it was nothing compared to the work of navigating the human heart.

Elena pulled into the driveway of Oak Watch as the sun was setting, painting the sky in shades of orange and gold. Inside, she could hear Grace practicing piano—the halting, determined repetition of someone learning a new piece.

Home. Family. The foundation that made everything else possible.

She took a breath, gathered her things, and stepped into the warm light of the house.

Tomorrow she would call Addison and tell her about the nomination. Tomorrow she would start navigating the complicated terrain of leadership and relationships and faith.

But tonight, she would be Mom. Wife. The woman who held things together even when everything inside her was shifting into new configurations.

Tonight, that was enough.
