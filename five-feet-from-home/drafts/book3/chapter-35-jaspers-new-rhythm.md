# Chapter 35: Jasper's New Rhythm

**POV:** Jasper Barrett
**Location:** The Forge/Oak Watch
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The email from the London consultancy sits in Jasper's inbox for three days before he opens it.

He knows what it is. Harper forwarded it with a one-line subject: *They're serious this time.* The firm—one of the top crisis management groups in Europe—has been courting BSS for two years. Partnership. Global expansion. Offices in London, Singapore, Dubai. The kind of opportunity that would have made Jasper's pulse quicken five years ago.

Now, sitting at his desk at The Forge on a Wednesday morning, coffee cooling beside him, he reads the offer and feels nothing.

Well. Not nothing. He feels tired.

The proposal is generous. They want BSS to anchor their North American operations. Jasper would oversee strategy, manage the big-picture clients, travel quarterly to coordinate with international teams. It's prestigious. Lucrative. Exactly the kind of expansion he used to dream about.

He closes the email and deletes it.

Then he opens a new document and starts typing.

---

By noon, he's written a memo. Clean, direct, two pages. He sends it to Harper, Addison, and the senior team with a meeting request: 2 PM, conference room.

At 1:45, he walks downstairs to The Forge's main floor. The office hums with midweek energy—phones ringing, keyboards clicking, the low murmur of strategizing. He passes Harper's office, where she's on a call, gesturing sharply at her screen. He passes the bullpen, where three junior consultants are building a crisis timeline on a whiteboard.

This place used to feel like a war room. Now it feels like a home.

The conference room overlooks the city, floor-to-ceiling windows framing Charlotte's skyline. Jasper sets his laptop on the table and waits.

Harper arrives first, tablet in hand, expression unreadable. "You deleted the London email."

"I did."

"Without responding."

"I'm responding now."

Addison walks in next, followed by two of their senior consultants—Marcus, ex-FBI, handles cybersecurity cases, and Leah, former White House comms director, runs their political portfolio. They settle into chairs, waiting.

Jasper doesn't waste time.

"I'm turning down the London expansion," he says.

No one reacts. Harper just nods. Addison leans back in her chair, arms crossed, watching him.

"I know we've been talking about it for two years," Jasper continues. "I know it's a good opportunity. But it's not the right move. Not for me. Not anymore."

"Why not?" Leah asks.

"Because I don't want to run a global operation. I want to run this operation. Here. With this team."

"London would give us access to markets we can't touch from Charlotte," Marcus says, not argumentative—just factual.

"I know. And if someone else wants to take that on, we can talk about it. But it's not going to be me."

Harper sets down her tablet. "What are you proposing instead?"

Jasper opens his laptop and pulls up the memo. "I'm proposing a restructure. Addison takes over as managing partner. I step into a mentor role—strategic oversight, senior client management, training. I'll still be involved, but I won't be running day-to-day operations."

Addison's eyes widen. "Jasper—"

"You're ready," he says simply. "You've been ready for a year. And I'm ready to step back."

"Step back to what?" Harper asks.

"To being present. To working nine to five. To going home for dinner." He pauses. "To being the kind of partner and father I should have been from the start."

The room is quiet. Not uncomfortable—just still. Weighted.

"You'll get bored," Leah says.

Jasper smiles faintly. "I don't think I will."

"You'll miss the field work," Marcus adds.

"Maybe. But I won't miss the eighteen-hour days. Or the red-eyes to London. Or the phone calls at two in the morning."

Harper leans forward, elbows on the table. "What about mentorship? You said strategic oversight and training. What does that look like?"

"Weekly sessions with junior staff. Case reviews. Strategy development. I'll still be in the office, still working with clients. But I'm not the first call anymore. Addison is."

Addison shakes her head. "I can't just take over—"

"Yes, you can," Jasper says, cutting her off gently. "You've been running half the cases solo for six months. The clients trust you. The team trusts you. I trust you."

"What if I screw it up?"

"Then you'll fix it. The same way I did."

Harper looks at Addison, then back at Jasper. "You've thought this through."

"I have."

"And Elena knows?"

"She knows. We've been talking about it for months."

"What does she think?"

Jasper's smile softens. "She thinks I should have done this two years ago."

---

The meeting lasts another hour. They talk logistics—transition timelines, client communication, how to position the restructure externally. Marcus suggests promoting two junior consultants to fill the gaps Addison will leave. Leah proposes a press release framing the change as strategic evolution, not downsizing.

By the time they adjourn, Jasper feels lighter than he has in years.

---

That evening, he leaves the office at five-thirty.

It's strange, walking out while the sun's still up. Strange to see the parking lot half-full, other people heading home. Strange to drive through rush hour traffic instead of late-night empty roads.

But it's good strange.

When he pulls into the driveway at Oak Watch, Grace is in the front yard with a soccer ball, practicing dribbling. She looks up when she hears the car, her face breaking into a grin.

"Daddy!"

He gets out and she runs over, ball forgotten. He catches her in a hug, lifting her off the ground.

"You're home early," she says, delighted.

"I am."

"Are you sick?"

He laughs. "No. Just trying something new."

Elena's at the door, leaning against the frame, arms crossed. She's smiling, the kind of smile that says *I told you so* without saying anything at all.

"Hey," Jasper says, setting Grace down.

"Hey yourself."

Grace grabs his hand. "Can you play soccer with me?"

"Sure."

"Really?"

"Really."

She drags him toward the yard, already explaining the rules of the game she's invented—something involving zombies and goal-scoring. Jasper lets himself be pulled along, glancing back at Elena.

She mouths, *I'm proud of you.*

He nods. *I know.*

---

Later, after dinner—pasta with marinara that Grace helped make, sauce splattered on her shirt—Jasper sits at the kitchen table with his laptop while Elena loads the dishwasher.

"You're still working," she observes.

"Just answering a few emails."

"That's more than you used to do when you were home."

He looks up. "Is that a problem?"

"No. It's progress."

He closes the laptop. "I told the team today. About stepping back."

"How did they take it?"

"Better than I expected. Harper didn't even argue."

"Because she saw it coming."

"Did you?"

Elena dries her hands and leans against the counter. "I've been watching you change for two years, Jasper. This isn't a surprise. It's a confirmation."

"Of what?"

"That you finally figured out what matters."

Grace runs into the kitchen, hair damp from her bath, wearing pajamas with cartoon planets. "Daddy, will you read to me?"

Jasper stands. "What are we reading?"

"The one about the girl who builds the rocket."

"Good choice."

He follows her upstairs, Elena watching from the kitchen with that same small smile.

---

In Grace's room, Jasper sits on the edge of her bed while she burrows under the covers, clutching a stuffed elephant. He opens the book—a picture book about a girl who wants to go to space—and starts reading.

Grace interrupts within two pages.

"Daddy, are you going to keep coming home early?"

"I'm going to try."

"Every day?"

"Most days."

"What about when there's an emergency?"

He sets the book down. "If there's a real emergency, I'll still go. But I'm going to let other people handle more of the emergencies now."

"Like Aunt Addie?"

"Like Aunt Addie."

Grace considers this, her expression serious. "Is she good at emergencies?"

"She's very good."

"As good as you?"

"Better, I think."

Grace grins. "Don't tell her that. She'll get a big head."

Jasper laughs. "Our secret."

He finishes the book, tucks her in, kisses her forehead. "Love you, Gracie."

"Love you too, Daddy."

He's almost to the door when she calls out, "Daddy?"

"Yeah?"

"I like it when you're home."

He smiles. "Me too, kiddo."

---

Downstairs, Elena's in the living room with a glass of wine and a book. Jasper sits beside her, close enough that their knees touch.

"She asleep?" Elena asks.

"Not yet. But she will be."

Elena sets down her book. "You okay?"

"Yeah. I think I am."

"No regrets about London?"

"None."

"Not even a little?"

He turns to look at her. "Five years ago, I would have taken that deal in a heartbeat. I would have convinced myself it was the right move. That it was what we needed."

"And now?"

"Now I know what we need. And it's not an office in London."

Elena leans her head on his shoulder. "You're getting wise in your old age."

"I'm getting tired in my old age."

"That too."

They sit in silence for a while, the kind of comfortable quiet that only comes after years of shared life. Jasper can hear Grace's voice upstairs, talking to her stuffed animals. He can hear the hum of the dishwasher in the kitchen. He can feel Elena's warmth beside him.

This is enough.

More than enough.

---

The next morning, Jasper's back at The Forge by eight-thirty. He makes coffee in the break room—real coffee, not the sludge from the automatic machine—and heads to his office.

At nine, he has his first mentorship session with one of the junior consultants, a sharp twenty-six-year-old named Callie who's been handling small-scale media crises. She walks in with a notebook and a nervous energy that reminds Jasper of himself a decade ago.

"Mr. Barrett—"

"Jasper," he corrects.

"Jasper. Thank you for meeting with me."

"Of course. What are we working on?"

She launches into a case brief—a mid-sized nonprofit dealing with donor backlash over a controversial board decision. Jasper listens, asking questions, guiding her through the strategy. He doesn't give her the answer. He helps her find it.

By the end of the session, Callie looks less nervous and more confident.

"This was really helpful," she says.

"Good. Same time next week?"

"Yes. Absolutely."

After she leaves, Jasper sits back in his chair, staring at his calendar. Three more mentorship sessions this week. A client meeting on Thursday. A strategy review with Addison on Friday.

Nine to five. Home for dinner. Day trips only.

It's not the life he thought he'd be living.

But it's the life he wants.

---

**End of Chapter 35**
