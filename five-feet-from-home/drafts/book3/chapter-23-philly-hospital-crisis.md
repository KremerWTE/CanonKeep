# Chapter 23: Philly Hospital Crisis

*POV: Addison Price*

---

The call came through at 11:43 p.m. on a Tuesday.

Addison Price was in her apartment, halfway through a glass of wine and a documentary about Everest expeditions, when her phone lit up with Harper's name.

"This better be good," Addison said by way of greeting.

"Philadelphia. Hospital system. Ransomware attack." Harper's voice was clipped, efficient, all business. "Patients at risk. Media's already circling. FBI's en route. Client wants BSS on-site by morning."

Addison muted the TV. "How bad?"

"Bad enough that the CEO called Jasper directly at eleven p.m. and used the words 'life and death' three times in two minutes."

"Jasper taking it?"

"No. You are. He's sending Jasper Barrett as support, but you're lead."

Addison felt the familiar adrenaline spike—part fear, part excitement, all focus. "When's the flight?"

"Six a.m. out of Charlotte. I'm sending you the briefing materials now. Jasper will meet you at the airport."

"Not Jasper. You said Jasper's support."

"I meant Jasper Barrett. The other Jasper."

It took Addison's exhausted brain a moment to catch up. "You mean Jasper's sending himself as my support?"

"That's what I said. He'll be there to back you up, but you're running point. His words."

Addison stood, already mentally packing. "Understood. Anything else?"

"Yeah. Don't screw this up. If people die because a hospital can't access their records, we're not just talking about a PR crisis. We're talking about lawsuits, criminal investigations, and BSS getting dragged through every news cycle for the next year."

"Got it. No pressure."

"Addie." Harper's voice softened, just slightly. "You've got this."

"Thanks." Addison ended the call and stared at her apartment—the half-empty wine glass, the paused documentary, Detective Penguin sitting on the shelf where she'd placed him after the zoo trip with Grace.

She thought about texting Kendra. About calling Hawk. About reaching out to anyone in the five feet around her who might ground her before she walked into seventy-two hours of hell.

Instead, she finished the wine in two swallows, set the glass in the sink, and started packing.

---

Jasper met her at the gate, looking like he'd had about as much sleep as she had.

"You good?" he asked, falling into step beside her.

"Define good."

"Functional. Focused. Not about to have a breakdown in the middle of a crisis."

"Then yes, I'm good." Addison adjusted her bag on her shoulder. "Why are you coming with me?"

"Because this is the kind of crisis where things go sideways fast, and I want someone I trust watching your back." Jasper handed her a coffee—black, no sugar, exactly how she liked it. "Also because Elena told me I should."

"Elena told you to come to Philadelphia?"

"Elena told me you've been running yourself into the ground and you need someone making sure you don't do anything stupid." He gave her a look. "She's not wrong."

Addison wanted to argue. Wanted to insist she was fine, that she didn't need a babysitter, that she could handle a hospital ransomware crisis with one hand tied behind her back.

But the truth was, she was tired. The kind of tired that lived in her bones and made her second-guess decisions she would normally make on instinct.

"Fine," she said. "But I'm still lead."

"Wouldn't have it any other way."

---

The Philadelphia hospital system was called Mercy Grace Health, which Addison found darkly ironic given the situation.

They arrived at the main campus just after nine a.m. to find controlled chaos. Administrators in suits hurried through hallways. Nurses clustered at stations, whispering in tight groups. Security had been doubled. And in the CEO's office on the fifth floor, a man named Dr. Raymond Calloway looked like he'd aged ten years overnight.

"Thank God you're here." Calloway stood when they entered, shaking Jasper's hand and then Addison's. "I don't know what we're going to do. The entire system is locked. Patient records, medication schedules, surgical planning—everything. We've got people writing orders on paper like it's 1985."

"Walk me through it," Addison said, setting her bag down and pulling out her laptop. "When did you first notice the breach?"

"Yesterday morning. Six a.m. Night shift tried to access records and got a ransom message instead." Calloway pulled up an image on his computer—a screen filled with red text demanding payment in Bitcoin. "They want five million dollars. Forty-eight hours to pay or they start deleting files."

"How much of that forty-eight hours is left?"

"Thirty-six."

Jasper swore quietly. Addison's mind was already running scenarios. "Have you contacted the FBI?"

"They're downstairs now, setting up in the IT department. But they're focused on investigation, not resolution. I need someone who can actually fix this."

"We're not miracle workers, Dr. Calloway. If your system's locked, we can't just wave a wand and make it unlock."

"But you can negotiate. You can manage the media. You can keep this from becoming a national story that destroys everything we've built." Calloway's voice cracked slightly. "We have cancer patients who need their treatment plans. Transplant patients whose anti-rejection protocols are time-sensitive. A woman in labor upstairs whose medical history we can't access because it's all locked behind this—this—"

"We get it," Addison interrupted gently. "And we'll do everything we can. But I need full access. IT, administration, FBI liaison, media team. If I'm running point, I need total cooperation."

"You have it. Whatever you need."

"Good. Then let's get to work."

---

The IT department was in the basement—a warren of server rooms and workstations that smelled like burnt coffee and stress. The FBI had taken over the main conference room. A young agent with sharp eyes and a no-nonsense expression introduced herself as Agent Monica Reyes.

"You're Barrett Security?" Reyes asked, looking between Addison and Jasper.

"I'm Addison Price, managing partner. This is Jasper Barrett, founder. We're here to support crisis management and negotiation."

"Good. Because we need to talk about what you're not going to do." Reyes crossed her arms. "You're not going to pay the ransom. You're not going to negotiate directly with the attackers. And you're not going to do anything that interferes with our investigation."

Addison met her gaze steadily. "Agent Reyes, with all due respect, my job is to protect this hospital's patients and reputation. If paying the ransom is the fastest way to restore critical systems, that's a conversation the hospital's leadership needs to have."

"Paying ransoms funds criminal organizations and encourages more attacks."

"And not paying ransoms costs lives. I'm not here to debate ethics with you. I'm here to solve a problem."

Reyes's jaw tightened, but before she could respond, Jasper stepped in. "How about this. We run parallel tracks. You investigate. We manage containment and explore all options—including negotiation if it comes to that. We share information, stay out of each other's way, and focus on the same end goal: getting this hospital back online."

Reyes considered this, then nodded curtly. "Fine. But if you do anything that compromises this investigation, I'll have you removed from the premises."

"Understood," Addison said. "Now, can we see the ransom message? All of it, not just the screenshot."

---

The next twelve hours were a blur.

Addison set up a command center in a conference room adjacent to the CEO's office. Jasper worked with the FBI to trace the origin of the attack. The hospital's CTO—a woman named Dr. Patel who looked like she hadn't slept in days—walked them through every failed attempt to bypass the encryption.

"We've tried everything," Patel said, her voice hoarse. "Backups, system restores, manual decryption. They knew exactly what they were doing. This wasn't some script kiddie attack. This was professional."

"Do you have offline backups?" Addison asked.

"Some. But they're weeks old. Any patient admitted or treated in the last month won't be in those records."

"How many patients is that?"

"Roughly four thousand."

Addison felt the weight of that number settle on her shoulders. Four thousand people whose lives depended on information locked behind a ransomware screen.

She thought about Elena, collapsing at the dinner table. About the hospital where she'd been treated, the records that had saved her life. About what would have happened if those records had been inaccessible.

"We need to consider paying," Addison said quietly.

Jasper looked at her. "You sure?"

"No. But I'm sure we can't let people die while we play hardball with hackers." She turned to Dr. Calloway, who'd been sitting silently in the corner. "Do you have the funds?"

"Our insurance covers up to ten million for cyber incidents. But paying sets a precedent. Every hospital in the country becomes a target."

"They're already targets. The question is whether you're willing to gamble with lives to make a point."

Calloway closed his eyes. "If I authorize payment, I need to know we'll get the decryption key. That this won't just be five million dollars down a hole."

"There are no guarantees. But we can negotiate terms. Proof of life, so to speak. Make them unlock a sample of files before we transfer funds."

"And the FBI?"

"Will hate it. But ultimately, it's your decision, not theirs."

Calloway opened his eyes. "Do it. Negotiate. But don't commit to payment until I give final approval."

---

Addison spent the next six hours in a digital back-and-forth with the attackers through an encrypted messaging platform the FBI had set up.

The hackers were professional. No theatrics. No gloating. Just cold, transactional communication.

*We need proof you can decrypt the files,* Addison typed.

The response came back in minutes. *Select 100 files. Send list. We decrypt as proof.*

Addison coordinated with Dr. Patel to compile a list—a mix of critical patient records and administrative files. They sent it over.

Two hours later, the files were unlocked.

"They're real," Patel confirmed, her hands shaking as she reviewed the decrypted data. "They can do it."

Addison looked at Jasper. He nodded.

She typed: *Five million. Bitcoin. Transferred upon full system decryption. No partial unlocks. All or nothing.*

The reply took longer this time. Addison imagined them conferring, weighing the risk.

Finally: *Agreed. Transfer to wallet address provided. Decryption begins immediately upon confirmation.*

Addison forwarded the message to Dr. Calloway. "It's your call."

---

The hospital board convened an emergency meeting at two a.m. Addison presented the options: pay and likely restore the system within hours, or refuse and potentially face weeks of downtime, lawsuits, and lives lost.

The vote was unanimous.

At 3:17 a.m., five million dollars in Bitcoin was transferred to an anonymous wallet.

At 3:45 a.m., the decryption key was delivered.

By 4:30 a.m., the IT team confirmed the system was coming back online.

Addison stood in the command center, watching the progress bars crawl across Dr. Patel's screen. Jasper was on the phone with Harper, updating her. Agent Reyes was in the corner, stone-faced, probably writing a report that would bury them all.

"It's working," Patel whispered. "It's actually working."

At 5:02 a.m., the system was fully restored.

Dr. Calloway walked into the room, his tie loosened, his eyes red-rimmed. He crossed to Addison and shook her hand.

"You saved us."

Addison felt the exhaustion crash over her like a wave. "We did what needed to be done."

"No. You led. You made the hard call. You—" His voice broke. "Thank you."

---

By the time they made it back to the airport, Addison had been awake for over forty hours.

Jasper bought them both terrible airport coffee and sat across from her at the gate. "You okay?"

"Define okay."

"Functional. Not having a breakdown."

"Then no. I'm not okay." Addison stared into her coffee cup. "We just paid five million dollars to criminals. The FBI's pissed. The media's going to have a field day when this leaks. And we have no guarantee it won't happen again."

"But people are alive. Patients are getting treatment. The hospital's operational."

"Is that enough?"

Jasper was quiet for a moment. Then he said, "You know what I learned when Elena was in the hospital? That sometimes the win isn't perfect. Sometimes it's just... enough. You keep people alive. You keep the lights on. You make the hard call and live with the consequences."

"That's not very comforting."

"It's not supposed to be. It's just true." He set down his coffee and looked at her directly. "You don't need me on these anymore."

Addison's head snapped up. "What?"

"You don't need me. You handled that entire crisis start to finish. You negotiated with hackers, managed the FBI, convinced a hospital board to make a multimillion-dollar decision at two in the morning. I was just... there. You didn't need me."

"Jasper—"

"I'm not saying this to make you feel bad. I'm saying it because it's true. You're ready to run these on your own. Hell, you've been ready. I've just been too stubborn to admit it."

Addison didn't know what to say. She'd spent years working beside Jasper, learning from him, building her skills in his shadow. The idea that she'd stepped out of that shadow—that she was standing on her own now—felt both terrifying and exhilarating.

"What does that mean?" she asked quietly.

"It means when we get back to Charlotte, we're having a conversation about your title. And your equity stake. And what it looks like for you to be a full partner, not just in name but in reality."

"You're serious."

"I don't joke about business, Addie. You know that."

The flight started boarding. Jasper stood, grabbing his bag. Addison followed, her mind spinning.

As they settled into their seats, she pulled out her phone. There were messages from Harper, from Cole, from the hospital's PR team.

And one from Kendra: *Heard you were in Philly. Hope you're okay. Thinking about you.*

Addison stared at the message. She thought about the zoo day with Grace. About Chris Donnelly asking Kendra to dinner—because of course she'd heard about that through the grapevine. About the undefined thing between them that refused to solidify.

She typed back: *Home soon. Can we talk?*

Kendra's reply came instantly: *Yes. Please.*

Addison closed her eyes as the plane taxied down the runway. Seventy-two hours of hell. A hospital saved. A partnership redefined. And a conversation waiting for her that scared her more than any ransomware attack ever could.

But for now, she let herself sleep.

---

*End of Chapter 23*
