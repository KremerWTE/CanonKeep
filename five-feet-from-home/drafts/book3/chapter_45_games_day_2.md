# Chapter 45: Games Day 2

**POV: Kendra**
**Location: Madison, Wisconsin**

---

The hotel room smells like Tiger Balm and desperation.

Kendra Barrett—she still hasn't gotten used to the name, even after six months of marriage—lies flat on the bed at 5:47 AM, staring at the ceiling and taking inventory of everything that hurts.

Her shoulders: screaming.

Her quads: destroyed.

Her lower back: a dull, persistent roar that started somewhere around Event 2 yesterday and hasn't stopped.

Her hands: torn open in three places despite the tape.

"You awake?" Chris's voice is soft from the chair by the window. He's been up for an hour already—she heard him moving around, making coffee in the little Keurig, probably scrolling through the leaderboard for the hundredth time.

"Unfortunately."

He moves to the bed, sits on the edge. His hand finds her shoulder, and even that gentle pressure makes her wince. "How bad?"

"On a scale of one to ten? Twelve."

"Want me to get ice?"

"I want you to get me a new body." She forces herself to sit up, every muscle protesting. Through the window, she can see the first hints of dawn touching Lake Mendota. Madison in August. The CrossFit Games. Day Two.

She's currently sitting in 9th place overall.

Ninth. Out of forty women.

Yesterday feels like a fever dream. Event 1—the swim-run-swim that nearly drowned half the field. She finished 11th, middle of the pack, exactly where she needed to be. Event 2—a brutal couplet of overhead squats and burpee box jump-overs. She'd pushed too hard, chasing the leaders, finished 8th but wrecked her legs in the process. Event 3—a sprint chipper that she somehow, miraculously, finished 4th.

The math worked out. Three events. Average finish of 7th. Sitting in 9th overall going into Day Two.

Top ten. She's in the top ten at the CrossFit Games.

"Your parents are already at breakfast," Chris says, checking his phone. "Your mom wants to know if you want eggs or oatmeal."

"I want ibuprofen and a miracle."

"I'll tell her eggs." He leans down, kisses her forehead. Six months married, and he still does this—shows up, handles the details, loves her without needing her to be anything other than what she is. "You've got this, Ken."

She wants to believe him. But her body is giving her very clear feedback that "this" might be beyond what she's got left.

---

Event 4 is announced at 7:30 AM, posted on the big screen in the athlete village with all the ceremony of a death sentence.

**Event 4: Heavy Grace**
**For time:**
**30 Deadlifts (275/185 lbs)**
**30 Box Jump-Overs (30/24 inches)**

Time cap: 8 minutes.

Kendra reads it three times, hoping the numbers will change. They don't.

One hundred eighty-five pounds on the deadlift. For women. For thirty reps.

Her max deadlift is 315. She can move 185, no problem. On a fresh day. On a day when her back isn't already compromised from yesterday's overhead squats and box jump-overs.

Today is not that day.

"Okay," Sara says, appearing at her shoulder. She'd flown in last night, couldn't miss Day Two. Elena's sister, Kendra's coach, one of the few people who knows exactly how much this costs. "Talk to me."

"My back is already fried."

"I know."

"Thirty deadlifts at 185 is going to destroy me."

"Probably."

"And then thirty box jump-overs on top of that—"

"Kendra." Sara's voice cuts through the spiral. "I know. Your body knows. Everyone watching knows. This is going to hurt. The question is: how much do you want it?"

Kendra watches the other athletes on the screen—most of them younger, fresher, built for this kind of punishment. She's 29. Not old, but not 22 either. Not someone who grew up doing CrossFit. She came to this late, built her engine through sheer force of will and three years of brutal training.

She's not supposed to be here. Everyone knows it.

Which is exactly why she has to prove them wrong.

"I want it," she says.

"Then let's get you warm."

---

The warm-up area is controlled chaos. Forty women moving through the same space, loading barbells, jumping on boxes, trying to convince their bodies that yes, we're doing this again.

Kendra starts slow. Hip openers. Cat-cow stretches. Anything to get her lower back to unclench. She moves to an empty barbell, starts with 95 pounds. The pull off the floor sends a warning shot through her lumbar spine.

She ignores it.

135 pounds. Better. Her hamstrings are doing the work, taking pressure off her back.

155 pounds. This is manageable.

185 pounds. This is the weight. She pulls it once, twice. Her back is screaming, but it's holding.

"Three-minute warning!" The announcement echoes through the space.

Chris appears with her water bottle and a protein bar she won't eat. "You look strong."

"I look like I'm about to throw up."

"Same thing." He grabs her face, makes her look at him. "Listen to me. You don't have to win this event. You just have to survive it. Middle of the pack keeps you top ten. That's the goal."

He's right. She knows he's right. But something in her—the part that got her here in the first place—wants more than survival.

"One minute!"

She chalks her hands. Tapes over the tears from yesterday. Checks her belt one more time.

Sara materializes at her side. "Remember: You've done Grace a hundred times. This is just heavy Grace. Same mechanics. Same breathing. Don't let the weight change how you move."

Kendra nods. She's done Grace—the benchmark workout named after a firefighter who died on 9/11—more times than she can count. Thirty clean and jerks at 95 pounds. This is the same, just deadlifts instead of clean and jerks. Just heavier. Just worse.

"Athletes to the floor!"

---

The competition floor is a rectangle of pain.

Forty barbells, loaded to 185 pounds, lined up in perfect rows. Forty boxes, set to 24 inches, waiting like sentries. Forty women, standing behind their lanes, trying to look confident.

Kendra finds her lane—number 23. Chris is in the stands somewhere with her parents and Sara's family. She doesn't look for them. Can't afford the distraction.

The crowd noise is deafening. Thousands of people packed into the Alliant Energy Center, screaming for their favorites, for the athletes they've followed all year.

No one's screaming for Kendra. She's not a favorite. She's not a known quantity.

She's just the girl who somehow, impossibly, made it this far.

"Athletes, you have eight minutes," the head judge announces. "Three, two, one—go!"

The clock starts.

Kendra steps to her barbell. The weight feels heavier than it did in warm-up. Everything always feels heavier under the lights.

She sets her grip. Pulls.

The bar comes up clean. One.

She drops it, resets. Pulls. Two.

Her back is already talking to her. Not screaming yet, just... reminding her it's there. That it's not happy.

Three. Four. Five.

She's pacing smart, breaking at sets of five. Quick drops, quick resets. Don't let the bar sit. Don't give your body time to stiffen.

Six. Seven. Eight.

Around her, barbells are clanging. Some athletes are going unbroken, pulling rep after rep in an unbroken rhythm. She's not those athletes. She's the athlete who survives by being smart.

Nine. Ten.

Halfway through the deadlifts. Her back is screaming now. Each pull sends fire through her lumbar spine. But the mechanics are still good. Hamstrings loading. Chest up. Not rounding.

Eleven. Twelve. Thirteen.

She's breathing hard, sweat already soaking through her shirt. The crowd is a wall of noise, but she's learned to tune it out. There's only the barbell. Only the next rep.

Fourteen. Fifteen.

She risks a glance to her left. The athlete in lane 22—a girl from Canada, early twenties, built like a tank—is already on her 25th rep. She's going to finish the deadlifts in under three minutes.

Kendra's not that athlete.

Sixteen. Seventeen. Eighteen.

Her grip is going. The bar is slipping despite the chalk. She resets, pulls. Nineteen.

The bar slips. She catches it, resets. Twenty.

Ten left. Just ten more.

Twenty-one. Twenty-two.

Her back is beyond screaming now. It's threatening mutiny. Each pull feels like someone's jamming a knife between her vertebrae.

She pulls anyway.

Twenty-three. Twenty-four.

The Canadian girl is on the box now, jumping. Kendra blocks her out.

Twenty-five. Twenty-six.

Four left. Four deadlifts, then thirty box jump-overs. She can see the finish line from here.

Twenty-seven. Twenty-eight. Twenty-nine.

One more. Just one.

She sets her grip. Her hands are shaking. Her back is barely holding. She pulls.

The bar comes up. Thirty.

She drops it and moves to the box, legs already heavy.

---

Box jump-overs are a special kind of hell when your legs are already destroyed.

Jump up. Step over. Jump down. Repeat. Thirty times. On a box that's high enough to make you think about each rep but not so high that you can't recover.

Kendra's strategy: sets of ten. Three sets. Don't stop moving.

She jumps. One.

Her quads are burning from yesterday's box jump-overs. Her calves are tight. But she's moving.

Two. Three. Four. Five.

The rhythm is there. Jump, step, down. Jump, step, down.

Six. Seven. Eight.

She no-reps on nine—her feet don't clear the top of the box. She has to redo it.

Nine, for real this time. Ten.

First set done. She pauses, hands on knees, gulping air. Around her, athletes are finishing. The Canadian girl just crossed the time mark. Under six minutes.

Kendra's at six-fifteen and counting.

She gets back on the box. Eleven. Twelve.

Her hip flexors are cramping. Each jump feels like she's lifting cement blocks instead of legs.

Thirteen. Fourteen. Fifteen.

Halfway. Just fifteen more.

Sixteen. Seventeen.

Her foot catches the edge of the box. She stumbles, catches herself. The judge marks it no-rep.

She redoes it. Seventeen, for real.

Eighteen. Nineteen. Twenty.

Ten left. She can hear Chris somewhere in the stands, screaming her name. Or maybe she's imagining it. Everything's starting to blur.

Twenty-one. Twenty-two.

Her quads are threatening to quit. She talks to them, promises them rest later, ice baths, anything.

They don't believe her, but they keep moving.

Twenty-three. Twenty-four. Twenty-five.

Five. Just five more jumps.

Twenty-six. Twenty-seven.

Three.

Twenty-eight. Twenty-nine.

One.

She jumps. Clears the box. Steps over. Lands.

"Time!" the judge shouts. "Seven minutes, forty-two seconds!"

Kendra collapses forward, hands on the box, everything in her body screaming. She finished. She didn't quit. She made it through.

Around her, athletes are still moving. Some have finished faster. Some are still grinding through deadlifts. She doesn't care.

She's done.

Sara is there immediately, water bottle in hand. "That was gutsy. How's the back?"

"Terrible."

"Can you walk?"

"Barely."

"Good enough." Sara helps her off the floor, back to the warm-down area. "You finished middle-pack. That's exactly where you needed to be."

Kendra checks the display board through blurred vision. She finished 13th in the event.

Not great. But not terrible.

Chris finds her in the warm-down area, pulls her into a hug that's probably too tight for someone whose body is actively falling apart. "You were amazing."

"I was slow."

"You were smart. You survived." He kisses her temple. "That's what matters."

---

They have four hours until Event 5.

Kendra spends it in a cycle of ice, stretching, and existential dread.

Her back won't stop spasming. Every time she tries to sit up, it seizes. Sara tapes her like she's wrapping a mummy, kinesiology tape criss-crossing her lower back and hips. It helps. Marginally.

"What's Event 5?" her mom asks, bringing yet another protein shake Kendra can't stomach.

"Sprint workout." Kendra's lying on the hotel floor, legs up the wall. "They haven't released details yet."

"Can you sprint?"

"I can try."

Her mom sits beside her, touches her hair the way she used to when Kendra was little and home sick from school. "You don't have to do this, you know. If your body's telling you to stop—"

"I'm not stopping."

"Kendra—"

"Mom." Kendra opens her eyes, looks at her. "I'm not stopping. I didn't come this far to quit on Day Two."

Her mom is quiet for a long moment. Then she nods. "Okay. Then we'll be in the stands, cheering."

---

Event 5 is announced at 2:00 PM.

**Event 5: Madison Sprint**
**3 Rounds For Time:**
**400m Run**
**15 Thrusters (95/65 lbs)**
**15 Bar-Facing Burpees**

Time cap: 12 minutes.

Kendra reads it and feels something like hope.

She can run. Her engine is good. Thrusters and burpees will hurt, but they're manageable. This is a test of lungs and will, not just strength.

This is her event.

"You're smiling," Chris observes.

"I can do this one."

"You could do the last one."

"I survived the last one. I can win this one." She's already moving, shaking out her legs, trying to convince her back to cooperate for one more hour. "This is where I move up."

Sara's grinning. "There she is. There's the athlete I trained."

---

The warm-up for Event 5 is lighter. A few easy runs around the building. Some air squats and burpees. Nothing that will tax her already-depleted system.

Her back is holding. Barely. But holding.

Chris walks her to the staging area, his hand in hers. "Don't be stupid."

"Too late for that."

"Kendra. I mean it. If your back goes, you pull out. You don't wreck yourself for 9th place."

"It's not about the place." She turns to face him. "It's about proving I belong here."

He studies her face, sees something there that makes him nod. "Okay. Then go prove it."

---

The starting line for Event 5 is outside the venue, athletes lined up on the street.

The sun is brutal. August in Wisconsin. Ninety degrees and humid. Perfect running weather, if perfect means absolutely terrible.

Kendra finds her lane. Stretches her calves one more time. Checks her shoes.

The crowd is lining the streets, thousands deep. This is the signature event of the day, the one everyone wants to see. Sprinting in the heat. Pure suffering.

She's good at suffering.

"Athletes ready!" The starter's voice echoes down the line. "Three, two, one—go!"

They surge forward.

Kendra settles into the pack, not pushing the pace. The run is only 400 meters, but it's the first of three. She needs to pace smart.

The athlete from Canada—same one who crushed the deadlifts—takes the early lead. She's a runner, lean and fast. Kendra lets her go.

By the first turn, the pack has spread. Kendra's sitting in 8th, comfortable, breathing steady.

She hits the 200-meter mark and picks up the pace slightly. Her legs are tired, but they're responding. The cramping from earlier has eased.

She finishes the first 400m in 7th place, hits her barbell, starts loading for thrusters.

---

Thrusters are a full-body punishment.

Squat clean the bar to your shoulders. Drop into a full squat. Stand up and press overhead in one motion. Repeat. Fifteen times.

At 65 pounds, they're light enough to move fast. Heavy enough to make you hate everything.

Kendra goes unbroken. All fifteen reps in one set, forty-five seconds. Her lungs are burning, but her legs are good.

She drops the bar and moves to the line for burpees.

Bar-facing burpees. Chest and thighs to the ground, jump up, jump laterally over the barbell. Repeat.

She's done thousands of burpees. These are just fifteen more.

She moves fast, efficient. Down, up, over. Down, up, over.

By the tenth rep, her hip flexors are cramping again. She pushes through.

Eleven. Twelve. Thirteen. Fourteen. Fifteen.

Round one done. She's back on the run in under three minutes.

Somewhere in the chaos, she's moved up to 6th place.

---

Round two is where it gets ugly.

The second 400m run feels twice as hard. Her legs are heavy. Her lungs are raw. The heat is oppressive.

But she's moving. Still 6th. Still in the hunt.

She hits the barbell for the second set of thrusters and her back spasms.

Not a twinge. Not a warning. A full, crushing spasm that makes her drop the bar after three reps.

She doubles over, gasping. Sara is screaming from the sideline, something about breathing, about moving.

Kendra resets. Pulls the bar back to her shoulders. Gets three more reps before her back seizes again.

Three and three. Then three and two. Then two and two.

It takes her ninety seconds to finish fifteen thrusters. An eternity.

She moves to burpees, and her back protests every single rep. Down is agony. Up is worse.

But she moves. Because quitting isn't an option.

Round two done. She's dropped to 8th.

---

Round three is pure will.

The third 400m run is survival. Her legs are moving, but she's not sure how. Everything hurts. Her back. Her quads. Her lungs.

She's passed by two athletes on the run. She doesn't have the speed to stay with them.

She hits the barbell for the final set of thrusters, and her vision is starting to narrow.

This is it. Last round. Last fifteen thrusters. Last fifteen burpees.

She breaks the thrusters into sets of five. They're ugly reps, each one slower than the last. But they count.

Burpees are even worse. She's moving in slow motion, each rep taking twice as long as the first round.

But she's moving.

Somewhere in the stands, Chris is screaming. Her parents are screaming. Sara is screaming.

She can't hear any of it. There's only the bar, the ground, the finish line.

Fourteen burpees done. One left.

She drops to the ground. Pushes up. Jumps over the bar.

"Time!" the judge yells. "Ten minutes, fifty-three seconds!"

Kendra collapses. Not dramatically. Just... collapses. Her body has nothing left.

Someone's pouring water over her head. Someone else is helping her sit up. The world is spinning, but she's done.

She checks the board through blurred vision.

7th place in the event.

She moved up. Despite the back spasms, despite the heat, despite everything—she moved up.

"Jesus Christ, Kendra." Sara is kneeling beside her, half-laughing, half-crying. "That was insane."

"Did I...?" She can't finish the sentence.

"You're 9th overall still. Solidly 9th. You gained ground on the athletes below you, held your position against the ones above."

9th. Still 9th.

But now it's real. Now it's not a fluke from Day One. She's proven she can compete at this level.

Chris appears, pulls her into his arms. She's soaked in sweat and probably smells terrible, but he doesn't care.

"I'm so proud of you," he whispers.

She can't respond. Can only lean into him and try to remember how to breathe.

---

That night, back in the hotel room, Kendra lies in an ice bath and tries to process the day.

Two events. Event 4: 13th place. Event 5: 7th place.

Five events total. Sitting 9th overall going into Day Three.

Her body is destroyed. Her back is a disaster. Tomorrow there will be three more events—the final day, the last push.

But tonight, she's still in this.

Chris sits on the bathroom floor beside the tub, scrolling through the leaderboard on his phone. "You know what's crazy? You're only twelve points out of 5th place."

"Feels like a million miles."

"It's three good events. Three solid finishes, and you're top five at the CrossFit Games."

Top five. The thought is absurd. Impossible.

Except... it's not. Not anymore.

She's here. She's competing. She belongs.

"Tomorrow," she says.

"Tomorrow," Chris agrees.

She closes her eyes and lets the ice numb everything. The pain. The fear. The impossible hope that maybe—just maybe—she's not done climbing yet.

Tomorrow is Day Three.

Tomorrow, she finds out who she really is.

---

**End of Chapter 45**
