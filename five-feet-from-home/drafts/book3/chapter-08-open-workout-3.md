# Chapter 8: Open Workout 3

**POV:** Kendra
**Location:** Charlotte Gym
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The third Open workout drops on a Thursday night, exactly two weeks after the first.

Kendra's at Chris's place this time, sprawled on his couch with her feet in his lap. They've made it a ritual now—Open announcement night, takeout Thai food, laptop open on the coffee table. Workout 25.2 had come and gone the week before—double-unders and overhead squats, a grinding test of coordination and leg endurance. She'd placed seventy-third in the region. Solid, but not spectacular.

Tonight feels different. She can feel it in the air, in the way her body's humming with anticipation even though she's supposed to be relaxed.

When Dave Castro's face appears on the screen at 8:01 PM, Chris squeezes her ankle. "Here we go."

Castro doesn't waste time. He never does.

**25.3**
**Ladder for Time:**
**Deadlifts (225/155 lbs)**
**Handstand Push-Ups**

**3 Deadlifts, 3 HSPU**
**6 Deadlifts, 6 HSPU**
**9 Deadlifts, 9 HSPU**
**12 Deadlifts, 12 HSPU**
**15 Deadlifts, 15 HSPU**
**18 Deadlifts, 18 HSPU**
**21 Deadlifts, 21 HSPU**

**Time Cap: 12 Minutes**

Kendra sits up slowly, staring at the screen.

"Holy shit," Chris says.

She doesn't respond. She's already doing the math. One fifty-five pounds on the deadlift—heavy, but manageable. Handstand push-ups—her specialty. This is a workout built for strong shoulders and a high pain threshold.

This is her workout.

"You're going to crush this," Chris says.

Kendra shakes her head, but she's smiling. "Don't jinx it."

"I'm serious. Deadlifts and handstand push-ups? That's literally your jam."

He's not wrong. She's been doing handstand push-ups since her second year of CrossFit, and she's gotten good—really good. Strict reps, no kip, full range of motion. And deadlifts are one of her strongest lifts. One fifty-five is heavy, but it's not crushing. She can move it fast if she stays smart.

The real question is whether she can hold the pace.

"I'm doing it tomorrow morning," she says. "First thing."

"You want me there?"

She looks at him. His face is open, earnest. He always asks. He never assumes.

"Yeah," she says. "I do."

---

She doesn't sleep.

Not really. She drifts in and out, her mind running through the workout on a loop. Three deadlifts, three handstand push-ups. Transition. Six deadlifts, six handstand push-ups. Transition. By the time the ladder gets to twenty-one reps, her shoulders will be cooked. The question is whether she can get there.

Most people won't finish. The twelve-minute time cap is brutal. If you slow down—if you take too many breaks—you'll hit the cap before you finish the twenty-ones.

Kendra's plan is simple: go unbroken as long as possible. No rest until her body forces her to.

When her alarm goes off at 5:30 AM, she's already awake.

Chris is still asleep, one arm draped over her waist. She slips out from under him carefully, pulls on her training clothes, and heads to the kitchen. Coffee. Protein shake. She forces herself to eat half a banana even though her stomach's tight.

By six, she's in the car. By 6:15, she's at the gym.

Sara's already there, of course. So are a handful of other athletes—people Kendra's trained with for months, all here to watch or compete. The energy's different today. Sharper. Everyone knows this workout is a separator.

Sara's setting up the barbell in the center of the floor—one fifty-five pounds, plates loaded and secured. A few feet away, there's a taped square on the floor with an AbMat and a set of parallettes for the handstand push-ups.

Kendra drops her bag and starts warming up. Slow jog. Hip openers. Shoulder rolls. She picks up a PVC pipe and does a few overhead presses, feeling the range of motion, making sure everything's loose. Then she moves to the barbell—empty bar first, then sixty-five pounds, then ninety-five, then one fifteen.

At one fifty-five, the bar feels heavy but controlled. She does three reps, smooth and fast. Her back feels good. Her grip is solid.

She walks over to the wall and kicks up into a handstand. Holds it for ten seconds, then lowers into a push-up. Head to the floor, arms locked out. She does five reps, unbroken, then drops down and shakes out her shoulders.

"You ready?" Sara asks, walking over with her phone.

"Yeah," Kendra says. Her heart is pounding, but her breathing is steady. "Let's do this."

Chris arrives just as she's chalking her hands. He slides onto one of the benches along the wall, coffee in hand, and gives her a small nod. No words. Just presence.

Sara holds up her phone. "Camera's rolling. You know the drill."

Kendra steps up to the barbell. The gym is quiet. A few people have gathered to watch, but no one's talking. It's the kind of silence that feels like respect.

"Three, two, one… go."

Kendra pulls the bar. One. Two. Three. The deadlifts are fast, controlled. She drops the bar and moves immediately to the wall, kicking up into a handstand. Three push-ups, unbroken. She drops down, spins, and moves back to the bar.

Six deadlifts. Still fast. Her breathing is hard but even. She moves back to the wall. Six handstand push-ups, unbroken. Her shoulders are starting to burn, but she's not slowing down.

Nine deadlifts. The bar feels heavier now, but she's still moving. She transitions to the wall. Nine handstand push-ups. She gets through seven unbroken, then has to drop, shake out her arms, and finish the last two.

"Three minutes!" Sara calls.

Twelve deadlifts. Kendra's lower back is starting to fatigue, but her form is still good. She drops the bar and moves to the wall. Twelve handstand push-ups. She gets five, drops, breathes, gets four more, drops, breathes, finishes the last three.

Fifteen deadlifts. The weight is grinding now. Each rep is slower, more deliberate. Her hands are slipping on the bar despite the chalk. She finishes the fifteen and moves to the wall.

Fifteen handstand push-ups.

This is where it gets ugly.

She kicks up and starts pressing. One. Two. Three. Four. Her shoulders are screaming. She drops, gasps for air, kicks back up. Five. Six. Seven. Drop. Breathe. Kick up. Eight. Nine. Ten. Drop.

"Six minutes!" Sara shouts.

Kendra's vision is narrowing. Everything is noise and fire and effort. She kicks back up. Eleven. Twelve. Drop. Kick up. Thirteen. Fourteen. Fifteen.

She drops to the floor and stumbles back to the barbell.

Eighteen deadlifts.

Her grip is gone. Her back is screaming. But she pulls. One. Two. Three. She's breaking every three reps now, gasping between sets. The bar crashes down after each set of three, loud in the silent gym.

Eighteen reps done.

Eighteen handstand push-ups.

Kendra moves to the wall and kicks up. Her shoulders have nothing left. She gets three reps. Drops. Breathes. Kicks up. Two reps. Drops. Breathes. Kicks up. Three more.

She's at eight reps. Ten more to go.

"Nine minutes!" Sara calls.

Three minutes left. Twenty-one deadlifts and twenty-one handstand push-ups to go.

Kendra looks at the barbell. Then she looks at Chris. He's leaning forward now, elbows on his knees, watching her with that steady, unshakable faith.

She moves back to the wall.

Kick up. Press. One. Drop.

Kick up. Press. Two. Drop.

She's doing singles now. Each rep is a fight. But she's not stopping.

Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine. Ten.

She drops to the floor, gasping, and moves back to the barbell.

Twenty-one deadlifts.

She pulls the bar. One. Two. Three. Drops it. Pulls again. Four. Five. Six. Drops it. Her hands are shaking. Her lower back is on fire.

"Ten minutes!" Sara shouts.

Two minutes left.

Kendra pulls. Seven. Eight. Nine. Drops the bar. Pulls. Ten. Eleven. Twelve. Drops. Pulls. Thirteen. Fourteen. Fifteen.

Six reps left.

She's crying now, she realizes. Not from pain, exactly. From effort. From the sheer fucking will it takes to keep moving.

She pulls. Sixteen. Seventeen. Eighteen.

Three reps left.

The gym is loud now—people are clapping, shouting her name. She can hear Chris's voice somewhere in the noise, steady and sure.

She pulls. Nineteen. Twenty.

One rep left.

She bends down, grips the bar, and pulls. Twenty-one.

The bar crashes to the floor.

She moves to the wall. Twenty-one handstand push-ups. All that's left.

She kicks up.

Press. One.

Drop. Breathe. Kick up.

Press. Two.

Drop. Breathe. Kick up.

"Eleven minutes!" Sara shouts.

One minute left.

Kendra's body is shutting down. Her shoulders are gone. Her arms are shaking so hard she can barely hold the handstand. But she keeps kicking up. Keeps pressing.

Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine. Ten. Eleven. Twelve.

Nine reps left.

Thirty seconds.

Kick up. Press. Thirteen.

Kick up. Press. Fourteen.

Kick up. Press. Fifteen.

Twenty seconds.

Sixteen. Seventeen. Eighteen.

Three reps left.

Fifteen seconds.

Kick up. Press. Nineteen.

Kick up. Press. Twenty.

Ten seconds.

One rep left.

Kendra kicks up into the handstand. Her arms are shaking so hard she's not sure she can hold it. But she lowers her head to the floor. Presses.

Twenty-one.

She drops to the floor as Sara shouts, "Time! Eleven fifty-two!"

The gym erupts.

Kendra collapses onto her back, chest heaving, tears streaming down her face. She can't move. She can't breathe. She can't do anything except lie there and feel the overwhelming, all-consuming relief of being done.

Chris is there first, kneeling beside her, his hand on her shoulder. "You did it," he says, his voice rough. "You finished."

Sara's there too, grinning, holding up her phone. "Eleven minutes, fifty-two seconds. That's insane, Kendra."

Kendra closes her eyes. She's too tired to respond. Too tired to do anything except breathe.

But she's done.

---

Twenty minutes later, after she's caught her breath and downed half a bottle of water, Sara pulls up the leaderboard on her phone.

"You need to see this," Sara says.

Kendra's still sitting on the floor, back against the wall, Chris beside her. She takes the phone and looks at the screen.

**Women's RX Division – North America**
**Rank: 47**

Forty-seven.

Not in the region. In North America.

Kendra stares at the number. Blinks. Reads it again.

"That's…" She can't finish the sentence.

"Top fifty in the continent," Sara says, her voice quiet but fierce. "Kendra, do you understand what that means?"

Kendra shakes her head. She doesn't. She can't process it.

Chris wraps his arm around her shoulders and pulls her close. "It means you're for real," he says quietly. "It means this isn't a dream anymore."

Kendra looks at the number again. Forty-seven.

Somewhere in the back of the gym, someone is watching.

His name is Chris Donnelly, and he's not here by accident. He's a consultant—former athlete, now talent scout, brought in by a handful of elite training programs to identify athletes with Games potential.

He's been watching Kendra for three weeks. Watching her train. Watching her move. Watching the way she suffers and keeps going.

And now he's watching her sit on the floor, exhausted and crying and grinning, staring at a leaderboard that says she's forty-seventh in North America.

He pulls out his phone and types a quick note.

*Kendra. Charlotte. Top 50 NAC on 25.3. Strong engine, great shoulders, mental toughness off the charts. Worth a closer look.*

He sends the note to his boss, then puts the phone away.

Then he walks out of the gym, quiet and unnoticed.

But he'll be back.

**End of Chapter 8**
