# Chapter 36: The Baking Disaster

**POV:** Addison
**Location:** Oak Watch Kitchen
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The idea starts innocently enough.

Addison's at Oak Watch on a Saturday afternoon, sitting at the kitchen island with her laptop, reviewing a case file for a Monday meeting. Elena's upstairs working on something for Maison Aurelia. Grace is at the table, coloring with the kind of focused intensity usually reserved for neurosurgeons.

Jasper walks through on his way to the garage, coffee mug in hand. "Anyone need anything from the store?"

"When's Mom's birthday?" Grace asks without looking up from her drawing.

Jasper stops. "Next Saturday. Why?"

"We should make her a cake."

"That's a nice idea, Gracie."

"Aunt Addie can help me."

Addison looks up from her laptop. "What?"

Grace finally glances over, her expression innocent and hopeful. "You'll help me, right? Mom loves cake."

"I—" Addison hesitates. She has exactly zero experience baking. She can make coffee, scrambled eggs, and reservations. That's the extent of her culinary skills. "I don't think I'm the right person for this."

"Please?" Grace bats her eyes, and Addison knows she's already lost.

Jasper's grinning from the doorway, entirely unhelpful.

"Fine," Addison says. "But if this goes badly, it's on you."

Grace beams. "It won't go badly. It's just cake."

---

It goes badly.

Immediately.

Grace insists they make the cake from scratch—"Because that's what Mom would do"—so Addison Googles "easy birthday cake recipe" and finds something that claims to be foolproof. Vanilla cake, buttercream frosting, minimal equipment required.

They gather ingredients. Flour, sugar, eggs, butter, vanilla extract, baking powder, milk. Grace reads the recipe aloud while Addison measures.

"Two cups of flour," Grace announces.

Addison dumps flour into the bowl. A cloud of white powder explodes into the air, coating the counter, her shirt, and Grace's hair.

Grace giggles. "You're supposed to sift it."

"What's sift?"

"I don't know. But Mom always does it."

Addison wipes flour off her face and keeps going.

"Three eggs," Grace reads.

"Okay. Can you crack them?"

"I'm really good at cracking eggs," Grace says confidently.

She is not good at cracking eggs.

The first one she cracks with both hands, slamming it against the bowl with enough force to shatter the shell into a dozen pieces. Bits of eggshell float in the mixture like tiny icebergs.

"Oops," Grace says.

"It's fine. We'll fish them out."

The second egg fares slightly better, though half the shell still ends up in the bowl. The third egg misses the bowl entirely and splatters onto the counter.

Addison closes her eyes and counts to five. "Okay. New plan. I'll crack the eggs. You stir."

Grace grabs a wooden spoon and starts stirring with chaotic enthusiasm, flinging batter onto the counter, the stove, and somehow the ceiling.

"Gently," Addison says.

"This is gently!"

By the time they get the batter into the pan, the kitchen looks like a crime scene. Flour everywhere. Eggshells scattered across the counter. A puddle of milk near the sink. Addison's hair is dusted white, and Grace has batter on her cheek.

They put the pan in the oven and set the timer.

"See?" Grace says, grinning. "Easy."

---

Twenty minutes later, the smoke alarm goes off.

Addison lunges for the oven, yanking it open. Smoke billows out, and inside, the cake is... collapsing. The edges are black and crispy, but the center is still liquid, bubbling like a volcanic crater.

"What happened?" Grace shouts over the alarm.

"I don't know!"

Addison grabs a towel and waves it under the smoke detector until it finally stops shrieking. She pulls the cake out and sets it on the counter. It looks like something from a disaster movie.

Grace stares at it. "That doesn't look right."

"No kidding."

Addison pulls out her phone and checks the recipe. "Wait. Did we preheat the oven?"

"What's preheat?"

"Oh no."

They stare at the smoking, half-cooked disaster in silence.

"We can try again," Grace says, ever the optimist.

Addison looks at the kitchen—the flour explosion, the eggshell graveyard, the batter splatter. "Yeah. Okay. We'll try again."

---

The second attempt starts with more confidence.

This time, Addison preheats the oven. She measures carefully. She cracks the eggs herself and inspects the batter for rogue shells. Grace helps stir, and they actually manage to get the batter into the pan without destroying the kitchen further.

"This one's going to be perfect," Grace declares.

It is not perfect.

Fifteen minutes in, Addison notices the recipe calls for one cup of sugar.

"Grace, did we add sugar?"

Grace looks up from her coloring. "I thought you did."

"I thought you did."

They stare at each other.

"We forgot the sugar," Addison says slowly.

"Can we add it now?"

"I don't think it works that way."

Grace frowns. "What happens if there's no sugar?"

"I guess we're about to find out."

When the timer goes off, Addison pulls out the cake. It looks okay—golden brown, no volcanic activity, no smoke. She lets it cool for a few minutes, then cuts a small piece and hands it to Grace.

Grace takes a bite.

Her face scrunches up immediately. "This tastes like salt."

"What?"

Addison tries a piece. It's not just unsweetened—it's actively bad. Salty, dense, somehow both dry and gummy at the same time.

"Did we—" Addison grabs the container she thought was sugar. She reads the label.

Salt.

"Oh my God."

Grace starts laughing. "We made a salt cake!"

"This isn't funny."

"It's kind of funny."

Addison looks at the cake, at the kitchen, at Grace's flour-dusted face and bright, delighted eyes. And despite everything, she starts laughing too.

"Okay. It's a little funny."

---

They're debating whether to try a third time when Elena appears in the doorway.

She stops. Takes in the scene. The flour-covered counters. The smoking disaster cake on the stove. The salty catastrophe on the cooling rack. Addison's batter-streaked shirt. Grace's eggshell-decorated hair.

"What," Elena says slowly, "happened here?"

"We're making you a birthday cake!" Grace announces.

"This is a birthday cake?"

"It's a work in progress," Addison says.

Elena crosses to the counter and picks up a piece of the salty cake. She sniffs it, then sets it back down very carefully. "How much progress are we talking?"

"We're on attempt number two," Grace says. "The first one caught fire."

"Caught fire?"

"A little bit," Addison clarifies. "And the second one is made entirely of salt."

Elena looks at her, expression unreadable. "You made a salt cake."

"It was an accident."

"Clearly."

Grace tugs on Elena's sleeve. "We can try again. Aunt Addie said so."

Elena glances at Addison, and there's something in her eyes—amusement, affection, maybe a little pity. "You know what? I think three's the charm."

"Really?" Grace lights up.

"Absolutely not," Elena says. "We should just buy one."

Grace's face falls. "But we wanted to make you something."

"And you did. You made me a salt cake and a volcanic disaster. That's very sweet."

"It's not sweet," Addison mutters. "It's extremely salty."

Elena laughs—a real laugh, warm and full. "Okay. New plan. Let's clean up this kitchen, order a cake from the bakery, and pretend this never happened."

"Can we take a picture first?" Grace asks.

"Of the kitchen?"

"Of the cakes. For memories."

Elena sighs, but she's smiling. "Fine. But we're not showing anyone."

---

They take pictures.

Grace poses with the volcanic cake, holding it up like a trophy. Addison holds the salty cake with a look of mock horror. Elena stands between them, shaking her head, laughing.

After they clean the kitchen—which takes twice as long as the actual baking—they order a cake from the fancy bakery downtown. White buttercream, raspberry filling, exactly what Elena loves.

When it arrives on Elena's birthday the following week, it's perfect. Elegant, professional, delicious.

But the photo of the floury disaster—Addison covered in batter, Grace grinning with eggshells in her hair, the smoking volcanic cake in the background—that's the one Elena frames.

She puts it on the shelf in the living room, next to the family photos and the picture of Grace's first swim meet.

"Why would you frame that?" Addison asks when she sees it.

"Because," Elena says simply, "it's my favorite gift this year."

"It's a disaster."

"It's perfect."

Grace runs over and points at the photo. "We should try again next year."

"Absolutely not," Addison says.

But she's smiling.

---

Later that evening, after the birthday dinner and the cake-cutting and the presents, Addison finds herself alone in the kitchen with Elena. Everyone else is in the living room—Jasper reading to Grace, Hawk and Kendra setting up a board game.

Elena's washing dishes, and Addison's drying, the rhythm easy and familiar.

"Thank you," Elena says quietly.

"For what? Destroying your kitchen?"

"For trying. For spending the afternoon with Grace. For being here."

Addison sets down the dish towel. "I didn't do much."

"You did enough."

There's something in Elena's voice—warmth, gratitude, affection. It makes Addison's chest feel tight in a way that's not entirely comfortable but not unwelcome either.

"I'm not good at this," Addison admits. "The domestic stuff. The baking and the... I don't know. The mom things."

"Grace doesn't need you to be good at baking. She just needs you to show up."

"I can do that."

"I know you can."

Elena hands her another plate, and their fingers brush. It's brief, casual, but it lingers in Addison's awareness longer than it should.

"The photo really is my favorite gift," Elena says.

"Even though we almost burned down your kitchen?"

"Especially because of that."

Addison laughs. "You're weird."

"I know."

They finish the dishes in comfortable silence, and when they're done, Elena pulls Addison into a hug. It's warm, steady, grounding.

"Happy birthday," Addison murmurs.

"Thank you for being part of it."

When they pull apart, Elena's smiling, and Addison feels something settle in her chest. Something like belonging.

Like family.

---

That night, lying in bed at home, Addison scrolls through the photos on her phone. There's the volcanic cake. The salty disaster. Grace's flour-covered grin. Elena's laugh.

She saves them all.

And when Hawk asks what she did this weekend, she tells him about the baking disaster—the smoke alarm, the eggshells, the salt cake.

He laughs so hard he has to sit down.

"You made a salt cake?"

"It was Grace's idea."

"Sure it was."

"I'm never baking again."

"That's probably for the best."

Addison smiles, curling into his side. "Elena framed the photo."

"Of course she did."

"You think I'm ridiculous."

"I think you're perfect."

She closes her eyes, feeling his warmth, his steady presence. "I think I might actually be happy."

"Yeah?"

"Yeah."

Hawk kisses the top of her head. "Good."

And it is.

It really, really is.

---

**End of Chapter 36**
