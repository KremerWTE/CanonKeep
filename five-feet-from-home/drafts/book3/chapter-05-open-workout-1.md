# Chapter 5: Open Workout 1

**POV:** Kendra
**Location:** Charlotte Gym
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The announcement drops at 8 PM on Thursday, exactly when Dave Castro promised.

Kendra's sitting on the living room floor of her apartment, legs stretched in a wide V, torso folded over her right knee. Chris is on the couch behind her, scrolling his phone with one hand while the other rests absently on her shoulder. The TV's muted—some basketball game neither of them is watching—but her laptop's open on the coffee table, live stream queued and ready.

When Castro's face fills the screen, she sits up.

"Here we go," she murmurs.

Chris leans forward. His hand slides from her shoulder to the back of her neck, a steady pressure. He knows what this means. Three weeks of waiting. Three weeks of structured training, deload protocols, mobility work, and mental prep. Three weeks of watching other athletes post their gym setups, their training logs, their confidence.

Now it's real.

Castro doesn't waste time. He never does. The workout flashes on screen in white block letters against black:

**25.1**
**10-Minute AMRAP:**
**5 Thrusters (95/65 lbs)**
**10 Chest-to-Bar Pull-Ups**

Kendra's stomach drops.

"Jesus," Chris says quietly.

She doesn't respond. She's already calculating. Thrusters—full squat clean into an overhead press, the kind of movement that lights up your quads, your shoulders, your lungs, everything. Ninety-five pounds for men, sixty-five for women. Chest-to-bar pull-ups, not regular pull-ups. Full range. Sternum to the bar.

Ten minutes of that.

It's a sprint disguised as endurance. It's going to hurt.

"That's brutal," Chris says. He's not wrong. "How many rounds do you think you can get?"

She shakes her head slowly, eyes still on the screen as Castro demonstrates the movement standards. "I don't know. Maybe eight? Nine if I'm smart about it."

"Smart how?"

"Don't go unbroken on the thrusters. Break at three and two. Stay on the bar for the pull-ups as long as I can." She's talking through it, building the strategy in real time. "The pull-ups will be fine early. It's the back half that'll wreck me. When my shoulders are cooked and I'm trying to hang on."

Chris nods. He doesn't train like she does—his workouts are steady, methodical, more about longevity than competition—but he understands the logic. He's seen her plan workouts before. He's seen her execute.

"When are you doing it?" he asks.

"Tomorrow. First thing."

"You want me there?"

She turns to look at him. His face is calm, open. It's an offer, not a demand. He's learned when to show up and when to let her have the space.

"Yeah," she says. "I'd like that."

His hand tightens briefly on her neck, then releases. "Then I'll be there."

---

She doesn't sleep well.

It's not anxiety, exactly. It's anticipation. Her body knows what's coming. She can feel it in the tightness of her hip flexors, the low hum of energy that won't settle. She runs through the workout in her head a dozen times, visualizing the bar path on the thrusters, the kip on the pull-ups, the transition between movements.

When her alarm goes off at 5:30 AM, she's already awake.

Chris is still asleep, one arm thrown over his face. She slips out of bed quietly, pulls on her sports bra and leggings, grabs her gym bag from the chair. In the kitchen, she makes coffee and downs half a protein shake. Her stomach's tight, but she needs fuel.

The gym's only ten minutes away, but she leaves early. She wants time to warm up. Time to get in her head.

When she pulls into the parking lot, Sara's car is already there.

Sara Whitaker—Elena's younger sister, one of the coaches at the gym, and the person who first convinced Kendra that she could actually be good at this. Sara's a former regionals competitor herself, lean and fierce with a no-bullshit approach to coaching that Kendra loves. She's also one of the few people who knows exactly how much this matters.

Inside, the gym smells like rubber and chalk and cold air. The overhead lights are harsh, fluorescent. The rig is set up in the center—pull-up bars gleaming, floor marked with tape for the barbell. Someone's already loaded a men's bar with ninety-five pounds. The women's bar sits beside it, sixty-five pounds racked and waiting.

Sara's at the whiteboard, writing out the workout in neat block letters. She glances over when Kendra walks in.

"Morning," Sara says. "You ready?"

"I don't know yet," Kendra admits. She drops her bag by the wall and starts pulling out her wrist wraps, her lifting belt. "Ask me in thirty minutes."

Sara smiles. "Fair."

There are a few other athletes warming up—two guys Kendra recognizes from the advanced class, and one woman she doesn't know, probably visiting from another gym. Everyone's quiet. Focused. The energy's different on Open workout days. It's not playful. It's not social.

It's work.

Kendra starts with a slow jog around the gym, then dynamic stretches—leg swings, arm circles, hip openers. Her body feels good. Loose. Strong. She moves to the barbell and starts with an empty bar, practicing the thruster movement. Squat clean, then press. Smooth. Controlled. She adds weight gradually—twenty-five pounds, then forty-five, then sixty-five.

The bar feels heavy, but manageable.

She moves to the pull-up bar and does a few easy reps. Chest to bar. Full extension at the bottom. Her shoulders feel solid. Her grip is good.

Sara walks over, arms crossed, watching. "How's it feel?"

"Fine," Kendra says. She drops from the bar and shakes out her hands. "I'm just trying not to overthink it."

"Good. Don't." Sara tilts her head toward the rig. "What's your strategy?"

"Break the thrusters at three and two. Stay on the bar for the pull-ups as long as I can. Try not to die in the first five minutes."

Sara laughs. "Solid plan. Stick to it. You've got the engine for this. Just don't let your ego take over."

Kendra nods. She knows what Sara means. It's easy to go out too hot, to chase a pace you can't sustain. The first two minutes always feel easy. It's minute six when everything falls apart.

Chris arrives at 6:45, just as Kendra's finishing her warm-up. He's in jeans and a hoodie, holding a travel mug of coffee. He gives her a small wave and settles onto one of the benches along the wall, out of the way.

She's grateful he's here. Grateful he doesn't make a big deal about it.

At 7:00 AM, Sara calls her over. "You're up."

Kendra walks to the barbell. The gym's quiet now. The other athletes have stopped to watch. It's part of the culture—when someone's doing their Open workout, you pause. You witness. You respect the effort.

Sara holds up her phone, camera ready. Every Open workout has to be recorded and submitted for validation. Kendra's done this before, but it still makes her heart kick harder.

"Ready?" Sara asks.

Kendra nods. She steps up to the bar. Her hands are chalked. Her wrists are wrapped. Her heart is pounding, but her breathing is steady.

"Three, two, one… go."

She pulls the barbell to her shoulders in one smooth clean, drops into a full squat, then drives up and presses overhead. One. She drops the bar and resets. Two. Three. Then she steps back, shakes out her arms, and hits two more. Five thrusters done.

She moves to the pull-up bar and jumps up. The first pull is easy. Chest to bar. Drop. Kip. Pull. She moves fast, efficient, and gets all ten reps unbroken.

Round one: done.

Back to the barbell.

The second round feels almost identical. The thrusters are heavy, but her legs are strong. The pull-ups are smooth. She finishes round two at 1:45 on the clock.

Third round. Fourth round. She's moving well. Her breath is hard but controlled. Sara's calling out her time after each round, steady and calm. "Three minutes. Four minutes."

By the fifth round, her shoulders are starting to burn.

She breaks the thrusters at three and two, just like she planned, but the transition to the bar feels slower. Her grip is still good, but her lats are tight. She gets eight pull-ups unbroken, then has to drop and do a quick two to finish.

Sixth round. Seven minutes on the clock. Her quads are screaming. The barbell feels heavier. She grinds through the thrusters, then moves to the bar. This time she only gets six pull-ups before she has to drop. She hangs for a second, shakes out her arms, jumps back up. Four more reps.

"Two minutes left!" Sara calls.

Kendra's heart is slamming against her ribs. Her lungs are on fire. She can hear Chris clapping from the bench, a steady rhythm. Someone else is counting her reps aloud now—one of the guys from the warm-up area.

Seventh round. She fights through the thrusters, each one slower than the last. Her press-out at the top is shaky. She gets to the pull-up bar and her hands are slipping. She only manages four reps before she drops. Jump. Three more. Drop. Jump. Three more.

"One minute!"

She's back at the barbell. Her vision is narrowing. Everything is noise and burn and effort. She gets three thrusters. Drops the bar. Gets two more. Moves to the bar.

She pulls. One. Two. Her grip is gone. She drops. Thirty seconds left.

She jumps back up. Pulls. Three. Four. Five.

"Time!" Sara calls.

Kendra drops from the bar and collapses forward, hands on her knees, gasping. Her whole body is shaking. Her shoulders feel like they've been filleted. Her lungs are raw.

But she's done.

Sara's there immediately, hand on her back. "Nice work. That was solid."

Kendra can't talk yet. She just nods, still trying to breathe. Chris walks over and hands her a water bottle. She takes it without looking up and drinks half of it in one go.

"How many rounds?" she finally asks, voice hoarse.

Sara checks her phone. "Seven rounds plus fifteen reps. That's damn good, Kendra."

Seven rounds and fifteen reps. In a ten-minute AMRAP, that's strong. Really strong. Kendra straightens up slowly, rolling her shoulders. Everything hurts, but it's the good kind of hurt. The kind that means she left it all out there.

Chris is grinning at her. "You looked like a machine."

She manages a tired smile. "I felt like I was dying."

"Yeah, but you didn't."

Sara's already pulling up the leaderboard on her phone, scrolling through the early results. It's only Friday morning—most people won't do the workout until Saturday or Sunday—but there are already a few hundred scores posted from around the world.

"Let's see," Sara murmurs, entering Kendra's score. She waits while the system processes it, then her eyes go wide. "Holy shit."

"What?" Kendra asks.

Sara turns the phone around. Kendra leans in, still breathing hard.

**Women's RX Division – South East Region**
**Rank: 82**

Eighty-two. Out of thousands of women in the region.

Kendra stares at the number. She blinks. Reads it again.

"That's—" She stops. She doesn't know what to say.

"Top one hundred," Sara says, her voice quiet but fierce. "That's Games-qualifier territory, Kendra. You know that, right?"

Kendra's throat tightens. She does know. She's watched the Open for years. She knows the cutoff lines. Top twenty in your region gets you to the Games. But being in the conversation—being anywhere near that number—feels impossible.

"It's only week one," she says, trying to keep her voice steady. "The scores will get better. People haven't done it yet."

"Sure," Sara agrees. "But you're in it. You're in the mix. That's what matters."

Chris wraps an arm around her shoulders, pulling her against his side. She's sweaty and shaking, but he doesn't care. "I'm proud of you," he says quietly, just for her.

She leans into him, lets herself feel it for a second. The exhaustion. The pride. The terrifying, exhilarating reality that maybe—maybe—this dream isn't as far away as she thought.

"Thanks," she whispers.

---

Later, after she's showered and changed, after Chris has gone to work and she's sitting alone in her apartment with a protein shake and her laptop, Kendra opens the leaderboard again.

Eighty-two.

The number hasn't changed, but more scores have come in. She scrolls through the names, looking at the numbers. Seven rounds plus twenty-two reps. Seven rounds plus eighteen reps. Eight rounds even.

The top women in the region are posting eight and nine rounds. Kendra's not there yet. But she's close. Closer than she's ever been.

Her phone buzzes. A text from Addison.

**Addison:** Heard you crushed it this morning. Proud of you.

Kendra smiles. Addison's been keeping tabs on her training. They've grown close over the past year—closer than Kendra ever expected. There's history between them, complicated and unresolved, but there's also something solid. Something like friendship, or maybe more than that.

**Kendra:** Thanks. Top 100 in the region. Feels surreal.

**Addison:** It's not surreal. It's earned. Keep going.

Kendra stares at the message for a long moment. Then she types back:

**Kendra:** I will.

She sets the phone down and closes her laptop. Her body aches. Her shoulders are going to be wrecked tomorrow. But she doesn't care.

She's in it now. The dream isn't just a dream anymore.

It's real.

**End of Chapter 5**
