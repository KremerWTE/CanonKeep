# Chapter 10: Grace's First Communion Prep

**POV:** Elena Barrett
**Location:** St. Mary's Catholic Church, Charlotte, NC
**Timeline:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The parish office smelled like old books and lemon furniture polish.

Elena sat in one of the burgundy chairs facing Father Thomas's desk, hands folded in her lap, while Grace fidgeted beside her and Jasper checked his phone for the third time in five minutes. The priest was running late—something about a hospital visit—and the silence in the small room felt heavier than it should.

"Mom," Grace whispered, tugging on Elena's sleeve. "How much longer?"

"Soon, sweetheart."

"But I'm bored."

"Then practice being patient," Elena said, the same thing her own mother used to say. Grace slumped dramatically in her chair, and Elena hid a smile.

Jasper glanced up from his phone. "Sorry. Addison needed—"

"It's fine," Elena said, though they both knew it wasn't entirely true. He was here. That was what mattered. The phone could wait.

Jasper seemed to realize it too, because he pocketed the device and reached over to ruffle Grace's hair. "You excited about First Communion?"

Grace brightened immediately. "I get to wear a white dress. Emma says it's like being a tiny bride, but for Jesus."

"That's... one way to put it," Jasper said, mouth twitching.

"And I get to have a party after, right?" Grace looked between them. "With cake?"

"Yes," Elena confirmed. "With cake."

"Can Addie come?"

"Of course Addie can come," Elena said. "She's family."

"What about Hawk? And Kendra?"

"Everyone you want can come, Gracie. It's your day."

Grace seemed satisfied with that answer, returning her attention to the children's Bible she'd pulled from her bag—a worn copy with a purple ribbon bookmark that she'd been reading every night before bed since they'd started First Communion prep classes.

Elena watched her daughter trace the illustrations with one finger, lips moving silently as she read. Eight years old and already so serious about her faith. It reminded Elena of herself at that age—sitting in Sunday school, asking her teacher a thousand questions about saints and miracles and why God let bad things happen.

Some questions never really got answered. You just learned to live with the mystery.

The door opened, and Father Thomas appeared—a man in his early sixties with silver hair and kind eyes, wearing the black clerical shirt and white collar that always made Grace stare.

"Sorry to keep you waiting," he said, settling into his chair. "Mrs. Henderson needed last rites. I couldn't leave her."

"Of course," Elena said. "We understand."

Father Thomas smiled, then turned his attention to Grace. "And how is my star student?"

Grace sat up straighter. "Good. I finished reading about the loaves and fishes."

"Excellent. What did you think?"

"That Jesus is really good at magic."

Father Thomas's eyes crinkled. "More like miracles, but close enough."

He opened a folder on his desk—Grace's name written neatly on the tab—and pulled out a few forms. Elena recognized the prep checklist they'd been working through: baptismal certificate, parish registration, attendance records for the weekly classes.

"Grace has been doing wonderfully in her formation classes," Father Thomas said. "Sister Margaret says she asks the best questions."

Grace beamed.

"But there's one thing we still need to finalize before the ceremony," the priest continued. "Grace will need a sponsor—someone to stand with her, support her faith journey, be a spiritual guide."

Elena nodded. They'd talked about this before. Most families chose godparents, but Grace's godparents—Jasper's college friend and Elena's cousin—lived out of state. It made more sense to choose someone local, someone who'd actually be present in Grace's life.

"We've been thinking about that," Jasper said. "And we wanted to ask—"

Elena put a hand on his arm, stopping him. An idea had been forming in the back of her mind for weeks now, but she hadn't voiced it yet. Hadn't been sure if it was crazy or brilliant.

Now, sitting here with Father Thomas watching them expectantly and Grace humming quietly to herself, Elena made a decision.

"What about Addison?" she said.

Jasper blinked. "Addie?"

"She's family," Elena said. "Grace adores her. She'd take the responsibility seriously."

"She's not even Catholic," Jasper pointed out.

"She could be."

The room went quiet. Father Thomas leaned back in his chair, steepling his fingers, watching the exchange with interest. Grace had stopped humming, her attention now fully on the adults.

"Elena," Jasper said carefully. "You're talking about RCIA. That's a year-long process."

"I know what it is."

"And you think Addie would be interested?"

Elena turned to face him fully. "I think Addie has been searching for something to ground her for years. I think she's been part of our family in every way that matters. And I think asking her to be Grace's sponsor—to walk this journey with her—might be exactly what she needs."

Jasper stared at her, something shifting in his expression. Surprise, maybe. Or recognition.

"You've been thinking about this for a while," he said.

"Since Grace started prep classes."

Father Thomas cleared his throat gently. "Mrs. Barrett, I appreciate the sentiment. But sponsors need to be practicing Catholics who've received all their sacraments. If Miss Price isn't confirmed—"

"She will be," Elena said. "By the time Grace makes her First Communion next spring, Addison will be ready."

"RCIA meets every Wednesday night," Father Thomas said. "It's a significant commitment."

"Addison doesn't do anything halfway," Elena said. "If she agrees, she'll show up."

The priest studied her for a long moment, then nodded slowly. "Alright. If Miss Price is interested, I'd be happy to speak with her about entering the catechumenate."

"Thank you, Father."

"Can I pick her dress?" Grace asked suddenly.

Everyone turned to look at her.

"What dress, sweetheart?" Elena asked.

"Addie's dress. For when she gets baptized. Can I help pick it?"

Elena's throat tightened unexpectedly. Of course Grace had been listening. Of course she'd already jumped ahead to the image of Addison standing in the baptismal font, white dress and all.

"We'll see," Elena managed. "First we have to ask her."

"She'll say yes," Grace said with complete certainty. "Addie loves me."

"She does," Jasper agreed quietly. He was looking at Elena now, something warm and wondering in his eyes. "She really does."

---

They finished the meeting going over logistics—dates, times, what Grace needed to bring to her final prep retreat. Father Thomas gave them a book on preparing children for the sacrament, along with a pamphlet about RCIA that Elena tucked into her purse.

In the parking lot, Grace ran ahead to the car, leaving Elena and Jasper alone.

"You ambushed me in there," Jasper said, but he was smiling.

"I did," Elena agreed. "Sorry."

"No you're not."

"Not even a little bit."

Jasper shook his head, unlocking the car. "Do you really think she'll say yes?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Elena paused, considering. "Because Addie's spent her whole life building walls. And we've spent the last year convincing her she doesn't need them—not with us. This is the next step. Letting her belong, officially. Letting her be part of something bigger than crisis management and quarterly earnings."

"Faith is a big leap for someone like Addie."

"Maybe," Elena said. "Or maybe she's been looking for permission to believe in something good."

Jasper studied her face, then reached out to tuck a strand of hair behind her ear. "You're really good at this."

"At what?"

"Seeing what people need before they know they need it."

Elena's chest warmed. "I learned from watching you."

"I'm serious, Elena. You saw that I needed to come home before I'd admitted it to myself. You're seeing that Addie needs roots. You're—" He paused, searching for words. "You're the heart of this whole operation. You know that, right?"

Elena felt tears prick her eyes, unexpected and sharp. She blinked them back, laughing softly. "Hormones. Still not fully balanced after Lucas."

"Liar," Jasper said gently. He kissed her forehead, then her mouth. "Call her tonight. Before you lose your nerve."

"I'm not nervous."

"You're terrified," Jasper said. "But you'll do it anyway. Because that's who you are."

He was right. She was terrified.

But she'd do it anyway.

---

That night, after Grace was asleep and Lucas was down for his longest stretch, Elena sat on the back porch with her phone in her hand and a glass of wine she hadn't touched.

The contact was already pulled up: Addison Price.

Elena had texted Addie a thousand times. Called her dozens. They'd had late-night conversations about work and marriage and the messy complexity of loving people who didn't always know how to love you back.

But this felt different. Bigger.

She pressed call before she could talk herself out of it.

Addie picked up on the second ring. "Hey. Everything okay?"

"Everything's fine," Elena said. "Why does everyone assume something's wrong when I call?"

"Because you usually text," Addie said. "Calling means someone's either bleeding or in labor."

Elena laughed despite herself. "Fair point. But no bleeding. No labor. Just... I wanted to talk to you about something."

There was a pause, then the sound of movement—Addie shifting, probably sitting up from wherever she'd been sprawled on her couch. "Okay. You have my attention."

Elena took a breath. "Grace has her First Communion in the spring."

"I know. She hasn't stopped talking about the dress."

"She needs a sponsor. Someone to stand with her during the ceremony, support her faith journey, be a spiritual mentor."

"Okay..." Addie's voice was cautious now, like she was trying to figure out where this was going.

"We want to ask you," Elena said.

Silence. Long enough that Elena checked to make sure the call hadn't dropped.

"Addie?"

"I'm here." Addie's voice was different now—smaller, uncertain in a way Elena had rarely heard. "Elena, I'm not... I'm not Catholic."

"I know."

"I'm not even sure I'm particularly religious."

"I know that too."

"Then why—"

"Because Grace loves you," Elena said simply. "Because you're family. And because I think this might be something you didn't know you were looking for."

Another pause. Elena could picture Addie's face—the walls going up, the analytical mind cataloging all the reasons this was a terrible idea.

"RCIA is a year-long program," Addie said finally.

"I know."

"Wednesday nights."

"I know."

"Elena, I barely have time to breathe between BSS projects and—"

"Addie." Elena kept her voice gentle. "I'm not asking you to solve a crisis or manage a client. I'm asking you to explore something that might matter. For you. Not for Grace, not for us. For *you*."

"I don't..." Addie trailed off. Elena heard her exhale, long and shaky. "I don't know if I'd be any good at it."

"At faith?"

"At any of it. Being a sponsor. Being... whatever it is you think I could be."

Elena's throat tightened. "Addie, you're already good at it. You just don't see it yet."

"See what?"

"That Grace looks at you and sees someone brave. Someone strong. Someone who shows up even when it's hard." Elena paused. "You've been her Tiger Aunt since the zoo trip. This is just making it official."

Addie let out a sound that might have been a laugh or might have been something closer to a sob. "Tiger Aunt. God, she still calls me that."

"She does. And she will for the rest of her life, probably."

"Elena, I—" Addie's voice cracked. "What if I screw this up?"

"You won't."

"You don't know that."

"I do," Elena said firmly. "Because I know you. And I know that when you commit to something—really commit—you don't do it halfway."

There was a long silence. Elena waited, heart pounding, wondering if she'd pushed too hard, asked too much.

Then Addie said quietly, "What would I have to do?"

Elena felt relief flood through her. "Wednesday night classes starting next month. Father Thomas leads them—he's kind, patient, good at explaining things. You'd go through the whole catechism, learn about the sacraments, figure out what you believe."

"And at the end?"

"At the Easter Vigil, you'd be baptized and confirmed. And then you'd stand with Grace for her First Communion six weeks later."

Addie was quiet again. Elena could almost hear her thinking—weighing variables, testing scenarios, building contingency plans the way she did with every BSS crisis.

But this wasn't a crisis. This was an invitation.

"Okay," Addie said finally.

"Okay?"

"Okay." Addie's voice was stronger now, more certain. "I'll do it. For Grace. And for... yeah. I'll do it."

Elena closed her eyes, relief and joy and something deeper washing over her. "Thank you."

"Don't thank me yet. I'll probably show up to class and argue with Father Thomas about transubstantiation or something."

"I'd expect nothing less."

Addie laughed—a real one this time, warm and genuine. "You know this is crazy, right?"

"Completely."

"Jasper must think I've lost my mind."

"Jasper thinks it's perfect," Elena said. "He just didn't want to push."

"And you have no problem pushing."

"Not when it matters," Elena agreed. "And this matters, Addie. To Grace. To us. To you."

Another pause, softer this time.

"I've never had a goddaughter before," Addie said quietly.

"Sponsor," Elena corrected gently. "But yes. You'll be her spiritual guide. Her faith mentor."

"That's terrifying."

"Welcome to parenthood," Elena said. "It's terrifying for everyone."

Addie laughed again, and Elena felt the tension finally break. They talked for another twenty minutes—logistics, dates, what Addie should expect from her first RCIA session. By the time they hung up, Elena's wine was warm and the stars were out and she felt lighter than she had in weeks.

Jasper appeared in the doorway, backlit by the kitchen light. "Well?"

"She said yes."

He crossed the porch, settling into the chair beside her. "You're crying."

"I'm not—" Elena touched her face, surprised to find tears. "Okay, maybe a little."

Jasper pulled her close, and Elena let herself lean into him, breathing in the familiar scent of his cologne and the detergent they used and home.

"Grace is going to lose her mind," Jasper said.

"I know."

"And Addie's going to panic at least three times before the first class."

"I know that too."

Jasper kissed the top of her head. "You did good, Elena."

Elena smiled against his shoulder. "We'll see. Ask me again after Addie's had to memorize the Apostles' Creed."

"She'll nail it in a week," Jasper said confidently. "She memorized the entire BSS operations manual in three days when we first started. The Creed's got nothing on her."

Elena laughed, pulling back to look at him. "Think she'll actually find something in it? Faith, I mean. Not just go through the motions for Grace."

Jasper considered. "I think Addie's been looking for something to believe in her whole life. She just didn't know it could be this."

"This?"

"Family. Community. Something bigger than herself that isn't about winning or losing." He paused. "Grace is just the excuse. What Addie's really searching for is permission to hope."

Elena's chest tightened. "When did you get so wise?"

"I had a good teacher," Jasper said, pulling her close again.

They sat like that for a long time, wrapped up in each other, the night soft and warm around them. Inside, Lucas made a small sound through the monitor—not quite crying, just shifting in his sleep. Grace's nightlight glowed from her window upstairs.

Their family. Their home. Their life.

And now Addie would be woven into it even deeper—not just as Jasper's partner or Elena's friend, but as Grace's sponsor, Lucas's aunt, part of the fabric of their faith and their future.

It felt right. Scary, but right.

The best things usually were.

**End of Chapter 10**
