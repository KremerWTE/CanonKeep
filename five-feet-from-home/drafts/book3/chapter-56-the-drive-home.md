# Chapter 56: The Drive Home

**POV:** Addison
**Location:** Blue Ridge Mountains to Charlotte
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

Addison can feel Kendra watching her.

Not constantly. Not obviously. But in the rearview mirror, every few minutes, their eyes meet. A glance. A heartbeat. Then both of them look away, pretending it didn't happen.

It's been happening for the last hour.

In the backseat, Harper's deep into wedding planning mode, talking Sara's ear off about venues and photographers and whether a spring wedding is too cliche. Sara's responding with the enthusiasm of someone who's genuinely interested, asking questions about budgets and guest counts and color schemes.

Addison should be listening. Should be engaged. Harper's one of her closest friends, and this is a big deal.

But all she can think about is last night.

The firelight. The confession. The way Kendra looked at her when she said *I don't know how to go back.*

Addison's hands tighten on the steering wheel.

She shouldn't have said what she said. Shouldn't have admitted that New York meant something. That it meant *everything*. It was selfish. Reckless. The kind of emotional honesty that complicates things beyond repair.

But she couldn't help it.

She's spent weeks—months, really—trying to shove her feelings into a box labeled *friendship* and *not complicated*. Trying to convince herself that what happened in New York was a one-time thing. A mistake. Something they could both walk away from.

But Kendra's eyes in the rearview mirror say otherwise.

And Addison's too tired to keep pretending.

"Addie, you're being quiet," Harper says from the backseat.

Addison blinks, refocusing. "Sorry. Just concentrating on the road."

"You've driven this route a hundred times," Harper says. "What's really going on?"

"Nothing."

"Bullshit." Harper leans forward, poking her head between the front seats. "You've been weird all weekend. So has Kendra. What happened?"

"Nothing happened," Kendra says quickly.

Too quickly.

Harper's eyes narrow. She looks between them, and Addison can practically see the gears turning. Harper's too smart for her own good. Too observant. If they're not careful, she's going to piece it together.

"We're fine," Addison says firmly. "Just tired."

Harper sits back, unconvinced. But she doesn't push.

Sara, bless her, changes the subject. "So, Addie, are you bringing anyone to the wedding? Or are you going stag?"

Addison almost laughs. "I don't have a date, Sara."

"You could ask someone," Sara says. "There's still time."

"Who would I ask?"

"I don't know. That guy from the gym? The one who's always asking you out?"

"Marcus?" Addison shakes her head. "Not interested."

"Why not? He's cute."

"He's fine. I'm just not interested."

Harper's watching her in the rearview mirror now, her expression thoughtful. Addison keeps her eyes on the road.

In the passenger seat, Kendra's staring out the window, jaw tight.

The car goes quiet.

---

They drop Sara off first.

Her house is in a neighborhood just outside downtown Charlotte—older homes with big yards, the kind of place where kids ride bikes in the street and neighbors actually know each other's names. Sara's husband, Mike, is on the porch with their youngest, a toddler who immediately starts shrieking "Mama!" when he sees the car.

Sara grins. "That's my cue."

She grabs her bag from the trunk and gives everyone quick hugs. "Thanks for this weekend. We need to do it again soon."

"Absolutely," Harper says.

"Next time, I'm picking the menu," Kendra adds. "No more burned chicken."

Sara laughs. "Deal."

She heads up the walk, scooping her son into her arms and kissing her husband on the cheek. It's achingly domestic. Achingly normal.

Addison wonders what that feels like.

Harper climbs into the front seat as Addison pulls away. Kendra's in the back now, and the shift in configuration feels significant somehow. Like they're already rearranging themselves, preparing to go back to their separate lives.

"Ethan's picking me up at my place," Harper says, buckling her seatbelt. "He wants to show me something for the wedding. I think it's cake samples."

"Cake samples sound good," Addison says.

"Right? I'm not complaining." Harper glances back at Kendra. "What about you? Chris picking you up?"

"No, he's at work," Kendra says. "I'm just heading home."

Harper nods, and they fall into silence again.

Addison drives on autopilot, navigating the familiar streets. The mountains are behind them now, replaced by strip malls and traffic lights and the steady hum of city life. It feels like coming down from a high. Like reality reasserting itself.

When they pull up in front of Harper's building, Harper doesn't get out right away.

"Okay," she says, turning to look at both of them. "I'm going to say this once, and then I'm going to drop it. But something's going on with you two. I don't know what, and I'm not going to pry. But if you need to talk—either of you—I'm here. Okay?"

Addison's throat tightens. "Thanks, Harper."

"We're fine," Kendra says from the backseat. "Really."

Harper studies them for another moment. Then she nods. "Okay. But the offer stands."

She climbs out, grabs her suitcase, and heads inside.

And then it's just Addison and Kendra.

---

The silence is deafening.

Addison pulls away from the curb and heads toward Kendra's apartment. It's only ten minutes away. Ten minutes alone together in a confined space.

Ten minutes to say something. Or nothing.

Addison keeps her eyes on the road.

Kendra's quiet in the backseat. Addison can feel her presence like a physical weight. Can feel the tension radiating off her.

"You can sit up front," Addison says finally. "If you want."

"I'm fine back here."

Addison nods. Of course she is.

They drive in silence. Past coffee shops and grocery stores and the gym where Kendra trains. Past the park where they've met up for runs on Sunday mornings. Past all the markers of their normal life—the life they're about to return to.

"Addie," Kendra says quietly.

Addison's hands tighten on the wheel. "Yeah?"

"I don't know what I'm doing."

Addison glances in the rearview mirror. Kendra's staring out the window, her reflection ghostly in the glass.

"Me neither," Addison admits.

"Chris is a good guy."

"I know."

"He loves me. He wants to marry me someday. Build a life together."

Addison's chest aches. "I know, Ken."

"So why can't I stop thinking about you?"

The question hangs in the air. Raw. Desperate.

Addison doesn't have an answer.

She pulls up in front of Kendra's building and puts the car in park. Turns in her seat to face her.

Kendra's eyes are red-rimmed. She's not crying, but she's close.

"I don't know," Addison says quietly. "But I think... I think you need to figure out what you want. Not what Chris wants. Not what you think you *should* want. What you actually want."

Kendra's laugh is bitter. "And what if I don't know?"

"Then you take time to figure it out."

"And what about you?" Kendra's voice cracks. "What do you want, Addie?"

Addison wants to say it. Wants to be honest. Wants to tell Kendra that she wants her—wants to know what it would be like to build something together, to stop hiding and pretending and dancing around the truth.

But saying it out loud would be cruel.

Because Kendra's not free. She's with Chris. And Addison's not going to be the person who asks her to blow up her life on a maybe.

"I want you to be happy," Addison says instead. "Whatever that looks like."

Kendra stares at her. "That's not an answer."

"It's the only one I can give you."

Kendra's face crumples, just for a second. Then she schools her expression, climbing out of the car and grabbing her bag from the trunk.

She stands on the sidewalk, looking back at Addison through the window.

Addison rolls it down.

"See you around?" Kendra says.

Addison nods. "Yeah. See you around."

Kendra lifts her hand in a small wave. Then she turns and heads inside.

Addison watches until she disappears through the front door. Then she sits there, engine idling, staring at nothing.

Her phone buzzes. A text from Harper.

**Harper:** You okay?

Addison types back: **Yeah. Just tired.**

**Harper:** Bullshit. But I love you anyway. Call me if you need me.

Addison sets the phone down and pulls away from the curb.

The drive to her own apartment is short. By the time she parks, the sun's setting, painting the sky in shades of pink and orange. It's beautiful. The kind of sunset people post on Instagram with captions about gratitude and new beginnings.

Addison feels none of those things.

She feels tired. Confused. Like she's standing at a crossroads with no map and no idea which direction to go.

Inside her apartment, everything's exactly as she left it. Clean. Organized. Empty.

She drops her bag by the door and sinks onto the couch.

Her phone buzzes again. This time it's a text from Kendra.

**Kendra:** Thanks for the weekend. For everything.

Addison stares at the message for a long time. Then she types back:

**Addison:** Anytime.

She sets the phone down and leans back, closing her eyes.

The cabin trip is over. They're back in Charlotte. Back to their normal lives.

But nothing feels normal.

And Addison has no idea how to fix it.

---

That night, Addison lies in bed and stares at the ceiling.

She thinks about Kendra. About the way she looked by the fire, her confession hanging between them. About the way their eyes met in the rearview mirror, over and over, like they couldn't help it.

She thinks about Chris. About the fact that he's a good guy, a steady guy, the kind of guy Kendra deserves.

She thinks about herself. About what she wants. About whether she's brave enough to admit it.

And she thinks about what comes next.

Because something has to give. They can't keep doing this—pretending they're just friends while the truth burns between them. It's not fair to Kendra. It's not fair to Chris.

And it's sure as hell not fair to Addison.

But what's the alternative?

Addison doesn't know.

All she knows is that the cabin trip changed something. They stopped pretending. Stopped hiding.

And now they have to figure out what that means.

One day at a time.

One conversation at a time.

One impossible choice at a time.

Addison closes her eyes and tries to sleep.

But all she sees is Kendra's face in the firelight, saying the words Addison's been too afraid to admit.

*I don't know how to go back.*

Neither does Addison.

And that terrifies her more than anything.

---

The week after the cabin trip, Addison throws herself into work.

She coaches back-to-back sessions at the gym. She revamps her training programs. She takes on new clients. She stays busy from the moment she wakes up until she collapses into bed at night.

It helps. Mostly.

But every time her phone buzzes, her heart jumps.

Every time she sees Kendra's name in her contacts, she has to resist the urge to call.

Every time she passes the coffee shop where they used to meet, she feels the absence like a physical ache.

Harper notices. Of course she does.

"You're avoiding something," Harper says one afternoon, cornering Addison in the BSS break room. "Or someone."

"I'm not avoiding anything," Addison says, pouring coffee she doesn't need.

"Addie." Harper's voice is gentle. "I'm your friend. Talk to me."

Addison sets the coffee pot down carefully. "There's nothing to talk about."

"Is it about Kendra?"

Addison's heart stops. "What?"

"You two have been weird since the cabin trip. Actually, since before that. Since New York." Harper crosses her arms. "Did something happen?"

Addison could lie. Could deflect. Could tell Harper it's nothing and hope she drops it.

But she's so tired of lying.

"I don't know what happened," Addison says quietly. "That's the problem."

Harper's expression softens. "Do you want to talk about it?"

Addison shakes her head. "Not yet. Maybe not ever. I don't know."

Harper nods. "Okay. But when you do—if you do—I'm here."

"Thanks, Harper."

Harper pulls her into a hug. "I love you, you know. Whatever's going on, we'll figure it out."

Addison hugs her back, throat tight. "I know."

But when Harper leaves, Addison's alone again.

With her thoughts. With her feelings. With the impossible situation she's in.

And no idea how to get out.

---

**End of Chapter 56**
