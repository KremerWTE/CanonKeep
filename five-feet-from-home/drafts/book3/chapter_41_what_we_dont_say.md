# Chapter 41: What We Don't Say

**POV: Kendra**
**Location: Charlotte**

The candles on the table flicker between us, casting shadows across Chris's face as he reaches for my hand. Six months. Half a year of Friday night dinners and Sunday morning coffee runs and texts that make me smile in the middle of brutal workouts.

"To us," he says, raising his glass.

I clink mine against his, the wine catching the candlelight. "To us."

The restaurant hums with conversation around us — couples celebrating anniversaries, families gathered for birthdays, the comfortable noise of people who belong to each other. Chris belongs to me now, I think. Or I belong to him. Maybe both.

"I got you something," he says, pulling a small box from his jacket pocket.

"Chris, you didn't have to—"

"I wanted to." He slides it across the table.

Inside is a delicate silver bracelet, a tiny barbell charm dangling from the chain. I laugh despite myself.

"So you never forget where we met," he says, grinning. "Though I'm pretty sure you could never forget a guy who couldn't do a pull-up to save his life."

"You can do three now," I remind him, fastening the bracelet around my wrist. The weight of it is pleasant, real. "That's progress."

"Thanks to my very patient trainer." His thumb traces circles on my palm. "I love you, you know that?"

"I know." I squeeze his hand. "I love you too."

And I do. That's the strange part — it's not a lie. I love his kindness, his terrible jokes, the way he looks at me like I hung the moon. I love how he shows up, how he remembers small things, how he's integrated himself into my life with an ease that surprised me.

He's met my parents — survived my dad's interrogation and my mom's pointed questions about his intentions. He's been to countless BSS gatherings, learned everyone's names, brought beer and asked interested questions about lifts and PRs. He fits.

Everyone except one person.

"What are you thinking about?" Chris asks, reading something in my expression.

"Nothing," I say automatically. "Just happy."

We finish dinner, split a dessert we're both too full for, drive back to my apartment with his hand on my thigh. Everything is good. Easy. Right.

So why does it feel like something's missing?

---

The BSS gathering is at Marcus and Jen's place the following weekend — their backyard transformed into a summer barbecue paradise with string lights and a playlist that's heavy on early 2000s throwbacks. Chris is at the grill with Marcus and Dev, debating the proper way to cook a burger while Jen rolls her eyes at me.

"Men and their meat," she says, handing me a beer. "Like it's rocket science."

"Let them have their moment," I say, scanning the yard.

There. By the cooler, laughing at something Sara just said. Addison.

She's wearing cutoff shorts and a faded tank top, her hair pulled back in a messy bun that somehow looks effortless. When she laughs, her whole face lights up, and something in my chest tightens.

We haven't talked much lately. Not really. Surface-level conversations at these gatherings — "Hey, how's training?" "Good, you?" — but nothing deeper. Nothing real. The easy friendship we'd built has developed fault lines I don't know how to cross.

"Kendra!" Grace appears at my elbow, tugging on my arm. "Come play cornhole with us!"

"In a minute, kiddo," I say, but she's already dragging me toward where Jasper has set up the boards.

"Teams," Grace announces with the authority of someone who's clearly been planning this. "You and Daddy versus me and Addie!"

Addison looks up, catches my eye, and something flickers across her face before she schools it into neutrality. "If that's okay with Kendra."

"Sure," I manage. "Prepare to lose, though."

"Big talk," Addison says, and there's a hint of her old teasing tone. "Grace, show them what we've got."

We play. Grace is enthusiastic but terrible, her beanbags sailing everywhere except the board. Addison is better than I expected — precise, focused, landing three bags in a row. Jasper provides color commentary that makes Grace giggle, and I try to ignore how aware I am of Addison's presence, the way she moves, the sound of her laugh.

Chris appears with fresh beers halfway through, dropping a kiss on my head. "Winning?"

"Barely," I admit.

He stays to watch, his arm around my waist, and I see Addison's eyes flick to where we're standing before she turns her attention back to the game.

We lose by two points. Grace celebrates like she won an Olympic gold medal, jumping on Addison for a hug that nearly topples them both. Addison catches her, spinning her around, and the pure joy on both their faces makes my chest ache.

"Good game," Jasper says, offering his hand. "Rematch next time?"

"You're on," I say.

Addison sets Grace down, ruffles her hair. "Go find your mom, troublemaker. I think she was looking for you."

Grace scampers off, and suddenly it's just the three of us — me and Chris and Addison, standing in an awkward triangle.

"So," Jasper says, reading the room. "I'm gonna go... check on burgers."

He escapes. Coward.

"Chris, right?" Addison extends her hand. "I don't think we've officially met. Addison."

"Chris." He shakes her hand, smiling his easy smile. "I've heard a lot about you."

"Have you?" Her eyes cut to me, unreadable.

"All good things," he assures her. "Kendra says you're an amazing godmother to Grace."

"Grace is easy to love," Addison says simply.

An uncomfortable silence stretches. I should say something, fill the gap, but my mind is blank.

"Well," Chris says eventually. "I should go rescue those burgers from whatever Marcus is doing to them. Nice meeting you, Addison."

"You too."

He leaves, and then it's just us. Just me and Addison and six months of things unsaid.

"So that's Chris," she says finally.

"Yeah. That's Chris."

"He seems nice."

"He is nice." I cross my arms. "Really nice, actually."

"Good." Addison nods, looking somewhere past my shoulder. "You deserve nice."

"Addie—"

"I should go help with the food," she interrupts. "Good seeing you, Kendra."

She walks away before I can respond, and I'm left standing there, holding a beanbag I didn't realize I'd picked up.

---

Later, after dark, after too many beers and not enough food, I find myself on Marcus and Jen's back porch, escaping the noise inside. The string lights cast everything in warm amber, and I can hear laughter from the living room, the bass line of whatever song just came on.

The door opens behind me. I expect Chris, but it's Sara.

"Mind if I join you?"

"Free country," I say.

She settles into the chair next to mine, nursing what looks like water. We sit in comfortable silence for a moment.

"So," she says eventually. "Six months with Chris."

"Six months," I confirm.

"You happy?"

"Yeah. I am."

"That wasn't very convincing."

I glance at her. Sara has this way of seeing through bullshit — comes with the territory of being a therapist, I guess. Or maybe she's just observant.

"I am happy," I say again, more firmly. "Chris is great. We're good together."

"But?"

"There's no but."

"Kendra." She gives me a look. "I've watched you stare at Addison all night like she's a puzzle you can't solve."

My stomach drops. "I don't—"

"It's okay," Sara says gently. "I'm not judging. I'm just... I see things, you know? And I see you seeing her."

"We're friends," I say weakly. "Or we were. I don't know what we are anymore."

"Maybe that's the problem." Sara takes a sip of her water. "Not knowing."

"There's nothing to know. She's Grace's godmother. She's Jasper's friend. She's... Addie."

"And what's Addie to you?"

The question hangs in the air between us. What is Addison to me? Friend doesn't feel right anymore, hasn't felt right for a while. But I don't have another word for it — for the way my heart picks up when she walks into a room, for the way I'm always aware of where she is, for the complicated knot of feelings I've been trying not to examine too closely.

"I don't know," I admit finally.

Sara reaches over, squeezes my hand. "That's honest, at least."

"I'm with Chris," I say, like that settles it.

"You are," she agrees. "And that's a choice you made. But Kendra? Eventually you're going to have to figure out what you're choosing and what you're running from."

Before I can respond, the door opens again. This time it is Chris, his face lit with that familiar smile.

"There you are," he says. "We're doing karaoke. Marcus has a whole setup. You have to come sing with me."

"I don't sing," I protest.

"Come on. It'll be fun." He holds out his hand.

I look at Sara. She gives me a small, knowing smile and a slight nod.

I take Chris's hand and let him pull me inside, back into the warmth and noise and laughter. Back into the life I've built, the relationship I've chosen, the safe and good and uncomplicated thing.

Through the crowd, I catch a glimpse of Addison. She's watching us, and when our eyes meet, something passes between us — a recognition, maybe, or a goodbye to something that never was.

Then she looks away, and the moment breaks.

Chris pulls me toward the karaoke setup, already scrolling through songs, debating between Journey and Bon Jovi like it's the most important decision in the world. I laugh at something he says, lean into his side, try to be present.

But part of me is still on that porch with Sara, stuck on her question.

What is Addison to me?

I don't know. But I'm starting to think I should figure it out before it's too late to matter.

---

That night, after Chris drops me off with a kiss that tastes like beer and promises of brunch tomorrow, I lie in bed and stare at the ceiling. The bracelet on my wrist catches the moonlight from the window — that tiny barbell charm, a symbol of where Chris and I started.

Six months. A good six months. So why do I feel like I'm mourning something I never had?

My phone buzzes. A text from Chris: *Had the best time tonight. Love you.*

I type back: *Love you too.*

And I do. I really do.

Just maybe not in the way I should.

I scroll through my photos, not meaning to look for anything in particular, but my thumb stops on one from a few months ago. Me and Addison at the gym, post-workout, both of us sweaty and laughing at something I can't remember now. Her arm is around my shoulders. I'm leaning into her like it's the most natural thing in the world.

We look like we belong together.

I stare at the photo for a long time before I make myself close it, put the phone down, close my eyes.

Tomorrow I have training. Next week, Chris and I are planning a trip to the mountains. In August, I leave for Madison, for the Games, for the biggest competition of my life.

I have a full, good life. A relationship with a man who loves me. Friends, family, purpose.

So why does it feel like something essential is missing?

Why does it feel like I lost something I never even named?

In the darkness of my room, with the weight of the bracelet on my wrist and the echo of Addison's laugh in my memory, I finally let myself acknowledge the truth I've been avoiding:

I don't know what Addison is to me. But I know that what we were — undefined, unnamed, carefully platonic — has become an undefined loss.

And I don't know how to grieve something that never officially existed.

The thought follows me into sleep, a question with no answer:

What do you call it when you lose someone who was never yours to begin with?
