# Chapter 48: Addison's Confirmation

*POV: Addison*

*Location: Charlotte Church*

---

The white dress feels too clean.

I smooth my hands down the fabric for the third time in as many minutes, standing in the vestibule of St. Patrick's Cathedral while organ music filters through the heavy wooden doors. Grace is beside me in her own white dress—smaller, simpler than the one she wore for her First Communion two months ago, but still special. Still hers.

She's holding my hand.

"You look pretty," she whispers, and I wonder if she can feel how sweaty my palm is.

"So do you." I squeeze her fingers gently. "Are you ready?"

She nods, solemn. Then, because she's seven and cannot be solemn for long: "I'm your sponsor. That's really important."

"It is," I say. "The most important."

It's the truth. When Father Mike asked who I wanted as my sponsor, I could've chosen Elena. Or Jasper. Or even Mel, who'd driven down from New York just for this. But Grace had looked at me with those wide brown eyes—Elena's eyes—and said, "I could do it. If you want." And something in my chest had cracked open.

You were mine, I'd thought. Now I'm yours.

The doors open. Mrs. Chen, the music director, catches my eye and nods.

It's time.

---

The cathedral is smaller than I expected. Not in actual size—the vaulted ceilings still stretch impossibly high, the stained glass still throws colored light across the pews—but in feeling. Like it's been built to a human scale, for human hearts.

I can see everyone as Grace and I process down the aisle. Jasper in the third row, holding baby Noah, who's miraculously quiet. Elena beside him, dabbing at her eyes with a tissue. Mel and Jordan together, Mel giving me a thumbs up that makes Jordan elbow her. Coach Sarah. Dr. Okonkwo and his wife. Half the BSS morning class, still in their gym clothes because they came straight from the six AM session.

Kendra's not here.

Of course she's not. She's in Madison, Wisconsin, where the temperature is probably perfect and the competition is fierce and she's doing exactly what she's supposed to be doing. Exactly what she's worked for.

I knew she wouldn't be here. I've known for months.

It still feels like there's a Kendra-shaped hole in the third pew.

Grace tugs my hand. I refocus. One foot in front of the other. The bishop is at the altar in his vestments, and Father Mike is beside him, beaming like a proud parent. I take my place with the other confirmands—there are five of us, all adults, all wearing white—and Grace stands next to me, her hand still in mine.

---

The Mass moves around me in familiar rhythms. I've been coming to St. Patrick's for eight months now, every Sunday at nine AM, often with Grace tucked against my side when Elena and Jasper bring the kids. I know when to stand, when to sit, when to kneel. I know the responses by heart.

But today feels different.

Today, I'm not just observing. I'm *entering*.

Father Mike gives the homily. He talks about the Holy Spirit, about Pentecost, about tongues of fire and the courage to speak truth. "Confirmation," he says, "is not the end of your journey. It's a beginning. You are saying yes to something bigger than yourself. You are saying yes to community, to service, to love."

I think about Grace's hand in mine. I think about Elena driving me to Mass those first few months when I didn't have a car, about Jasper gruffly offering to be my sponsor before I chose Grace, about the women in the BSS family who've folded me into their circle without question.

I think about Kendra, who doesn't understand this but who's never once mocked it.

*You do you,* she'd said when I told her I was going through RCIA. *If it makes you happy.*

It does make me happy. That's the strange part. I didn't expect that.

---

When it's time for the anointing, the bishop calls us forward one by one.

Grace walks with me. She's not supposed to—sponsors usually stay in the pews—but Father Mike had bent the rules, recognizing that a seven-year-old sponsor is not a typical situation and that Grace takes her responsibilities very seriously.

The bishop is old, maybe seventy, with kind eyes and spotted hands. He dips his thumb in the sacred chrism, and the scent of it—balsam and olive oil, sweet and earthy—rises between us.

"Addison," he says. "Be sealed with the Gift of the Holy Spirit."

He traces the cross on my forehead.

And I feel it.

Not a lightning bolt. Not a burning bush. Just a *shift*, like tumblers falling into place in a lock I didn't know I had. Like I've been standing outside a door my whole life, and suddenly it's opened, and there are people on the other side.

People who know me. People who want me.

"Amen," I whisper.

Grace is crying. I can hear her sniffling beside me, and when I look down, her face is scrunched up with the effort of not sobbing. I pull her against my side, and she wraps both arms around my waist.

"You did it," she whispers into my dress. "You're one of us."

---

After Mass, there's a reception in the parish hall. Someone's made coffee and set out grocery store sheet cake with "Congratulations Confirmands!" written in blue icing. The other confirmands have families here—spouses, children, parents—and they cluster in groups, taking photos, hugging.

I have the BSS family.

Elena gets to me first. She pulls me into a hug that smells like her perfume and baby spit-up, and she's crying again, or maybe still. "I'm so proud of you," she says. "Addie, I'm so proud."

"It's just Confirmation," I say, but my voice cracks.

"It's not just anything." She pulls back, holding my shoulders. "You chose this. You worked for this. That matters."

Jasper's next, awkward as always with affection, but he claps me on the shoulder and says, "Good job, kid," which from him is basically a declaration of love. Noah reaches for me from Jasper's arms, and I take him, grateful for the weight of something real and solid.

Mel hugs me so hard she lifts me off the ground. "Catholic!" she shouts. "You're Catholic!"

"Mel, inside voice," Jordan hisses, but she's grinning too.

Coach Sarah gives me a firm handshake and a "Well done." Dr. Okonkwo's wife presses a card into my hand with a prayer written inside. The BSS women descend in a wave of hugs and congratulations, and I'm overwhelmed by the sheer *presence* of them, the way they take up space and give it at the same time.

Grace hasn't left my side. She's glued to my hip, one hand fisted in my dress, and when someone asks if she wants cake, she just shakes her head and holds on tighter.

"You okay?" I ask her quietly.

She nods. Then, so softly I almost miss it: "I didn't think you'd pick me."

My heart stops.

I crouch down, still holding Noah on one hip, and look her in the eye. "Grace. There was no one else I wanted. Just you."

"Really?"

"Really."

She throws her arms around my neck, and Noah squeals, delighted to be squished between us.

---

The reception winds down. People start to leave, pressing cards and small gifts into my hands—a rosary from Dr. Okonkwo's wife, a book of prayers from one of the other confirmands, a Starbucks gift card from Mel because "Jesus wants you to have good coffee." I accept it all with a feeling that's somewhere between gratitude and disbelief.

Elena finds me by the coffee table, refilling my cup for the third time.

"You keep looking at the door," she says.

I freeze. "I'm not—"

"Addie." She's not accusatory. Just observant. "It's okay to miss her."

I stare into my coffee. "She's where she needs to be."

"That doesn't mean you don't wish she were here."

I don't say anything. What is there to say? Kendra's at the CrossFit Games, competing at the highest level, living her dream. I'm at my Confirmation, taking a step she doesn't fully understand into a faith she doesn't share. We're in different worlds right now.

We've been in different worlds for a while.

"I'm happy," I say finally. "I am. This is… this is everything I wanted."

"I believe you." Elena touches my arm. "You can be happy and still miss someone. Both things can be true."

I nod, not trusting my voice.

Across the room, Grace is showing Jasper how she signed my sponsor certificate, pointing to her careful seven-year-old signature. He's nodding seriously, like it's the most important document he's ever seen.

"Thank you," I tell Elena. "For all of this. For bringing me here."

"You brought yourself," she says. "We just walked with you."

---

Later, after everyone's gone and the parish hall is empty except for Mrs. Chen stacking chairs, I sit in one of the pews. The cathedral is quiet now, just the sound of my breathing and the distant hum of traffic outside.

I touch my forehead where the bishop anointed me. The oil is long absorbed, but I swear I can still feel it, a faint warmth.

*You're one of us,* Grace had said.

I pull out my phone. There are texts waiting—Mel sending me a string of party emojis, Jordan with a photo of the two of them at a Charlotte cafe, Coach Sarah with a simple "Proud of you." I scroll past them to Kendra's name.

The last message is from this morning, sent at 5 AM Wisconsin time: *Good luck today. You got this.*

I'd replied with a thumbs up because I didn't know what else to say.

Now I type: *It was beautiful. Grace cried. I cried. Everyone cried. I wish you could've been here.*

I stare at the message for a long time.

Then I delete the last sentence and send: *It was beautiful. Grace cried. I cried. Everyone cried.*

Three dots appear immediately. She's awake. Probably in the warm-up area, or maybe already done with her first event.

The reply comes: *Knew you'd kill it. Proud of you, Addie.*

Something in my chest loosens and tightens at the same time.

I type back: *How's the competition?*

*Brutal. I love it.*

Of course she does.

*Go win,* I send.

*Go be Catholic,* she replies, and I can hear her grin through the screen.

---

I sit there a while longer, phone in my lap, light filtering through the stained glass. The silence isn't empty. It's full—of prayers said and unsaid, of the weight of oil on my forehead, of Grace's small hand in mine, of Elena's pride and Jasper's gruff affection and the whole impossible family I've stumbled into.

Of Kendra's absence, which is also a kind of presence.

I'm one of them now. The BSS family. The Church. This community that's claimed me even when I didn't know I needed claiming.

And Kendra's in Madison, doing what she was made to do.

Both things are true.

Both things can be true.

I stand, smoothing my white dress one last time. In my purse is the certificate with Grace's signature, the rosary from Dr. Okonkwo's wife, the prayer card, the Starbucks gift card. Evidence of this day. Evidence of who I'm becoming.

When I walk out of the cathedral, the Charlotte sun is bright and warm. Elena's waiting by her car, Grace already buckled in the back seat.

"Thought you might want a ride," Elena calls.

I cross the parking lot, my white dress bright against the asphalt.

"Yeah," I say, climbing into the passenger seat. "Thanks."

Grace reaches forward from the back to touch my shoulder. "You're really Catholic now."

"I really am."

"Forever?"

"Forever."

She sits back, satisfied.

As we pull out of the parking lot, I catch one last glimpse of the cathedral in the side mirror—solid, permanent, waiting. I'll be back next Sunday. And the Sunday after that. And the Sunday after that.

I'm part of something now.

The thought should feel heavy, like an anchor. Instead, it feels like flight.

---

*End of Chapter 48*
