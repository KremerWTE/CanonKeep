# Chapter 9: Elena's New Chapter

**POV:** Elena Barrett
**Location:** Oak Watch, Charlotte, NC
**Timeline:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The townhouse had been hers for six weeks.

Elena stood in the kitchen of the Oak Watch property—her property—watching the afternoon light slant through the bay window onto countertops she'd negotiated, floors she'd walked a dozen times during inspections, walls that still smelled faintly of fresh paint. The staging furniture would be gone by Friday. The photographer had already come and gone. All that remained was the listing paperwork and the inevitable bidding war.

She'd forgotten how good this felt.

Not the money—though the commission would be excellent—but the *work* itself. The problem-solving. The quiet thrill of matching a family to a home, of seeing potential where others saw dated fixtures and cramped layouts. She'd walked away from real estate when Grace was born, trading open houses for story time, negotiations for nap schedules. It had been the right choice then.

But now...

Elena ran her hand along the granite countertop, cool and smooth under her palm. Grace was eight. Lucas had just started crawling. Jasper was home more—not perfect, but *present* in ways that counted. And Elena had discovered something she hadn't expected: she didn't want to go back to being just a mother, just a wife.

She wanted this too.

Her phone buzzed. A text from Jasper: *Leaving the office. Home by 6. Grace has soccer at 4:30 right?*

Elena smiled, typing back: *4:45. I'll grab her. You get Lucas from Sara's?*

Three dots appeared, then: *On it. Love you.*

Simple. Domestic. The kind of exchange that used to feel like a luxury when Jasper was on a plane to London or locked in a crisis war room. Now it was just... Tuesday.

She pocketed her phone and did one last walk-through—checking light switches, window locks, the temperamental garbage disposal the seller had promised to fix. Everything in order. Everything exactly as it should be.

Her own life felt the same way lately. In order. Balanced.

So why did she keep waiting for the other shoe to drop?

---

Grace's soccer practice ran long.

Elena stood on the sidelines with the other parents, half-watching Grace chase the ball across the field with the single-minded determination of an eight-year-old who'd decided she was going to be "the best defender ever." Beside her, another mom—Kelly? Kathy?—was talking about summer camp options, but Elena had stopped listening three minutes ago.

She was thinking about the nonprofit board meeting next Tuesday.

It was her third one. Charlotte Children's Foundation—Sara had recommended her after Elena mentioned wanting to do something meaningful beyond real estate. The work was good: fundraising galas, program development, connecting donors with causes. Elena had always been good at that kind of thing—the social chess game, reading rooms, making people feel valued.

She just hadn't realized how much she'd missed it.

"—don't you think, Elena?"

Elena blinked, refocusing on Kelly-or-Kathy. "Sorry, what?"

"The end-of-season party. Are you helping coordinate?"

"Oh." Elena smiled apologetically. "I'll have to check my calendar. Things have been busy."

Which was true. Between real estate showings, board meetings, managing Grace's schedule, and trying to be present for Lucas's milestones, Elena's days had filled up faster than she'd anticipated. It felt good. Purposeful.

But also... full.

The whistle blew. Practice over. Grace jogged toward her, ponytail bouncing, cheeks flushed pink.

"Did you see my slide tackle?" Grace demanded, breathless and grinning.

"I did. Very impressive."

"Coach says I'm getting really good at defense." Grace grabbed her water bottle, chugging half of it in one go. "Can we get pizza for dinner?"

"We'll see what Dad's planning," Elena said, herding Grace toward the car. "He's picking up Lucas."

Grace made a face. "Lucas just drools and cries. He's not even fun yet."

"Give him a few months. You'll change your mind."

"Doubt it."

Elena hid her smile as they climbed into the car. Grace chattered the whole drive home—about soccer, about her friend Emma, about the upcoming field trip to the science museum. Elena made the appropriate noises, asked the right questions, but her mind kept drifting.

The Oak Watch listing. The board meeting agenda. The email she still needed to send about the November gala. The showing she'd scheduled for Thursday morning.

She was doing what she'd wanted—building a life that was hers, not just an extension of Jasper's career or Grace's needs. So why did it feel like she was running just to stay in place?

---

Jasper was already home when they pulled into the driveway.

Elena found him in the living room, Lucas on a blanket doing tummy time while Jasper sat cross-legged beside him, one hand on his laptop, the other absently steadying Lucas when he wobbled.

"Hey," Jasper said, glancing up. He looked tired—the kind of tired that came from too many video calls and not enough sleep—but he smiled when he saw her. "How was practice?"

"Grace is apparently destined for the World Cup," Elena said, dropping her bag on the counter. "How was your day?"

"Long." Jasper closed the laptop. "Harper's in London. Addison's handling the Charlotte crisis. Cole's doing damage control on the Chicago thing."

Elena moved into the kitchen, pulling out ingredients for dinner on autopilot. "Sounds like the usual chaos."

"Yeah." Jasper stood, scooping Lucas up before he face-planted into the blanket. "Actually, there's something I wanted to talk to you about."

Something in his tone made Elena pause, knife halfway through a bell pepper.

"What kind of something?"

Jasper hesitated. Just for a second—but Elena had been married to him long enough to recognize the look. The one that meant he was weighing his words, deciding how much to say.

"We got an offer," he said finally. "A big one. Expansion into Europe—London, Paris, maybe Dubai. Harper's been laying groundwork for months."

Elena set the knife down carefully. "Okay."

"It's exactly what we talked about back when we started BSS. Going global. Building something that could operate on every continent." Jasper shifted Lucas to his other hip. "It's the kind of opportunity that doesn't come twice."

"Okay," Elena said again, slower this time.

Jasper watched her. "You're not saying anything."

"I'm waiting for you to tell me what you're thinking."

He let out a breath. "That's the problem. I don't know."

Elena crossed her arms, leaning against the counter. "When have you ever not known what you wanted with BSS?"

"When it meant being gone six months a year," Jasper said quietly. "When it meant missing Grace's recitals and Lucas's first steps and every Tuesday night dinner we've managed to pull off in the last year."

Elena's chest tightened. This was the Jasper she'd fought so hard to get back—the one who thought about family first, who weighed decisions against what they'd cost, not just what they'd gain.

But she could also see the conflict in his eyes. The part of him that still wanted the challenge, the thrill of building something enormous and unstoppable.

"What aren't you telling me?" she asked.

Jasper looked at her for a long moment. Lucas babbled, grabbing a fistful of Jasper's shirt.

"Harper thinks I should take it," he said. "Addison thinks it's too soon. Cole thinks we can build the infrastructure without me being there full-time, but that's optimistic at best." He paused. "And I think... I think I'm terrified of saying yes and losing everything we just got back. But I'm also terrified of saying no and wondering if I gave up the biggest opportunity of my career because I was too scared to try."

Elena felt something shift in her chest. Not anger. Not fear. Just... clarity.

"What do *you* want?" she asked.

"I just told you—I don't know."

"No." Elena pushed off the counter, crossing the space between them. "You told me what Harper wants. What Addison thinks. What you're afraid of. I'm asking what *you* want, Jasper. Not what's smart. Not what's safe. What do you actually want?"

Jasper stared at her. Lucas squirmed, and Jasper absently adjusted his grip, but his eyes never left Elena's face.

"I want both," he said finally, voice rough. "I want the expansion. I want to build something that matters. And I want to be here. I want to coach Grace's soccer team and watch Lucas grow up and have dinner with you on Tuesday nights. I want all of it."

"Okay," Elena said.

Jasper blinked. "Okay?"

"Okay." Elena reached up, cupping his face with one hand. "Then we figure it out together."

"Elena—"

"I'm serious." She held his gaze. "You're not the same person who almost chose London over Grace's recital. And I'm not the same person who stayed silent and hoped you'd notice I was drowning. We've both changed. So if you want this—really want it—then we find a way to make it work. Together."

Jasper exhaled, something in his shoulders loosening. "You make it sound simple."

"It won't be," Elena said honestly. "But we've done harder things."

Lucas made a grab for Elena's necklace, and she laughed, taking him from Jasper's arms. The baby weight was warm and solid against her chest, his small hand curling around her thumb.

Jasper watched them, something unreadable in his expression.

"I don't deserve you," he said quietly.

"Probably not," Elena agreed, kissing Lucas's head. "But you're stuck with me anyway."

Jasper's mouth curved—just slightly, but it was there. The smile that used to be rare, that Elena had fought so hard to bring back.

"So," she said, settling Lucas on her hip. "Tell me about this expansion."

---

They talked through dinner.

Grace complained about vegetables and Lucas fussed in his high chair and the pasta was slightly overcooked, but Jasper laid out the details anyway. The European offices. The Dubai possibilities. The infrastructure they'd need, the partners Harper had been courting, the timeline that would require at least quarterly trips abroad—maybe more in the first year.

Elena listened, asked questions, poked holes in the logistics.

"What about Addison?" she asked, twirling pasta on her fork. "If you're splitting time between continents, she'd essentially be running Charlotte solo half the time."

"She could handle it," Jasper said.

"I know she could. But does she want to?"

Jasper paused, considering. "I don't know."

"Then you need to ask her." Elena glanced at Grace, who was sneaking bites of pasta to the dog under the table. "Grace. Stop feeding Biscuit."

"I'm not!"

"You literally have sauce on your hand and Biscuit has sauce on his nose."

Grace giggled, wiping her hand on her napkin with zero remorse.

Jasper watched the exchange with that same unreadable expression from earlier—like he was cataloging the moment, storing it away.

"You're staring," Elena said.

"I'm appreciating," Jasper corrected. "There's a difference."

Elena felt warmth bloom in her chest. The kind that had nothing to do with attraction and everything to do with *this*—the ordinary chaos of family dinner, the rhythm they'd finally found.

"What about you?" Jasper asked.

Elena looked up. "What about me?"

"You just started building your own thing. Real estate. The nonprofit board. If I'm traveling more—even if it's structured—you'd be holding down more at home."

Elena considered that. It was true. The balance they'd found was still new, still fragile. Adding transatlantic flights and time zone differences would shift everything.

But she'd also just spent six weeks remembering what it felt like to build something of her own. To have work that mattered, goals that were hers, a life that existed beyond the roles of wife and mother.

She didn't want to give that up either.

"We'd need help," she said slowly. "Real help. Not just Sara babysitting in emergencies. Actual childcare we can count on."

Jasper nodded. "Done."

"And you'd need to be *here* when you're here. Not just physically present but actually engaged. No laptop at Grace's games. No crisis calls during bedtime."

"I can do that."

"Can you?" Elena held his gaze. "Because we've tried this before, Jasper. And it didn't work."

"I know." Jasper set his fork down. "But I also know what I almost lost last time. I'm not making that mistake again."

Elena wanted to believe him. God, she wanted to believe him.

But she'd also learned to protect herself. To ask for what she needed instead of hoping he'd figure it out.

"If we do this," she said carefully, "I need you to promise me something."

"Anything."

"If it starts to feel like before—like you're choosing the business over us—I need you to hear me when I say it. Not dismiss it. Not promise to fix it later. Actually hear me."

Jasper reached across the table, lacing his fingers through hers. His hand was warm, calloused from the gym, familiar.

"I promise," he said.

Elena squeezed his hand, then let go, turning her attention back to Lucas, who was now wearing more pasta than he'd eaten.

"Alright," she said. "Then let's figure it out."

---

Later, after Grace was in bed and Lucas was finally asleep, Elena found Jasper in his office.

He was at his desk, laptop open, but he wasn't typing. Just staring at the screen with that faraway look that meant he was running scenarios, testing variables, building contingency plans in his head.

Elena leaned against the doorframe. "Second-guessing already?"

Jasper glanced up, mouth quirking. "Always."

She crossed the room, perching on the edge of his desk. "Talk to me."

"It's a lot of moving pieces," Jasper said. "The London office would need a permanent presence—someone senior, someone I trust. Paris would be smaller initially, more consulting-focused. Dubai's still theoretical, but if it happens, it changes the whole model."

"Who would you send to London?"

"Harper's the obvious choice. She's already been building relationships there. But that leaves a gap here."

"Addison."

"Addison." Jasper rubbed his eyes. "She's ready for it. More than ready. But it's also a lot to ask."

"Then don't ask," Elena said. "Offer. Let her decide if it's what she wants."

Jasper looked at her, something shifting in his expression. "When did you get so good at this?"

"At what?"

"Running companies. Managing people. Seeing three moves ahead."

Elena laughed softly. "I've been watching you do it for ten years. I picked up a few things."

Jasper reached for her hand, tugging her closer until she slid off the desk and into his lap. His arms came around her waist, solid and sure.

"I love you," he said quietly. "In case I haven't said it enough today."

Elena leaned back against his chest, feeling the steady thrum of his heartbeat. "You can say it as many times as you want. I won't complain."

They sat like that for a while, the office quiet except for the hum of the laptop and the distant sound of a car passing outside.

"Are you scared?" Elena asked eventually.

"Terrified," Jasper admitted. "Of failing. Of screwing this up. Of losing you and Grace and Lucas because I got too focused on the wrong thing."

"You won't."

"You don't know that."

"No," Elena agreed. "But I know you. And I know you'll fight like hell to make sure you don't." She turned in his arms, meeting his eyes. "We both will."

Jasper studied her face—searching for doubt, maybe, or hesitation. But Elena had meant what she said earlier. They'd both changed. They were stronger now, smarter, more careful with each other.

Whatever came next, they'd face it together.

"Okay," Jasper said finally.

"Okay?"

"Okay." He kissed her, slow and deliberate. "We do this. Together."

Elena smiled against his mouth. "Together."

---

That night, lying in bed with Jasper already asleep beside her, Elena stared at the ceiling and let herself think about what was coming.

The expansion would change everything. Jasper would travel more, even with careful planning. She'd shoulder more at home, even with help. Grace would notice, would ask questions, would need reassurance that Daddy wasn't disappearing again.

But Elena would also have her real estate deals. Her nonprofit board. The life she was building that was *hers*.

It wasn't perfect. It wasn't even easy.

But it was theirs. Their new normal. Their next chapter.

And for the first time in a long time, Elena wasn't afraid of what that looked like.

She turned onto her side, watching Jasper sleep. His face was relaxed, peaceful in a way it rarely was during the day. She thought about the man he'd been when they first met—ambitious, driven, completely certain of what he wanted. Then the man he'd become—distant, distracted, choosing work over everything else. And now this version—still ambitious, still driven, but tempered by loss and love and the hard-won knowledge that success meant nothing if you had no one to share it with.

He was learning. They both were.

Elena closed her eyes, Jasper's breathing steady beside her, and let herself believe it would be enough.

Tomorrow, they'd start figuring out the details. Tonight, she'd just be grateful for the man who'd finally learned to come home.

And for the woman she was becoming—strong enough to build her own life and generous enough to help him build his.

Together.

Always together.

---

**End of Chapter 9**
