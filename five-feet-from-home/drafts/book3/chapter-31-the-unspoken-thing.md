# Chapter 31: The Unspoken Thing

**POV:** Addison
**Location:** Charlotte
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

Addison sees the text at 9:47 AM, between a conference call about the Meridian Defense contract and a meeting with potential investors.

**Kendra:** I'm in Charlotte today. Coffee?

It's been three weeks since they last saw each other in person. Three weeks since Kendra left for training camp in California, chasing the dream that's always been just out of reach. Three weeks of texts that felt shorter than they should be and phone calls that ended too quickly.

Addison stares at the message for longer than she should.

She has twelve things on her calendar today. A presentation at 2:00 PM that she's only half-prepared for. A dinner with Elena and Jasper that she can't cancel. A report that's due to a client by end of day.

But Kendra's in Charlotte. And Addison hasn't seen her in three weeks.

**Addison:** 11:00? Waterbean?

**Kendra:** Perfect. See you there.

Addison sets down her phone and tries to focus on the spreadsheet in front of her. She can't. Her mind keeps circling back to the last time she saw Kendra—at the airport, both of them pretending the goodbye was casual, temporary, nothing that required more than a quick hug and a "see you soon."

It wasn't casual. It wasn't temporary. And Addison's been pretending ever since that she's fine with the distance, the choices, the reality that Kendra's life is pulling her in directions that don't include Addison.

At 10:45, Addison closes her laptop, grabs her bag, and heads out.

---

Waterbean Coffee is the kind of place that's always busy but never feels crowded—exposed brick walls, mismatched furniture, baristas who know regulars by name. Addison arrives first and orders a cortado, then claims a table in the back corner, away from the windows.

She doesn't know why she's nervous. This is Kendra. They've had coffee a hundred times. They've had entire weekends together, entire conversations that stretched into the early morning hours. This should be easy.

But when Kendra walks in at 11:03—dark hair pulled into a messy bun, leggings and an oversized hoodie, exhaustion visible in the set of her shoulders—Addison's chest tightens in a way that feels too complicated to name.

Kendra orders at the counter, then spots Addison and smiles. It's a real smile, the kind that reaches her eyes. But there's something underneath it. Something tentative.

"Hey," Kendra says, sliding into the seat across from her.

"Hey." Addison tries to match the casual tone, but it comes out softer than she intended. "How was California?"

"Brutal. Good. Exhausting." Kendra wraps her hands around her coffee cup like she's trying to absorb its warmth. "I think I'm ready. Or as ready as I'm going to be."

"Semifinals are next week, right?"

"Yeah. Knoxville. Three days of hell." Kendra's smile is wry. "Chris is flying out with me. Sara's going to coach. It's… a lot."

Chris. Sara. The people in Kendra's corner. The people who've been there, showing up, supporting her through this.

Addison is not in that category. She's been in Miami, in DC, in Charlotte handling crises for clients who pay her to make problems disappear. She's been present in texts and phone calls, but not in the ways that matter.

"That's good," Addison says. "You need people in your corner."

"I do." Kendra's gaze is steady, searching. "I've missed you, Addie."

The nickname lands like a punch. Kendra's the only person who calls her that. Not Addison. Not Ms. Price. Addie.

"I've missed you too," Addison admits.

They're quiet for a moment. Around them, the coffee shop hums with conversation and the hiss of the espresso machine. But their corner feels separate, insulated.

"How's work?" Kendra asks finally.

"Good. Busy. We closed the Meridian contract. Jasper's talking about opening another regional office, maybe in New York." Addison takes a sip of her cortado, buying time. "Harper's killing it in Miami. She signed her first major client last week."

"That's amazing."

"It is." Addison sets down her cup. "How's training? Are you feeling strong?"

"Physically? Yeah. Mentally?" Kendra's smile is self-deprecating. "I'm terrified. I keep thinking I'm going to get to Knoxville and completely fall apart. That I've been chasing this dream for so long and when it actually matters, I won't be good enough."

"You're good enough," Addison says immediately. "You've always been good enough, Kendra."

Kendra looks at her. Really looks at her. And for a second, Addison sees everything—the history between them, the choices they made, the things they never said.

"Chris is great," Kendra says quietly. "He's supportive. He's there. He believes in me even when I don't believe in myself."

"That's good," Addison says. It comes out hollow.

"It is." Kendra pauses. "But sometimes I think about… what it would have been like. If we'd made different choices."

Addison's throat tightens. "Kendra—"

"I know. I'm not saying I regret anything. Chris is the right choice. He's stable and kind and he loves me." Kendra's voice is steady, but there's something underneath it. "But that doesn't mean I don't wonder."

Addison doesn't know what to say. Because she wonders too. All the time. She wonders what it would have been like if she'd been braver, if she'd said yes instead of pulling back, if she'd let herself want Kendra in the way she's always wanted her.

But she didn't. She chose safety. She chose distance. And now Kendra's here, sitting across from her, talking about Chris and semifinals and a future that doesn't include Addison in the way it once might have.

"I'm proud of you," Addison says finally. "For going after this. For not giving up."

"Thank you." Kendra's smile is soft. "That means a lot. Coming from you."

They talk for another thirty minutes. About safe things. Kendra's training schedule. Addison's latest client drama. The fundraiser Elena's planning for next month. They laugh at the right moments. They nod at the right times.

But there's something underneath the entire conversation. A current of things unsaid, choices unmade, futures that branched off in directions neither of them can change now.

When Kendra checks her phone and says she has to go—appointment with a physical therapist, something about her shoulder—Addison feels the loss of her before she's even left.

"Good luck in Knoxville," Addison says as they stand. "I'll be watching. Tracking the leaderboard."

"Thanks, Addie." Kendra pulls her into a hug. It's brief, casual. The kind of hug you give a friend.

But when Kendra pulls back, her hand lingers on Addison's arm for just a second too long.

"I'll text you after," Kendra says.

"Please do."

And then she's gone.

---

Addison sits alone at the table for an hour.

She doesn't pull out her laptop. She doesn't check her phone. She just sits, staring at her empty coffee cup, trying to understand what just happened.

Kendra talked about Chris. About how great he is. About the future they're building.

And Addison listened. Said the right things. Smiled at the right moments.

But underneath it all, there was something else. Something Kendra didn't say. Something Addison couldn't ask.

*What if we'd made different choices?*

Addison knows the answer. If they'd made different choices, Kendra would be here—not as a friend stopping by for coffee, but as something more. Something permanent. Something that didn't require pretending that distance and separate lives were fine.

But they didn't make different choices. Addison made the safe choice. The careful choice. The choice that protected her from risk and kept her in control.

And now Kendra's with Chris. Building a life. Chasing a dream. Moving forward.

Addison should be happy for her. She is happy for her.

But she's also sitting alone in a coffee shop, feeling something that looks a lot like grief.

She doesn't know what she lost. Only that she lost it.

And the worst part—the part that makes her chest ache in a way she can't quite name—is that she doesn't even know when it happened. When the possibility became impossible. When the door closed.

Maybe it closed years ago, when they first pulled apart. Maybe it closed when Kendra met Chris. Maybe it closed today, in this coffee shop, when Kendra talked about semifinals and the future and didn't once suggest that Addison was part of it.

Addison finally pulls out her phone. There's a text from Jasper.

**Jasper:** Meeting in 20. You coming?

She types back quickly.

**Addison:** On my way.

She stands, grabs her bag, and walks out of the coffee shop.

The sun is too bright. The air is too warm. Everything feels slightly wrong, like a song played in the wrong key.

But Addison's spent her entire career managing things that feel wrong. She knows how to compartmentalize. How to push down feelings that don't serve her. How to focus on the work, the tasks, the things she can control.

So that's what she does.

She walks to her car, drives to the office, and sits through a meeting about quarterly projections and client retention. She takes notes. She asks questions. She contributes ideas.

And the entire time, she's thinking about Kendra.

About the way she said *I've missed you, Addie.*

About the way she paused before saying *Chris is the right choice.*

About the way her hand lingered, just for a second, before she left.

---

That night, Addison sits in her apartment with a glass of wine and her laptop open.

She's supposed to be working on the presentation for tomorrow. But instead, she's scrolling through old photos.

There's one from two years ago—Kendra and Addison at a Fourth of July party, both of them laughing, Kendra's arm around Addison's shoulders. They look happy. Easy. Like they fit.

There's another from last summer—the two of them at a CrossFit competition, Kendra in her competition gear, Addison holding her water bottle, both of them grinning like idiots.

Addison stares at the photos for a long time.

Then she closes the laptop and finishes her wine.

Her phone buzzes. A text from Kendra.

**Kendra:** Thanks for coffee today. It was good to see you.

Addison reads the message three times before responding.

**Addison:** Good to see you too. Good luck next week. You're going to kill it.

**Kendra:** Hope so. Miss you already.

Addison stares at those three words. *Miss you already.*

She types and deletes four different responses before settling on:

**Addison:** Miss you too.

It's safe. It's appropriate. It doesn't say anything that might complicate things.

But it's also true.

Addison sets down her phone and walks to the window. Charlotte sprawls below her—city lights and traffic and the hum of a life that keeps moving whether she's ready or not.

She thinks about Kendra in Knoxville next week. Competing. Fighting for a spot at the Games. Chris in the stands, cheering her on. Sara coaching from the sidelines.

Addison won't be there. She'll be in Charlotte, or Miami, or DC. Somewhere that isn't Knoxville. Somewhere that isn't where Kendra is.

And that's fine. That's the choice she made.

But standing here, alone in her apartment with the taste of wine still on her tongue and the memory of Kendra's hand lingering on her arm, Addison can't shake the feeling that she made the wrong choice.

Not about Chris. Chris is good for Kendra. Chris is stable and kind and present in ways Addison never has been.

But about herself. About what she wanted. About whether she was brave enough to ask for it.

She wasn't.

And now she's standing at her window, watching the city move without her, wondering what it would have been like if she had been.

Addison finishes her wine and goes to bed.

She doesn't sleep well.

And when she wakes up the next morning, the first thing she does is check her phone.

There's a text from Kendra. Sent at 2:00 AM.

**Kendra:** I keep thinking about what I said today. About wondering. I shouldn't have said that. It wasn't fair to you. Or to Chris.

**Kendra:** I'm sorry.

Addison reads the message five times.

Then she types back:

**Addison:** Nothing to apologize for. We're good.

But they're not good. Not really.

They're something else. Something complicated and unfinished and achingly familiar.

Addison doesn't know what to do with that. So she does what she always does.

She gets up. She gets dressed. She goes to work.

And she pretends everything is fine.

**End of Chapter 31**
