# Chapter 46: The POH Invitation

**POV: Addison**
**Location: Charlotte**

---

The Palace of Honor headquarters doesn't look like much from the outside.

Addison Price pulls into the parking lot of what appears to be a renovated textile mill in Charlotte's South End district, checking the address on her phone for the third time. This is definitely the place—the discreet bronze plaque by the entrance confirms it: *POH Foundation - Private*.

She's been to charity galas. She's been to nonprofit board meetings. She's been to Elena's Wives' Circle events where millions of dollars changed hands over champagne and canapés.

This feels different.

Her phone buzzes. A text from Elena: *Remember—you're there as yourself, not as anyone's wife or sister. They want YOU.*

Addison rereads it twice, then gets out of the car.

---

Inside, the building is transformed.

The industrial bones remain—exposed brick, steel beams, high ceilings with original skylights—but everything else screams quiet, deliberate wealth. Not the ostentatious kind. The kind that doesn't need to announce itself.

A receptionist who looks like she could be Secret Service guides Addison to a private elevator. "Fourth floor. They're expecting you."

The elevator opens into a space that makes Addison's breath catch.

Floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking Charlotte's skyline. Furniture that's probably worth more than her first car. Art on the walls—not prints, originals. A conference table that seats twenty, made of wood so dark and polished it looks like liquid.

And standing by the windows: Elena Barrett and Lady Margaret Chen.

Lady Margaret. Not "Mrs. Chen." Not "Margaret." *Lady* Margaret—a title earned through her work with the Order of Malta, her humanitarian efforts spanning three continents, her role as one of the most influential women in international philanthropy.

Addison's done her research. She always does.

"Addison." Elena crosses the room, kisses both her cheeks. "Thank you for coming."

"Thank you for the invitation." Addison keeps her voice steady, professional. "Though I'll admit, I'm not entirely sure why I'm here."

Lady Margaret smiles. She's in her early sixties, silver hair cut in a sharp bob, wearing a cream suit that probably cost more than Addison's monthly rent. "Direct. I like that. Elena said you don't waste time with false modesty."

"I find it inefficient," Addison says.

"Good." Lady Margaret gestures to the conference table. "Shall we sit? This conversation is better had comfortably."

They settle into chairs that are somehow both elegant and ergonomic. A woman Addison doesn't recognize appears with coffee service—real china, not paper cups—and disappears just as quietly.

Elena pours. Lady Margaret watches Addison with the kind of attention that feels like being assessed by someone who misses nothing.

"You've heard of the Palace of Honor," Lady Margaret says. Not a question.

"I have. International humanitarian organization. Focuses on healthcare access, education infrastructure, refugee support. Operates in thirty-seven countries. Annual budget north of $200 million." Addison accepts her coffee. "You're on the executive board. Elena joined the advisory council last year."

"You did your homework."

"I always do."

Lady Margaret's smile deepens. "Elena, you undersold her."

"I told you she was exceptional."

"You told me she was competent. There's a difference." Lady Margaret sets down her cup. "Addison, how much do you know about POH's leadership structure?"

Addison recalibrates. This isn't a social call. This is a recruitment pitch. "I know you have an executive board—seven members, all appointed for life. An advisory council of about thirty people, mostly wealthy donors and political connections. And a network of what you call 'chapter leaders' in major cities, though I don't know much about that structure."

"The chapter leaders are the ones who make it work," Elena says quietly. "The board sets strategy. The council provides resources. But the chapter leaders—they're the ones on the ground, building relationships, identifying needs, making connections happen."

"Okay," Addison says slowly. "And you're telling me this because...?"

Lady Margaret leans forward slightly. "Because we want you to lead the Charlotte chapter."

The words hang in the air.

Addison processes. Reprocesses. "I'm sorry, what?"

"We're expanding POH's domestic presence," Lady Margaret explains. "For too long, we've focused primarily on international work. But there are profound needs here in the United States—healthcare deserts, educational inequity, infrastructure collapse in rural communities. We're launching five new domestic chapters this year. Charlotte is one of them."

"And you want me to lead it." Addison's brain is working overtime, looking for the angle, the catch. "Why?"

"Because you're uniquely positioned," Elena says. "You understand crisis management. You have business connections through BSS. You've built relationships with political families, nonprofit leaders, corporate executives. You know how to make things happen without needing credit for it."

"And," Lady Margaret adds, "because you're not doing this as someone's wife."

Addison goes very still. "I'm not sure I follow."

"Most of the women in POH leadership are there because of their husbands' positions. Ambassadors' wives. CEOs' wives. Senators' wives." Lady Margaret's tone is matter-of-fact, not judgmental. "There's nothing wrong with that—those connections have value. But they come with limitations. Those women are always representing someone else first, themselves second."

"You're not married," Elena says. "You're not engaged. You're not anyone's plus-one. You're a managing partner at a crisis management firm in your own right. You've built your own career, your own network, your own reputation."

"We want leaders who can stand on their own," Lady Margaret continues. "Who bring their own authority to the table. Who can make decisions without consulting a husband's political position or corporate interests."

Addison's mind is racing. This is... significant. More than significant. POH isn't just another nonprofit. It's a power structure. The kind that opens doors most people don't even know exist.

"What would leading the Charlotte chapter actually entail?" she asks.

Lady Margaret pulls a leather folder from the bag beside her chair, slides it across the table. "Everything's outlined here. But in short: you'd identify local needs that align with POH's mission. Build partnerships with organizations already doing the work. Facilitate connections between donors and projects. Coordinate with other chapter leaders on regional initiatives. And represent POH at events, conferences, and strategic planning sessions."

"How much time commitment are we talking?"

"Fifteen to twenty hours per week, initially. More during major projects. Less once you have systems in place." Elena's watching her carefully. "It's designed to run alongside a full-time career, not replace it."

"And the compensation?"

"There isn't any," Lady Margaret says bluntly. "Chapter leaders are volunteers. However, all expenses are covered—travel, events, project budgets. And you'd have access to POH's full network and resources."

Addison opens the folder. Inside: organizational charts, financial statements, project summaries, testimonials from other chapter leaders. It's comprehensive. Professional. Real.

"Can I ask you something?" Addison looks up at Lady Margaret. "Why now? Why me, specifically? There are dozens of women in Charlotte with more experience, more established philanthropic credentials—"

"Because they're already locked into their own organizations," Lady Margaret interrupts. "They're running their own foundations, chairing their own boards. We don't need someone who's dividing attention between five different commitments. We need someone hungry. Someone building. Someone who sees this as an opportunity, not an obligation."

"And you think that's me."

"I know that's you." Lady Margaret's gaze is unwavering. "Elena's told me how you've navigated Charlotte's social structures. How you've built relationships across industries, across political lines. How you see angles others miss. That's exactly what this role requires."

Addison looks at Elena. "You recommended me."

"I did."

"Why?"

Elena sets down her coffee cup with deliberate precision. "Because I've spent twenty-five years building influence as James Whitmore's wife. I've done good work. Important work. But it's always been filtered through that identity. I wanted to give you the chance to build something that's entirely yours."

The honesty of it hits Addison hard. She's known Elena was mentoring her, grooming her for something. But she'd assumed it was about supporting Jasper's career, becoming the perfect partner for a Whitmore son.

This is different.

This is about Addison's own legacy.

"I'm not even confirmed yet," Addison says quietly. It's been on her mind for weeks—the RCIA process, her journey toward the Catholic Church. Kendra's been supportive. Elena's been patient. But it feels presumptuous to take on a role with an organization rooted in Catholic social teaching when she hasn't even fully committed to the faith.

"We know," Lady Margaret says. "Your confirmation is scheduled for next month, correct?"

"Yes."

"Then we'll make the appointment official after that. But we wanted to extend the invitation now, so you have time to think about it. To ask questions. To understand what you'd be stepping into."

"And if I say no?"

"Then we'll respect that decision and find someone else." Lady Margaret leans back in her chair. "But I don't think you're going to say no."

"Why not?"

"Because this is what you've been preparing for, whether you realized it or not. Every connection you've made, every crisis you've managed, every relationship you've built—it's all been leading here. To a role where you can use that accumulated power to actually change things."

Addison looks down at the folder again. Inside: a structural map of Charlotte's nonprofit ecosystem. A list of potential partners. A budget projection for the first year. Someone—probably Elena—has already done preliminary work on this.

They've been planning this for months.

"We want your mind, not just your faith," Elena says softly. "The confirmation matters because POH is grounded in Catholic social teaching. But what we need from you is strategic thinking, relationship building, execution. The things you're already exceptional at."

"How long do I have to decide?" Addison asks.

"Take a month," Lady Margaret says. "Think about it. Do your own research—I'd be disappointed if you didn't. Talk to other chapter leaders. I'll connect you with Baltimore and Nashville, both launched in the last two years. See what they've built."

She stands, and Addison and Elena rise with her. "Thank you for coming, Addison. I hope you'll say yes. But either way, I'm glad we had this conversation."

They shake hands—Lady Margaret's grip is firm, confident—and then she's gone, leaving Addison alone with Elena.

"You ambushed me," Addison says.

"I gave you an opportunity," Elena corrects. "There's a difference."

"Is there?" But Addison's smiling despite herself. "You could have warned me."

"If I'd warned you, you would have spent a week preparing counterarguments for why you're not ready. You would have talked yourself out of it before you even heard the pitch."

"You don't know that."

"I absolutely know that." Elena picks up the leather folder, hands it back to Addison. "You're brilliant at seeing opportunities for other people. Terrible at recognizing them for yourself."

Addison accepts the folder, runs her thumb along the embossed POH seal on the cover. "This is a big deal."

"It's a huge deal."

"I could fuck it up."

"You could. But you won't." Elena's voice is certain. "Because you're too smart and too driven to accept a responsibility you can't handle. If you say yes to this, you'll make it work. That's who you are."

They walk to the elevator together. Outside the windows, Charlotte's skyline glitters in the afternoon sun. Addison's city. The place she's been building a life, a career, a network.

The place where, apparently, she could build something even bigger.

"Can I ask you something?" Addison says as they wait for the elevator. "What do you get out of this? Recommending me, I mean. What's your angle?"

Elena's smile is complicated—pride and ambition and something that might be hope. "I get to watch you do what I never could. Build power that's entirely your own. Not derivative, not borrowed, not contingent on anyone else's position. Just yours."

"That's not entirely true," Addison says. "This opportunity exists because you recommended me. Because you've spent months introducing me to the right people, teaching me how to navigate these spaces—"

"I opened doors," Elena interrupts. "You walked through them. There's a difference." The elevator arrives with a soft chime. "Besides, I'm not entirely altruistic. Having you lead POH Charlotte will be useful. For both of us."

There it is. The truth Addison's been waiting for.

"How?"

"Because POH operates in spaces BSS can't touch. Humanitarian work, healthcare access, refugee support—these aren't crisis management projects. But they create relationships with people who might need crisis management. Who might have friends or colleagues who need it. Who might, someday, pick up the phone and call Jasper because they met you at a POH event and trust your judgment."

"So I'd be a walking business card for BSS."

"You'd be building a network that benefits everyone involved. Including yourself." Elena steps into the elevator. "That's not exploitation, Addison. That's strategy."

The doors start to close, and Addison puts her hand out to stop them. "I need to think about this."

"I know. Take your time. Well—take a month. But use it wisely."

Addison steps in beside her. They ride down in silence, both watching the floor numbers descend.

"Elena?"

"Yes?"

"Thank you. For thinking I could do this."

Elena reaches over, squeezes her hand. "I don't think you can do this, Addison. I know you can. There's a difference."

---

That evening, Addison sits in her apartment with the POH folder spread across her coffee table.

She's read every page twice. Made notes. Highlighted sections. Started a preliminary strategic plan she's not even sure she'll need.

Her phone buzzes. Kendra, texting from Madison: *How'd the mystery meeting go?*

Addison stares at the message, trying to figure out how to summarize the last six hours.

Finally, she types: *They want me to lead the Palace of Honor's Charlotte chapter.*

Three dots appear immediately. Then: *Holy shit.*

*Yeah.*

*Are you going to do it?*

Addison looks at the folder again. At the organizational charts and budget projections and strategic plans. At the opportunity to build something that's entirely hers, that exists independent of Jasper or Elena or anyone else.

At the chance to prove she's more than competent. More than exceptional.

That she's a leader.

*I think so,* she types. *I think I have to.*

Kendra's response is immediate: *Then do it. And don't look back.*

Addison sets down the phone and picks up the folder again. Inside the back cover, she finds something she missed on the first read-through: a handwritten note on heavy cardstock, Lady Margaret's elegant script.

*Addison—*

*Leadership isn't about being ready. It's about being willing. I think you're both.*

*Welcome to POH.*

*— M*

Welcome to POH.

Like it's already decided. Like they already know she's going to say yes.

The presumption should annoy her. But instead, it feels like recognition. Like someone seeing her potential before she's fully claimed it herself.

She opens her laptop and starts drafting an email to the Baltimore chapter leader, requesting a call. If she's going to do this—and somewhere in the last hour, she's decided she is—she's going to do it right.

Research first. Questions second. Commitment third.

That's how you build something that lasts.

Outside her window, Charlotte glows in the dusk. Her city. Her network. Her opportunity.

Addison Price, chapter leader of Palace of Honor Charlotte.

It has a ring to it.

She allows herself a small smile and gets to work.

---

**End of Chapter 46**
