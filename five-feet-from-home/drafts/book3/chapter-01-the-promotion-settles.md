# Chapter 1: The Promotion Settles

**POV: Addison Barrett**

The nameplate arrived on a Tuesday.

Addison found it on her desk when she came back from the morning strategy meeting—a sleek rectangle of brushed steel with black lettering: *Addison Barrett, Junior Strategist*. She stood in the doorway of her office—*her office*—and stared at it for a long moment.

Three months ago, she'd been running projections in the bullpen, working Jasper's overflow, taking notes in client meetings. Now she had four walls, a door that closed, and a window that looked out over Charlotte's skyline. The office was small, barely larger than a closet, but it was hers.

She set her coffee down and picked up the nameplate. The metal was cool against her fingers. Real. Solid. Proof.

"Settling in?"

Addison turned. Jasper stood in the doorway, arms crossed, that familiar half-smile on his face. He looked tired—he always looked tired these days—but proud.

"Just admiring the hardware," Addison said, setting the nameplate back down. "Should I get it engraved? Maybe add 'First of Her Name'?"

"Save that for when you make partner." Jasper stepped into the office, glancing around at the bare walls, the empty shelves. "You going to personalize this place, or keep the serial killer aesthetic?"

"I'm thinking motivational posters. Maybe a cat hanging from a branch that says 'Hang in there.'"

"Please don't."

Addison grinned. She turned to her desk, where three case files sat in a neat stack. Her caseload. Not Jasper's overflow, not shared projects—*hers*. A tech startup in Durham needing restructuring. A manufacturing firm in Greensboro exploring acquisition targets. And, as of yesterday afternoon, something bigger.

"So," Jasper said, picking up the top file. "Harcourt Capital."

Addison's pulse quickened. "You're really doing this?"

"They asked for you specifically. Senior partner at a hedge fund with two billion under management calls and says, 'I want that analyst who worked the Meridian deal.' That's not a request I ignore."

"But it's New York."

"It's New York." Jasper set the file down. "Three-day engagement, maybe four. They're looking at a hostile takeover of a pharmaceutical company and need strategic positioning. It's complex, it's high-stakes, and it's yours if you want it."

Addison's mind was already racing. Hostile takeover meant opposition research, media strategy, board dynamics. It meant late nights, high pressure, and zero margin for error. It meant everything she'd been training for.

"When?" she asked.

"They want you up there Monday."

"That's four days."

"Plenty of time to prepare." Jasper leaned against her desk. "I'm not going to sugarcoat this, Addy. This is a test. Not from them—from me. You've been ready for bigger cases. This is your chance to prove it. Solo."

Solo. The word hung in the air between them.

Addison had worked dozens of cases in the past year. She'd built models, written reports, presented to clients. But always with Jasper as the safety net. Always with someone to catch her if she fell.

"You think I can do it?" she asked.

Jasper's expression softened. "I wouldn't offer if I didn't. But here's the thing—this isn't about what I think. It's about what you know. So I'm asking: Are you ready?"

Addison looked at the nameplate again. *Junior Strategist.* She'd earned that title. She'd put in the hours, done the work, proven herself. But this was different. This was stepping out of the shadow and into the light.

She met Jasper's eyes. "I've been ready."

His smile widened. "Good. Because they're expecting a call from you by end of day."

---

The rest of the afternoon blurred into preparation.

Addison pulled everything she could find on Harcourt Capital—portfolio holdings, previous deals, leadership profiles. She studied the pharmaceutical company they were targeting, mapped out the board composition, identified potential allies and adversaries. She built a preliminary framework for the engagement, outlining key questions, strategic levers, potential obstacles.

By six PM, her office looked like a war room. Papers covered her desk. Post-it notes dotted her whiteboard. Her laptop screen glowed with open tabs—financial statements, news articles, SEC filings.

She barely noticed when Harper appeared in the doorway.

"You planning to sleep here tonight?"

Addison looked up, blinking. Harper stood with her coat on, bag over her shoulder, ready to leave. Through the window behind her, the sky had gone dark.

"What time is it?" Addison asked.

"Six-thirty. Most people have left." Harper stepped into the office, surveying the chaos. "Jasper told me about New York. Congratulations."

"Thanks." Addison rubbed her eyes. "Just trying to get ahead of it."

"By memorizing the entire pharmaceutical industry in one sitting?"

"Something like that."

Harper picked up one of the files, flipped through it. "You know you don't have to prove anything, right? They asked for you. They already believe you can do this."

"That's what makes it scarier," Addison said. "If they'd asked for Jasper and I was backup, I could just execute. But they want me. Which means I have to be worth wanting."

Harper set the file down. "You are."

"You don't know that."

"I do, actually." Harper pulled up a chair, sat down. "You want to know why Jasper's giving you this case? It's not because the client asked. He could have convinced them to take someone more senior. He's giving it to you because he knows you're going to nail it. And more importantly, because you need to know you're going to nail it."

Addison leaned back in her chair. "When did you get so wise?"

"I've always been wise. You were just too busy being brilliant to notice."

They sat in comfortable silence for a moment. Outside, Charlotte's downtown lights twinkled against the night sky. Somewhere in the building, a vacuum cleaner hummed.

"You want to grab dinner?" Harper asked. "You look like you could use a break."

Addison glanced at her desk, at the mountain of work still waiting. Then she looked at Harper, at the genuine concern in her friend's eyes.

"Yeah," she said. "That sounds perfect."

---

They ended up at a Thai place two blocks from the office, tucked into a corner booth with pad thai and green curry between them. The restaurant was quiet on a Tuesday night—just a handful of other diners, soft music playing overhead.

"So how's the family?" Harper asked, twirling noodles around her fork. "Lucas must be getting big."

"He's seven months now. Crawling everywhere, getting into everything. Mom's convinced he's going to be walking by nine months." Addison smiled. "Dad keeps sending me pictures throughout the day. Lucas with his blocks. Lucas with his stuffed giraffe. Lucas asleep in his crib."

"That's sweet."

"It's excessive. But yeah, it's sweet." Addison took a bite of curry. "How about you? How's the analyst grind treating you?"

Harper shrugged. "It's fine. Good cases, good clients. But..."

"But?"

"I don't know. I keep thinking there's something more. Something bigger." Harper set her fork down. "You ever feel like you're doing exactly what you're supposed to be doing, but you can still see the ceiling?"

Addison considered this. "You mean like you can see the limits?"

"Exactly. Like, I can see the path. Analyst to junior strategist to senior strategist to partner. Ten years, fifteen maybe. It's a good path. But it's *the* path, you know? The only path."

"You want to make your own path."

"I want to build something," Harper said. "Not just execute someone else's vision. I want to create."

Addison studied her friend. There was a restlessness in Harper's eyes, an energy that hadn't been there before. It reminded her of how Jasper looked when he talked about the firm's future—like he could see something no one else could see yet.

"You have something in mind?" Addison asked.

Harper hesitated. "Maybe. It's not ready yet. But soon."

"When it is, I want to hear it."

"You'll be the first." Harper raised her water glass. "Well, second. After Jasper."

They clinked glasses. Addison felt a warmth spread through her chest—not from the curry, but from the moment. This was what mattered. Not just the cases and the clients and the promotions, but this. The friendships. The shared ambitions. The sense that they were all building something together.

Her phone buzzed. A text from Jasper: *Did you call Harcourt?*

Addison checked the time. Seven-fifteen. She'd gotten so wrapped up in preparation, she'd forgotten the actual call.

"I have to make a phone call," she said, pulling out her wallet.

"The client?"

"The client."

Harper waved her wallet away. "This one's on me. Go. Call New York. Be brilliant."

Addison stood, squeezed Harper's shoulder. "Thanks."

"Anytime."

---

She made the call from her office, door closed, city lights painting shadows across her desk.

The senior partner at Harcourt Capital was named Richard Chen. His voice on the phone was clipped, efficient, all business.

"Ms. Barrett. Thank you for calling."

"Mr. Chen. I understand you're looking at a strategic positioning for the MedCore acquisition."

"We are. And we need someone who can think three moves ahead. Your work on the Meridian deal caught our attention. The way you structured their defense strategy—that wasn't textbook. That was original thinking."

Addison's heart raced. Someone had noticed. Someone at a two-billion-dollar fund had noticed her work specifically.

"I appreciate that," she said. "What's your timeline?"

"Aggressive. We're making our move in six weeks. We need positioning locked down in four. That means we need someone here next week, full engagement, no distractions."

"I can be there Monday."

"Excellent. My assistant will send you the details. Come prepared, Ms. Barrett. This isn't a learning opportunity. This is the real thing."

"I understand."

"Good. We'll see you Monday."

The line went dead.

Addison sat in the silence of her office, phone still pressed to her ear. The real thing. No safety net. No backup.

Just her.

She looked at the nameplate on her desk, at her reflection in the dark window, at the city spreading out below her.

"I've been ready," she whispered to the empty room.

And this time, she believed it.

---

She stayed late, working through the case files, building her initial strategy. By the time she left the office, it was past ten. The Forge was dark except for security lights and the glow from a few offices where workaholics like her burned the midnight oil.

As she walked to her car, her phone buzzed again. This time it was a text from her dad: *Lucas just took three steps. THREE. We got it on video. Call me tomorrow.*

Addison smiled, feeling the familiar pull between two worlds. The world of boardrooms and hedge funds and hostile takeovers. And the world of first steps and stuffed giraffes and family dinners.

She'd spent so long trying to balance them. Trying to be enough in both places.

Maybe the answer wasn't balance. Maybe it was integration. Maybe it was being fully present wherever she was—giving everything to the case when she was working, giving everything to her family when she was home.

She unlocked her car, slid behind the wheel.

Monday, she'd go to New York. She'd walk into that hedge fund office and she'd prove that Jasper's faith wasn't misplaced. That Richard Chen's expectations weren't too high. That she was exactly as good as she needed to be.

But tonight, she'd go home. She'd watch the video of Lucas's first steps. She'd hug her parents and tell them about her day.

Because that was part of being ready too. Knowing what you were fighting for.

Addison started the car and drove home through the quiet Charlotte streets, her nameplate in her bag, her future spreading out before her like the road ahead.

*Junior Strategist.*

It was just the beginning.
