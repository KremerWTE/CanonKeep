# Chapter 32: Semifinals Day 3

**POV:** Kendra
**Location:** Knoxville, TN
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

Kendra doesn't sleep the night before Day 3.

She tries. She lies in bed with her eyes closed, breathing slowly, trying to will her body into rest. But her mind won't stop running through scenarios.

She's in 5th place. Top five go to the Games. One event left. A chipper—high volume, mixed modalities, designed to test everything.

If she finishes top ten in the final event, she's safe. Top five is almost guaranteed.

But if she falls apart—if her body gives out, if she makes a mistake, if the women behind her have the workout of their lives—she could drop to 6th. Or 7th. Or worse.

One event. One shot. Everything she's trained for comes down to the next two hours.

At 4:30 AM, she gives up on sleep and gets out of bed.

Chris stirs. "You okay?"

"Can't sleep," Kendra whispers.

He sits up, rubbing his face. "Want to talk about it?"

"I don't know what to say." Kendra sits on the edge of the bed, staring at her hands. Her palms are torn up from yesterday's rope climbs, covered in tape and calluses. "I'm terrified I'm going to fail."

"You won't."

"You don't know that."

Chris gets up and comes to sit beside her. He doesn't try to fix it. He just sits, his shoulder pressed against hers, solid and warm.

"You're right," he says quietly. "I don't know that. But I know you. And I know you've done everything you could to prepare. The rest is just execution."

"Just execution," Kendra repeats. She laughs, but it's shaky. "That's the hardest part."

"I know." Chris puts his arm around her. "But you've done hard things before. You'll do this one too."

They sit like that for a long time. The hotel room is dark and quiet. Outside, Knoxville is still asleep.

Eventually, Kendra stands. "I'm going to go for a walk. Clear my head."

"Want company?"

"No. I need to be alone for a bit."

Chris nods. "I'll be here when you get back."

---

The streets around the hotel are empty. Kendra walks with no destination in mind, just movement. Her legs are sore—quads tight, calves knotted—but the motion feels good. Grounding.

She thinks about the last three days. The workouts. The scores. The way her body has held up under the volume and intensity.

She thinks about Chris, sitting in the stands, tracking every rep. Sara, coaching from the sidelines, fierce and relentless. Elena, who texted last night to wish her luck. Addison, who hasn't said much but who Kendra knows is watching the leaderboard.

She thinks about the coffee shop in Charlotte. The way Addison looked at her. The things Kendra said that she shouldn't have.

*I keep thinking about what it would have been like. If we'd made different choices.*

It was true. But it was also unfair. Addison didn't ask for that. Didn't deserve to carry the weight of Kendra's second-guessing.

Kendra pulls out her phone and looks at Addison's response from last night.

**Addison:** Nothing to apologize for. We're good.

They're not good. Kendra knows that. But there's nothing she can do about it right now. Not today. Not when everything she's worked for is on the line.

She tucks the phone back in her pocket and keeps walking.

By the time she gets back to the hotel, the sun is starting to rise. Chris is awake, sitting at the desk with coffee and a bagel waiting for her.

"Feeling better?" he asks.

"A little." Kendra takes the coffee and drinks half of it in one go. "What time is athlete check-in?"

"9:00 AM. Event briefing at 10:00. First heat at 11:30."

Kendra's in Heat 5. That means she'll compete around 1:00 PM. Enough time to warm up, get her mind right, and watch the earlier heats to see how the workout plays out.

"Okay," she says. "Let's do this."

---

The final event is exactly what Kendra expected: a chipper from hell.

**Event 6: The Gauntlet**

**For Time:**
- 50 Wall Balls (20/14 lbs)
- 40 Box Jump-Overs (24/20 inches)
- 30 Toes-to-Bar
- 20 Dumbbell Snatches (50/35 lbs)
- 10 Bar Muscle-Ups

**Time Cap: 20 Minutes**

It's a grinder. High reps on every movement. Technical skills mixed with pure volume. And bar muscle-ups at the end, when you're already destroyed.

Kendra watches the first two heats from the warm-up area. The wall balls look manageable. The box jump-overs are brutal—athletes are slowing down, stumbling, missing jumps. The toes-to-bar are shredding people's grips. The dumbbell snatches are heavy enough to make shoulders scream. And the bar muscle-ups? Only half the athletes are finishing them.

By Heat 3, Sara finds Kendra in the warm-up area.

"You ready?" Sara asks.

"I think so." Kendra's been moving through a warm-up for the last forty-five minutes—light wall balls, easy box jumps, strict toes-to-bar, some muscle-up drills. Her body feels okay. Not great, but functional.

"Here's the plan," Sara says. "Wall balls unbroken if you can, but don't redline. Box jump-overs, steady pace. Don't rush, don't trip. Toes-to-bar in sets of ten, maybe fifteen if you're feeling strong. Dumbbell snatches, fast singles. And the muscle-ups? One at a time. No failed reps. Failed reps will break you."

Kendra nods. It's a smart strategy. Conservative but sustainable.

"What if I don't finish?" Kendra asks.

"Then you don't finish. But you will." Sara's eyes are fierce. "You've done every one of these movements a thousand times. This is just one more workout. Go out there and execute."

---

Heat 5 is called at 12:58 PM.

Kendra walks to Lane 4 for the third day in a row. Same judge—Amy, the woman who's been counting her reps all weekend. Same setup.

But this time, it's different. This time, everything is on the line.

Kendra stands in front of the wall ball target and takes a breath. Around her, seven other women are doing the same. Some look calm. Some look terrified.

Kendra feels both.

The clock counts down.

Ten seconds.

Kendra picks up the fourteen-pound medicine ball and holds it against her chest.

Five seconds.

She closes her eyes. Breathes.

*You've got this.*

"GO!"

Kendra throws the ball at the target. Catches it. Squats. Throws. Catches. Squats. Throws.

The wall balls are monotonous. Rhythm and breath. She gets to thirty before her legs start to burn. Forty before her shoulders start to ache. She finishes all fifty and drops the ball.

First movement done.

She moves to the box. Twenty-four inches. She's jumped higher. She's jumped more. This is nothing.

Except her legs are already tired. And the box looks higher than it should.

She jumps. Lands. Steps down. Jumps. Lands. Steps down.

At rep fifteen, she clips the box with her shin. It doesn't knock her down, but it rattles her. She slows down, focuses on clean reps.

Thirty. Thirty-five. Forty.

Box jump-overs done.

She moves to the pull-up bar. Toes-to-bar. Thirty reps.

She jumps up and kips. Her toes touch the bar. One. Two. Three.

By rep ten, her grip is slipping. By rep fifteen, her core is on fire. She drops from the bar and shakes out her hands.

Chris is in the stands. She can hear him, faintly, shouting her name.

She jumps back up. Five more reps. Drops. Jumps. Five more. Drops.

Thirty toes-to-bar done.

Her hands are shredded. Her shoulders are gone. But she's not done yet.

Dumbbell snatches. Twenty reps. Fifty pounds.

She grabs the dumbbell with her right hand and snatches it overhead. One. Drops it. Switches hands. Two. Drops it.

The weight feels like a truck. Her shoulders are screaming. But she keeps moving. Right hand. Left hand. Right. Left.

At rep fifteen, she almost drops the dumbbell on her foot. She stumbles back, gasping.

"Come on, Kendra!" Sara's voice cuts through the noise. "Five more! You've got this!"

Kendra grabs the dumbbell again. Right. Left. Right. Left. Right.

Twenty dumbbell snatches done.

She drops the dumbbell and staggers to the pull-up bar.

Ten bar muscle-ups. The final movement.

Kendra has done muscle-ups a thousand times. But never after this much volume. Never when her shoulders are this fried, her grip this compromised, her body this wrecked.

She jumps to the bar and pulls. Her chest rises. She presses out. One.

She drops and shakes out her arms. Chalks her hands.

Jumps. Pulls. Presses. Two.

By rep five, every muscle-up feels like moving through concrete. Her kip is sloppy. Her press-out is shaky. But she's getting them.

Six. Seven.

The arena is roaring. Or maybe that's just in her head.

Eight.

Her hands slip. She catches herself, re-grips, pulls.

Nine.

One more. One more rep and she's done.

Kendra hangs from the bar, breathing hard. Her arms are shaking. Her vision is narrowing.

*One more.*

She kips. Hard. Pulls with everything she has left. Her chest rises to the bar. She presses out.

Her arms lock.

Ten.

The buzzer sounds.

Kendra drops from the bar and collapses onto the floor. She can't move. Can't breathe. Can't think.

Judge Amy is standing over her, smiling. "Good work, Kendra. Fourteen minutes, thirty-seven seconds."

Fourteen thirty-seven. Kendra has no idea if that's good or bad. She just knows she finished.

Sara is there immediately, pulling her to her feet. "That was incredible. That was everything."

"Did I make it?" Kendra gasps.

"Won't know until all the heats finish." Sara hands her a water bottle. "But you left it all out there. That's all you can do."

---

The next ninety minutes are torture.

Kendra sits in the recovery area with ice packs on her shoulders and her hands wrapped in gauze. Chris is beside her, tracking the remaining heats on his phone.

Heat 6 finishes. Heat 7. Heat 8.

The leaderboard updates in real time, but Kendra can't look. She's too afraid of what she'll see.

Finally, at 3:47 PM, the last heat finishes.

Chris's phone buzzes. He looks at it. His eyes go wide.

"Kendra."

"What?" Her voice is hoarse.

"Look."

He turns the phone toward her.

**Women's RX Division – South East Region**
**Final Overall Standings**

1. Emma Lawson
2. Haley Adams
3. Olivia Kerstetter
4. **Kendra Hale**
5. Sydney Wells

Fourth.

Kendra stares at the screen. Fourth place overall. Out of forty women.

She's going to the Games.

She's going to Madison.

"Oh my God," she whispers.

Chris pulls her into a hug so tight she can barely breathe. "You did it. You're going to the CrossFit Games."

Sara is crying. Actually crying. "I knew you could do it. I knew it."

Kendra can't process it. Can't believe it. She's dreamed about this moment for years. Imagined it. Trained for it. And now it's real.

She's going to the CrossFit Games.

The tears come before she can stop them. She's sobbing into Chris's shoulder, shaking, unable to speak.

"I'm so proud of you," Chris murmurs. "So damn proud."

When she finally pulls back, her face is wet and her hands are shaking. But she's smiling. Actually smiling.

Sara grabs her phone. "We need to get a picture. For Instagram. For posterity. For everything."

Kendra poses with Chris and Sara, holding up four fingers. Fourth place. Qualifier for the Games.

The photo is terrible—Kendra's face is blotchy, her hair is a mess, her eyes are red. But she doesn't care.

Because she did it.

She made it.

---

That night, Kendra sits in the hotel room with Chris, too exhausted to move but too wired to sleep.

Her phone is blowing up. Texts from Elena, Jasper, Harper, Sara's entire gym. Congratulations. Well done. You earned it.

And one from Addison.

**Addison:** Saw the leaderboard. Fourth place. I knew you could do it. So proud of you.

Kendra reads the message three times.

Then she types back:

**Kendra:** Thank you. For everything. For believing in me even when I didn't believe in myself.

The response comes fast.

**Addison:** Always.

Kendra stares at that word. *Always.*

It's simple. But it's also everything.

She sets down her phone and looks at Chris. He's lying on the bed, scrolling through photos from today, smiling at every one.

"You okay?" he asks.

"Yeah." Kendra climbs onto the bed beside him and rests her head on his shoulder. "I'm better than okay. I'm going to the CrossFit Games."

"You are." Chris kisses the top of her head. "And you're going to kill it."

Kendra closes her eyes. Her body aches. Her hands are wrecked. Her shoulders feel like they've been through a war.

But she's going to Madison.

She's going to the CrossFit Games.

And for the first time in years, the dream doesn't feel impossible.

It feels real.

---

**End of Chapter 32**
