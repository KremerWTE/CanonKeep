# CHAPTER 52: The Sunday Table

**POV: Elena Barrett**
**Location: Charlotte Compound**

Elena Barrett has hosted Sunday dinners at the compound for as long as she can remember.

It started when the kids were young. Jasper would be traveling for work, and she'd invite Sara and her family over to keep from going crazy alone with three children under five. Then it became a tradition. Every Sunday, the table filled with people. Family. Friends. Colleagues who became family.

Now, decades later, the tradition continues.

But tonight feels different.

Elena stands in the compound's kitchen, overseeing the final preparations. The dining table is set for fourteen. Roast chicken, vegetables, fresh bread, three different salads. Grace helped with the desserts — an apple pie and lemon bars. Lucas is setting out wine glasses.

"Mom, where do you want the flowers?" Grace asks, holding a bouquet of dahlias from the garden.

"Center of the table. Beautiful, sweetheart."

Jasper appears in the doorway. "Need anything?"

"Just you not sneaking appetizers before everyone gets here."

He grins. "Too late."

Elena shakes her head, smiling. After all these years, some things never change.

The guests start arriving at 5 PM.

Sara and her husband Tom come first, with their two teenage boys in tow. Then Harper and Ethan, who bring wine and stories about their latest adventure — a weekend in the mountains. Chris arrives alone.

"Kendra's running late," he says, kissing Elena on the cheek. "She'll be here soon."

"No problem. We're still waiting on Addison anyway."

But something in Chris's voice catches Elena's attention. A slight tension. She makes a mental note.

Addison arrives ten minutes later. She looks tired. Professional as always — tailored pants, simple blouse — but there are shadows under her eyes.

"Sorry I'm late," she says.

"You're not. Come in. Wine?"

"Please."

By 5:30, everyone is gathered in the living room, drinks in hand. The house is full of noise and laughter. Lucas is showing Sara's boys a video game. Grace is telling Harper about her internship. Jasper and Tom are debating politics in the corner.

Elena watches it all with quiet satisfaction. This is what she built. Not just a company or an organization. A family.

Then Kendra arrives.

She slips in through the side door, apologizing for being late. Chris stands immediately, crosses the room, and wraps an arm around her. She leans into him, smiling. But Elena sees the smile doesn't quite reach her eyes.

And when Kendra's gaze flicks across the room to Addison, Elena sees something else.

Addison is staring into her wine glass, carefully not looking at Kendra.

Elena's instincts prickle. She's been reading people for forty years. She knows when something is off.

She files it away.

---

Dinner is chaotic in the best way.

Fourteen people crammed around the long table. Plates being passed. Conversations overlapping. Lucas tries to tell a story but gets interrupted three times. Grace argues with Jasper about climate policy. Sara's boys eat like they've never seen food before.

Elena sits at the head of the table, Jasper at the other end. She watches. Listens.

Chris is talking about a project at work. Kendra sits beside him, nodding, contributing here and there. But her attention keeps drifting. She pushes food around her plate.

Addison is three seats down, between Harper and Tom. She's engaged in conversation, laughing at something Harper says. But it's performative. Elena can tell.

And not once do Addison and Kendra make eye contact.

"Elena?" Sara's voice pulls her back. "You okay? You look like you're planning something."

Elena smiles. "Just enjoying the chaos."

"Liar. You've got your strategist face on."

"Do I?"

"You do. What are you plotting?"

"Nothing. Yet."

Sara laughs and goes back to her conversation.

By the time dessert comes out, everyone is full and happy. Lucas brings in the pie and lemon bars with a flourish. Grace starts making coffee.

Elena stands, tapping her glass with a spoon. The table quiets.

"Thank you all for being here," she says. "These Sunday dinners are important to me. Not just because I love cooking for too many people—" Laughter. "—but because they remind me what matters. Family. Community. The people we choose to surround ourselves with."

She looks around the table. "This year has been extraordinary. Kendra, you finished eighth at the CrossFit Games. That's remarkable. Addison, you launched a strategic alliance that's going to change the way BSS and POH operate. Harper, you and Ethan are building something beautiful together. Grace, Lucas, you're both growing into incredible adults. Sara, you've been my rock through everything. And Jasper—" She meets her husband's eyes. "You've built a legacy. All of you have."

She raises her glass. "To us. To what we've built. To what comes next."

Everyone raises their glasses. "To us."

They drink.

The moment passes. Conversations resume. But Elena doesn't sit down yet.

She watches Addison take a long sip of wine. Watches Kendra excuse herself to the bathroom. Watches Chris frown slightly as Kendra walks away.

Something happened.

Elena doesn't know what. But she knows these people. She knows the patterns. And right now, the pattern is broken.

---

After dinner, people scatter.

The younger crowd migrates to the game room. Sara and Tom help clean up in the kitchen. Chris steps outside to take a phone call.

Elena finds Jasper on the back patio, smoking a cigar.

"Since when do you smoke?" she asks.

"Since I decided I'm old enough not to care."

She sits beside him. "What do you think?"

"About what?"

"Tonight. Everyone."

Jasper considers. "Good energy. Kendra seems a little off, but she's probably still recovering from the Games."

"And Addison?"

"Tired. She's been working nonstop on the alliance."

Elena nods slowly. "Did they seem strange to you?"

"Who?"

"Addison and Kendra."

Jasper takes a drag of his cigar. "Strange how?"

"I don't know. Just... off."

"You're reading too much into it."

"Maybe."

But Elena doesn't think so.

She's seen this before. The careful distance. The avoiding eye contact. The overcompensating in conversations with other people.

Something happened between them.

The question is: what?

---

Later, Elena finds Addison in the library, ostensibly looking for a book.

"Hey," Elena says softly.

Addison turns. "Hey. Just needed a quiet minute."

"Big crowd tonight."

"Yeah. Good, though."

Elena sits in one of the leather chairs. "You've been working hard on the alliance. How's it going?"

"Good. We're launching the pilot programs next month."

"That's exciting."

"It is."

There's a long pause. Elena waits. She's learned over the years that silence is often more effective than questions.

Addison breaks first. "Can I ask you something?"

"Of course."

"How do you do it? Balance everything? Work, family, POH, all of it? How do you make sure you don't lose yourself in the process?"

Elena studies her. "That's a big question."

"I know."

"What brought it on?"

Addison looks away. "I don't know. Just thinking."

Elena doesn't push. Not yet. "The truth is, I don't always balance it well. There have been times when I've lost myself. When I've let the work consume me. When I've made choices I regret."

"Like what?"

"Too many to count." Elena's voice is gentle. "But the ones I regret most are the times I wasn't honest. With myself or with the people I care about. When I avoided hard conversations because I was scared of what they'd mean."

Addison is very still.

"Whatever you're dealing with," Elena continues, "the hardest part is always the honesty. But it's also the most important part."

Addison nods slowly. "What if being honest means hurting people?"

"It usually does. But dishonesty hurts them more. Eventually."

They sit in silence for a moment.

Then Addison says, "Thank you."

"For what?"

"For always knowing what to say."

Elena smiles. "I don't. But I try."

Addison stands. "I should get back."

"Addison?"

She pauses.

"Whatever it is," Elena says, "you don't have to carry it alone."

Addison's eyes glisten slightly. She nods and leaves.

Elena sits in the library for a long time after, thinking.

---

An hour later, most of the guests have left. Sara and her family head home. Harper and Ethan leave for a late movie. The house is quieter now.

Elena finds Kendra on the back patio, sitting alone on the steps.

"Mind if I join you?" Elena asks.

"Of course not."

Elena sits beside her. The night air is cool. Above them, the stars are just starting to appear.

"Beautiful night," Elena says.

"Yeah."

"How are you feeling? Post-Games?"

"Sore. But good."

"Eighth in the world. That's incredible, Kendra."

"Thank you."

They sit in comfortable silence for a bit. Then Elena says, "You know, when I was your age, I thought I had everything figured out. I'd met Jasper. We were building the business. Everything felt like it was falling into place."

"What happened?"

"Life happened. Complications. Hard choices. I learned that having it all figured out is a myth. The best you can do is be honest about what you want and hope the people around you can handle that honesty."

Kendra doesn't respond.

"I'm not trying to pry," Elena continues. "But if you ever need to talk — about anything — I'm here."

Kendra's voice is quiet. "What if you don't know what you want?"

"Then you figure it out. And in the meantime, you don't pretend. That's the worst thing you can do. To yourself and to the people who love you."

Kendra nods. Her eyes are bright with unshed tears.

"Chris is a good man," Elena says gently.

"I know."

"But being with a good man isn't enough if you're not being honest with yourself."

Kendra turns to look at her. "Did Addison say something?"

"No. Should she have?"

Kendra looks away quickly. "No. I just— never mind."

Elena doesn't push. She's said enough.

They sit for a few more minutes. Then Kendra stands. "I should find Chris. We're heading out soon."

"Okay."

Kendra pauses. "Elena? Thank you. For everything."

"Anytime."

Kendra goes inside. Elena stays on the patio, watching the stars.

She was right. Something happened between Addison and Kendra.

And whatever it is, it's going to come to a head soon.

---

By 10 PM, everyone has left except Jasper, Elena, Grace, and Lucas.

They clean up the last of the dishes. Grace yawns. Lucas is already half-asleep on the couch.

"Good dinner," Jasper says, wrapping an arm around Elena's waist.

"It was."

"What's on your mind?"

"What makes you think something's on my mind?"

"Forty years of marriage."

Elena smiles. "Addison and Kendra."

"What about them?"

"Something happened."

"Like what?"

"I don't know. But they're avoiding each other. And Chris doesn't seem to notice."

Jasper frowns. "You think they had a fight?"

"Maybe. Or something else."

"Like what?"

Elena hesitates. "I'm not sure. But I'm going to keep an eye on it."

"Elena." Jasper's voice is cautious. "Don't meddle."

"I'm not meddling. I'm observing."

"Those are the same thing with you."

She kisses his cheek. "I prefer to think of it as caring."

"That's what worries me."

---

Later that night, Elena lies in bed, unable to sleep.

She replays the evening in her mind. The careful distance between Addison and Kendra. The way Chris kept reaching for Kendra and she kept pulling away, just slightly. The way Addison drank more wine than usual.

Elena has seen this pattern before. In colleagues. In friends. In herself, once upon a time.

Two people caught between what they should want and what they actually want.

She thinks about her conversation with Addison. About honesty. About hard choices.

She thinks about Kendra. About the tears she was holding back.

And she thinks about Chris. Good, steady Chris, who has no idea that the ground beneath him is shifting.

Elena knows she can't fix this. This isn't her problem to solve.

But she can be ready. When it all falls apart — and it will fall apart — she can be there to help pick up the pieces.

That's what family does.

She closes her eyes and tries to sleep.

Tomorrow is Monday. A new week. New challenges.

And somewhere, a storm is building.

Elena can feel it.

She just hopes they're all ready when it breaks.
