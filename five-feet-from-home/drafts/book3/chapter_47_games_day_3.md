# Chapter 47: Games Day 3

**POV: Kendra**
**Location: Madison, Wisconsin**

---

Kendra wakes up at 4:17 AM and immediately regrets being alive.

Every single part of her body is screaming. Not the good kind of sore that comes from a hard workout. The kind of deep, structural pain that suggests she may have permanently broken something.

Her back is the worst. She tries to roll over and her lumbar spine sends a message that's very clear: *Absolutely fucking not.*

She lies there in the dark, listening to Chris breathing beside her, and takes inventory.

Shoulders: wrecked.

Quads: destroyed.

Hip flexors: non-functional.

Hands: torn open despite yesterday's re-taping.

Lower back: possibly held together only by kinesiology tape and prayer.

Day Three. The final day. Three more events between her and the finish line.

She's currently in 9th place overall. Ninth. Out of forty of the best female CrossFit athletes in the world.

The leaderboard is tight. She's only eight points ahead of 10th place. Only fifteen points behind 7th. Everything's still in play.

Which means today matters. Every rep. Every second. Every decision.

No pressure.

Chris stirs beside her. "You awake?"

"Barely."

"How bad?"

"Remember that scene in *Saving Private Ryan* where the medic gets shot and he's just lying there saying 'Mama'?"

"That bad?"

"Worse."

He reaches over, finds her hand in the darkness. "You can pull out. No one would judge you. You've already proven—"

"I'm not pulling out."

"Kendra—"

"Chris." She turns her head to look at him, even though it's too dark to see his face clearly. "I came here to compete. Not to participate. Not to 'just finish.' I'm 9th right now. I can hold that. I can maybe even move up. But I have to finish."

He's quiet for a long moment. Then he squeezes her hand. "Okay. Then let's get you ready."

---

By 6:00 AM, Kendra's in the hotel gym doing the world's gentlest warm-up.

Her parents are there—her dad on a bike, spinning easy; her mom doing some kind of yoga flow that looks peaceful and makes Kendra jealous. Sara arrives at 6:15 with coffee and a foam roller.

"Let me see the back," Sara says.

Kendra lifts her shirt. The kinesiology tape from yesterday is still mostly intact, bright blue strips criss-crossing her lower back. But there's visible swelling along her lumbar spine.

Sara prods gently. Kendra hisses.

"Yeah," Sara says. "That's not good."

"Can I compete on it?"

"Can you? Yes. Should you?" Sara sits back. "That's above my pay grade, Ken. You need to decide what your body can handle."

"My body can handle one more day."

"Your body is telling you very clearly that it's breaking down."

"Then I'll break down after Event 9." Kendra takes the foam roller, starts working on her quads. Every pass makes her want to cry. "I didn't come this far to quit on the last day."

Her mom appears with a protein shake. "Drink this. And Sara's right—you need to listen to your body."

"I am listening. It's saying 'this is going to suck but you can do it.'"

"I think it's saying 'please stop.'"

Kendra drinks the shake to avoid answering. Her mom means well. They all do. But they don't understand what's at stake here.

This isn't about health. This isn't about being smart or playing it safe.

This is about proving—to herself, to everyone who doubted her—that she belongs at this level.

---

Event 6 is announced at 7:30 AM.

**Event 6: The Long Chipper**
**For time:**
**50 Wall Balls (20/14 lbs)**
**40 Toes-to-Bar**
**30 Dumbbell Snatches (50/35 lbs)**
**20 Handstand Push-Ups**
**10 Rope Climbs**

Time cap: 20 minutes.

Kendra reads it three times, calculating.

Wall balls: manageable. Her legs are destroyed, but she can grind through fifty reps.

Toes-to-bar: her midline is still good. Forty reps will hurt, but she can do them.

Dumbbell snatches: thirty reps at 35 pounds. Light enough to move fast.

Handstand push-ups: this is where it gets interesting. Her shoulders are fried, but she's always been solid at HSPUs.

Rope climbs: her weakness. Ten rope climbs with dead legs and a compromised back.

This event is going to separate the field. The athletes with well-rounded skills will thrive. The specialists will suffer.

Kendra's not a specialist. She's built her career on being decent at everything, exceptional at nothing.

This is her event.

"You're smiling again," Chris observes.

"I can podium this one."

"Don't get cocky."

"Not cocky. Strategic." She's already moving, heading back to the room to change. "This is a pacing event. It's not about going fast. It's about going smart. Breaking at the right times. Not redlining."

Sara's grinning. "There's the athlete I trained."

---

The warm-up area is quieter on Day Three.

Everyone's hurting. Everyone's tired. The energy is different—less competitive fire, more grim determination.

Kendra works through her prep methodically. Light wall balls to get her legs firing. A few toes-to-bar to check her midline. Some dumbbell snatches to feel the weight. A couple of handstand push-ups against the wall.

Everything works. Nothing feels good, but everything works.

The rope climb practice is brutal. She gets up the rope once and her back spasms halfway down. She hangs there for a second, breathing through it, before finishing the descent.

Ten of those. She needs to complete ten of those.

"Strategy?" Sara asks.

"Wall balls in sets of twenty, fifteen, fifteen. Toes-to-bar in sets of ten. Dumbbell snatches unbroken if possible. Handstand push-ups in sets of five. Rope climbs... one at a time, don't die."

"Solid plan. Stick to it."

---

The competition floor for Event 6 is set up like an obstacle course.

Forty lanes, each with a wall ball target, a pull-up bar, a dumbbell, a handstand push-up station, and a rope hanging from the ceiling.

Kendra finds her lane—number 23, same as yesterday—and does a final check. Ball. Bar. Dumbbell. Rope.

Everything's in place.

Chris is in the stands with her family. She can see them, a cluster of familiar faces in a sea of strangers. Her dad gives her a thumbs up. Her mom looks like she's praying.

Maybe prayer will help. At this point, Kendra will take any advantage she can get.

"Athletes, you have twenty minutes," the head judge announces. "Three, two, one—go!"

The clock starts.

Kendra picks up the fourteen-pound medicine ball and starts throwing it at the target ten feet above her head.

---

Wall balls are a special kind of misery.

Squat down until your hip crease passes your knee. Stand up explosively and throw the ball at a target. Catch it. Repeat. Fifty times.

Kendra breaks at twenty. Her legs are already burning, but she's moving well. The ball is hitting the target every time. No misses.

She takes three breaths and hits fifteen more. Thirty-five down.

Another break. Three more breaths. Final fifteen.

Forty-eight. Forty-nine. Fifty.

She drops the ball and moves to the pull-up bar.

Around her, athletes are at different stages. Some are already on dumbbell snatches. Some are still grinding through wall balls. She's middle of the pack. Exactly where she wants to be.

---

Toes-to-bar are grip and midline.

Hang from the bar. Bring your toes up to touch the bar. Lower with control. Repeat.

Kendra gets ten unbroken, then drops. Her hands are already slipping. She chalks up, jumps back on.

Ten more. Drop. Chalk. Jump.

Ten more. Drop. Chalk. Jump.

Ten more.

Forty toes-to-bar done in just under three minutes. She's on pace.

Dumbbell snatches are next.

---

The thirty-five-pound dumbbell feels heavier than it should.

Dumbbell snatch: pull the weight from the ground to overhead in one motion, alternating arms.

Kendra goes unbroken. Right arm, left arm, right arm, left. Fifteen each side. Thirty total.

Her shoulders are screaming, but she's through.

Handstand push-ups are waiting.

---

HSPUs are pure shoulder strength.

Kick up into a handstand against the wall. Lower your head to the ground. Press back up. Repeat.

Kendra breaks them into sets of five, just like she planned. Five, rest. Five, rest. Five, rest. Five.

Twenty done. Her shoulders are cooked, but she's through.

Now comes the hard part.

---

Rope climbs.

Ten of them. Fifteen feet up, touch the beam, climb back down. Repeat ten times.

Kendra stares at the rope. This is going to break her.

She grabs the rope and starts climbing.

The first ascent is okay. Her legs are doing most of the work, wrapped around the rope, pushing up. Her arms are just guiding.

She touches the beam, starts descending. Halfway down, her back spasms.

She freezes, hanging on the rope, breathing through the pain.

The judge is watching. "You okay, athlete?"

"Yeah." She's not. But she keeps moving.

She reaches the ground. One done. Nine to go.

---

Rope climbs two through five are ugly but functional.

Her legs are cramping. Her back is seizing every other climb. But she's moving.

Six. Seven. Eight.

By the eighth rope climb, she's moving in slow motion. Each foot of ascent takes twice as long as the first climb.

But she's moving.

Nine.

One more. Just one more climb.

She grabs the rope for the tenth time. Her hands are torn open, bleeding through the tape. Her back is one giant spasm. Her legs have nothing left.

She climbs anyway.

Hand over hand. Feet wrapped, push. Hand over hand. Feet wrapped, push.

Halfway up, she nearly falls. Her grip slips, and for a terrifying second she's sliding down the rope, friction burning her hands.

She catches herself. Resets. Keeps climbing.

Fifteen feet has never felt so far.

She touches the beam.

Starts descending.

Ten feet. Five feet.

Her feet hit the ground.

"Time!" the judge yells. "Sixteen minutes, twelve seconds!"

Kendra collapses against the rope, holding it for balance, trying to remember how to breathe.

She checks the board through blurred vision.

6th place.

Sixth. She finished 6th in the event.

She moved up. Again.

---

They have three hours until Event 7.

Kendra spends it in a medical tent, getting her back evaluated by a Games physician.

"You've got significant muscle strain in your lumbar region," the doctor says, prodding her spine with professional detachment. "Possibly a minor disc issue, though I can't confirm without an MRI."

"Can I compete?"

"I can't advise you to compete with this level of injury."

"That's not what I asked. Can I? Physically?"

The doctor sighs. "Physically, yes. You're not risking paralysis or permanent damage. But you're risking making the injury significantly worse. If you continue to compete and your back fully gives out, you could be looking at months of recovery instead of weeks."

"But I can finish today."

"Probably. If you're careful. If you listen to your body. If you pull out the second something feels catastrophically wrong."

Kendra nods. "Okay."

"That wasn't permission—"

"I know. But thank you."

She leaves the medical tent with fresh kinesiology tape, a bottle of prescription-strength anti-inflammatories, and strict instructions to pull out if anything feels "catastrophically wrong."

She has no intention of pulling out.

Sara's waiting outside the tent. "Well?"

"He said I'll probably be fine."

"That is absolutely not what he said."

"Close enough." Kendra checks her phone. Two and a half hours until Event 7. "What's the next workout?"

Sara pulls it up on her phone, even though they both already know. The announcement came out while Kendra was in the medical tent.

**Event 7: The Pyramid**
**1-2-3-4-5-4-3-2-1 Reps For Time:**
**Power Cleans (155/105 lbs)**
**Ring Muscle-Ups**

Time cap: 12 minutes.

Kendra reads it and feels something like dread.

Power cleans at 105 pounds. She can move that weight. But forty-five total reps, with her back already compromised?

And ring muscle-ups. Twenty-five of them. A movement that requires explosive hip drive—exactly the thing her back doesn't want to do right now.

"This is going to be bad," she says.

"Yeah," Sara agrees. "It is."

---

Event 7 is bad.

Worse than bad. It's a disaster.

Kendra's back gives out on the third round of power cleans. Not a twinge. Not a spasm. It fully, completely seizes.

She's standing with the barbell at her shoulders, and suddenly she can't move. Can't lower the bar. Can't finish the rep.

She stands there for ten seconds—an eternity—before her body finally unlocks enough to drop the bar.

The judge marks it no-rep. She has to redo it.

It takes her four attempts to complete one power clean.

She moves to ring muscle-ups and can barely generate enough hip drive to get above the rings.

By the time she's halfway through the workout, she's dropped from 6th place in the event to 18th.

She finishes at 11:47, thirteen seconds under the time cap, in 14th place.

It's her worst finish of the weekend.

When she steps off the floor, Sara is waiting with ice and sympathy. Chris looks like he wants to pull her from the competition entirely.

"I'm fine," Kendra says before either of them can speak.

"You're not fine," Chris says. "You can barely stand."

"I can stand well enough for one more event."

"Kendra—"

"One. More. Event." She meets his eyes. "I'm not pulling out with one event left."

He looks at Sara. Sara shrugs. "It's her call."

Chris turns back to Kendra, and she can see the war happening behind his eyes. The part of him that wants to protect her versus the part that knows she needs to do this.

Finally, he nods. "Okay. One more event. But if your back gives out again—"

"It won't."

She has no idea if that's true. But she needs it to be.

---

Event 8 is announced at 4:00 PM.

The final event. The last workout between Kendra and the finish line.

**Event 8: Madison Heavy**
**For time:**
**50-Foot Handstand Walk**
**3 Snatches (185/135 lbs)**
**50-Foot Handstand Walk**
**3 Snatches (185/135 lbs)**
**50-Foot Handstand Walk**
**3 Snatches (185/135 lbs)**

Time cap: 6 minutes.

Kendra reads it and feels her stomach drop.

Handstand walks. Her weakness. She can do them, but she's not fast. Fifty feet takes her close to a minute on a good day.

And heavy snatches. One hundred thirty-five pounds. That's 90% of her max. Three reps is manageable. Nine total reps, with handstand walks between each set?

This is designed to break people.

"Can you do this?" Sara asks quietly.

"I don't know." Kendra's being honest. "The snatches will be okay if my back holds. But the handstand walks..."

"You've been practicing."

"Not with a destroyed back and dead shoulders."

Sara's quiet for a moment. Then she says, "You don't have to podium this event. You don't even have to finish top ten. You just have to finish. That's the goal. Get through it, and you're done."

Get through it.

One more event. Six minutes of work. That's all that stands between Kendra and the finish line.

She can do six minutes. She can do anything for six minutes.

---

The warm-up for Event 8 is careful.

Kendra practices handstand walks, but every step sends feedback through her shoulders into her back. It's not catastrophic. Just... not good.

She loads a barbell to 135 pounds and pulls three snatches. Her back holds. Barely.

This is going to come down to pure grit.

Chris finds her ten minutes before she's due on the floor. "Hey."

"Hey."

"You know I love you, right?"

"I know."

"And I'm proud of you. Regardless of what happens in the next six minutes."

"I know." She turns to face him fully. "But I'm not settling for 'regardless.' I'm finishing this. And I'm finishing it strong."

He studies her face, sees the determination there, and nods. "Okay. Then go be great."

---

The competition floor for Event 8 is simple.

A fifty-foot lane marked with tape. A barbell loaded to 135 pounds at the fifteen-foot mark, the thirty-five-foot mark, and the fifty-foot mark.

Handstand walk to the first bar. Do three snatches. Handstand walk to the second bar. Do three snatches. Handstand walk to the third bar. Do three snatches.

Six minutes to complete it.

Kendra stands at the starting line and tries not to think about how much this is going to hurt.

"Athletes ready!" the announcer calls. "Three, two, one—go!"

Kendra kicks up into a handstand.

---

Handstand walking with a compromised back is as terrible as she expected.

Each step requires core stability, shoulder strength, and balance. Her core is on fire. Her shoulders are barely functional. Her balance is questionable.

But she's moving.

Ten feet. Twenty feet.

She falls at twenty-five feet, has to restart.

Kick up. Keep walking.

She reaches the first barbell at fifty-eight seconds. Nearly a full minute for fifty feet.

She drops to her feet, grabs the bar.

The snatch is a full-body movement. Pull the bar from the ground to overhead in one explosive motion. It requires perfect timing, aggressive hip drive, and shoulder stability.

Kendra's hip drive is compromised by her back. But she pulls anyway.

The bar comes up. Overhead. One.

She drops it, resets. Two.

Drops it, resets. Three.

Back to handstand walking.

---

The second fifty-foot section is slower.

Her shoulders are shaking. Her core is barely holding. She falls twice, has to restart both times.

She reaches the second barbell at two minutes, forty seconds.

Three more snatches. Her back spasms on the first pull. She fights through it.

One. Two. Three.

Final handstand walk.

---

Fifty feet has never felt so far.

Kendra kicks up into a handstand and immediately knows this is going to be close.

The time cap is six minutes. She's at three minutes and change. She has less than three minutes to cover fifty feet and complete three snatches.

She takes one step. Two steps. Her shoulders are shaking so badly she can barely balance.

Five steps. Ten steps.

She falls. Restarts.

The clock is ticking. Four minutes. Four-thirty. Five minutes.

She's at forty feet. Ten feet left.

She takes three more steps and falls again.

Fifty-five seconds left.

She kicks up one more time. Her shoulders are gone. Her core is gone. Everything is gone except the finish line.

Five steps. Six. Seven.

Her hands cross the line at the forty-eight-foot mark.

She drops to her feet, grabs the barbell.

Forty seconds.

First snatch. The bar feels like it weighs a thousand pounds. But it comes up. One.

Thirty seconds.

Second snatch. Her back is screaming. She pulls anyway. Two.

Twenty seconds.

Third snatch. This is it. Last rep. Last workout. Last moment.

She sets her grip. Pulls.

The bar comes off the ground. Her back spasms. She keeps pulling.

The bar reaches her hips. She drives. Extends. Jumps.

The bar goes overhead.

"Time!" the judge yells. "Five minutes, fifty-three seconds!"

Kendra drops the bar and collapses.

Not dramatically. Not for show. She just... collapses. Because her body has nothing left to give.

Sara is there immediately. "You did it. You finished."

"What place?" Kendra gasps.

Sara checks the board. "Fourteenth."

Fourteenth. Not her best. Not her worst. Solidly middle of the pack.

But she finished.

She completed all eight events over three days. She competed against the best athletes in the world.

And she held her own.

Chris appears, pulls her into a hug that probably hurts but she doesn't care. "I'm so proud of you."

"Did I...?" She can't finish the sentence. Can't ask if she held 9th place overall. Can't handle knowing yet.

"Let's get you cleaned up," Sara says gently. "The final results won't post for another hour. Let's get you hydrated and iced and then we'll check."

---

One hour later, Kendra sits in the athlete recovery area with ice on both shoulders, both knees, and her lower back.

Her entire family is crammed into the space—Chris, her parents, Sara, even Elena who flew in this morning as a surprise.

Someone's phone beeps. The final leaderboard is posted.

No one moves.

"Somebody look," Kendra finally says.

Chris pulls out his phone. Refreshes. Waits for the page to load.

His face does something complicated.

"What?" Kendra demands. "Tell me."

"You're ninth," he says. "Overall. For the entire weekend. You're ninth."

Ninth.

The word doesn't feel real.

She competed at the CrossFit Games and finished top ten.

Top ten. In the world.

"Holy shit," Sara breathes.

"Language," Kendra's mom says automatically, but she's crying.

Elena appears beside Kendra, touches her shoulder gently. "You did it. You proved you belong."

"I finished ninth," Kendra says, like she's testing the words. "Ninth at the Games."

"Ninth at the fucking Games," Chris corrects, pulling her against his chest. "You're incredible. You know that?"

Kendra doesn't know what she knows. Her body is broken. Her back might never recover. She can barely lift her arms.

But she finished ninth. At the CrossFit Games. In the world.

She closes her eyes and lets herself feel it. The exhaustion. The pain. The impossible, overwhelming pride.

She came to Madison to prove she belonged.

Mission accomplished.

---

**End of Chapter 47**
