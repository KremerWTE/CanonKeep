# Chapter 12: Open Finals

**POV:** Kendra Mitchell
**Location:** Sara's CrossFit Gym, Charlotte, NC
**Timeline:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The final Open workout dropped at 8 PM on a Thursday.

Kendra sat on her couch, phone in hand, Chris beside her with his laptop, both of them staring at the CrossFit Games Instagram like it held the secrets of the universe. Around them, half the gym crew had gathered—Sara perched on the arm of the chair, her wife Jordan cross-legged on the floor, Max and Julia sharing the loveseat, all of them waiting.

"Come on," Max muttered. "Post it already."

"Patience," Sara said, but her leg was bouncing.

Kendra's heart hammered against her ribs. Three weeks of Open workouts—three Fridays of pushing through pain and doubt and the voice in her head that said she wasn't good enough. And now, the final test.

23.3.

The workout that would determine if she'd advance to Quarterfinals or spend the next year wondering what if.

The Instagram post loaded.

Everyone leaned forward at once.

**23.3**

**FOR TIME:**

**1000m Row**

**Then, 5 Rounds of:**

**10 Wall Balls (20/14 lb to 10/9 ft)**

**15 Double-Unders**

**5 Bar Muscle-Ups**

**Time Cap: 15 minutes**

The room erupted.

"Holy hell," Julia breathed.

"That's a chipper on steroids," Max said.

"The row alone is brutal," Jordan added.

Kendra just stared at the screen, her brain already breaking down the movements. The row would torch her legs. Wall balls—her weakness, always had been—would burn her shoulders. Double-unders she could handle. But muscle-ups...

Five muscle-ups per round. Twenty-five total.

She'd done that in training. Barely. On her best days.

This would not be a best day. This would be a survival day.

"Ken?" Chris's hand found hers. "You good?"

She nodded, not trusting her voice yet.

Sara slid off the chair arm, crouching in front of Kendra. "Talk to me. What are you thinking?"

"That I might throw up," Kendra said honestly.

"Before or after you crush this thing?"

"Probably both."

Sara grinned. "That's my girl. When do you want to tackle it?"

Kendra glanced at Chris, then back at Sara. The workout had to be completed by Monday at 8 PM. Most athletes would hit it Friday night or Saturday morning—fresh, rested, ready to throw down.

But Kendra had learned something about herself over the last three weeks: she performed better under pressure when she'd had time to visualize, to gameplan, to let her body settle into the strategy.

"Sunday afternoon," she said. "Three o'clock. Gives me two days to prep and stay loose."

"Smart." Sara stood. "We'll dial in your pacing tomorrow, test your muscle-up capacity Saturday, then rest you hard Sunday morning. You'll be ready."

Kendra wanted to believe her.

She really, really did.

---

Friday was misery by design.

Sara put her through pacing drills on the rower—hitting the 1000m split over and over until Kendra's legs screamed and her lungs burned and she could hold exactly 1:58 per 500m with her eyes closed. Then wall balls—not for reps, just for rhythm. Finding the bounce, the breath, the sweet spot where the ball left her hands at the exact right moment to kiss the target.

"Smoother," Sara called. "You're muscling it. Let the hips do the work."

Kendra adjusted, dropping deeper into the squat, exploding up with her legs instead of her arms. The ball sailed up, kissed the ten-foot line, dropped back into her hands.

"There," Sara said. "That's your tempo. Remember it."

By the time they finished, Kendra could barely lift her arms. Chris met her in the parking lot with a protein shake and that patient, steady look that meant he wasn't going to let her spiral.

"How bad?" he asked.

"Bad," Kendra admitted, gulping the shake. "Sara thinks I can hold ninety seconds per round on the chipper if I don't redline on the row."

"Can you?"

"I don't know."

Chris studied her face, then leaned in to kiss her forehead. "Yes, you do. You're just scared to believe it."

He was right. He was always right.

Kendra closed her eyes, breathing him in—the familiar scent of his cologne, the steadiness that had become her anchor over the past year. He'd been there for every Open workout, every moment of doubt, every post-WOD collapse. Never pushing, never demanding. Just... present.

"What if I miss Quarterfinals by one spot?" she whispered.

"Then we figure out what's next," Chris said simply. "But Ken, you're not gonna miss by one spot. You're gonna make it. And then you're gonna do the damn thing."

Kendra pulled back to look at him. "You sound pretty confident for someone who's never done a muscle-up."

"I'm confident in you," Chris said. "That's different."

---

Saturday was muscle-up day.

Sara had her test capacity first thing—how many she could string together before failure. Kendra hit twelve unbroken, then her grip gave out and she dropped off the bar, hands shaking.

"Again," Sara said. "Ninety seconds rest."

Kendra chalked up, jumped, pulled. Ten that time. Then eight. Then six.

"Okay." Sara made a note on her clipboard. "In the workout, you'll break them early. Three-two for the first two rounds, then singles as you fatigue. Don't be a hero."

"What if I can hold triples?"

"You can't." Sara's voice was firm. "Not after a thousand-meter row and wall balls. Your grip will be smoked. Embrace the singles and keep moving."

Kendra wanted to argue. Wanted to believe she could power through, that she was strong enough to go unbroken.

But Sara knew her body better than she did. Had coached her through two years of competitions, knew exactly where Kendra's ego would sabotage her capacity.

"Fine," Kendra muttered. "Singles."

"Good girl." Sara grinned. "Now go home. Eat. Sleep. Do nothing remotely athletic until tomorrow."

"I hate rest days."

"I know. Do it anyway."

---

Saturday night, Kendra couldn't sleep.

She lay in bed beside Chris, staring at the ceiling, running the workout in her head over and over. The row. The wall balls. The double-unders. The muscle-ups. Fifteen minutes. Nine hundred seconds.

It didn't seem like enough time.

"Ken." Chris's voice was soft in the darkness. "Your heart's racing. I can feel it."

"Sorry."

"Don't apologize. Just talk to me."

Kendra turned onto her side, facing him. In the dim light from the window, she could just make out his features—the strong jaw, the kind eyes, the face that had become home.

"I'm scared," she admitted.

"Of what?"

"Failing. Not being good enough. Getting this close and falling short."

Chris reached out, tucking a strand of hair behind her ear. "Kendra, you're already good enough. You qualified for the Open. You've survived three workouts that broke half the field. You're in the fight."

"But what if—"

"No." Chris's voice was gentle but firm. "No what-ifs. Not tonight. Tonight, you rest. Tomorrow, you show up and do the work. That's all you can control."

Kendra felt tears prick her eyes, unexpected and sharp. "I don't know how you do it."

"Do what?"

"Believe in me even when I don't believe in myself."

Chris smiled, pulling her close. "That's easy. I've seen what you're capable of. I've watched you fight through every WOD, every doubt, every moment you wanted to quit but didn't. You're the strongest person I know, Ken. You just can't see it yet."

"I'm working on it."

"I know." He kissed her temple. "And one day, you'll believe it as much as I do. Until then, I'll believe enough for both of us."

Kendra pressed her face into his chest, breathing him in, letting his steadiness calm the storm in her mind.

"Thank you," she whispered.

"Always," Chris said.

---

Sunday at 3 PM, the gym was packed.

Word had spread that Kendra was attempting 23.3, and half of Charlotte's CrossFit community had shown up to watch. The wives club lined one wall—Maddie, Viv, Tali, Cam, Izzy—all in matching "Hot Girls Who Lift" tanks. Elena stood with Grace, who'd insisted on making a poster that said "GO KENDRA!!!" in rainbow letters. Addison and Hawk had claimed spots near the rower, both wearing identical expressions of calm assessment.

And Chris stood right beside the rig, hands in his pockets, eyes locked on Kendra.

"You ready?" Sara asked, adjusting the judge's tablet.

Kendra rolled her shoulders, shook out her hands, stared at the rower like it had personally offended her.

"Ready," she said.

"Alright." Sara raised her voice, cutting through the noise. "This is Kendra Mitchell's attempt at 23.3. I'll be judging. Everyone else—cheer loud, stay clear of the floor, and no coaching once we start."

The room quieted. Kendra stepped up to the rower, slid her feet into the straps, gripped the handle.

Sara held up her phone, counting down. "Three. Two. One. Go."

Kendra pulled.

---

The first five hundred meters were controlled. Smooth. Exactly the pace she'd drilled a hundred times—1:58 split, sustainable power, breathing steady.

At seven hundred meters, her legs started to burn.

At nine hundred, her lungs were screaming.

She hit one thousand meters at 3:37—three seconds slower than her best time, but exactly on pace for her game plan.

She stumbled off the rower, legs shaking, grabbed the wall ball.

*Ten reps. Just ten reps. You've done a thousand wall balls. This is nothing.*

She dropped into the squat, exploded up, released. The ball hit the target, dropped back. Again. Again.

By rep seven, her shoulders were on fire.

By rep ten, she could barely breathe.

Wall ball down. Jump rope.

Kendra's hands found the handles on muscle memory. She started jumping—fast, smooth, the rope whipping overhead. Fifteen double-unders. She'd done this in her sleep.

But her calves were screaming from the row, her grip already fatiguing, and she tripped on rep twelve.

*Damn it.*

She reset. Finished the last three. Dropped the rope.

Muscle-ups.

The bar stared down at her, cold and unforgiving.

Kendra jumped, pulled, felt her body rise. Hips to bar. Press out. One.

She dropped, reset. Two.

Three.

Her grip was already slipping. She chalked up fast, jumped again. Four.

Five.

First round done.

Sara's voice cut through the noise: "Four minutes! You're on pace!"

Kendra grabbed the wall ball.

---

The second round was hell.

Her legs were cement. Her shoulders screamed with every wall ball. The double-unders took two attempts. And the muscle-ups—

She got three, then her hands gave out. Chalked again. Pulled up for the fourth, barely made it over. The fifth took three attempts, her body refusing to cooperate, her brain screaming at her to *move*.

She dropped off the bar, gasping.

"Seven minutes!" Sara called. "Keep moving!"

Kendra grabbed the wall ball.

---

By round three, the room had started chanting.

"KEN-DRA! KEN-DRA!"

She could hear Grace's voice above the others—high and bright and completely certain that Kendra was going to finish.

Wall balls. Ten reps that felt like a hundred.

Double-unders. She tripped twice, had to restart, finally strung together fifteen.

Muscle-ups.

One.

Chalk.

Two.

Chalk.

Three.

Her hands were raw. Her forearms were cramping. She couldn't feel her fingers.

Four.

*One more. Just one more.*

She jumped. Pulled. Her body barely cleared the bar, but she pressed out, locked her arms.

Five.

She dropped, stumbled, caught herself on the rig.

"Ten minutes!" Sara shouted. "Two rounds left! You've got this!"

Kendra wanted to believe her.

---

Round four was survival.

Every wall ball was a negotiation with her body. Every double-under a battle against fatigue. And the muscle-ups—

She got the first one. Barely.

The second took two tries.

The third took four.

By the time she finished the fifth, she'd burned through ninety seconds on muscle-ups alone.

"Twelve minutes!" Sara's voice was urgent now. "Three minutes for the last round! MOVE, KENDRA!"

Kendra grabbed the wall ball, legs shaking so hard she could barely squat.

*You didn't come this far to quit now.*

Ten wall balls. Slower than before, but unbroken.

Double-unders. She tripped on rep nine, wanted to scream, reset and finished.

One round left. Five muscle-ups.

The bar looked impossibly high.

Kendra chalked her hands, gripped the bar, pulled.

One.

The crowd was deafening now. She could hear Chris's voice cutting through—steady, sure, *come on, Ken, you've got this.*

Two.

Her grip was gone. She dropped, shook out her hands, jumped again.

Three.

"Ninety seconds!" Sara screamed.

Kendra's vision tunneled. There was nothing but the bar, her breath, the burning in every muscle.

She jumped.

Four.

Sixty seconds.

One more.

She jumped, pulled with everything she had left. Her body barely cleared the bar—she was hanging, not pressing out, just hanging—

*Press. PRESS.*

She locked her arms, pressed up, felt her elbows straighten.

Five.

She dropped off the bar and collapsed.

The gym erupted.

Sara was there, shoving the tablet in her face. "TIME! Fourteen minutes, forty-two seconds! YOU'RE DONE!"

Kendra couldn't breathe. Couldn't think. Couldn't do anything but lie on the floor and shake.

Then Chris was there, pulling her up, wrapping her in his arms. "You did it. Jesus, Ken, you did it."

"Did I make it?" she gasped. "Did I—"

"We won't know until tomorrow," Sara said, crouching beside them. "But Ken, you just posted a top-fifty time in our region. You crushed it."

Kendra let her head fall against Chris's shoulder, too exhausted to cry, too relieved to speak.

Grace appeared, shoving her poster into Kendra's face. "YOU WERE SO COOL! You climbed the bar like Spider-Man!"

Kendra laughed—wet and shaky and completely overwhelmed. "Thanks, Gracie."

Elena was there too, handing her a water bottle. "Drink. Slowly."

Kendra obeyed, hands trembling so hard Chris had to steady the bottle.

Around them, the gym crew was cheering, high-fiving, replaying the final muscle-up on someone's phone. Addison met Kendra's eyes across the room and nodded once—approval, respect, the silent acknowledgment of one warrior to another.

Kendra nodded back.

She'd done it. Whatever happened next—Quarterfinals or not—she'd shown up and done the work.

That had to count for something.

---

Monday night, Kendra sat on her couch with her laptop, Chris beside her, staring at the CrossFit Games leaderboard.

The final standings had just been posted.

She was terrified to look.

"Want me to check?" Chris asked.

"No. Yes. I don't know."

"Ken—"

"Just... give me a second."

She took a breath, clicked refresh, scrolled to the North America women's division.

Her name loaded.

**31st Place - North America**

Kendra's stomach dropped. "I missed it."

Top thirty advanced directly to Quarterfinals. She was one spot out.

"Wait," Chris said, leaning closer. "Look at the notes."

There—small text at the bottom of the leaderboard:

*Athletes ranked 31-40 will advance as alternates pending video review validation.*

"Video review," Kendra breathed.

"Which means if anyone in the top thirty gets flagged for no-reps or standards violations—"

"I'm in."

They stared at the screen, not daring to hope.

Then Kendra's phone buzzed. A text from Sara: *Two athletes just got flagged for muscle-up standards. Check the board.*

Kendra refreshed.

The leaderboard shifted.

**29th Place - North America**

**QUARTERFINALS QUALIFIER**

Kendra stared at the screen. Read it again. Then again.

"Chris," she whispered. "Chris, I—"

He was already pulling her into his arms, laughing and kissing her hair and murmuring *I knew it, I knew you'd make it.*

Kendra couldn't speak. Could barely breathe.

She'd done it.

Against every doubt, every fear, every voice that said she wasn't good enough—

She'd made Quarterfinals.

Her phone exploded with texts. The wives club group chat going insane. Sara sending champagne emojis. Elena's message simple and perfect: *So proud of you.*

And Addison: *Warrior saint. Told you.*

Kendra laughed through tears, clutching her phone, Chris's arms solid around her.

"What now?" she asked.

"Now?" Chris pulled back to look at her, his smile wide and proud. "Now you rest. Then you train. Then you go to Quarterfinals and show them what you're made of."

"I'm terrified."

"Good," Chris said. "Fear means it matters. And Ken—this matters."

Kendra nodded, wiping her eyes. He was right.

This mattered.

Not because of rankings or competitions or proving herself to strangers on the internet.

But because she'd fought for something. Believed in something. Become someone she almost didn't recognize—someone brave enough to try, strong enough to finish, and humble enough to know she hadn't done it alone.

"Thank you," she said quietly.

"For what?"

"For believing in me. For being here. For—" She gestured vaguely, overwhelmed. "All of it."

Chris kissed her, slow and sure. "Always."

Later, after the texts had slowed and the adrenaline had faded, Kendra pulled up the video of her final muscle-up. Watched herself hang from the bar, shaking and exhausted, then press up and lock out.

It wasn't pretty. It wasn't perfect.

But it was hers.

And for the first time in her life, that was enough.

**End of Chapter 12**
