# CHAPTER 50: The POH Summit

**POV: Addison**
**Location: Charlotte Compound**

The conference room at the Charlotte Compound looks different today.

Addison has been in this room a hundred times — for BSS strategy sessions, for POH planning calls, for family dinners that turn into business meetings. But today, it feels formal. Official. The long table is set with portfolios and water glasses. The screens on the wall display the POH logo and agenda. There are name cards at each seat.

Today is the first major POH leadership summit since Addison was appointed to the council.

She arrives fifteen minutes early, wearing a navy suit she bought specifically for this. Professional but not corporate. Powerful but not intimidating. She wants to get this right.

Lady Margaret Ashford is already there, of course. She's sitting at the head of the table, reading something on her tablet, a cup of tea beside her. She looks up when Addison enters.

"Good morning, Addison."

"Good morning, Lady Margaret."

"Nervous?"

Addison hesitates. There's no point lying to Margaret. "A little."

Margaret smiles. "Good. That means you care." She gestures to the seat two down from her. "You're here. Council member. Your voice matters as much as anyone's."

Addison sits. She opens her portfolio and glances at her notes. She's been preparing for this presentation for weeks. Charts. Data. Projections. Case studies. She knows the material cold. But this is different. This isn't pitching a client. This is shaping the future of POH.

Elena arrives next, sharp as always in a cream blouse and dark pants. She gives Addison a quick smile and takes her seat across the table. Then Jasper, who nods at Addison before sitting beside Margaret. Then the other council members: David Chen from the West Coast chapter, Maria Santos from the international division, and James Whitfield, one of the original POH founders.

By 9 AM, everyone is seated.

Margaret stands. "Welcome. Thank you all for being here. This is our first full council summit in six months, and we have a great deal to cover. But before we begin, I want to acknowledge the newest member of this council." She looks at Addison. "Addison Barrett joined us officially last quarter. She's already proven herself invaluable to our operations, particularly in integrating BSS's crisis response capabilities with POH's humanitarian mission. I look forward to her contributions today."

Addison feels everyone's eyes on her. She nods, trying to look confident.

Margaret continues. "Our agenda today is ambitious. We'll review financials, discuss expansion into Southeast Asia, and address the integration challenges we've been facing. But first, I'd like to hear from Addison. She's prepared a proposal that I think will be of great interest to all of you."

Addison's heart jumps. This is it.

She stands, smoothing her jacket. "Thank you, Lady Margaret." She clicks the remote and the screen behind her changes to the first slide of her presentation.

**POH + BSS: A Strategic Alliance for Impact**

"Over the past year," Addison begins, "Barrett Standard Solutions and People of Hope have worked together on several joint initiatives. Haiti. Puerto Rico. The wildfire response in California. Each time, we've seen something remarkable happen: BSS's operational efficiency and crisis response capabilities combined with POH's humanitarian mission create outcomes that neither organization could achieve alone."

She clicks to the next slide. A map of the world with red dots marking crisis zones. "But we've also seen the limitations. Our collaboration has been ad hoc. Reactive rather than proactive. We respond to crises as they happen, but we're not positioned to prevent them or prepare for them."

David Chen leans forward. "What are you proposing?"

Addison clicks again. The slide changes to an organizational chart. "A formal strategic alliance. Not a merger — both organizations maintain their independence and identity. But we create a shared framework for collaboration. Joint training programs. Shared logistics infrastructure. Coordinated response protocols. And most importantly, a unified approach to what I call 'proactive humanitarianism.'"

Maria Santos raises an eyebrow. "Proactive humanitarianism?"

"Yes. Instead of only responding to disasters after they happen, we position ourselves to mitigate them before they escalate. BSS has the intelligence capabilities. POH has the community connections. Together, we can identify vulnerable areas, strengthen local infrastructure, and deploy resources before a crisis hits."

She clicks to the next slide. A case study. "Here's an example. Six months ago, BSS was monitoring political instability in a region of Central America. We identified early warning signs of violence. Normally, we'd pass that intelligence to our clients and move on. But what if, instead, we partnered with POH to deploy community organizers, set up refugee support systems, and pre-position medical supplies? When the violence did break out, we'd already be there. Not as rescuers, but as partners."

James Whitfield frowns. "That sounds expensive."

"It is," Addison admits. "Which is why we need a sustainable funding model." She clicks again. "I'm proposing a hybrid approach. BSS clients pay for the intelligence and security services they need. A percentage of those fees goes into a joint fund with POH. That fund supports the humanitarian operations. We call it 'the Alliance Fund.' Clients get world-class crisis management. Communities get disaster prevention and relief. And both organizations benefit."

Elena speaks up. "You're describing a pipeline from private sector wealth to humanitarian relief."

"Exactly." Addison meets her eyes. "We can do good and do well. They're not mutually exclusive."

There's a long silence.

David Chen breaks it. "What's the timeline?"

Addison flips to the next slide. A Gantt chart. "Phase one: six months. We formalize the alliance structure, set up the fund, and run pilot programs in three regions. Phase two: twelve months. Scale successful pilots and expand to new regions. Phase three: ongoing. Full integration of joint operations."

Margaret is studying the chart carefully. "And governance? Who makes decisions?"

"Joint oversight committee. Three members from BSS, three from POH, one independent chair. Majority vote on all major decisions. Neither organization can be overruled by the other."

"What about branding?"

"Each organization keeps its identity. Joint operations are branded as 'BSS-POH Alliance' or something similar. We're partners, not a single entity."

Maria leans back. "It's ambitious."

"It is," Addison agrees. "But I believe it's also necessary. The world is getting more complex. Crises are getting harder to manage. Neither BSS nor POH can solve these problems alone. But together? We can make a real difference."

She clicks to the final slide. A single sentence: *We can do good and do well.*

"That's my pitch," Addison says. "Questions?"

They come fast.

David wants to know about liability. Maria asks about tax implications. James pushes back on the funding model. Jasper asks pointed questions about operational integration.

Addison fields them all. She's prepared. She knows the answers. And when she doesn't, she's honest about it.

After thirty minutes, Margaret raises a hand. "Thank you, Addison. I think we've covered the key concerns. Let's take a fifteen-minute break and reconvene to discuss."

Addison sits down. Her hands are shaking slightly. She's not sure if that went well or not.

Elena catches her eye and gives a small nod. Good sign.

---

During the break, Addison steps outside to get some air. The compound is quiet. A few staff members move between buildings. The gardens are in full bloom.

Jasper finds her by the fountain.

"You did well in there," he says.

"I couldn't tell."

"They're cautious. That's their job. But you made a strong case."

Addison exhales. "I just hope it's enough."

Jasper is quiet for a moment. Then he says, "You know, when Elena first started talking about POH, I was skeptical. I didn't see how it fit with what we do at BSS. But she was right. There's a balance between profit and purpose. You just articulated that better than anyone I've heard."

Addison looks at him. "Do you support the proposal?"

"I do. And I'll say so when we go back in."

"Thank you."

He nods. "But be ready for pushback. James is old school. He'll resist anything that feels too corporate. And David will want more data."

"I have more data."

"Good. Use it."

They walk back inside together.

---

When the meeting reconvenes, Margaret opens the floor. "Thoughts?"

James speaks first. "I appreciate the vision, Addison. But I'm concerned about mission drift. POH has always been grassroots, community-driven. If we tie ourselves too closely to a for-profit entity, even one as ethical as BSS, we risk losing that identity."

"I understand that concern," Addison says carefully. "But I'd argue that the alliance strengthens POH's mission rather than dilutes it. You get access to resources, expertise, and infrastructure that would take years to build on your own. And BSS benefits from POH's credibility and community connections. It's symbiotic."

Maria jumps in. "What about conflicts of interest? What happens when a BSS client's interests conflict with a POH operation?"

"We address that in the governance structure," Addison says. "The oversight committee has veto power over any operation where there's a potential conflict. And we establish clear ethical guidelines up front."

David nods slowly. "I'd want to see those guidelines in writing."

"Absolutely. I can have a draft to you by end of week."

Margaret looks around the table. "Other concerns?"

Elena raises a hand. "I have a comment, not a concern." She looks at Addison. "I've worked with Addison for years. I've seen her in the field, in the boardroom, and in crisis situations. She understands both sides of this equation — the business side and the humanitarian side. If anyone can make this alliance work, it's her. I support the proposal."

Addison feels a surge of gratitude.

Jasper speaks next. "I support it as well. BSS has always operated on the principle that doing the right thing and doing profitable business aren't mutually exclusive. This proposal embodies that principle. I'm in."

David looks at his notes. "I'm cautiously optimistic. I'd like to see a pilot program before we commit to full-scale implementation."

"That's fair," Addison says. "That's why I proposed a phased approach."

James sighs. "I'm still concerned. But I'm willing to give it a shot. Pilot program. Clear ethical guidelines. And we revisit in six months."

"Agreed," Addison says.

Margaret looks around the table. "It seems we have consensus. Addison, you have approval to move forward with phase one. We'll establish the oversight committee, draft the ethical guidelines, and launch pilot programs in three regions. We'll reconvene in six months to assess."

Addison can barely breathe. "Thank you."

"Don't thank us yet," Margaret says, but she's smiling. "You have a lot of work ahead of you."

---

The rest of the meeting covers financials, expansion plans, and operational updates. Addison contributes where she can, but her mind is still reeling.

They approved it. She just got approval to launch a strategic alliance between BSS and POH. This is huge.

By the time the meeting ends at 4 PM, Addison is exhausted. Her brain feels like it's been through a blender.

As people file out, Elena hangs back. "Walk with me?"

They stroll through the compound gardens, not speaking for a while.

Finally, Elena says, "You impressed them today."

"I hope so."

"You did. Margaret doesn't smile like that unless she's genuinely pleased. And getting James on board? That's not easy."

Addison laughs. "He still seems skeptical."

"He is. But he respects you. That's more important."

They walk in silence for a bit longer. Then Elena says, "You know, when Jasper and I started BSS, it was just the two of us in a tiny office with a dream and a lot of ambition. We wanted to build something that mattered. Not just financially, but ethically. We didn't always get it right. But we tried."

She pauses by a bench and sits. Addison joins her.

"POH was my attempt to balance the scales," Elena continues. "To use the resources we'd built through BSS to do something purely good. But I always saw them as separate. BSS was business. POH was charity. What you're proposing — this alliance — it's what I should have seen from the beginning. They're not separate. They're two sides of the same coin."

Addison feels a lump in her throat. "I learned that from you."

Elena smiles. "Maybe. But you articulated it better than I ever could. And now you're going to make it real. That's leadership, Addison."

"I don't feel like a leader."

"Good leaders never do." Elena stands. "Come on. Let's get some coffee. You're going to need caffeine for the next six months."

They walk back toward the main building. Addison feels lighter than she has in weeks.

She's shaping POH now. Not just participating. Not just contributing. Shaping.

And it feels right.

---

That evening, Addison calls Kendra from her hotel room.

"Hey," Kendra says. "How'd the summit go?"

"They approved my proposal."

"That's amazing! I knew they would."

"Did you?"

"Of course. You're brilliant. And terrifying when you present. In a good way."

Addison laughs. "How are you feeling? Still recovering from the Games?"

"Barely walking. But worth it."

"Eighth in the world, Kendra. That's incredible."

"Thanks." There's a pause. "How long are you in Charlotte?"

"Flying back tomorrow. We have that NYC trip next week, right?"

"Yeah. Financial client. Should be straightforward."

"Good. I could use straightforward after today."

"Me too."

They talk for a few more minutes, about nothing in particular. When they hang up, Addison stares at her phone for a long moment.

Something shifts in her chest. A feeling she doesn't want to name.

She pushes it away.

Tomorrow, she flies home. Next week, New York. And then six months of building something that could change everything.

Focus on that.

Not on whatever that feeling was.

She closes her eyes and lets exhaustion pull her under.
