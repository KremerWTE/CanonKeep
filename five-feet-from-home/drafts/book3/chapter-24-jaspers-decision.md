# Chapter 24: Jasper's Decision

*POV: Jasper Barrett*

---

The house was quiet when Jasper Barrett got home.

It was just past nine p.m.—early by his old standards, reasonable by the new ones he'd been trying to maintain for the past six months. He set his briefcase by the door and walked through the first floor, following the sound of Elena's voice.

She was in the living room, curled up on the couch with a book. Lucas was asleep in the bassinet beside her, one tiny fist curled near his face. The lamp in the corner cast everything in warm, golden light.

"You're home," Elena said, looking up. She was in leggings and one of his old Duke t-shirts, her hair loose around her shoulders.

"I'm home." Jasper crossed to her, kissed her temple, then settled onto the couch beside her. "Grace asleep?"

"Since eight. She made me promise you'd wake her up when you got home, but I told her that was a bad idea."

"Probably wise." He glanced at Lucas, still marveling at how small he was. "How was your day?"

"Good. Quiet. Lucas slept through his afternoon nap time, which means he'll probably be up at three a.m., but otherwise—good." She set her book down. "How was yours?"

Jasper thought about the call he'd had with the international expansion team. About the offer they'd put on the table—again. About the number they'd floated that would have made him a very wealthy man doing work he was exceptionally good at in cities he'd barely have time to see.

"Long," he said. "Can we talk?"

Elena's expression shifted—just slightly, a wariness creeping into her eyes. "That sounds ominous."

"It's not. I mean—it's big. But not ominous." He reached for her hand, laced his fingers through hers. "The expansion team called again."

"Jasper—"

"Let me finish." He squeezed her hand gently. "They called. They made their pitch. They offered me a number that would set us up for life and then some. Full operational control, my own team, the ability to build something global and permanent."

Elena's hand had gone very still in his. "And what did you say?"

"I told them I needed to talk to you first. That I don't make decisions like this without you anymore."

"But you're considering it."

"No." Jasper turned to face her fully. "I'm not. I'm turning it down."

Elena stared at him. "What?"

"I'm turning it down. The expansion. The global role. All of it."

"Jasper, that's—that's everything you've been working toward."

"I know."

"That's the opportunity you've been building BSS to reach. That's the endgame."

"I know." He reached up, brushed a strand of hair behind her ear. "And now that it's here, I don't want it."

Elena's eyes were filling with tears. "You don't mean that."

"I do. Elena, I spent my whole career building toward that moment. I told myself that's what success looked like—global reach, elite clients, operating in every major city, every crisis, every high-stakes situation. I thought if I could just get there, I'd have proven something. That I'd arrived."

"And now?"

"Now I know what I was trying to prove, and I don't need to prove it anymore." He glanced at Lucas, then at the stairs where Grace was sleeping. "I don't want to be the guy who's never home. Who misses recitals and first steps and the thousand small moments that make up a life. I don't want to be five feet from home and a thousand miles away."

Elena let out a choked laugh-sob. "You can't just walk away from something that big."

"I'm not walking away. I'm choosing something better."

"What if you regret it?"

"I won't. You want to know how I know?" He pulled her closer, until her head was resting against his shoulder. "Because when I think about the next ten years, the version where I take that job looks impressive on paper and empty everywhere else. The version where I stay here, where I mentor people like Addie and build something sustainable instead of something global—that's the version where I'm actually happy."

"Jasper..." Elena's voice broke. "I don't want you to resent me. Or the kids. I don't want you looking back in five years and wondering what you gave up."

"That's not going to happen."

"You don't know that."

"I do. Because I already lived the other version. I already spent years chasing the thing I thought I wanted, and it almost cost me everything that actually mattered." He tilted her chin up so she was looking at him. "I'm not making that mistake again."

Elena kissed him then—soft and desperate and full of relief. When she pulled back, tears were streaming down her face.

"So what are you going to do instead?" she asked.

"Mentor. Advise. Be here." He smiled. "Addison's ready to take over lead operations. Harper's running the day-to-day. Cole and Dean have the tactical side covered. I don't need to be in every meeting, on every plane, at every crisis. I can be the guy who built the thing and now trusts other people to run it."

"You trust Addison that much?"

"I do. I saw it in Philadelphia. She doesn't need me anymore—not the way she used to. She's ready. And honestly, it's a relief."

Elena laughed again, this time with more joy than tears. "A relief?"

"Yeah. Because it means I did my job. I built something that doesn't fall apart the moment I step back. That's legacy, Elena. That's the thing I'm actually proud of."

She wiped her eyes, smiling through the tears. "When did you get so wise?"

"About six months ago, when my wife nearly died and my daughter asked if I was on our team." He kissed her forehead. "I'm a slow learner, but I get there eventually."

---

They sat together in the quiet, Lucas's soft breathing the only sound. Jasper thought about the phone call he'd have to make tomorrow, the one where he'd officially turn down the expansion role. He thought about the conversations with the team, the restructuring, the shift from operator to advisor.

It should have felt like loss. Like stepping back. Like giving up.

Instead, it felt like finally stepping into the right-sized life.

"Can I ask you something?" Elena said, her voice soft.

"Anything."

"Are you scared?"

"Terrified," Jasper admitted. "I've spent my entire adult life defined by the work. By the crises I solve, the clients I serve, the reputation I've built. Stepping back from that—even partially—means figuring out who I am when I'm not the guy running into the fire."

"And who do you think you are?"

Jasper looked around the room. At the photos on the mantel—Elena and Grace at the beach, Lucas's first portrait, the family shot from the baptism. At the books on the shelves, the toys in the basket by the couch, the signs of a life lived in the margins between crises.

"I think I'm a husband. A dad. A mentor. Someone who built something worth building and is smart enough to let other people carry it forward." He squeezed Elena's hand. "I think I'm the guy who finally figured out what 'home' means."

Elena rested her head on his shoulder. "I like that guy."

"Yeah?"

"Yeah." She kissed his jaw. "He's my favorite."

---

They went upstairs together, moving quietly so as not to wake Grace. Jasper checked on her anyway, slipping into her room and standing beside her bed.

She was sprawled across the mattress, her stuffed penguin—Sir Waddles—tucked under one arm. Her hair was a mess of tangles, and she'd kicked off her blanket at some point.

Jasper pulled the blanket back over her, tucking it gently around her shoulders. She stirred, mumbling something about flamingos, then settled back into sleep.

"Love you, Bug," he whispered.

In the hallway, Elena was waiting. "You're getting sentimental."

"I'm allowed."

"You are." She took his hand, led him to their bedroom. "So what happens now?"

"Now? Now I make the call. I restructure. I figure out what my new role looks like." He sat on the edge of the bed, pulling off his shoes. "And I probably spend the next six months second-guessing myself every time something big comes through."

"But you'll stay?"

"I'll stay. Here. In this. With you." He looked up at her. "That's the deal I'm making. With you, with myself, with whatever version of success actually matters."

Elena crossed to him, stood between his knees, and cupped his face in her hands. "You know what I think?"

"What?"

"I think you just became the man I always knew you could be."

"That's a hell of a thing to say."

"It's true. You were always brilliant, always capable, always the guy who could solve impossible problems. But this—choosing us, choosing to be present—this is the thing I wasn't sure you'd ever figure out."

"And now that I have?"

She smiled. "Now we get to find out what the rest of our lives looks like when you're actually in them."

---

Later, when Elena was asleep and the house had settled into its late-night stillness, Jasper found himself in the kitchen, staring out the window at the backyard.

The swing set he'd built for Grace was barely visible in the darkness. The garden Elena had started last spring was a tangle of shadows. Beyond that, Oak Watch stretched out into the quiet Carolina night.

Jasper thought about the crises still waiting—because there were always crises. The NFL ownership dispute that Harper was managing. The supply chain whistleblower that Addison had contained. The hundred small fires that made up BSS's day-to-day operations.

He thought about the global expansion role, the cities he'd never see, the clients he'd never work with, the reputation he'd never build beyond what he already had.

And then he thought about Grace at the zoo with Addison, standing on one leg like a flamingo. About Lucas learning to smile. About Elena rebuilding her strength, her business, her life. About the five feet around him that contained everything that actually mattered.

He pulled out his phone and opened a new message to the expansion team's lead contact.

*Thank you for the offer. After careful consideration, I've decided to decline. BSS will continue to operate at our current capacity, with a focus on mentorship and sustainable growth rather than global expansion. I'm confident in the team we've built and excited about this next chapter.*

He hit send before he could second-guess it.

The response came back within minutes—a polite acknowledgment, an expression of disappointment, an open door if he ever changed his mind.

Jasper deleted the message and set his phone down.

"You're really doing this."

He turned to find Elena in the doorway, wrapped in a robe, her hair sleep-mussed.

"I really am."

"No regrets?"

"Not a single one." He crossed to her, pulled her into his arms. "This is the right call. I know it is."

"How?"

"Because for the first time in my career, I made a decision based on what I want my life to look like, not what I think success is supposed to look like." He kissed the top of her head. "That's growth, right?"

"That's everything." Elena pulled back just enough to look at him. "You know what happens now?"

"What?"

"Now you have to actually figure out how to be present. How to be the mentor, the husband, the dad who's here. That's harder than running toward crises."

"I know. But I'm ready to try."

"Good. Because we're holding you to it."

"I'd be disappointed if you didn't."

---

They went back upstairs together, back to the bedroom where Lucas was starting to stir in his bassinet. Jasper picked him up, walked him to the window, and stood there in the darkness with his son in his arms.

"Here's the thing, buddy," Jasper whispered. "Your old man spent a long time figuring out what mattered. Took your mom almost dying and your sister asking if I was on the team to wake me up. But I'm here now. I'm choosing this. Choosing you, and Grace, and your mom, and the life we're building in this house."

Lucas made a small sound—not quite a cry, just an acknowledgment that someone was holding him.

"I'm going to mess up," Jasper continued. "Probably a lot. I'm going to take calls I shouldn't take and miss moments I should be there for. But I'm going to try. And when I fail, I'm going to get back up and try again. That's the promise I'm making you."

Behind him, Elena was back in bed, watching. "He's not going to remember this, you know."

"I know. But I will."

"Good."

Jasper settled Lucas back in the bassinet, watched him drift back to sleep, then climbed into bed beside Elena. She curled into him, her head on his chest, her hand over his heart.

"Thank you," she whispered.

"For what?"

"For choosing us. For being brave enough to walk away from the thing you thought you wanted."

"It's not brave. It's just finally being smart enough to know the difference between impressive and important."

"Same thing."

"Maybe."

They lay there in the quiet, the house settling around them. Jasper thought about the morning, about the calls he'd have to make, the conversations he'd have to have, the restructuring that would follow.

But for now, he was exactly where the five feet around him needed him to be.

And that was enough.

More than enough.

It was everything.
