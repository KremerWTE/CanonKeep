# Chapter 53: Harper's Proposal

**POV:** Harper
**Location:** Miami
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The text comes at 2 PM on a Thursday.

Harper's in her Miami office—the corner suite she negotiated when she took over the satellite expansion, floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking Biscayne Bay, the kind of view that still makes her pause sometimes when she looks up from her laptop. She's on a video call with a potential Dubai partner when her phone buzzes on the desk.

**Ethan:** When are you free this weekend?

She types back one-handed, still half-listening to the man on her screen explain regulatory frameworks.

**Harper:** Sunday afternoon. Why?

**Ethan:** I'm coming to Miami. Want to show you something.

Harper's chest does something complicated. Ethan hasn't visited in three weeks—not because he doesn't want to, but because she's been drowning in work. London expansion meetings. Paris partner negotiations. Late nights building financial models that would make or break BSS's European presence.

She'd told him she was too busy. That she'd come to Charlotte when things settled down.

He'd said okay. He always said okay.

But now he's coming here.

**Harper:** Okay. Sunday works.

**Ethan:** Perfect. I'll pick you up at 4.

She sets the phone down and tries to refocus on the call. But her mind keeps drifting back to the text. *Want to show you something.* Ethan's not usually cryptic. He's steady, straightforward, the kind of man who says exactly what he means.

Which means this is something he can't explain over text.

The call ends twenty minutes later. Harper closes her laptop and stares out at the bay. The water is turquoise and endless, dotted with sailboats and the occasional yacht. It's beautiful. Aspirational. Everything she wanted when she fought for this role.

But it's also lonely.

She picks up her phone again and scrolls to Ethan's contact. His photo is from six months ago—sitting on his couch, laptop balanced on his knees, grinning at something she'd said off-camera. He looks relaxed. Happy. Like he's exactly where he wants to be.

Harper wonders if he still feels that way.

If she does.

---

Sunday arrives overcast and humid.

Harper spends the morning answering emails and reviewing contracts for the Paris office. By the time 4 PM rolls around, she's changed outfits three times—settling finally on jeans and a white linen blouse, casual but put-together. She doesn't know where they're going. Doesn't know what Ethan wants to show her.

But the nervous energy in her chest suggests it's something that matters.

Ethan texts when he's downstairs. Harper grabs her sunglasses and heads down to meet him.

He's leaning against his rental car—a sleek black sedan that looks expensive even by Miami standards. When he sees her, his face breaks into that easy smile she's been carrying in her head for three weeks.

"Hey," he says, pushing off the car.

"Hey yourself." Harper closes the distance between them, and he pulls her into a hug that feels like coming home. She breathes him in—familiar cologne, the faint scent of coffee, something that's just *Ethan*. When they pull apart, she studies his face. "You look good."

"You look better," he says, and there's something in his voice that makes her heart kick harder.

"So," Harper says, trying to sound casual. "Where are we going?"

Ethan opens the passenger door for her. "You'll see."

---

They drive south along the coast, windows down, the smell of salt air mixing with exhaust and the faint sweetness of blooming jacarandas. Ethan doesn't offer details, just keeps one hand on the wheel and the other resting on the center console, close enough that Harper could reach for it if she wanted to.

She doesn't. Not yet.

After twenty minutes, he pulls into a marina. It's smaller than the big tourist spots—more intimate, with weathered docks and fishing boats bobbing alongside a few sleek sailboats. Ethan parks and kills the engine.

"This is our spot," Harper says slowly.

Ethan glances at her, something soft in his expression. "Yeah. It is."

Their spot. The place they came on their third date, when Ethan was visiting Miami for work and Harper had just started thinking maybe long-distance wasn't as impossible as it sounded. They'd walked the dock at sunset, sharing street tacos and talking about everything and nothing. It was the night Harper realized she was falling for him.

The night she started believing this could actually work.

"Come on," Ethan says, climbing out of the car.

Harper follows, her pulse picking up. They walk down to the dock, past boats with names like *Second Chance* and *Wanderlust*. The sun's breaking through the clouds now, turning the water gold. Ethan stops at the end of the dock, where it's quiet and private, just the two of them and the sound of water lapping against wood.

Harper turns to face him. "Ethan, what's going on?"

He looks at her for a long moment. His hands are in his pockets, shoulders tense in a way that's not like him. He's nervous, she realizes. Ethan Reed—steady, unflappable Ethan—is *nervous*.

"I've been thinking a lot," he says finally. "About us. About this."

Harper's stomach drops. "Okay."

"You're building an empire," Ethan continues. "Miami, London, Paris, maybe Dubai. You're doing exactly what you're supposed to be doing, Harper. You're incredible at it."

"But?" Harper hears herself say, even though she doesn't want to.

"But nothing." Ethan takes a step closer. "There's no *but*. I'm not asking you to slow down or choose between me and your career. I'm saying I see you. I see what you're building. And I want to build it with you."

Harper blinks. "What?"

Ethan reaches into his pocket and pulls out a small velvet box.

The world tilts.

"I know this life is crazy," Ethan says, voice steady now. "I know you're in Miami and I'm in Charlotte and next month you might be in Paris or London or halfway around the world. I know you work eighty-hour weeks and sometimes we go days without seeing each other." He opens the box. Inside is a ring—simple, elegant, a single diamond on a platinum band that catches the light and throws it back in a thousand directions. "But I also know I love you. I know I want to wake up every morning and figure out how to make this work. I know I want to be the person you call when you close a deal or when everything falls apart or when you just need to hear a voice that isn't a client or a partner or your boss."

Harper's eyes are stinging. She can't breathe.

"I know this isn't traditional," Ethan says, and now he's smiling—that soft, crooked smile that undoes her every time. "I know proposing to someone who's about to spend half the year on another continent probably seems insane. But I don't care. I want you, Harper. All of you. The empire-building, workaholic, brilliant, beautiful you." He pauses. "So. Will you marry me?"

Harper stares at him. At the ring. At the man who just flew to Miami to propose on a dock because he knows her well enough to skip the fancy restaurant and the big production.

The man who sees her ambition and doesn't ask her to shrink it.

The man who wants to build *with* her, not in spite of her.

"Yes," she whispers. Then louder: "Yes."

Ethan's grin could light up the whole city. He slips the ring onto her finger—it fits perfectly, because of course it does—and then he's kissing her, one hand cupping her face, the other at her waist. Harper kisses him back, arms around his neck, and for the first time in weeks the knot in her chest loosens.

When they finally pull apart, she's laughing and crying at the same time.

"You're insane," she says.

"Probably," Ethan agrees. "But you said yes anyway."

Harper looks down at the ring on her finger. It's real. He's real. This is happening.

"I have to call Elena," she says suddenly.

Ethan laughs. "Right now?"

"Yes, right now." Harper's already pulling out her phone. "And Jasper. Oh my God, Jasper's going to lose his mind."

"Can I at least kiss you again first?"

Harper grins. "You have the rest of your life to kiss me."

"Good," Ethan says, and does it anyway.

---

Harper calls Elena first.

They're sitting on the hood of Ethan's rental car, Harper's hand still trembling slightly as she holds the phone. It rings three times before Elena picks up.

"Hey, Harper. What's—"

"I'm engaged," Harper blurts out.

Silence. Then: "What?"

"Ethan proposed. Like, ten minutes ago. I said yes." Harper's grinning so hard her face hurts. "I'm getting married, Elena."

Elena makes a sound that's half-laugh, half-squeal. "Oh my God. Oh my *God*, Harper. Are you serious?"

"Completely serious. He flew to Miami and proposed on our dock and I'm—" Harper looks at Ethan, who's watching her with that soft, adoring expression that makes her chest ache. "I'm really happy."

"I'm so happy for you," Elena says, and her voice is thick with emotion. "You deserve this. You deserve someone who sees you and loves you exactly as you are."

Harper's throat tightens. "Thanks, Elena."

"When are you coming home? We need to celebrate. The whole BSS family—we're throwing you a party."

"I'm back next weekend," Harper says. "But I'm calling Jasper right after this."

"He's going to cry," Elena warns.

"I know."

They talk for a few more minutes—Elena asking about the ring, the proposal, whether Harper screamed or cried or both. When they finally hang up, Harper immediately dials Jasper.

He answers on the second ring. "Harper. What's up?"

"I'm engaged," Harper says.

Jasper's silent for a beat. Then: "Say that again."

"Ethan proposed. I said yes. We're getting married."

Another beat. Then Jasper laughs—a sound Harper doesn't hear from him often, full and genuine and a little disbelieving. "Jesus, Harper. Congratulations."

"Thanks, boss."

"He's good for you," Jasper says, and there's something serious in his tone now. "He makes you happy. That's all I've ever wanted for you."

Harper's eyes sting again. Jasper Barrett isn't the sentimental type—he's strategic, controlled, always three moves ahead. But he's also the man who gave her a chance when no one else would. Who trusted her with the expansion. Who's been part of her life for years now, part of her family in all the ways that count.

"You're going soft on me," Harper says, voice light to cover the emotion.

"Don't tell anyone," Jasper says. "Ruins my reputation."

Harper laughs. "Your secret's safe."

"When are you back?"

"Next weekend. Elena's already planning a party."

"Good. We'll do it right. The whole team. Cole, Addison, everyone." He pauses. "Seriously, Harper. I'm proud of you. Not just for this. For everything."

Harper swallows hard. "Thanks, Jasper. That means a lot."

When she hangs up, Ethan's watching her with a knowing smile.

"He cried, didn't he?"

"He did not," Harper says. "But it was close."

Ethan pulls her close, and she leans into him, letting herself feel it—the joy, the relief, the bone-deep certainty that this is right.

"I love you," she says.

"I love you too," Ethan murmurs against her hair. "Future Mrs. Reed."

Harper laughs. "I'm keeping my last name."

"I know. I wouldn't expect anything less."

---

The party happens the following Saturday.

Harper flies back to Charlotte on Friday night, and by Saturday afternoon, Elena has transformed Jasper and Elena's backyard into something out of a magazine. String lights, a champagne bar, tables loaded with food. The entire BSS crew is there—Jasper and Elena, Addison and Kendra, Cole and his wife, Sara and her husband. Even a few clients Harper's grown close to over the years.

When Harper arrives with Ethan, everyone erupts into applause.

"Speech!" Cole shouts, grinning.

Harper laughs, shaking her head. "I'm not giving a speech."

"You absolutely are," Elena says, pressing a champagne glass into her hand. "Come on. Tell us how he proposed."

So Harper does. She tells them about the dock, the ring, the way Ethan said he wanted to build her empire with her. By the time she finishes, half the women are tearing up and the men are clapping Ethan on the back.

"You're a brave man," Jasper says to Ethan, shaking his hand. "Marrying someone who works harder than I do."

"I know what I'm getting into," Ethan says, smiling.

Addison pulls Harper into a hug. "I'm so happy for you."

"Thanks, Addie." Harper squeezes her tight. "You're next, you know."

Addison pulls back, laughing. "I don't even have a boyfriend."

"Details," Harper says, winking.

Kendra's standing a few feet away, talking to Chris, but she catches Harper's eye and lifts her glass in a silent toast. Harper smiles back. She and Kendra aren't close the way she and Addison are, but there's respect there. Solidarity. They're both women building something in a world that doesn't always make space for them.

As the afternoon stretches into evening, Harper finds herself standing at the edge of the yard, Ethan's arm around her waist, watching the people she loves celebrate her happiness.

This is her family. Not by blood, but by choice. By sweat and late nights and shared victories and losses. By the slow, steady work of building something that lasts.

"Happy?" Ethan asks quietly.

Harper looks up at him. "Yeah. I really am."

"Good." He kisses her temple. "Because we've got a wedding to plan."

Harper groans. "Don't remind me."

"You're going to be one of those brides who has a color-coded spreadsheet, aren't you?"

"Absolutely."

Ethan laughs, and Harper grins, leaning into him as the party continues around them.

She's building an empire. She's planning a wedding. She's got London and Paris and maybe Dubai on the horizon.

And for the first time in her life, Harper Sinclair has it all.

Or at least, she's building it.

One deal, one flight, one yes at a time.

---

**End of Chapter 53**
