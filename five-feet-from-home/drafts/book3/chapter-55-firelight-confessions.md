# Chapter 55: Firelight Confessions

**POV:** Kendra
**Location:** Blue Ridge Cabin
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

Harper and Sara go to bed early.

Not actually early—it's past eleven—but early for them. Early enough that Kendra knows it's intentional. Harper catches her eye on the way upstairs, raises an eyebrow in a silent question. *You okay?*

Kendra nods. She's not okay. Hasn't been okay since New York. But she's not going to say that out loud. Not to Harper. Not yet.

"Night," Sara calls from the landing, her voice warm with wine and exhaustion.

"Night," Kendra and Addison say in unison.

Then it's just the two of them.

The fire's burning low in the stone fireplace, casting flickering orange light across the living room. Outside, the mountains are invisible in the darkness, but Kendra can feel them—solid and ancient and impossibly vast. The kind of presence that makes you feel small in the best way.

Addison's on the couch, legs tucked under her, staring into the fire. She's still in her hiking clothes—leggings and a faded Duke Basketball t-shirt that's soft from a thousand washes. Her hair's down, which is rare. She usually keeps it pulled back, efficient and out of the way.

Kendra likes it down.

"More wine?" Addison asks, not looking at her.

"Sure."

Addison pours what's left of the bottle into their glasses—not much, maybe half a glass each. They've been drinking all weekend, but not enough to blur the edges. Not enough to use as an excuse.

Kendra takes her glass and settles into the armchair across from the couch. Close, but not too close. A safe distance.

Or at least, she tells herself it's safe.

They sit in silence for a while. Not uncomfortable, exactly. Just weighted. Like they're both waiting for the other to speak first. To acknowledge the thing they've been dancing around since they got here.

Since New York.

Finally, Addison breaks. "We should talk about it."

Kendra's stomach clenches. "Yeah. We should."

Addison takes a sip of wine, still not looking at her. "I don't know how, though."

"Me neither."

The fire pops. A log shifts, sending up a spray of sparks. Kendra watches them rise and disappear into the chimney.

"It was just a kiss," Kendra hears herself say. The words sound hollow even to her own ears.

Addison finally looks at her. "Was it?"

Kendra's throat tightens. "I don't know."

"Me neither."

They stare at each other across the space between them. The firelight makes shadows of Addison's face—sharp cheekbones, the line of her jaw, eyes that look almost black in the dim light.

Kendra wants to reach for her. Wants to cross the space between them and kiss her the way she did in New York. Wants to stop pretending this is something she can ignore.

But she doesn't move.

"I have a boyfriend," Kendra says quietly.

"I know."

"A good boyfriend. A guy who loves me and wants to build a life with me."

Addison nods. "Chris. I know."

"So why—" Kendra stops. Takes a breath. "Why can't I stop thinking about you?"

The confession hangs in the air between them. Raw. Honest. Terrifying.

Addison sets her wine glass down carefully. "I don't know. But I can't either."

Kendra closes her eyes. This is a mistake. This whole conversation is a mistake. She should go to bed. Should pretend this never happened. Should go home and focus on Chris and the life they're building and stop thinking about the way Addison looked at her in New York.

But she can't.

"What are we doing, Addie?"

Addison's quiet for a long moment. When she speaks, her voice is rough. "I don't know. I've been asking myself the same question for weeks."

"And?"

"And I don't have an answer." Addison leans forward, elbows on her knees. "I know what we *should* do. We should forget New York happened. We should go back to being friends. You should focus on Chris. I should focus on coaching. We should move on."

"But?"

Addison looks at her, and the expression on her face makes Kendra's chest ache. "But I don't want to."

Kendra's breath catches. "Addie—"

"I'm not asking you to choose," Addison says quickly. "I'm not asking for anything. I'm just... I'm being honest. I don't want to forget New York. I don't want to pretend it didn't mean something."

Kendra sets her own glass down before she drops it. Her hands are shaking. "What did it mean?"

Addison stands abruptly, crossing to the fireplace. She stares into the flames, her back to Kendra. "I don't know. That's the problem. I don't know if it was just... proximity. Adrenaline. Two people caught up in a moment." She turns, meeting Kendra's eyes. "Or if it was something more."

"And if it was more?" Kendra whispers.

"Then we're screwed," Addison says, but she's almost smiling. "Because you have Chris. And I have... I don't know. My career. My life in Charlotte. A thousand reasons why this is a terrible idea."

Kendra stands too, closing some of the distance between them. Not all of it. Just enough. "You didn't answer my question."

"What question?"

"What did New York mean to you?"

Addison's quiet. The fire crackles. Somewhere upstairs, a floorboard creaks.

"It meant everything," Addison says finally. "And that scares the hell out of me."

Kendra's chest is so tight she can barely breathe. "Me too."

They stand there, three feet apart, the fire burning between them. Kendra can see Addison's pulse in her throat. Can see the way her hands are clenched at her sides, like she's physically holding herself back.

Kendra understands. She's doing the same thing.

"I don't know how to go back," Kendra says. "To before. To just being friends."

"I don't either."

"But I can't—" Kendra stops. Forces herself to say it. "I can't do this to Chris. He doesn't deserve that."

"No," Addison agrees quietly. "He doesn't."

The acknowledgment should help. Should make this easier. It doesn't.

"So what do we do?" Kendra asks.

Addison's smile is sad. "I guess we figure it out. One day at a time. One conversation at a time."

"That's not an answer."

"I know." Addison moves closer, just one step. "But it's all I have."

Kendra could close the distance. Could reach for Addison the way she did in New York. Could kiss her and damn the consequences.

But she doesn't.

Instead, she steps back.

"I should go to bed," Kendra says.

Addison nods. "Yeah. Me too."

But neither of them moves.

They stand there in the firelight, the space between them charged with everything they're not saying. Everything they can't say.

Finally, Kendra turns and heads for the pullout couch. She can feel Addison watching her. Can feel the weight of all the things they just confessed hanging in the air.

She climbs under the covers and closes her eyes, listening to Addison bank the fire and turn off the lights.

The bed dips as Addison climbs in on the other side. Six inches between them. Maybe less.

Kendra keeps her eyes closed. Keeps her breathing even. Pretends she's already asleep.

But she's not.

She's hyperaware of every sound. The rustle of blankets. Addison's breathing evening out. The settling of the cabin around them.

"Ken?" Addison's voice is barely a whisper.

Kendra's throat tightens. "Yeah?"

"I'm glad we talked."

Kendra swallows hard. "Me too."

Silence. Then: "Goodnight."

"Goodnight, Addie."

Kendra lies awake for a long time after that, staring into the darkness, thinking about everything they said and everything they didn't.

About Chris, steady and loving and waiting for her back in Charlotte.

About Addison, three feet away, close enough to touch.

About the choice she's going to have to make.

Eventually.

But not tonight.

Tonight, she lets herself exist in the space between. The not-knowing. The possibility.

Tomorrow, they'll drive back to Charlotte. Harper and Sara will chatter in the backseat. Kendra will watch the mountains fade in the rearview mirror.

And Addison will drive, eyes on the road, hands at ten and two.

And nothing will be resolved.

Everything will still be changing.

But at least now, they've stopped pretending.

---

The next morning is awkward.

Not terribly so—they're too good at performing normalcy for that. But there's a carefulness to the way Kendra and Addison move around each other. A hyperawareness of space and proximity.

Harper notices. Of course Harper notices.

"You two fight or something?" she asks while they're packing up the cars.

"No," Kendra says quickly. "Why?"

Harper gives her a look. "You're being weird. Both of you."

"We're not being weird," Addison says, hefting her duffel into the trunk.

"You absolutely are." Harper crosses her arms. "Did something happen?"

"No," Kendra and Addison say in unison.

Harper's eyes narrow. "Okay, that was suspicious."

Sara appears on the porch, her own bags in hand. "What's suspicious?"

"These two," Harper says, jerking her thumb at Kendra and Addison. "Being weird."

Sara studies them. "They have been kind of quiet."

"We're fine," Addison says firmly. "Can we just get on the road? I have a session at three."

Harper doesn't look convinced, but she drops it.

They finish loading the cars in silence.

---

The drive back is exactly as Kendra predicted.

Harper and Sara in the back, trading stories about wedding planning and Sara's kids. Addison driving, focused on the road. Kendra in the passenger seat, watching the mountains recede in the side mirror.

Every few minutes, her eyes drift to the rearview mirror. To Addison's face, reflected there.

And every time, Addison's looking back.

Their eyes meet. Hold. Look away.

Again and again and again.

It's torture.

It's also the most honest they've been with each other in weeks.

By the time they hit Charlotte city limits, Kendra's exhausted. Not from the trip—from the effort of pretending. Of holding herself together when all she wants to do is fall apart.

Addison drops Sara off first, then Harper. Then it's just the two of them.

"Your place or Chris's?" Addison asks.

"Mine," Kendra says. Chris is at work. She has a few hours before she has to see him. Before she has to smile and pretend everything's normal.

Addison pulls up in front of Kendra's apartment building and puts the car in park.

They sit in silence for a moment.

"Thanks for driving," Kendra says finally.

"No problem."

Kendra reaches for the door handle, then stops. "Addie?"

"Yeah?"

"I meant what I said last night. I don't know how to go back."

Addison's hands tighten on the steering wheel. "I know."

"But I have to try."

Addison nods. Her jaw's tight. "I know that too."

Kendra opens the door and climbs out. She grabs her bag from the backseat and closes the door.

Through the window, Addison's watching her. Her expression is carefully neutral, but her eyes tell a different story.

Kendra lifts her hand in a small wave.

Addison nods once. Then she pulls away from the curb and drives off.

Kendra stands on the sidewalk, watching until the car disappears around the corner.

Then she heads inside, already dreading the moment Chris gets home.

Already wondering how long she can keep doing this.

Pretending she's not in love with someone else.

---

**End of Chapter 55**
