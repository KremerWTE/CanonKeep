# Chapter 43: Grace's First Communion

**POV: Elena Barrett**
**Location: Charlotte Church**

Grace is wearing white, and I'm already crying.

She hasn't even walked down the aisle yet — we're still in the church lobby, straightening veils and smoothing dresses and taking a thousand pictures — but the tears are already coming. Jasper catches my eye and smiles, his own eyes suspiciously bright.

"Mom, you said you weren't going to cry yet," Grace says, but she's grinning, clearly pleased with herself.

"I lied," I admit, dabbing at my eyes with the tissue Sara quietly handed me. "You're too beautiful. How am I supposed to not cry?"

And she is beautiful. Seven years old in a white dress that makes her look like she's playing dress-up and also like she's growing up too fast. Her hair is in soft curls that Addison spent an hour on this morning, pinned back with a small white flower clip. She's glowing.

"Five minutes," one of the church coordinators calls out.

Lucas appears, looking uncomfortable in his dress shirt and tie. "Do I have to sit still the whole time?"

"Yes," Jasper and I say in unison.

"Can I at least bring my—"

"No," we say again.

He sighs dramatically, the put-upon older brother, but I see him reach over and straighten Grace's veil with a gentleness that makes my heart squeeze.

Addison emerges from the bathroom, and Grace immediately runs to her.

"Addie! You look so pretty!"

Addison laughs, catching Grace in a hug. She does look pretty — wearing a simple navy dress, her hair down for once, a delicate cross necklace at her throat. But more than pretty, she looks happy. Proud. Like she understands the significance of this moment.

"You're the pretty one today," Addison tells Grace. "Are you ready?"

"I think so." Grace fidgets with her dress. "What if I forget what to do?"

"You won't. We practiced, remember? And I'll be right there with you."

Addison is Grace's sponsor — the person chosen to guide her in faith, to stand with her as she receives the Eucharist for the first time. When Grace asked her months ago, Addison had looked so surprised, almost overwhelmed. But she'd said yes without hesitation.

And then she'd shown up. To every preparation class, every practice session, every meeting with Father Michael. She'd learned alongside Grace, asking questions with genuine curiosity, engaging with the faith she was still finding her way toward.

"Okay, families, let's line up!" the coordinator calls.

We shuffle into order — Grace and Addison near the front with the other First Communicants and their sponsors, Jasper and me and Lucas further back with the families. The processional music starts, a soft instrumental that fills the church.

Jasper takes my hand. Lucas stands between us, surprisingly still.

And we walk our daughter down the aisle toward the altar, toward this sacrament, toward a moment that feels too big for my heart to hold.

---

The Mass is beautiful.

Father Michael speaks about the Eucharist, about communion, about what it means to receive the body and blood of Christ. Grace is in the front pew, sitting up very straight, her attention focused in a way I rarely see outside of particularly exciting books.

Addison sits beside her, and I watch them during the homily. Watch Addison lean down to whisper something that makes Grace smile. Watch Grace reach over and take Addison's hand, holding it like it's the most natural thing in the world.

They belong to each other, I think. Whatever complicated relationship Addison and Kendra are navigating, whatever questions Addison is still working through about faith and family and belonging — this, right here, is clear. Grace and Addison are each other's people.

The moment comes for Communion. The First Communicants stand, line up with their sponsors. Grace looks tiny next to some of the older children, but she walks with confidence, Addison's hand on her shoulder.

Father Michael blesses the elements. The ancient words fill the church: "This is my body, given for you. This is my blood, shed for you."

Grace kneels at the altar rail. Addison kneels beside her, one hand still on Grace's back — a quiet support, a physical reminder that she's not alone.

Father Michael approaches. He holds the host before Grace.

"The body of Christ, Grace."

"Amen," she says clearly.

She receives the Eucharist, her small hands folded in prayer, and my tears spill over.

Jasper's arm comes around my shoulders. "I know," he whispers. "I know."

We watch as Addison receives Communion too — she's been through RCIA, was confirmed just six months ago, and there's something profound about watching her and Grace share this moment. Two people, both newly welcomed into the Catholic faith, both still figuring out what it means, both holding onto it with their whole hearts.

The rest of the Mass passes in a blur of standing and sitting and singing, the ritual washing over me in waves. When we finally process out, following Grace and the other children into the bright Sunday morning, I feel wrung out and full at the same time.

"Mom!" Grace runs to me the moment we're outside, white dress flying. "I did it!"

"You did, baby. You were perfect." I hug her tight, breathing in the smell of her strawberry shampoo and the faint incense from the church.

"Did you see me? Did you see when Father Michael—"

"I saw everything," I assure her. "Every single moment."

Jasper scoops her up, spinning her around. "I'm so proud of you, Gracie-girl."

Lucas hovers nearby, trying to look cool but clearly invested. "You did good, I guess. For a little kid."

"I'm not a little kid," Grace protests from Jasper's arms. "I just had my First Communion. That means I'm basically a grown-up."

"Oh, definitely," Addison says, appearing beside us. "Totally a grown-up now. Which means you can start paying rent."

Grace's eyes go wide. "Wait, what?"

We all laugh, and Addison winks at her. "Kidding. You've got a few more years."

"Can we take pictures?" Sara calls out, camera already in hand. "Everyone together!"

We gather on the church steps — me and Jasper and Lucas, Grace in the center in her white dress, Addison standing beside her. Sara directs us like a professional photographer, making us shift and smile and do it again with different groupings.

"Just Grace and Addison," she calls.

They stand together, Addison's hand on Grace's shoulder again. Grace beams at the camera. Addison looks down at her with such tenderness that I have to look away, blinking back fresh tears.

"Elena, you okay?" Sara asks quietly when she's done with pictures.

"Just emotional," I say. "She's growing up so fast."

"She is. But she's growing up good." Sara squeezes my arm. "You and Jasper are doing something right."

More families are spilling out of the church now, everyone taking pictures and congratulating their children. Grace is immediately swept up by her friends, a gaggle of seven-year-olds in white dresses comparing notes on how it felt to receive Communion for the first time.

I watch Addison watching Grace, and then I make my way over to her.

"Thank you," I say.

She looks surprised. "For what?"

"For being here. For being her sponsor. For showing up to all those classes and really engaging with it." I pause. "For loving her the way you do."

Addison's eyes get suspiciously shiny. "Elena, you don't have to thank me. Grace is... she's special. Being part of this was an honor."

"Still. Thank you." I pull her into a hug, and she hugs back, tight.

When we separate, Jasper has his phone out.

"Kendra's calling in," he says. "She wants to see Grace."

He sets up the FaceTime, and suddenly Kendra's face fills the screen — she's in what looks like a hotel room, her hair wet like she just showered, clearly exhausted but smiling.

"There's the First Communicant!" she says when Grace crowds into frame.

"Kendra! Did you see? I got to have the Eucharist!"

"I know! Mom sent me pictures. You look absolutely beautiful, Gracie."

"I wish you were here," Grace says, and I see something flicker across Kendra's face.

"Me too, kiddo. But I'm competing tomorrow, so I had to stay in Madison. You understand, right?"

"I guess." Grace brightens. "How are the Games? Are you winning?"

"I'm doing okay. Eleventh place right now."

"That's so good! Addie, did you hear? Kendra's in eleventh place!"

The camera pans awkwardly as Jasper tries to get Addison in frame. For a second, we all watch as Kendra and Addison look at each other through a screen, a thousand miles apart.

"Hey," Addison says.

"Hey," Kendra replies. "You look nice."

"Thanks. You look tired."

"I am tired. But in a good way." A pause. "Thanks for being Grace's sponsor. It means a lot."

"She made it easy."

Another pause, loaded with things neither of them is saying. Grace, oblivious, keeps chattering about the Mass and what they're doing later.

"I should go," Kendra says eventually. "Got to rest up for tomorrow. But Gracie? I'm so, so proud of you."

"Thanks, Kendra! Win a medal!"

"Working on it." Kendra's smile doesn't quite reach her eyes. "Love you guys."

We say goodbye, and Jasper ends the call. Grace immediately runs back to her friends, and I'm left thinking about the look on my daughter's face when she saw Addison and Kendra on screen together.

Something is shifting. I can feel it. Like tectonic plates moving slowly beneath the surface, changing the landscape in ways we won't understand until after it's already different.

---

The celebration at our house afterward is exactly what Grace wanted: cake, presents, and all her favorite people crammed into our living room.

Marcus and Jen brought their kids. Dev and his girlfriend brought flowers. Sara made a photo album of Grace's RCIA journey that has me crying again.

But the best moment comes later, after most people have left, after Lucas has escaped to his room and Grace is running on pure sugar and excitement.

She finds Addison on the back porch, sitting on the steps and looking at the garden.

"Addie?"

Addison turns, pats the step beside her. "Hey, you. Having a good day?"

"The best day." Grace settles in close, leaning against Addison's side. "Thank you for being my sponsor."

"Thank you for asking me to be your sponsor."

They sit in comfortable silence for a moment. Then Grace says, "Addie, do you think God is happy with me?"

Addison considers the question with the seriousness it deserves. "I think God is absolutely delighted with you, Grace Barrett."

"How do you know?"

"Because you ask good questions. Because you show up with your whole heart. Because you're kind and brave and you make the people around you better." Addison tucks a curl behind Grace's ear. "That's exactly the kind of person God loves."

"Are you the kind of person God loves?"

"I'm working on it," Addison says softly. "Some days I'm better at it than others. But I'm trying."

"That's what Father Michael says. That God loves us even when we're still trying."

"Father Michael is smart."

Grace leans heavier into Addison's side. "I'm glad you're my person, Addie."

And there it is — that phrase that's become Grace's highest honor. My person.

Addison's arm comes around Grace's shoulders. "I'm glad you're my person too, Gracie."

I watch them from the kitchen window, these two people who found each other in the middle of complicated family dynamics and chose to build something beautiful. Grace, with her enormous heart and endless questions. Addison, still figuring out faith and family, but showing up anyway.

Jasper appears beside me, following my gaze.

"They're good together," he says.

"They are."

"You think Kendra and Addison will ever figure their stuff out?"

I lean into his side. "I don't know. But I hope so. For both their sakes."

"Grace would like that," he observes.

"Grace isn't the only one."

We watch as Grace tugs Addison back inside, chattering about the cake and can they please watch Moana and also can Addison sleep over tonight please please please?

And Addison, who probably has a hundred other things she should be doing, looks down at Grace and says yes.

Because that's what you do for your people. You show up. You stay. You say yes even when it's not convenient.

Grace's First Communion is over, but the day isn't. There's still cake to eat and a movie to watch and a celebration to continue.

And watching my daughter — in her white dress, surrounded by people who love her, asking the big questions with her whole heart — I think about faith. About what it means to believe in something you can't fully explain. About showing up even when you're still figuring it out.

About receiving grace and giving it in equal measure.

Grace Barrett received her First Communion today. She took the body and blood of Christ with hands that still seem too small for something so sacred.

But maybe that's the point. Maybe we're all too small for the sacred things. Maybe faith is about showing up anyway, reaching out anyway, saying yes anyway.

Maybe it's about finding your people and holding on tight.

I blink back tears for the hundredth time today and go to cut more cake.

My daughter just had her First Communion, and the day is far from over.
