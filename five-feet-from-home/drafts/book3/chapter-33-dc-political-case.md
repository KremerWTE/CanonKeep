# Chapter 33: DC Political Case

**POV:** Addison
**Location:** Washington DC
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The senator's office smells like old carpet and filtered coffee, the kind of scent that clings to government buildings no matter how much money gets thrown at renovations.

Addison sits in a leather chair across from Senator Patricia Halvorsen, hands folded in her lap, expression neutral. Jasper's beside her, one leg crossed over the other, perfectly still. They've been here for forty minutes, listening to the senator's chief of staff walk them through the ethics investigation with the kind of careful language that means everything is worse than they're saying.

"So what we're looking at," the chief of staff says—his name is David, mid-forties, thinning hair, probably hasn't slept in three days—"is a complaint filed by the minority leader's office alleging that Senator Halvorsen accepted campaign contributions from a donor with financial interests in legislation she sponsored."

"Did she?" Addison asks.

Senator Halvorsen doesn't flinch. She's in her late fifties, sharp-eyed, with the kind of presence that fills a room. "The donor contributed to my campaign. That's public record. The legislation I sponsored benefits dozens of companies, including his. That's also public record. What isn't public record is any quid pro quo arrangement, because there wasn't one."

"But it looks bad," Jasper says quietly.

"It looks bad," the senator agrees.

Addison uncrosses her legs and leans forward slightly. "Walk me through the timeline. When did the donor first contribute? When was the legislation introduced? When did his company benefit?"

David pulls out a file and slides it across the table. Addison opens it, scanning the documents. Dates. Dollar amounts. Committee votes. Public statements.

She reads quickly, efficiently, the way Jasper taught her. Not just looking for problems—looking for the narrative. The story that makes sense. The story that can be sold.

"This donation," she says, tapping one line on the page. "It's from a PAC, not the individual."

"Correct," David says.

"And the PAC supports a dozen senators. Not just Senator Halvorsen."

"Correct."

"And the legislation was bipartisan. Co-sponsored by a Republican and introduced in a committee the senator doesn't chair."

"Also correct."

Addison closes the file and looks at Jasper. He's watching her, not the senator. There's something in his expression—pride, maybe. Or trust. She's not sure which.

"This isn't an ethics violation," Addison says, turning back to the senator. "This is opposition research dressed up as oversight. They're trying to muddy the water before your reelection campaign."

Senator Halvorsen nods slowly. "That's my read too."

"Then we don't play defense," Addison says. "We flip the narrative. You didn't take dirty money—you championed bipartisan legislation that benefits your constituents. The donation was legal, transparent, and one of many. If they want to call that corruption, we make them explain why every other senator who took money from the same PAC isn't under investigation."

David looks at Jasper. "She always this direct?"

Jasper smiles faintly. "She's gotten worse."

Addison ignores him. "We'll need a media strategy. Senator, you'll do a press conference. Not defensive—proactive. You frame this as what it is: a political hit job. Meanwhile, we'll compile a list of every senator who's taken money from that PAC and voted on similar legislation. We bury them in hypocrisy."

The senator leans back in her chair, studying Addison with something that looks like approval. "I like her."

"Everyone does," Jasper says dryly.

---

They leave the Capitol building an hour later, the late afternoon sun cutting through the October cold. Addison pulls her coat tighter as they walk toward the car Jasper hired for the week—a black sedan with a driver who doesn't ask questions.

"You handled that well," Jasper says as they slide into the backseat.

"You didn't say much," Addison counters.

"I didn't need to. You had it."

She glances at him. He's looking out the window, jaw set, the same expression he gets when he's thinking three moves ahead. They've been doing this for two years now—her stepping into the lead on certain cases, him stepping back. It's a rhythm they've built slowly, carefully, and it works.

"We're going to need to pull voting records," she says, shifting into logistics mode. "Every senator who's taken money from that PAC. And we need a timeline of when the minority leader's office filed the complaint versus when Halvorsen announced her reelection bid."

"Already on it," Jasper says. He pulls out his phone and types a quick message. "Harper's running the data now. We'll have a full report by tonight."

Addison nods. "Good. I'll draft the press statement. We hit them before they have time to shape the story."

Jasper pockets his phone and looks at her. "You're getting scary good at this."

"I learned from the best."

"Flattery doesn't suit you."

"It's not flattery if it's true."

He laughs—a quiet sound, barely there, but real. It's one of the things she's learned about Jasper over the years: he doesn't waste energy on performative emotion. When he laughs, he means it.

The car pulls up to their hotel, a boutique place in Georgetown with understated elegance and staff who know how to be invisible. Addison's stayed here before—twice, both times with Jasper, both times for cases that required discretion.

They check in separately, out of habit more than necessity, and meet thirty minutes later in the hotel bar.

---

The bar is quiet at six on a Thursday evening. Low lighting, leather booths, the kind of place where lobbyists have off-the-record conversations and staffers decompress after brutal committee hearings. Addison orders a gin and tonic. Jasper orders bourbon, neat.

They sit in a corner booth, files spread across the table between them, laptops open.

"Harper sent the voting records," Jasper says, turning his screen toward her. "Twelve senators took money from the same PAC in the last cycle. Seven of them voted on similar legislation. Two of them are on the ethics committee."

Addison scans the list, her mind already building the argument. "We go after the committee members first. Frame it as a conflict of interest. If they're investigating Halvorsen for something they've done themselves, the whole thing falls apart."

"Agreed." Jasper takes a sip of his bourbon. "You should write the press statement tonight. I'll review it in the morning before we meet with her communications director."

"You trust me to write it alone?"

He looks at her, expression serious. "You've been writing them alone for six months, Addison. I just review them because you ask me to."

She blinks. He's right, of course. The last four statements she drafted, Jasper barely touched. A word here, a comma there. Mostly he just sent them back with a single word: *Good.*

"I'm ready to run these alone," Jasper says quietly. "You know that, right?"

Addison sets down her drink. "What?"

"These cases. The political ones. The media strategy, the narrative control. You're better at it than I am now."

"That's not true."

"It is." He leans back, one arm draped over the edge of the booth. "You read people faster than I do. You see the angles I miss. You're younger, sharper, and you don't carry the same baggage I do."

She shakes her head. "Jasper—"

"I'm not saying this to flatter you," he interrupts. "I'm saying it because it's true. You could handle Halvorsen's case solo. You could handle most of the DC work solo."

Addison stares at him, caught off guard. She's spent two years chasing his approval, trying to prove she's ready for this. And now that he's offering it, she doesn't know what to say.

"I don't want to run them solo," she says finally.

Jasper tilts his head. "Why not?"

"Because I like having you there."

The words hang between them, heavier than she intended. It's not about competence or confidence. It's about partnership. About the rhythm they've built. About knowing that when things go sideways—and they always do—she won't be alone.

Jasper's expression softens. "I'm not going anywhere, Addie."

"I know."

"I'm just saying you're ready. When you want to take the lead, you can. No questions asked."

She nods slowly, absorbing it. "Okay."

"Okay?"

"Yeah." She picks up her drink again, takes a slow sip. "But not yet."

Jasper smiles. "Fair enough."

---

They work through dinner—room service, eaten at the table in the bar while they build the press strategy. Addison drafts the statement, sharpening the language until it's clean and cutting. Jasper reviews the voting records, flagging the senators whose hypocrisy is most glaring.

By nine, they have a plan.

By ten, they have a full dossier.

"We'll meet with her comms director at eight," Jasper says, closing his laptop. "Press conference at noon. That gives the story time to break before the evening news cycle."

"And if the minority leader's office tries to preempt us?"

"They won't. They think we're scrambling. They think Halvorsen's going to play defense."

Addison smiles. "They're going to be disappointed."

"They usually are."

She finishes her drink and leans back, feeling the exhaustion settle into her shoulders. It's been a long day—flight at six this morning, meetings all afternoon, strategy session all evening. But it's the good kind of tired. The kind that comes from doing work that matters.

"You ever miss it?" she asks.

"Miss what?"

"The field work. The crisis management. The two AM calls and the last-minute flights."

Jasper considers this, swirling the last of his bourbon. "Sometimes. But not the way I used to."

"What changed?"

"I did." He sets down his glass. "I used to think the crisis was the point. The adrenaline, the pressure, the impossible problem that only I could solve. But that's not the point. The point is what happens after the crisis. The people who are still standing. The relationships that survive."

Addison watches him, quiet. She knows what he's talking about. Elena. Grace. The family he almost lost because he couldn't see past the five feet in front of him.

"You're getting philosophical in your old age," she says lightly.

"I'm getting tired in my old age."

"You're not old."

"I'm older than you."

"Everyone's older than me."

He laughs. "Fair."

They sit in silence for a moment, the low hum of the bar around them. It's comfortable, this quiet. The kind of silence that only happens between people who've worked together long enough to stop performing.

"I'm glad you're here," Addison says.

Jasper looks at her, surprised. "Yeah?"

"Yeah. I like working with you. I like this." She gestures vaguely at the table, the files, the empty glasses. "The partnership. The rhythm. I don't want to do it alone."

"You won't have to," Jasper says. "Not unless you want to."

"Good."

He stands, picking up his laptop and files. "Get some sleep. Tomorrow's going to be a long day."

"I know."

"And Addie?"

She looks up.

"You did good today."

She smiles. "Thanks, Jasper."

---

Later, alone in her hotel room, Addison pulls out her phone and scrolls through her messages. There's one from Hawk, sent an hour ago.

**Hawk:** How's DC?

**Addison:** Productive. Senator's case is solid. We'll flip the narrative tomorrow.

**Hawk:** Of course you will.

She smiles at the screen.

**Addison:** Jasper said I'm ready to run these cases alone.

**Hawk:** You've been ready.

**Addison:** I told him I'm not ready yet.

There's a pause. Then:

**Hawk:** Why not?

**Addison:** Because I like having him there. The partnership works.

**Hawk:** That's fair. You don't have to do everything alone, you know.

**Addison:** I'm learning that.

**Hawk:** Good. Get some sleep. I'll see you when you're back.

**Addison:** Miss you.

**Hawk:** Miss you too, War Goddess.

She sets the phone down, feeling something warm settle in her chest. She's spent so much of her life trying to prove she doesn't need anyone. But the truth is, she does. She needs Jasper's steady presence. She needs Hawk's quiet support. She needs the people who see her—really see her—and stay anyway.

She's not ready to run these cases alone.

And maybe that's okay.

**End of Chapter 33**
