# Chapter 7: The EOHSJ Dinner

**POV:** Addison
**Location:** NYC
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The invitation arrives during lunch.

Addison's sitting in the hotel restaurant, picking at a Cobb salad and reviewing witness statements, when her phone buzzes. It's an email from Elena, forwarded from someone named Lady Margaret Ashford.

The subject line reads: *Dinner Invitation – Sovereign Order of Saint John*

Addison frowns and opens it.

> *Dear Ms. Price,*
>
> *On the recommendation of Mrs. Elena Barrett, I would be honored to host you for dinner this evening at the Cathedral Rectory of Saint Patrick's. This is an informal gathering of members and friends of the Sovereign Order of Saint John—an opportunity to meet others engaged in charitable and humanitarian work.*
>
> *Cocktails at 7:00 PM. Dinner at 8:00 PM. Business attire.*
>
> *I do hope you can join us.*
>
> *Warm regards,*
> *Lady Margaret Ashford, DCHS*

Addison reads it twice, then checks the time. It's 1:30 PM. The dinner is in five and a half hours.

She calls Elena.

Elena picks up on the second ring. "Did you get the invitation?"

"I did," Addison says. "Want to tell me what this is about?"

Elena's voice is warm, a little amused. "It's exactly what it sounds like. Lady Margaret is a Dame of the Sovereign Order of Saint John. She's involved in Catholic charities, international relief work, that sort of thing. I met her at a Vatican fundraiser last year, and when I mentioned you'd be in New York, she insisted on introducing you to the network."

"Why?"

"Because you're brilliant," Elena says simply. "And because the work we're doing—the Phoenix Foundation, the expansion plans—aligns with what they do. These are people who move resources, Addie. Serious resources. Margaret thought you should meet them."

Addison sets down her fork. "Elena, I'm not Catholic. I'm barely anything."

"Neither was half the room at the last dinner I went to," Elena says. "It's not about religion, Addie. It's about the work. And it's about relationships. Trust me."

Addison's silent for a moment. She doesn't love the idea of walking into a room full of titled strangers and pretending to belong. But Elena wouldn't push this unless she thought it mattered.

"Fine," Addison says. "I'll go."

"Good." Elena sounds pleased. "And Addie? Just be yourself. You don't have to perform for these people. They'll see your value."

"Right," Addison says. "Easy."

Elena laughs. "You'll be fine. Call me after."

The line goes dead.

Addison stares at her phone for a long moment, then texts Jasper.

**Addison:** Elena set me up with a dinner tonight. Sovereign Order of Saint John. Catholic charity network. You know anything about this?

His response comes thirty seconds later.

**Jasper:** I know Margaret Ashford. Smart woman. Good connections. Go. Listen. Don't commit to anything without running it by me first.

**Addison:** Wasn't planning to.

**Jasper:** I know. That's why I'm telling you to go.

Addison smiles despite herself and sets the phone down.

Looks like she's going to dinner.

---

Saint Patrick's Cathedral is stunning, even from the outside.

Addison arrives just before seven, stepping out of a cab onto Fifth Avenue. The cathedral looms above her, all Gothic spires and limestone, lit up against the evening sky. The rectory is adjacent—a stately building with tall windows and a brass plaque beside the door that reads *Private*.

She's wearing a tailored black dress, simple and professional, with her good heels and a wool coat. Her hair is pulled back in a sleek bun. She looks composed. Polished. The kind of woman who belongs in rooms like this.

Even if she doesn't feel like it.

She presses the buzzer, and a voice answers almost immediately. "Ms. Price?"

"Yes."

"Come in. Second floor."

The door clicks open.

Inside, the rectory is warm and quietly elegant. Dark wood paneling, soft lighting, a staircase that curves upward. Addison climbs to the second floor and follows the sound of voices to a large sitting room with high ceilings and a fireplace.

There are about twenty people scattered throughout the room, holding cocktails and speaking in low, cultured tones. The men are in suits. The women are in understated dresses, pearls, tailored blazers. No one looks younger than forty.

Addison immediately feels like an imposter.

A woman near the door notices her and smiles. She's in her mid-sixties, silver-haired, wearing a cream blouse and a string of pearls. "You must be Addison. I'm Margaret Ashford."

Addison shakes her hand. "Lady Ashford. Thank you for the invitation."

"Please, call me Margaret." Her accent is British, refined but not cold. "Elena speaks very highly of you. I'm delighted you could join us."

"I appreciate the opportunity," Addison says, because it feels like the right thing to say.

Margaret gestures to the room. "Come. Let me introduce you to a few people."

The next thirty minutes are a blur of handshakes and names. Sir Anthony Something-or-Other, a retired ambassador. Dr. Isabella Moreno, a physician who runs a mobile clinic network in Central America. Father Kieran, a soft-spoken Jesuit who oversees disaster relief in East Africa. Dame Claire, a woman in her fifties who works in microfinance.

They're all polite. They all ask thoughtful questions. And they all seem to assume Addison belongs here, which is the most disorienting part.

"Elena mentioned you're with Barrett Security Solutions," Dame Claire says. She has a crisp South African accent and sharp eyes. "Crisis management, yes?"

"That's right," Addison says. "We handle corporate crises, litigation support, reputation management."

"And the Phoenix Foundation?" Claire asks. "Elena mentioned you're involved with that as well."

Addison hesitates, then nods. "I serve on the board. It's a nonprofit focused on veteran reintegration and at-risk youth programs."

"That's excellent work," Claire says. "We're always looking for partners in that space. The Order funds several youth programs in conflict zones. Perhaps we should speak more after dinner."

"I'd like that," Addison says, and she means it.

The conversation moves on, but Addison's mind is racing. These aren't just wealthy donors. They're operators. They're people who understand logistics, risk, execution. People who move millions of dollars into fragile regions and make things happen.

People who could be useful.

At eight o'clock, they're called into the dining room.

The table is long and formal, set with white linens and crystal glasses. Addison's seated between Father Kieran and a man named Jonathan Hart, who introduces himself as a policy advisor for an international relief organization.

The food is simple but excellent—roasted chicken, spring vegetables, crusty bread. The wine is poured generously. The conversation flows easily, shifting from politics to economics to theology without ever feeling heavy.

Addison mostly listens. She's good at that—reading the room, absorbing the dynamics. But halfway through the meal, Margaret Ashford addresses her directly from the head of the table.

"Addison, Elena tells me you're not Catholic. I hope this evening hasn't been too alienating."

Addison blinks, caught off guard by the directness. "Not at all," she says carefully. "I'm… curious, I suppose. This isn't a world I'm familiar with."

"That's honest," Margaret says with a smile. "I appreciate honesty. Tell me—what drew you to the Phoenix Foundation?"

It's a simple question, but the whole table has gone quiet. Addison can feel them waiting.

She sets down her fork. "The work," she says. "We're trying to build something sustainable. Not just charity, but real infrastructure. Programs that give people tools, not handouts. Skills. Stability. A way forward."

"That's ambitious," Jonathan Hart says.

"It has to be," Addison replies. "Otherwise it's just feel-good theatre."

Margaret's smile widens. "I like that. Too many nonprofits are feel-good theatre. All mission statements and no results."

"Exactly," Addison says. "We're trying to build something that lasts."

Father Kieran leans forward slightly. "And do you see faith as part of that work?"

Addison hesitates. It's the question she's been dreading. "I think faith matters to the people we serve," she says slowly. "So yes, in that sense, it's part of the work. But I don't think you need to share their faith to help them. You just need to respect it."

There's a pause. Then Father Kieran nods. "That's a fair answer."

Margaret raises her glass. "To fair answers. And to honest work."

The table echoes the toast, and the conversation shifts. But Addison can feel something has shifted, too. She's passed some kind of test.

---

After dinner, the group moves back to the sitting room for coffee and cognac. Addison's talking with Dame Claire about microfinance models when Margaret appears at her elbow.

"Walk with me for a moment?" Margaret says.

Addison excuses herself and follows Margaret to a quieter corner of the room, near the windows overlooking Fifth Avenue.

Margaret's expression is kind, but serious. "You handled yourself well tonight, Addison. That's not easy in a room like this."

"Thank you," Addison says. "Though I'm not sure I understood half of what I was doing."

Margaret laughs softly. "That's the point. You weren't performing. You were listening. You were honest. That's what matters."

Addison doesn't know what to say to that, so she waits.

Margaret studies her for a moment, then says, "Elena thinks very highly of you. She believes you have the capacity to do extraordinary things."

"Elena's generous," Addison says.

"Elena's perceptive," Margaret corrects. "And she's rarely wrong about people." She pauses, then adds, "The Order is always looking for people of character. People who understand that real change requires more than good intentions. It requires discipline. Strategy. The willingness to do hard things."

"That sounds like something I understand," Addison says.

"I thought it might." Margaret's gaze is steady. "Faith can grow, Addison. Character cannot be taught. You have character. The rest, if you're interested, can come later."

Addison feels something tighten in her chest. It's not pressure—it's recognition. The sense that Margaret sees her. Not the armor, not the performance. The actual person underneath.

"I appreciate that," Addison says quietly.

Margaret smiles. "I'm not recruiting you, my dear. I'm simply planting a seed. If it takes root, wonderful. If not, you'll still have friends in this network. Either way, you're welcome here."

She squeezes Addison's arm gently, then drifts back toward the other guests.

Addison stands by the window for a moment longer, looking out at the city lights, her reflection faint in the glass.

She doesn't know what just happened. But she knows it mattered.

---

She leaves the rectory just after ten, stepping back out into the cold New York night. The cathedral is still lit, glowing against the darkness. She pulls out her phone and calls Elena.

Elena answers on the first ring. "How was it?"

"Strange," Addison says. "Good, but strange."

"Did Margaret talk to you?"

"Yeah. She said something about character and faith and planting seeds. I'm still processing it."

Elena's quiet for a moment, then she says, "She sees something in you, Addie. Something I've seen for a long time."

"What's that?"

"That you're capable of more than you think. And that you don't have to carry it all alone."

Addison's throat tightens. She doesn't trust herself to speak.

"Get some sleep," Elena says gently. "We'll talk more when you're home."

"Okay," Addison manages.

"And Addie?"

"Yeah?"

"I'm proud of you."

The line goes quiet. Addison stands on the sidewalk, the city moving around her, and lets the words settle.

She doesn't know what comes next. But for the first time in a long time, she's not afraid of it.

---

**End of Chapter 7**
