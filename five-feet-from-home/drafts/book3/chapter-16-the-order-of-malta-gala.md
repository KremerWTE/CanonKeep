# Chapter 16: The Order of Malta Gala

**POV: Addison Clarke**

The Plaza Hotel ballroom looked like something out of a Gilded Age fantasy. Crystal chandeliers the size of small cars. Gold leaf on every available surface. A string quartet playing Vivaldi in the corner while six hundred people in black tie sipped champagne and discussed things like papal audiences and endowments to Vatican museums.

Addison stood at Elena Barrett's elbow, wearing a navy silk gown that had cost more than her first car, and tried not to feel like she'd accidentally wandered onto the set of a period drama.

"Relax," Elena murmured. "You look like you belong here."

"I feel like I'm about to be exposed as a fraud."

"Everyone here feels that way. They've just had more practice hiding it." Elena accepted a champagne flute from a passing waiter, handed it to Addison. "The woman at three o'clock in the emerald gown — that's Lady Margaret Ashford. British. Married to a hedge fund manager. She's on the board of the Order. I want you to meet her."

"What do I say?"

"Let her do most of the talking. She'll ask about your background. Be honest but strategic. Rhodes Scholar is good. Defense contractor work is good. Leave out the part where you started as an executive assistant."

"Why?"

"Because they'll use it to dismiss you. Better to lead with credentials." Elena's smile was sharp. "Come on."

They crossed the ballroom, weaving through clusters of people who all seemed to know each other. Old money recognizing old money. Addison was acutely aware that she was neither old nor money — just a girl from Richmond who'd gotten very good at performing competence.

"Margaret," Elena said warmly. "You look stunning."

Lady Margaret Ashford turned, and Addison was struck by how ordinary she looked. Mid-fifties, graying hair, a pleasant face that wouldn't stand out in a crowd. Nothing about her screamed wealth or power.

But the way people moved around her — deferential, aware — told a different story.

"Elena. I was hoping to see you tonight." Lady Margaret's accent was crisp, perfectly BBC. "And you've brought a guest."

"This is Addison Clarke. My son Jasper's partner. Addison, Lady Margaret Ashford."

Partner. Not girlfriend. Not significant other. Partner. Addison filed that away.

"Lovely to meet you," Addison said, offering her hand.

Lady Margaret's handshake was firm. Assessing. "Elena has mentioned you. Rhodes Scholar, correct?"

"Yes ma'am. I studied international relations at Oxford."

"And now you're with Whitmore Industries?"

"I work on strategic communications and crisis management. Most recently, we handled a congressional inquiry into one of our defense contracts."

"The Meridian contract." Lady Margaret's eyes sharpened. "I read about that. Congressman Barfield tried to make it a scandal. You managed to flip the narrative entirely."

Addison blinked. "You follow defense contracting?"

"I follow power dynamics. And watching a thirty-year-old woman outmaneuver a sitting congressman is always interesting." She sipped her champagne. "Elena, you've found yourself quite the protégée."

"I've found myself a friend," Elena corrected gently. "Who happens to be exceptionally talented."

"Mm." Lady Margaret's attention was fully on Addison now. "Tell me, my dear. What brings you to the Order of Malta Gala? Are you Catholic?"

"I was raised Presbyterian. But I'm interested in the Order's work." Addison had done her homework on the flight to New York. The Order of Malta was a Catholic lay religious order focused on humanitarian work. Ancient lineage. Papal recognition. Lots of very wealthy people using it as a vehicle for both charity and networking.

"Interested in the work, or interested in the network?" Lady Margaret's tone was amused, not accusatory.

Addison decided to be honest. "Both. I think they're inseparable."

"Smart girl." Lady Margaret glanced at Elena. "You're grooming her for something."

"I'm introducing her to people who might appreciate her talents."

"Which is the same thing." Lady Margaret turned back to Addison. "Do you know what the Order actually does? Beyond the galas and the papal audiences?"

"Humanitarian relief. Medical care in conflict zones. Support for refugees."

"Correct. But also diplomacy. We have observer status at the United Nations. We maintain relationships with governments that won't talk to each other officially. We move money and resources in ways that traditional NGOs can't." She paused. "We're useful. To the Church. To various governments. To people who need things done quietly."

Addison felt the conversation shifting. This wasn't small talk anymore. This was a job interview.

"That sounds like important work," she said carefully.

"It is. And we're always looking for young people who understand how power actually works. People who can navigate complex political situations. People who can be discreet." Lady Margaret's gaze was steady. "People like you."

"I'm not sure I'm qualified for—"

"You convinced a congressional committee to drop an inquiry through strategic media manipulation and political pressure. You're overqualified." Lady Margaret smiled. "We should talk about your future. The Order needs women like you."

Elena's hand found Addison's elbow. A gentle warning. Don't commit to anything tonight.

"I'm honored," Addison said. "I'd love to learn more about the Order's work."

"Good. I'll have my assistant reach out to schedule a proper conversation." Lady Margaret glanced past them. "If you'll excuse me, I see Cardinal Burke. He's been avoiding me for twenty minutes."

She swept away, leaving Addison slightly dizzy.

"What just happened?" Addison asked.

"You were recruited," Elena said simply. "Lady Margaret doesn't make casual offers. If she wants to talk to you about the Order, she's thinking long-term. Board positions. Leadership roles. Access to networks that go well beyond Charlotte society."

"I don't even know if I want that."

"You don't have to decide tonight. But you should take the meeting." Elena steered them toward a less crowded corner of the ballroom. "This is how it works, Addison. You meet the right people. You demonstrate competence. Doors open. And then you decide which doors you want to walk through."

Addison took a long sip of champagne. Six months ago, she'd been organizing Jasper's calendar and booking conference rooms. Now she was being recruited by the Order of Malta at a gala in New York City.

The trajectory was dizzying.

"I don't know if I'm ready for this," she said quietly.

"No one's ever ready. You just learn to fake it convincingly." Elena's smile was knowing. "Come on. There are more people I want you to meet."

---

The next two hours were a blur of introductions. A software CEO who'd just endowed a hospital in Haiti. A former ambassador to the Holy See. A philanthropist who ran a foundation focused on education in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Each conversation followed the same pattern. Elena would introduce Addison. They'd ask about her background. She'd mention Oxford, the Rhodes, the defense contracting work. They'd look impressed. Someone would say, "We should stay in touch."

By 10 PM, Addison had collected seventeen business cards and fielded six invitations to lunch, coffee, or "a proper chat about opportunities."

"You're exhausted," Elena observed as they waited for the coat check. "I can see it in your eyes."

"I'm not used to performing for this long."

"It gets easier. Eventually, it stops feeling like performing. It becomes your actual personality." Elena's tone was wry. "Whether that's good or bad is up for debate."

Outside, the September air was crisp. New York in early fall, before the weather turned properly cold. Elena's driver was waiting at the curb.

"Come on. I'm taking you for a proper drink."

"Elena, it's 10 PM. I'm dead on my feet."

"One drink. I promise. There's a place I want to show you."

They ended up at a speakeasy in the West Village, the kind of place with no sign and a bouncer who clearly recognized Elena. Inside, it was all dark wood and low lighting, jazz playing softly in the background.

Elena ordered two Old Fashioneds and led them to a corner booth.

"This is where I used to come," she said, settling into the leather seat. "When I was your age. Before I married James. Before I became Mrs. Barrett."

"You lived in New York?"

"For three years. Worked at Morgan Stanley. Made obscene amounts of money. Had a studio apartment in Chelsea that cost more than most people's mortgage." Elena's smile was nostalgic. "I thought I was going to conquer the world."

"What happened?"

"I met James at a charity gala. He was charming. Ambitious. Building his father's company into something bigger. He made me feel like I could be part of something important." She sipped her drink. "And I fell in love with that feeling more than I fell in love with him."

Addison didn't know what to say. Elena never talked about her marriage like this. Never admitted cracks in the foundation.

"I'm not saying I regret it," Elena continued. "I built a good life. Raised two sons. Created networks that have moved millions of dollars into productive causes. But sometimes I wonder what would have happened if I'd stayed in New York. Kept climbing at Morgan. Gone for the managing director position."

"You would have been brilliant at it."

"Maybe. Or maybe I would have hit the same ceiling every other woman in finance hits. The one where you're good enough to manage other people's money but not quite trusted to make the really big decisions." Elena met her eyes. "The question is: what ceiling are you willing to accept?"

"I don't know what you mean."

"Yes, you do." Elena's voice was gentle but firm. "You're brilliant, Addison. Genuinely brilliant. You could go back to policy work. Join a think tank. Get a PhD and become an academic. Work your way up through traditional channels and maybe, if you're lucky and very patient, have real influence by the time you're fifty."

"Or?"

"Or you could build influence now. Through networks. Through relationships. Through understanding how power actually moves in rooms like the one we just left." Elena leaned forward. "Lady Margaret wasn't exaggerating. The Order of Malta has access that most governments don't. Observer status at the UN means they attend sessions, participate in discussions. They can't vote, but they can lobby. They can broker deals. They can move resources."

"And you think I should be part of that."

"I think you should consider it. Along with the other opportunities that are going to come your way." Elena paused. "You're at an inflection point. You can keep working for Whitmore Industries, supporting Jasper's career, being the smart woman behind a successful man. And there's no shame in that. I did it for twenty-five years."

"But?"

"But you could also build your own platform. Your own network. Your own influence that doesn't depend on who you're dating or married to." Elena's eyes were intense now. "I'm giving you the roadmap I wish someone had given me. But you have to decide if you want to follow it."

The jazz band transitioned into something slower, melancholic. Around them, other patrons talked in low voices, conducting their own negotiations, their own power plays.

"What does Jasper think about all this?" Addison asked.

"Jasper loves you. And he's secure enough to want you to succeed on your own terms."

"That's not an answer."

Elena smiled. "It's the answer I have. You should ask him directly what he thinks. But I suspect he already knows you're going to outgrow the role of supportive partner. The question is whether you outgrow it together or separately."

The words hit harder than Elena probably intended. Because Addison had been feeling it too. The sense that she and Jasper were on diverging paths. He was being groomed to run a division of Whitmore Industries, maybe take over strategic development. A good career. An important role.

But Addison was being positioned for something different. Something that didn't have a clear job description or reporting structure. Something that looked like influence without official authority.

"I don't want to lose him," Addison said quietly.

"Then don't. But also don't shrink yourself to keep him comfortable. If he loves you — really loves you — he'll grow to accommodate who you're becoming."

"And if he can't?"

"Then you'll have a decision to make." Elena finished her drink. "But that's a problem for future Addison. Tonight, I just want you to understand what you're being offered. Real power. Real access. The ability to make things happen with a phone call."

"In exchange for what?"

"Commitment. Discretion. Patience. You'll be building something that takes years to fully mature. Networks aren't instant. But once they're built, they're extraordinarily valuable."

Addison thought about Lady Margaret. About the other people she'd met tonight. About the business cards in her purse that represented doorways to worlds she'd only read about.

Six months ago, she'd been happy organizing Jasper's calendar.

Now she was being recruited by the Order of Malta.

The velocity was terrifying.

"What if I'm not good enough?" she asked. "What if I take these meetings and they realize I'm just faking it?"

"Everyone's faking it. The difference is how convincingly you perform competence." Elena signaled for the check. "You've already proven you can perform at the highest levels. The DC crisis. The Wives' Circle Gala. Tonight. You walked into a room full of people who've been operating in those circles for decades, and you held your own."

"Because you prepped me."

"I gave you context. You did the execution." The check arrived. Elena didn't even look at it, just placed her card on the tray. "Stop doubting yourself. Or at least, stop letting the doubt be visible. Confidence is a performance, Addison. And you're very good at performing."

They left the speakeasy and walked back toward the hotel. The city was still alive even past eleven — taxis honking, people streaming out of theaters, the energy that made New York feel like the center of the universe.

"Can I ask you something?" Addison said.

"Of course."

"Why me? Why are you investing so much time in mentoring me specifically?"

Elena was quiet for a block. They passed a street vendor selling hot dogs, a group of NYU students arguing about philosophy, a couple kissing under a streetlight.

"Because you remind me of who I was," Elena said finally. "Before I learned to hide it. Before I accepted that my role was to support rather than lead. You still have that hunger. That ambition. And I don't want to see it dampened the way mine was."

"That's not the whole reason."

Elena smiled. "No. It's not." She stopped walking, turned to face Addison fully. "The whole reason is that Whitmore Industries needs someone like you. Not just competent. Brilliant. Someone who understands that the real power doesn't happen in boardrooms. It happens in ballrooms. In quiet conversations over expensive whiskey. In relationships that take years to build but can move millions with a single phone call."

"And you think I can be that person."

"I think you already are that person. You just haven't fully claimed it yet." Elena started walking again. "My eldest son is being groomed to run the company. He'll be good at it. Brilliant, even. But he doesn't have the social intelligence. The political instincts. The ability to read a room and know exactly who to talk to and what to say."

"And Jasper?"

"Jasper has some of that. But he's also comfortable in his brother's shadow in a way that limits his ceiling." Elena's voice was matter-of-fact, clinical. "He'll run strategic development. Handle the complex accounts. Be the diplomatic face of the company. It's an important role."

"But not the most important role."

"No. The most important role — the one that actually sustains the ecosystem that makes everything else possible — is Partner of H. The person who manages the relationships, brokers the introductions, maintains the network. Currently, that's me. Eventually, it needs to be someone else."

There it was. The explicit statement of what Addison had been suspecting for weeks.

"You're grooming me to be your successor."

"I'm grooming you to be your own version of what I built. Whether that's as Partner of H, or in some other configuration, is up to you." Elena stopped at the hotel entrance. "But I need you to understand what I'm offering. And what it will cost."

"What will it cost?"

"Your anonymity. Your privacy. The luxury of being underestimated." Elena's smile was sharp. "Once you step into this role — fully, publicly — you'll be scrutinized. Criticized. Held to impossible standards. People will question your credentials, your motivations, your relationship with my son. They'll say you're using him. That you're ambitious in the pejorative sense. That you don't belong."

"That sounds terrible."

"It is terrible. It's also the price of power." Elena touched her arm gently. "But if you can endure it — if you can build the network and maintain the relationships and play the long game — you'll have influence that most people can only dream about. Real influence. The kind that changes outcomes."

Addison thought about Lady Margaret. About the Order of Malta, with its UN observer status and its Vatican connections and its ability to move resources in conflict zones. About the Wives' Circle and its carefully cultivated network of political spouses and nonprofit leaders. About the defense contractor relationships and the congressional connections and the endless web of people who owed favors or needed favors or could provide access.

All of it orbiting around women like Elena. Women who'd built power in the spaces between official structures.

Women who'd learned to be indispensable.

"I need to think about this," Addison said.

"Good. You should think about it. Talk to Jasper. Talk to people you trust." Elena headed toward the elevator. "But also know that the opportunity won't wait forever. These networks are built on relationships. And relationships require cultivation. If you're going to do this, you need to commit."

They rode the elevator in silence. Elena got off at the fourteenth floor, the executive suite she'd booked for the night. Addison continued to the seventeenth, to the room Jasper had booked for them before she'd even agreed to come to New York.

He was there when she walked in, sitting on the edge of the bed with his laptop, clearly working on something even at 11:30 PM.

"How was it?" he asked, looking up.

"Overwhelming." Addison kicked off her heels, collapsed onto the bed beside him. "Your mother introduced me to approximately six hundred people, I got recruited by the Order of Malta, and then she took me to a speakeasy and told me I'm being groomed to be the next Partner of H."

Jasper was very still. "She said that explicitly?"

"More or less."

"And how do you feel about it?"

Addison stared at the ceiling. "Terrified. Excited. Confused. All of the above." She turned her head to look at him. "Did you know? What she was doing?"

"I suspected. Mom doesn't do anything without a long-term strategy." He closed his laptop, set it aside. "The question is: do you want it?"

"I don't know. Maybe? Yes?" She sat up. "Is that okay? That I want it?"

"Why wouldn't it be okay?"

"Because it means I want something that isn't about supporting your career. Something that's mine. That might actually compete with your priorities sometimes."

Jasper was quiet for a moment. "I had coffee with Christopher last week. We were talking about the succession plan, about what happens when Dad retires. And Christopher said something that stuck with me."

"What?"

"He said: 'The business is the business. But the ecosystem around the business — that's what Mom built. And whoever ends up running the company needs someone maintaining that ecosystem. If you're smart, you'll make sure Addison is that person.'"

Addison blinked. "Christopher said that?"

"Christopher's not stupid. He knows his limitations. He's brilliant with operations and finance. But he's not a people person. He doesn't want to spend his evenings at charity galas and political fundraisers. He wants someone else handling that."

"And he thinks I should be that person."

"He thinks you could be that person. If you want to be." Jasper took her hand. "Look, I'm not going to lie and say this doesn't complicate things. If you become Partner of H — if you build your own network and power base — it changes the dynamics between us. You won't need me the same way."

"I'll always need you."

"Want me, maybe. But need? I'm not sure." His smile was sad. "And that's not a bad thing, Addison. That's growth. I just want to make sure we're building something together, not parallel tracks that eventually diverge."

"I want to build something together."

"Then we figure out how to do that. How to support each other's ambitions. How to create a partnership where we're both growing."

Addison leaned against him, felt the solid warmth of his presence. Jasper was good. Genuinely good. He wanted her to succeed even if it meant redefining their relationship.

But she also heard the uncertainty underneath. The question of whether they could sustain this. Whether wanting different things meant wanting different futures.

"I'm going to take the meeting with Lady Margaret," she said. "Learn more about the Order. See if it's actually something I want to pursue."

"Good."

"And I'm going to have more conversations with your mother. Figure out what Partner of H actually looks like in practice."

"Also good."

"But I'm not deciding anything tonight. Or this month. I'm just... exploring."

"Exploring is good." Jasper kissed the top of her head. "Just promise me one thing."

"What?"

"Don't lose yourself in the exploration. Don't become so focused on building the network that you forget why you wanted to build it in the first place."

"Why did I want to build it?"

"Because you care about people. Because you want to make things better. Because you see problems and you want to solve them." He pulled back to look at her. "My mother builds networks because she loves power. There's nothing wrong with that. But you're different. You build networks because you love impact. Don't let the power corrupt the impact."

Addison felt tears prick her eyes. This was why she loved him. This understanding. This willingness to see her clearly and call her to her best self.

"I promise," she whispered.

They got ready for bed in comfortable silence. Addison washed off her makeup, changed into pajamas, climbed under the covers. Jasper set an alarm for seven AM — they had a flight back to Charlotte at noon.

But as she lay in the dark, listening to Jasper's breathing even out into sleep, Addison couldn't stop thinking about the ballroom. About Lady Margaret's assessing gaze. About Elena's quiet intensity.

About the future that was being offered to her, glittering and dangerous and utterly seductive.

She could have power. Real power. The kind that moved mountains and brokered peace and changed outcomes.

All she had to do was claim it.

And accept the cost.

Addison closed her eyes, tried to sleep. But her mind was already racing ahead. The Order of Malta meeting. The next Wives' Circle event. The relationships to cultivate, the connections to maintain, the endless performance of competent ambition.

She was terrified.

She was ready.

And somewhere deep inside, beneath the fear and the excitement and the uncertainty, Addison felt a truth she'd been avoiding.

She wanted this. All of it.

The power. The influence. The ability to make things happen with a phone call.

She wanted it more than she'd ever wanted anything.

Even if it meant becoming someone she didn't fully recognize.

Even if it meant outgrowing the life she'd carefully built.

Even if it meant risking everything.

She wanted it.

And that wanting — fierce and unapologetic and undeniable — was the most honest thing she'd felt in months.
