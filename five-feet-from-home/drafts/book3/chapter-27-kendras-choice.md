# Chapter 27: Kendra's Choice

**POV: Kendra Miller**
**Location: Charlotte**

Kendra sat in her truck in the parking lot of the BSS facility, staring at her phone screen like it might explode. The text sat in her drafts folder, three words that felt simultaneously too small and too enormous for what she was trying to say.

"I need to talk."

Her thumb hovered over the send button. It was 7 AM on a Saturday, which meant Addison was probably already awake—she'd learned that Addison didn't really sleep in, even on weekends. But knowing someone was awake and actually reaching out to them were different things.

Kendra had been drafting and deleting versions of this message for three days.

The thing with Chris had started so casually that she almost hadn't noticed it happening. A conversation after training that lasted longer than it should have. Coffee that turned into breakfast. His hand on her lower back when he was spotting her during a heavy lift, staying there a beat longer than strictly necessary.

And then, two weeks ago, an actual date. Dinner at a restaurant that required reservations. Wine that cost more than twenty dollars a bottle. Conversation that went beyond training plans and macros and recovery protocols.

He'd kissed her in the parking lot afterward, and she'd let him. More than let him—she'd kissed him back.

That should have been simple. Guy likes girl, girl likes guy back, they date, everyone moves on with their lives. Except Kendra's life had stopped being simple the moment Addison Barrett had walked into it six months ago.

She deleted the draft and started again.

"Can we talk? Something I need to tell you."

Too ominous. Like she was about to confess to murder.

"Hey, got a minute? Want to run something by you."

Too casual, like she was asking about workout programming.

"I need to talk to you about Chris."

Too direct. But maybe direct was better. Maybe after everything that had happened between them—the nights in Addison's apartment, the careful distance they'd maintained since, the unspoken understanding that what they'd had was finite—maybe direct was the only honest option.

Kendra took a breath and hit send before she could overthink it anymore.

The reply came back in less than a minute: "Of course. My place? One hour?"

Kendra's stomach did something complicated. An hour. That gave her just enough time to spiral completely.

She drove home, showered, changed into clean clothes, and then stood in front of her closet having a minor crisis about whether her outfit choice mattered. It shouldn't matter. This was just a conversation between two people who used to sleep together and were now... what? Friends? Not quite. Colleagues? Not exactly. Two people navigating the complicated aftermath of something that had been intense and real and was now definitively over.

She settled on jeans and a hoodie—the universal uniform of someone trying not to try too hard.

The drive to Addison's apartment took twelve minutes. Kendra had made this trip enough times that her truck seemed to know the route on autopilot. But this time felt different. Final, somehow.

Addison answered the door in workout clothes, hair pulled back, no makeup. It was a version of her that Kendra had seen plenty of times before, but it still did something to her chest. Made her remember early mornings waking up tangled together, Addison's face soft with sleep.

"Hey," Addison said, stepping back to let her in. "Coffee?"

"Sure," Kendra said, grateful for something to do with her hands.

Addison's apartment was exactly as Kendra remembered—clean lines, expensive furniture, books on every surface. It had felt intimidating the first time she'd been here. Now it just felt like Addison. Controlled and precise and somehow still warm underneath all that carefully maintained order.

They sat on opposite ends of the couch with their coffee mugs, and Kendra was acutely aware of the deliberate space between them.

"So," Addison said. "Chris."

Kendra almost laughed at how well Addison could read her. "Yeah. Chris."

"You're dating," Addison said. Not a question.

"We're... something," Kendra said. "I don't know what to call it yet."

Addison nodded, her expression neutral in a way that Kendra had learned meant she was working very hard to keep it that way. "How long?"

"Officially? Two weeks. But it's been building for a while."

"I know," Addison said quietly.

Kendra looked at her. "You know?"

"I'm not blind, Kendra. I've seen the way you look at him. The way he looks at you." Addison sipped her coffee. "I've been expecting this conversation."

That should have made it easier, but somehow it made it harder. Because if Addison had been expecting it, that meant she'd been preparing for it. Building walls, reinforcing defenses.

"I didn't plan this," Kendra said. "I didn't... I wasn't looking for something with him."

"I know that too," Addison said. "But you found it anyway."

Kendra set her coffee mug down before she could grip it hard enough to break. "I need to try this. With Chris. For real."

"Okay."

"That's it? Just okay?"

Addison's composure cracked just slightly. "What do you want me to say, Kendra? That I'm happy for you? I am. That it's fine? It is. We were never going to be a long-term thing. We both knew that."

"Knowing it and living it are different," Kendra said.

"They are," Addison agreed. "But that doesn't change the reality. You and I—what we had was good. Really good. But it was also temporary. We both understood that from the beginning."

Kendra wanted to argue, wanted to say that understanding something intellectually and being okay with it emotionally were completely different things. But she also knew Addison was right. They'd never promised each other forever. They'd barely promised each other next week.

"Are we okay?" Kendra asked, and hated how small her voice sounded.

Addison was quiet for a moment. When she spoke, her voice was carefully controlled. "We'll always be okay. Different, but okay."

"Different how?"

"I don't know yet," Addison admitted. "We'll figure it out. But Kendra—" She paused, and Kendra saw something vulnerable flash across her face before she locked it down again. "If this is what you want, if Chris is what you want, then you should go for it. Don't hold back because you're worried about me or about what it means for us."

"I can't just stop caring about you," Kendra said.

"I'm not asking you to. I'm asking you to let yourself be happy with someone who can give you more than I can."

The weight of that statement settled between them. Because that was the truth neither of them had wanted to say out loud—Addison couldn't give her more. Not a real relationship, not a future, not the kind of partnership that looked like something beyond hotel rooms and stolen hours.

"What can Chris give me that you can't?" Kendra asked, even though she thought she knew the answer.

Addison considered the question seriously. "Simplicity. A relationship that doesn't require strategy or careful management. Someone whose job doesn't revolve around manipulating outcomes and managing power dynamics. Someone who can just... be with you, without calculating the optics or the complications."

"You make yourself sound mercenary," Kendra said.

"I'm realistic," Addison corrected. "My life is complicated, Kendra. My work requires things from me that don't leave a lot of room for uncomplicated relationships. And you deserve uncomplicated."

"What if I don't want uncomplicated?"

"Then that's your choice," Addison said. "But you should make it knowing what complicated actually means in my world. And I don't think you want that. Not really."

Kendra wanted to argue, but she couldn't. Because Addison was right—she didn't want complicated. She didn't want to be someone's secret or someone's liability or someone's calculated risk. She wanted simple. She wanted someone who looked at her and saw a future instead of a problem to be managed.

Chris looked at her like that. Like she was possibility instead of complication.

"He asked me to be his girlfriend," Kendra said. "Official, like high school style labels and everything."

Addison smiled, and it looked genuine. "What did you say?"

"I said I needed to think about it. And to have this conversation first."

"Why?"

"Because you matter," Kendra said simply. "What we had matters. And I didn't want to just... move on without talking to you about it."

Addison set down her coffee mug and turned to face Kendra more fully. "I appreciate that. Really. But Kendra, you don't need my permission to date someone. You don't owe me that."

"It's not about permission," Kendra said. "It's about respect. And closure, maybe."

"Closure," Addison repeated, like she was testing the word. "Is that what this is?"

"I don't know. Is it?"

Addison was quiet for a long moment. Then: "Do you remember what I told you that first night? After we slept together the first time?"

Kendra remembered. They'd been lying in Addison's bed, sheets tangled around them, and Addison had been uncharacteristically uncertain.

"You said you didn't know how to do this," Kendra said. "Casual, no expectations, no promises."

"I said I'd probably be terrible at it," Addison corrected. "And I was right. Because here we are, and I'm trying very hard to be okay with you choosing someone else, and I'm not entirely succeeding."

It was the most honest thing Addison had said all morning, and it cracked something open in Kendra's chest.

"You could ask me not to," Kendra said quietly. "Choose Chris, I mean. You could ask me to stay."

Addison's expression was pained. "I could. But I won't."

"Why not?"

"Because that would be selfish," Addison said. "And because I care about you enough to want you to be happy, even if that happiness doesn't include me."

Kendra felt tears prickling at the corners of her eyes and blinked them back. "I don't want to lose you."

"You won't," Addison said. She reached across the space between them and took Kendra's hand, just for a moment. "I'm still your strategist. Still invested in your career. Still someone you can talk to. That doesn't change."

"But the rest of it does."

"The rest of it does," Addison agreed. "But that's okay. It has to be okay, because the alternative is holding onto something that isn't sustainable and resenting each other for it eventually."

Kendra knew she was right. But knowing it didn't make it hurt less.

"So what now?" Kendra asked.

"Now you go tell Chris yes," Addison said. "You let yourself have a real relationship with someone who can be present for it. You train for semifinals and make the CrossFit Games and live your life."

"And you?"

"I do my job," Addison said. "I keep building my career and proving I can handle the complicated stuff. And I'm genuinely happy for you, Kendra. I am. Even if it's also hard."

They sat in silence for a while, the morning light streaming through Addison's windows and illuminating all the dust motes floating in the air. Kendra thought about all the mornings she'd spent in this apartment, in Addison's bed, in the careful bubble they'd created where the outside world couldn't touch them.

"I should go," Kendra said finally.

Addison nodded. "Yeah."

They stood, and for a moment they just looked at each other. Then Kendra stepped forward and hugged her, and Addison hugged her back, and it felt like goodbye even though they'd both just said it wasn't.

"Thank you," Kendra said into Addison's shoulder. "For being honest. For not making this harder than it had to be."

"Thank you for telling me," Addison said. "For not just ghosting or letting me find out from someone else."

When they pulled apart, Kendra could see that Addison's eyes were bright, but she wasn't crying. Controlled, even now.

Kendra left the apartment and sat in her truck for a few minutes, processing. Then she pulled out her phone and texted Chris: "Yes. Official girlfriend status accepted."

His reply came back immediately: "Yeah? You sure?"

Kendra smiled despite the tightness in her chest. "I'm sure."

"Dinner tonight? Celebrate properly?"

"Dinner sounds good."

She started her truck and pulled out of the parking lot, catching one last glimpse of Addison's building in her rearview mirror. It felt like closing a chapter. Not erasing it—she'd never want to erase what she and Addison had shared—but acknowledging that it was complete.

That afternoon, she met Chris at the facility for a training session. He grinned when he saw her, open and uncomplicated in a way that made her chest feel lighter.

"Girlfriend," he said, testing out the word.

"Boyfriend," she replied, and it felt strange and right at the same time.

They trained together, and it was easy. No weight of unspoken things, no careful navigation of boundaries. Just two people who liked each other, working out side by side.

Later, over dinner at a casual Italian place, Chris asked, "You okay? You seem a little quiet."

"I talked to Addison this morning," Kendra said. "Told her about us."

Chris nodded slowly. "How'd that go?"

"It was... good. Hard, but good. She was really understanding."

"She's classy," Chris said. "I'll give her that."

"She is," Kendra agreed.

"Are you okay with it?" Chris asked. "Moving on, I mean?"

Kendra considered the question honestly. "I think so. It's weird, letting go of something that was good. But it's also right. You and me—this feels right."

Chris reached across the table and took her hand. "It does."

And sitting there, in a restaurant full of other people having normal Saturday night dates, Kendra felt something settle in her chest. Not the intensity she'd felt with Addison—that had been like standing too close to a fire, exhilarating and dangerous. This was different. Steadier. Sustainable.

She thought about what Addison had said about Chris being able to give her simplicity. At the time, it had felt like a criticism. But now she understood it was a gift. Simplicity wasn't boring. It was breathing room. It was a relationship that didn't require constant negotiation or careful risk management.

It was exactly what she needed.

That night, lying in bed in her own apartment, Kendra's phone buzzed with a text from Addison: "Good luck in Knoxville. You're going to kill semifinals."

Kendra smiled and typed back: "Thanks. For everything."

Three dots appeared, then: "Anytime."

And that was it. The chapter closed, the new one beginning. Kendra felt the loss of what she and Addison had been, but she also felt the possibility of what she and Chris could become.

Different, but okay. That's what Addison had said.

Kendra was learning to believe it.
