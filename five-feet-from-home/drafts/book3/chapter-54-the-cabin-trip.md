# Chapter 54: The Cabin Trip

**POV:** Addison
**Location:** Blue Ridge Mountains
**Book:** Book 3 - The Expansion

---

The cabin is exactly as Addison remembers it.

Tucked into the side of a mountain two hours north of Charlotte, all weathered wood and stone chimneys, windows that look out over ridges layered blue and purple in the distance. It belongs to Sara's family—has for three generations—and it's the kind of place that demands you unplug. No cell service. No Wi-Fi. Just mountains and silence and the occasional black bear ambling through the yard.

Addison pulls into the gravel driveway and cuts the engine. Beside her, Kendra unbuckles her seatbelt and stares up at the cabin through the windshield.

"This is gorgeous," Kendra says.

"Wait until you see the view from the deck," Addison says, climbing out.

The back door opens and Harper emerges, dragging a suitcase that's way too big for a two-night trip. "Little help?"

Addison jogs over and grabs the other handle. "What did you pack, your entire closet?"

"I didn't know what we'd need," Harper says defensively. "Sara said to bring layers."

"Layers, not your whole wardrobe."

They haul the suitcase up the porch steps just as Sara appears in the doorway, wiping her hands on a dish towel. She looks more relaxed than Addison's seen her in months—hair in a messy bun, wearing leggings and an oversized flannel, barefoot.

"You made it," Sara says, grinning. "I was starting to think you got lost."

"Kendra made us stop three times," Harper says.

"I had to pee!" Kendra protests, appearing on the porch with her own duffel bag. "Addison wouldn't let me bring coffee in the car."

"Because you spill coffee in every car you've ever been in," Addison says.

Kendra flips her off, but she's smiling.

Sara laughs and holds the door open. "Come on. I've got wine chilling and dinner in the oven. We're doing this right."

---

The inside of the cabin is all warm wood and stone, a river-rock fireplace dominating one wall, mismatched furniture that's comfortable rather than stylish. There's a long pine table in the dining area, a kitchen that smells like garlic and rosemary, and a deck that wraps around the back with Adirondack chairs facing the mountains.

Addison drops her bag by the stairs and exhales. She didn't realize how tense she was until just now—shoulders tight, jaw clenched, the constant low-grade hum of stress that's been her baseline for months.

This is the first time she's been alone with these women since New York.

Since the night she and Kendra kissed.

Since everything changed.

"So," Harper says, dropping onto the couch. She's wearing her engagement ring—Addison still can't get used to seeing it—and she's glowing in that annoying way people do when they're stupidly happy. "House rules. No work talk. No checking emails. No sneaking off to get cell service."

"Says the woman who probably has a hotspot hidden in her suitcase," Sara says, pouring wine into four mismatched glasses.

"I do not," Harper says. Then, after a beat: "It's in my toiletry bag."

Kendra laughs, accepting a glass from Sara. "This is going to be a disaster."

"The best kind of disaster," Addison says, and raises her glass. "To girls' trips and bad decisions."

"I'll drink to that," Sara says.

They clink glasses, and Addison takes a long sip. The wine is good—crisp and cold, something French that Sara probably picked up specifically for this trip. Everything about this weekend is intentional. A pause. A breath. A chance for the four of them to remember who they are when they're not drowning in work and obligations and the weight of expectation.

Addison just hopes they can pull it off.

---

Dinner is chicken roasted with lemon and herbs, roasted vegetables, crusty bread that Sara made this morning. They eat on the deck, even though it's cool enough that they need blankets draped over their laps. The sun's setting behind the mountains, painting the sky in shades of peach and lavender.

It's perfect.

It's also awkward in ways Addison can't quite name.

Harper's talking about wedding planning—venues and guest lists and whether Ethan's family will be offended if they elope. Sara's asking questions, engaged and enthusiastic. Kendra's quieter than usual, picking at her chicken, her eyes flicking to Addison every few minutes.

Addison keeps her gaze on her plate.

"So, Addie," Sara says, refilling her wine glass. "How's the title-holder life treating you?"

Addison looks up. "Busy. Good. Intense."

"That's it? That's all we get?" Harper leans forward, grinning. "Come on. You won the biggest competition of your career. You're coaching full-time now. Spill."

Addison shrugs. "It's everything I wanted. It's also exhausting."

"Are you training for next year's comp?" Kendra asks. Her voice is careful. Neutral.

"Probably not," Addison admits. "I think I'm done competing. I want to focus on coaching."

Harper raises her glass. "To knowing when to pivot."

"To pivoting," Sara echoes.

They drink, and the conversation shifts—Sara talking about her kids, Harper complaining about a difficult client in London. But underneath it all, Addison can feel the thing no one's saying.

New York.

The kiss.

The way Kendra looked at her the morning after, like she was trying to memorize something she knew she couldn't keep.

Addison drains her wine glass and reaches for the bottle.

---

Later, after the dishes are done and the fire's crackling in the living room, they settle in with more wine and a deck of cards. Sara suggests poker. Harper vetoes in favor of Uno. They compromise on Rummy, which none of them are good at.

"This game makes no sense," Kendra says, staring at her cards.

"You're just bad at it," Harper says.

"You're literally losing."

"Irrelevant."

Addison laughs despite herself. This is what she needed—the easy banter, the ridiculous arguments over card games, the way they can just *be* together without it meaning something.

Except it does mean something.

Kendra's sitting across from her, cross-legged on the rug, hair pulled into a loose ponytail. She's in joggers and a hoodie that's definitely Chris's, and every time she laughs, Addison's chest does something complicated.

"Earth to Addie," Sara says, waving a hand in front of her face. "It's your turn."

"Right. Sorry." Addison discards and draws, not really paying attention.

Harper studies her. "You okay?"

"Fine. Just tired."

"Bullshit," Harper says, but she's smiling. "You've been weird all night."

"I haven't—"

"You have," Sara agrees. "So has Kendra, actually."

Kendra's head snaps up. "What? No, I haven't."

"You've barely said ten words since we got here," Harper points out. "And you keep looking at Addie like she's about to vanish."

Addison's stomach drops. Kendra's face flushes red.

"I do not—"

"It's fine," Addison cuts in, setting her cards down. "Can we just play the game?"

Harper and Sara exchange a look, but they let it drop.

The game continues, but the ease is gone. Addison can feel the weight of Harper's gaze, Sara's quiet scrutiny. They know something's off. They just don't know what.

And Addison's not ready to tell them.

Not yet.

Maybe not ever.

---

They call it a night around eleven.

Harper and Sara head upstairs to the two guest bedrooms, leaving Addison and Kendra to claim the pullout couch in the living room. It's big enough that they won't have to share, but it's still too close. Too intimate.

Addison helps Kendra make up the bed—fitted sheet, blankets, pillows that smell like cedar and lavender. The fire's died down to embers, casting flickering shadows across the walls.

"This is nice," Kendra says quietly. "The cabin. This whole trip."

"Yeah," Addison agrees. "It is."

Kendra smooths the blanket, not looking at her. "I'm glad we're here. All of us. It's been too long."

Addison knows what she means. Since New York, they've all been orbiting each other—too busy, too careful, too aware of the thing they're not talking about.

"Ken," Addison starts, then stops.

Kendra looks up. "Yeah?"

Addison wants to say something. Anything. To acknowledge what's sitting between them like a third person in the room. But she doesn't know how. Doesn't know what it would change.

"Nothing," Addison says finally. "Just... I'm glad you're here too."

Kendra holds her gaze for a long moment. Then she nods and climbs under the covers on her side of the bed.

Addison does the same, turning off the lamp and settling into the darkness.

The cabin is quiet except for the settling of old wood and the distant hoot of an owl. Upstairs, she can hear the faint murmur of Harper and Sara talking, their voices too low to make out words.

Kendra's breathing evens out quickly. She's always been able to fall asleep anywhere.

Addison lies awake, staring at the ceiling, hyper-aware of the space between them. Six inches. Maybe less. Close enough to reach across. Close enough to touch.

But she doesn't.

Instead, she closes her eyes and tries not to think about New York. About the way Kendra tasted. About the way they fit together, like two pieces of a puzzle Addison didn't know she was trying to solve.

Tries not to think about what it means that she's here, in a cabin in the mountains, lying three feet from the woman she can't stop thinking about.

The woman who has a boyfriend.

The woman who's building a life that doesn't include her.

Addison pulls the blanket tighter and tells herself this weekend will be fine.

They'll hike. They'll drink wine. They'll laugh and remember why they're friends.

And then they'll go home.

And everything will go back to normal.

She almost believes it.

---

The next morning dawns cold and bright.

Addison wakes to the smell of coffee and the sound of Sara banging around in the kitchen. Kendra's already up, her side of the bed empty and neatly made. Through the window, Addison can see her on the deck, mug in hand, staring out at the mountains.

Harper shuffles downstairs in pajama pants and a Harvard sweatshirt, hair sticking up in every direction. "Coffee?"

"Please," Addison says, sitting up.

Harper pours her a mug and hands it over. "Sleep okay?"

"Fine."

Harper gives her a look that says she doesn't believe it, but she doesn't push. Instead, she settles onto the couch with her own coffee, tucking her feet under her.

"Sara wants to hike to the waterfall today," Harper says. "Apparently it's only three miles."

"Only three miles," Addison repeats. "When was the last time you hiked three miles?"

"I work out six days a week."

"In a gym. With air conditioning."

Harper throws a pillow at her. "I can hike three miles."

Sara appears from the kitchen, holding a spatula. "Breakfast in ten. And yes, Harper, you can absolutely hike three miles. It's mostly downhill."

"Mostly?"

"Well. Until the way back."

Harper groans. "I'm going to die."

"You're not going to die," Kendra says, coming in from the deck. Her cheeks are pink from the cold, and she's smiling—the first real smile Addison's seen from her since they arrived. "But you might regret those heels you wore yesterday."

"They were wedges," Harper protests. "Very practical wedges."

"For a boardroom, maybe," Sara says. "Not for hiking."

They dissolve into laughter, and for a moment, it feels easy again. Like they're just four friends on a weekend trip, no complications, no secrets.

Addison lets herself believe it.

Just for a little while.

---

The hike is beautiful and brutal in equal measure.

The trail winds through dense forest, then opens onto rocky overlooks with views that stretch for miles. The air smells like pine and damp earth, and the only sounds are their footsteps and the occasional birdcall.

Harper complains for the first mile, then goes quiet, focused on not twisting an ankle. Sara leads the way, pointing out wildflowers and rock formations, clearly in her element. Kendra keeps pace beside Addison, and they fall into an easy rhythm—breathing hard on the uphills, catching their breath on the flat stretches.

"This is incredible," Kendra says, pausing at an overlook. The valley spreads out below them, a patchwork of green and gold.

"Yeah," Addison agrees. She's standing close enough to Kendra that their arms are almost touching. Close enough that she could reach out, take her hand, pull her closer.

She doesn't.

Instead, she takes a sip from her water bottle and keeps walking.

The waterfall, when they reach it, is worth the effort. It tumbles over moss-covered rocks into a clear pool, the sound of it loud and soothing all at once. Sara immediately pulls off her shoes and wades in, shrieking at the cold.

"You're insane!" Harper yells.

"It's amazing!" Sara yells back. "Get in here!"

Harper shakes her head, but she's grinning. Kendra glances at Addison.

"You going in?"

"Hell no," Addison says. "That water's probably forty degrees."

Kendra laughs. "Yeah, me neither."

They sit on the rocks, watching Sara splash around like a kid while Harper takes approximately a thousand photos on her phone.

"This was a good idea," Kendra says quietly.

"Yeah," Addison says. "It was."

Kendra turns to look at her, and for a second, the world narrows to just the two of them. The waterfall fades. Harper and Sara disappear.

There's only Kendra, looking at her with something unreadable in her eyes.

"Addie—"

"We should head back," Addison says abruptly, standing. "It's getting late."

Kendra's face shutters. "Yeah. Okay."

They hike back in near silence.

---

That night, they cook badly.

Or rather, Harper and Addison cook badly while Sara tries to supervise and Kendra drinks wine and offers unhelpful commentary.

"That's way too much garlic," Kendra says, watching Addison dump half a bulb into the pasta sauce.

"There's no such thing as too much garlic," Addison shoots back.

"That's going to burn," Sara warns, pointing at Harper's chicken.

"It's fine," Harper insists. It is not fine. Two minutes later, smoke is billowing from the pan and the smoke detector is shrieking.

Kendra nearly falls off her stool laughing.

They end up salvaging most of dinner—the pasta's edible, the chicken's only slightly charred—and they eat on the deck again, wrapped in blankets, the mountains dark shapes against a sky full of stars.

"I forgot how many stars you can see out here," Harper says, tipping her head back.

"No light pollution," Sara says. "It's one of my favorite things about this place."

They sit in comfortable silence, passing the wine bottle around.

Addison feels the weight in her chest loosen, just slightly. This is good. This is what she needed.

But underneath the laughter and the wine and the easy conversation, New York is everywhere.

In the way Kendra's hand brushes hers when they reach for the same glass.

In the way Addison catches herself watching Kendra's profile against the firelight.

In the way they both look away when their eyes meet.

Tomorrow night, Addison thinks. Tomorrow night by the fire, when it's just her and Kendra and the truth they've been avoiding.

Tomorrow.

---

**End of Chapter 54**
